Briareus.jl

Briareus computes laser deposition, view-factor radiation transport and drive symmetry for laser-driven high-energy-density experiments: indirect-drive hohlraums, backlighter foils and other laser-irradiated targets. It covers the calculations that a view-factor code such as VisRad is used for and exchanges files with VisRad. It adds gradient-based optimization of pointing, defocus, power balance and wavelength detuning; reduced laser–plasma corrections (backscatter, cross-beam energy transfer, inner-beam absorption); a time-dependent drive with a moving wall, closing entrance holes and a synthetic Dante; and shot-review checks of worst-case beam clearance, NIF's target-configuration rules and unconverted light.

pkg> add Briareus

A first calculation evaluates a NIF hohlraum at an illustrative cone pointing (aim height z and radial offset r per cone, mm; an example, not a shot's pointing), then moves the inner and outer rings of cones along the axis and re-balances their power to null the capsule flux's P2 and P4 at the same mean flux, with the beams kept clear of the entrance-hole rims and the capsule. Once the package has loaded, the scenario builds in 2.5 s on 16 threads and the optimization takes 5 s on its first call (mostly compilation) and 1 s after:

using Briareus

target = hohlraum(length = 10.1, radius = 2.875, leh_radius = 1.55, capsule_radius = 0.9)
cones = GroupPointing(nif_quads(power = 8.0); fields = (:z, :r))
quads = apply(cones, parameters(cones; inner23 = (z = 3.09, r = -1.09),
                                inner30 = (z = 3.63, r = -0.60),
                                outer44 = (z = 5.20,), outer50 = (z = 5.01,)))

ring(b) = b.group in (:inner23, :inner30) ? :inner : :outer
pointing = GroupPointing(quads; by = ring, fields = (:z, :power), relative = true)
scenario = Scenario(target, pointing; observe = :capsule)

x0 = initial_parameters(pointing)    # no aim shift, 8 TW per quad
report(evaluate(scenario, x0))       # 384 TW, Tr = 262.7 eV, P2/P0 = -15.737 %

drive = mean_flux(evaluate(scenario, x0))    # mean capsule flux, held fixed
keepout = (ApertureKeepOut(target), SphereKeepOut(target))
objective = symmetry_objective(modes = (2 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0); drive, keepout)
lower, upper = bounds(pointing, x0; power = 0.6)     # ±1 mm of aim, ±60 % of power
result = optimize_pointing(scenario, objective; x0, lower, upper)  # 20 iterations
report(evaluate(scenario, result.x)) # 422.1 TW, P2/P0 = -0.000 %, P4/P0 = +0.000 %

The starting P2/P0 of −15.7 % is expected: equal power per quad puts 1/3 of it on the inner cones, near HYBRID-E's peak cone fraction, and a static solve without cross-beam energy transfer or the hohlraum's dynamics is not balanced there. The N221204 benchmark has a static P2/P0 of −26 % at 5.5 ns; the shot's balance comes from wavelength-detuned transfer, wall motion, the gold bubble and closing entrance holes, which drive_history models. The optimum lowers the inner-cone fraction to 0.15 and stops where the 30° beams reach the entrance-hole keep-out.

The model in brief

  • Targets are assembled from meshed surfaces (cylinders, annuli, spheres, rectangles, frusta) or imported CAD. Each patch side that radiates is an element of the radiation model. Curved components carry the exact surface their mesh approximates, and the radiation model integrates over it. Laser entrance holes are transparent apertures that record the flux escaping through them.
  • Beams enter from facility ports with elliptical super-Gaussian phase-plate spots, an f-number cone, pointing and defocus. A precomputed quadrature over the cone directions makes deposition a smooth function of those parameters, so derivatives come from automatic differentiation. An energy-conserving ray trace is the reference.
  • Radiation is diffuse, gray or multigroup. View factors account for occlusion, and a radiosity solve gives the incident flux on every element. Wall albedo can depend on time and on the local radiation temperature. Because the system is linear in the laser source, the response of the capsule flux to deposition is computed once and reused by optimizers.
  • Symmetry is measured by Legendre and real spherical-harmonic moments, integrated exactly over each facet's solid angle.
  • Time dependence is quasi-static: at each time, the radiosity is solved for the deposition, the wall state and the geometry of that time.

Glossary

  • View factor $F_{ij}$: the fraction of the diffuse radiation leaving element $i$ that arrives at element $j$, set by geometry and occlusion alone.
  • Radiosity: the power per unit area leaving a surface, emission plus reflection (re-emission, for a hohlraum wall). With the view factors it closes a linear system for the flux on every element; Integrating sphere solves the case with an exact answer.
  • Albedo: the fraction of the incident x-ray flux a wall re-emits. Gold hohlraum walls reach 0.8–0.9 at peak drive.
  • LEH: the laser entrance hole at each end of a hohlraum. It admits the beams and loses the x rays that reach it.
  • P2/P0 (and Pₗ/P0): the ratio of the capsule flux's Legendre moment of degree $l$ to its mean. P2 > 0 is pole-hot, P2 < 0 waist-hot. Real spherical harmonics $Y_{lm}$ extend this to three-dimensional asymmetry, such as mode 1 from a missing bundle.
  • Cone fraction: the inner cones' share of the total laser power on NIF. It is the main control of P2 through the pulse.
  • CBET: cross-beam energy transfer, the exchange of power between crossing beams through ion-acoustic waves in the LEH plasma. A wavelength detuning Δλ between cones sets its direction and size. See Laser–plasma models.
  • Dante: the absolutely calibrated, filtered x-ray diode array that measures the radiation temperature seen through an LEH.

Lindl et al., Phys. Plasmas 11, 339 (2004) review indirect-drive hohlraum physics; the tutorials cite the primary sources for each topic.

Units and conventions

Units are mm, ns, TW (TW·ns = kJ), TW/mm² for flux (to_Wcm2 multiplies by 10¹⁴) and eV for temperature. Port angles are in degrees: polar angle from +z, azimuth counterclockwise from +x, which on NIF points to Switchyard 1.

A beam's direction is fixed by its port ($\mathbf{k} = -\hat{\mathbf{p}}$), and pointing translates its axis through aim. Best focus is at aim + defocus·k: positive defocus moves the focus along the beam, as in VisRad and the NIF CMT. Spot axes are given in the port's ($\hat\theta$, $\hat\phi$) frame.

Away from focus, the spot is the focal profile convolved with the f-cone. The deposition operator samples that convolution at the cone directions: each surface point receives, from each cone direction that reaches it unobstructed, the focal profile where that ray crosses the best-focus plane, weighted by the ray's own incidence angle. Power is conserved at any distance from focus and incidence, and shadows near focus are correlated with the part of the spot they block.
