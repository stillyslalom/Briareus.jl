Radiation

view_factors computes occlusion-aware view factors (the exact point-to-polygon kernel near the emitter, adaptive toward shared edges; about 0.4 s for a 3,000-element hohlraum), and solve the gray diffuse radiosity. For closed enclosures, closed = true removes discretization leakage while keeping reciprocity. Wall albedo can be constant, tabulated in time, or the Marshak wall-loss scaling WallLossAlbedo, iterated to self-consistency with the local radiation temperature. flux_response precomputes the linear map from deposition to capsule flux, so optimizers never re-solve. Large models are never densified: above 10⁴ elements the view factors are a Float32Matrix with iterative solves, above 1.5 × 10⁴ an HMatrix. spectral_solve adds multigroup radiosity about a gray solution, with the laser-produced gold M-band.

drive_history follows the drive in time from a PulseShape per cone, quad or beam. Each wall element loses energy by its temperature history and re-emits stored energy when the drive falls; the wall moves (WallMotion), the entrance holes close as prescribed (GapClosure) or computed (LipClosure), and the capsule absorbs on a shrinking radius (CapsuleAlbedo). dante1 and dante_unfold record the drive as the Dante instrument would. retune_drive finds the power histories that hold a drive quantity through time under per-key power limits. The quantity is the modes of a capsule (CapsuleModes), the x-ray flux of a flat face (FaceFlux) or the laser irradiance of a backlighter foil (FoilIrradiance), held either to the values of a reference shot or uniform at a prescribed level. With NIF bundle 31 down, retuning the other 46 quads takes the mode-1 drive asymmetry from 2% to 0.02% at the peak.

Briareus.Float32Matrix — Type
Float32Matrix(A::AbstractMatrix)
Float32Matrix(undef, m, n)

A dense matrix stored in Float32 that behaves as a Float64 matrix. Indexing returns the stored value widened, and products with Float64 vectors and matrices (*, mul!, also transposed) accumulate in Float64. A product is therefore exactly the Float64 product with the matrix Float64.(A.data), and the only error against a Float64 original is the rounding of each entry (relative ≤ 2⁻²⁴ ≈ 6e-8). The type halves the memory of a view-factor matrix. It is used by view_factors(target; storage = Float32), and by RadiationModel by default for more than Briareus.LARGE_MODEL[] elements (research notes, "Large radiation models").

Matrix–vector products are threaded over row blocks (column blocks when transposed) and are memory-bound. Matrix–matrix products widen column panels of the matrix to Float64 and pass them to BLAS.

Briareus.view_factors — Method
view_factors(target; near = 3.0, level = 1, visibility = target.visibility, visibility_grid = 4,
             storage = Float64, tol, eta, leaf, maxrank) -> Matrix{Float64}, Float32Matrix or HMatrix

Dense view-factor matrix F[i, j], the fraction of diffuse radiation leaving element i that arrives at element j. Reciprocity Aᵢ Fᵢⱼ = Aⱼ Fⱼᵢ holds exactly by construction, and rows sum to one for a closed enclosure (counting apertures); see closure_error. storage = Float32 assembles into a Float32Matrix instead, with half the memory, each factor rounded to single precision and products in Float64. storage = :hmatrix assembles a hierarchical HMatrix, with near cluster pairs dense and far ones of uniform visibility low rank (keywords tol, eta, leaf, maxrank; Briareus.HMATRIX_DEFAULTS). On a hohlraum its assembly and storage grow as N^1.3–1.6 instead of N², and at 17–29k elements it is 2–3× faster and 5–8× smaller.

Flat elements use the exact differential-area-to-polygon view factor onto the other element, integrated over the emitter. For pairs closer than near element sizes, the integral uses 4^level sub-triangle points; the element size is √2 × the largest centroid-to-vertex distance, the side of a square element. This rule stays accurate for elements that share an edge, such as at the wall–cap corner of a hohlraum. Beyond that distance the integral uses the centroid, averaged over the two directions. A near pair of flat elements (not on a curved surface) that lie wholly in front of each other takes their exact exchange instead, the double contour integral $A_i F_{ij} = \frac{1}{2π}\oint_i\oint_j \ln r\, d\mathbf{l}_i\cdot d\mathbf{l}_j$ over their outlines, to about 1e-13. For an element folded across several facets, the outlines are those of the facets themselves. Elements that share an edge then cost about 100× less than by the point rule, which also leaves an error of about 5e-4 on them.

Elements of a component with a CurvedSurface are integrated over the curved patch, with quadrature points on the true surface with its normals and areas. Between two curved elements the integral is point to point: 4^level sub-triangle points per triangle when near, one per triangle out to three times that distance, and the projected centroid beyond. Onto a planar partner the integral uses the exact polygon. Elements of a DiskSurface are exact polar sectors, planar receivers whose arcs are divided finely. Some near pairs lie about one axis and wholly in front of each other: a sector with another sector or with a chord rectangle of a cylinder seen from inside. Examples are an end cap and the wall at its corner, the annuli and slot of a waist gap, and an LEH disk and its bore. Such a pair takes the double contour above with the arcs exact, to about 1e-12, where the point rule left 1e-3 to 4e-2. A curved concave element also has a view of itself, which gives the diagonal Fᵢᵢ. Flat facets standing in for a curved wall misrepresent the exchange between nearby elements by an amount of order one relative to its size, which matters next to a concentrated source such as a laser spot.

Elements of more than two facets (remeshed CAD, remesh_mesh) are not integrated facet by facet. A flat element receives through its outline, which gives exactly the sum over its facets within round-off. Where the tangent plane of the point cuts the element, it receives piece by piece, each piece being its facets in one plane. Near pairs integrate over cells that lump the facets of the emitter, of side size / (2^(level+2) √2) before refining toward the receiver, or 2^(level+1) on a curved surface. A pair then costs a few times a two-facet pair whatever the facet count of the emitter. The facet count of the receiver still matters, since the factor of each point sums its outline, or its pieces when the tangent plane of the point cuts it. An element on a finely faceted curved wall, with hundreds of sliver facets, tens of pieces and a jagged outline of hundreds of edges, therefore costs tens of times more per near pair. Such parts should be given their fitted surface (curved_components), and they are then integrated on the curved surface instead.

Visibility is a single centroid-to-centroid ray between the flat facets. The facets of a curved surface (cylinder, sphere or cone) do not block a ray between two points inside it, since the true surface cannot. On curved elements the centroid facing test is relaxed by the spread of the true normals across the flat facet. Pairs that the curved patches exchange across are therefore kept, since a general triangle on a cylinder has its plane tilted axially by about its size over 2R. The point kernels apply the exact facing per point.

A pair with only a partial view of each other, past the rim of an entrance-hole bore or a gap or around the capsule, is all or nothing by its centroid ray. visibility = :sampled estimates the visible fraction instead; it is the default for targets with rims (cad_target, visrad_target). Up to four probe rays per element, from points near its extremes to the centroid of the other element, test whether the pair straddles an occluder. If they all agree with the centroid ray, the factor is identical to the :centroid one. Otherwise, rays between the visibility_grid² sample points of each element give the kernel-weighted visible fraction, which scales the unobstructed factor and keeps reciprocity. The samples lump the area of the element into a g × g grid of its tangent plane. On an element folded across more than two facets (remeshed CAD), the samples are put on its facets, and its facets do not occlude them. On a hohlraum with 0.1 mm LEH bores and a 20 µm waist gap at 0.5 mm, the sampling brings the largest row-sum error before balancing from 1.0% to 0.19% on the walls and from 5.9% to 0.7% on the LEH apertures, for 2–4× the time. Features narrower than a sample cell, such as the slot of the gap, are not resolved (research notes, "Partial visibility"). Against a Monte Carlo radiosity on the same facets, it reduces the balanced capsule a₂/a₀ error there from 0.51 to 0.31 pp at 0.5 mm, and from 0.30 to 0.14 pp at 0.25 mm (research notes, "Monte Carlo radiosity reference").

Briareus.HMatrix — Type
HMatrix

View factors in hierarchical form, from view_factors(target; storage = :hmatrix) and by default from RadiationModel above Briareus.HMATRIX_MODEL[] elements. The form stores the symmetric exchange matrix G = diag(A) F (Gᵢⱼ = Aᵢ Fᵢⱼ), once per unordered pair so that reciprocity is exact, partitioned over a cluster tree of the elements. Then F = diag(A)⁻¹ S G S with a diagonal balancing S, and enforce_closure! updates only S. The type is an AbstractMatrix{Float64}. Products with Float64 vectors and matrices (*, mul!, also transposed, and with dual vectors) accumulate in Float64, which is all that the iterative radiosity path uses. Indexing one entry searches the blocks, for inspection only, and Matrix(H) expands the matrix by products with the identity.

Clusters split the elements by the surface they lie on (the curved surface of a component, or the component). They then bisect the centroids along the longest axis of their box down to leaf elements. No cluster therefore mixes capsule and wall, or cap and wall at a corner; such blocks seldom compress, and their low-rank error falls on the small rows. A pair of clusters whose boxes are eta × the larger diameter apart is a candidate for low rank, decided block by block:

  • Rows and columns whose elements face no centroid of the other cluster are zero, by a box test. A block that a convex closed part, such as a capsule, hides wholly is also zero. The test is that the inscribed ball blocks every segment between the boxes, by a cone test from each corner.

  • The shaft between the boxes, their convex hull, is searched for facets that could occlude a segment between them (the shaft culling of Haines & Wallace). The search excludes the facets of a curved surface that both clusters lie inside, of a convex part that one of them lies on, and of a plane that both lie in front of or in. An empty shaft makes every facing pair wholly visible, so its entries need no ray, and the block is cross-approximated. The approximation is ACA with partial pivoting on the exact pair kernel, recompressed by SVD to tol relative Frobenius error and checked on sampled entries. A block that does not compress within maxrank terms is refined.

  • A shaft with facets of only one convex part, such as the capsule with both clusters outside its circumscribed ball, resolves each pair exactly by the balls of the part. A segment that misses the circumscribed ball is visible, one through the inscribed ball is hidden, and only the few between take a ray. With the resulting mask the block is zero, low rank without further rays, or refined with the mask down to dense leaf pairs at a shadow edge.

  • Any other occupied shaft is refined down to leaf pairs. A leaf pair whose shaft contains facets of up to 8 parts with a certificate is decided in the same way, pair by pair and exactly as the ray would decide it. The certificates are:

    • for planar parts such as washers, LEH caps and discs, the point where the segment crosses the plane of the part, inside or outside its facets with margin;
    • for a cylinder or cone part, its axisymmetric bounds. A prism of facets parallel to its axis, such as a wall of chord rectangles, is also decided by the side lines of its cross-section. A segment qualifies when its part within the axial range of the prism stays inside the half-plane of every side, as a segment through a waist gap onto the wall does;
    • for a convex part, its balls or the plane of an end element lying on it.

    Other leaf pairs take the visibility ray of every pair. Wholly hidden pairs give zero, wholly visible ones a cross approximation, and partly visible ones, such as at a rim or with :sampled fractions, stay dense. With visibility = :sampled the certificates resolve the whole bundle of sample rays of a pair at once, with margins grown by the spread of the samples, and otherwise each of its rays. The fractions are those of view_factors.

  • Pairs that the facing slack admits, whose rays start farther off curved facets, are judged on their ray ends when the exclusions of the shaft hold with that margin, and take the ray otherwise.

  • Rows cut by the facing test, at the terminator of a sphere, are refined against the children of the column cluster beside the low-rank rest. They are low rank on the rows that face a child wholly, and dense against a leaf or when few. A small block with only a few facing pairs is stored on those pairs.

Near pairs are dense (Float32), with the same entries as view_factors (_vf_pair). Blocks whose entries are all zero are not stored.

On the hohlraum presets (10–29k elements) the default tol = 1e-6 changes the capsule moments by ≤ 1e-8 and the incident flux by ≤ 2e-6 relative per element against the Float32 matrix or a tighter HMatrix (research notes, "Hierarchical view factors" and, for the surface-split cluster tree and tol, "The H-matrix's N^1.5 remainder"; for the zero blocks and cut rows, "H-matrix near field"; for the visibility certificates, "H-matrix visibility certificates").

Briareus.Enclosure — Type
Enclosure

Result of enclosure: the reduced target, and how it was cut from source.

  • target: the components of source cut to their kept patches (components left empty are dropped).
  • index[i]: the element of source that element i of target is.
  • kept[e]: whether element e of source passed the selection. Both sides of a two-sided patch are kept when either passes, so target can contain sides with kept false.
  • flux[e]: the relative flux on element e of source that determined the selection (NaN for elements beyond radius, which cast no rays); see enclosure.
  • escape: the fraction of the rays from the point that leave the bounding sphere or reach infinity, which is the solid-angle fraction of the openings seen from the point.
  • open_area: Σ over kept elements of area × the fraction of the rays of the element that escape (mm²). By reciprocity this estimates the open area of the cavity, that is, its entrance holes where no aperture or window closes them.
  • leak_area: the same sum over rays that end on dropped elements (mm²), which is the radiation that the kept set sends to surfaces that the reduced target no longer has.
  • return_area: the radiation that those surfaces would send back, Σ over kept elements of area × the mean over the rays of the element of albedo × flux at the dropped element hit (mm², in units of the reference flux). It is the error of the cut, to compare with open_area, which leaves through the openings, and with the area of the cavity.
  • back_area: the sum over rays that end on one-sided surfaces seen from behind (mm²), which also absorb without radiating in the full target.
  • back: the fraction of the rays from the point whose first hit was a one-sided surface seen from behind. It is 1 for a point inside the capsule, and otherwise a sign of wrongly oriented surfaces.
  • rays: the rays cast per element.

Briareus.cad_target — Method
cad_target(parts; orient = true, toward = (0, 0, 0), inside = nothing,
           remesh = nothing, coarsen = nothing, fit = remesh !== nothing, material = gold(),
           two_sided = false, visibility = :sampled, closed = false, enclosure_kw = (;)) -> Target

A radiation-ready target from CAD meshes in one call. It does what the Radiation in an imported CAD hohlraum tutorial does by hand, and what visrad_target does with the CAD that a VisRad workspace bundles. parts contains name => mesh pairs, the CAD parts (as read_stl_solids returns them), and Components, taken as they are, such as primitives, apertures and parts prepared by hand. The components take part in the cavity selection but are not oriented, remeshed or fitted. The components of the target follow the order of parts.

Each CAD part becomes a component of material radiating from its front (from both sides when two_sided), through these steps:

  1. orient = true repairs its windings with orient_mesh: closed solids face out of their material, open sheets face the point toward (mm).
  2. With inside, a point (mm) in the cavity, enclosure keeps only the elements that bound the free space around it, among the facets of all the parts as oriented (enclosure_kw are its keywords). The selection needs consistent windings.
  3. remesh (mm) divides the kept facets into compact, near-planar elements of about that size (remesh_mesh), and coarsen (mm) instead merges whole facets into elements (coarsen_mesh). At most one of them may be given. Only the kept facets are divided, which is much cheaper when most of an assembly lies outside the cavity. With inside, the selection is then repeated on the elements, and an element that straddles the edge of the lit region averages its flux over its area.
  4. fit splits each remeshed part into its cylinders, cones, spheres and the rest. It is on by default when remesh is given, and it may also be a named tuple of keywords of curved_components and fit_surfaces. An example is minfraction = 0, which also fits the curved faces of under 10⁻³ of the part area that stay flat by default. The fit uses the kept CAD facets, whose vertices lie on the true surfaces, so the radiation model integrates over the curved walls. The split is computational. The pieces, <part>/cyl1, …, <part>/rest, record their part, and a part that is one surface is not renamed. elements, Scenario(...; observe), the keep-outs, the clearance and TaLIS checks and the reports (energy_balance, component_power, write_results, report) take and show the parts (see parts). fit = false keeps flat elements.

The view factors of the target default to visibility = :sampled (see view_factors). CAD cavities have rims, such as entrance-hole bores and gaps, that partly shadow element pairs, and a single centroid ray would set their visibility to all or nothing.

closed = true declares the target a closed enclosure, so RadiationModel balances its view factors (enforce_closure!). It applies when apertures (aperture components among the parts) cover every opening of the kept cavity, and open_fraction shows where facets leak. On a remeshed, fitted CAD cavity with 0.1 mm bores and a 20 µm waist gap closed by apertures, balancing brings the LEH and gap loss from 0.7–0.8% above a continuum Monte Carlo to within 0.2%. It brings the capsule drive within 0.06%, against up to 0.16% raw, and moves its a₂/a₀ by under 0.08 pp, toward the reference at 0.5 mm (research notes, "Closure on bored, windowed and CAD cavities"). The default is false, with raw view factors, since a cavity selected from CAD is open wherever no aperture was given.

material, two_sided, remesh and coarsen take one value for every CAD part, a function of the part name (a Symbol), or a dictionary keyed by name (Symbol or String). The facets of a part missing from a remesh or coarsen dictionary are kept. A part missing from a two_sided dictionary is one-sided, and one missing from a material dictionary is an error. For example, remesh = n -> n === :capsule ? 0.15 : 0.5 remeshes a capsule finer than the walls. An account of what the cavity selection drops comes from enclosure on the target built without inside.

Briareus.enclosure — Function
enclosure(target, point = (0, 0, 0); threshold = 0.05, albedo = 0.9, rays = 64,
          points = 8, point_rays = 4096, radius = Inf, seed = 1) -> Enclosure
enclosure(components, point; kwargs...)

The elements of target that bound the free-space region containing point (mm). They are returned as a new Target (enc.target) with each component cut to those patches, with diagnostics (Enclosure; show prints the elements and area kept per component). Imported CAD assemblies model a target as closed solids, with outer faces, end faces and mounting structure that cannot exchange radiation with the cavity, while view factors cost O(n²) in the elements. Only element sides that face the free space are found, which are the fronts of one-sided surfaces and either side of two-sided ones. CAD windings must therefore be made consistent first (orient_mesh, visrad_target(...; orient = true)).

The selection uses the flux that an isotropic source at the point would put on each element, directly and after diffuse re-emission from the surfaces with the uniform albedo albedo. This albedo is a geometric weight and does not come from the materials. Every element within radius of the point casts rays rays from random points on it, in cosine-weighted directions stratified in angle, against the facets of the target. Apertures are transparent, as everywhere. The flux is gathered along the rays:

Eⱼ = E⁰ⱼ + albedo × (mean of E over the element sides that the rays of j hit first).

Rays that escape, by leaving the sphere of radius about the point or hitting nothing, contribute nothing, and so do rays that meet a one-sided surface from behind. E⁰ⱼ is the direct flux, cos θ/(4π d²) averaged over points random points on the element, and zero where the line of sight to the point is blocked. Lines of sight, and the rays from the point, pass through surfaces seen from behind, so the point may lie inside the capsule. The iteration (Gauss–Seidel in element order, to 10⁻⁶) starts from E⁰. Elements with E ≥ threshold × E_ref are kept. E_ref is the mean flux on the element sides that point_rays rays from the point (a Fibonacci lattice) first hit, which sets the scale of the cavity. enc.flux contains E/E_ref. On a NIF CAD workspace (155k elements) the selection takes a few seconds on a desktop.

Each element gathers through separate rays, which sample its whole view whatever its size or shape. A small patch or a sliver deep in a recess or behind a shield is therefore found through the surfaces around it, which have a view of the cavity. Surfaces outside, such as outer walls and the far sides of mounts, get no flux at all. A surface with a view of the cavity only through an opening, such as mounting structure beyond an entrance hole, gets about albedo × its view factor to the cavity. It also gets the flux that its neighbors outside gather in turn, attenuated by albedo at each step, so structure outside is not selected through its view of itself. The default threshold 0.05 drops a surface facing a hole of radius a from more than about 4a away on its axis. The rim of a hole in a thick wall has about half its view on the cavity and is kept. With threshold = 0, every element with any flux at all is kept, that is, everything connected to the cavity by lines of sight, however weakly, within the bound set by radius. The random numbers come from seed and the element index, so the result does not depend on the thread count. An element whose flux is within the sampling noise of the threshold can fall either way.

Removing elements outside the view of the cavity does not change the radiosity of the rest. Removing a surface seen through an opening turns the radiation that it would have returned into an escape, which return_area estimates against open_area; a lower threshold reduces it when it matters. Thin films that CAD models as solids, such as a capsule tent or an entrance-hole window, bound the free space like walls. With the point inside a tent, the result is the pocket of the tent around the capsule, with open_area near zero. Such parts should be left out of the target, since they transmit x-rays at their thickness, or the point should be placed on the side wanted.

Briareus.RadiationModel — Type
RadiationModel(target; F = view_factors(target), closed, kwargs...)

Target plus view factors, which is everything needed to solve for x-ray fluxes given laser deposition. kwargs pass through to view_factors. closed = true, for elements (apertures included) that form a closed enclosure, removes discretization closure error with enforce_closure!. View-factor rows sum to one only to about 1e-3, and multiple bounces at high albedo amplify that leak or excess. At α = 0.925, a 5e-4 shift in the mean row sum of the wall moves the capsule drive by 0.3% and its P2 by about 0.05%. closed defaults to the declaration of the target (which hohlraum makes) when the view factors are computed here, and to false for a given F, which is used as is. closed = false gives the raw view factors of a closed target.

For a target of several cavities, such as two halfraums across a package or two hohlraums side by side, closed is a collection of cavities. Each cavity is the elements of one closed enclosure, given as a vector of element indices or of component or part names. A part name selects every piece of the part, entrance holes included, which is what the cavity of a hohlraum contains. The rows of each cavity are balanced to sum to one over its elements (enforce_closure!(F, A; cavities)). Elements in no cavity, such as the outer face of a package or free parts in the open, keep their raw rows apart from the reciprocal scaling of their entries with cavity elements. One cavity that contains every element is exactly closed = true. The caller declares which elements form a cavity, and open_fraction(target; cavity) checks the declaration by rays.

The model caches LU factorizations of the radiosity matrix I - diag(α) F keyed on the albedo vector (the two most recently used), so solve and flux_response with unchanged albedos skip the factorization. The cache is thread-safe. Do not mutate F after the first solve (or call Briareus.empty_cache!(model) after mutating it).

Large models, with more than Briareus.LARGE_MODEL[] = 10,000 elements by default, store the view factors they compute in a Float32Matrix. Above Briareus.HMATRIX_MODEL[] = 15,000 they use a hierarchical HMatrix (storage = :auto; Float64, Float32 or :hmatrix select a storage). Large models are solved by GMRES without factorizations (solver = :auto; :direct or :iterative select a solver). The storage of a given F is kept unless storage names another, and an HMatrix is solved only iteratively. Each GMRES iteration is one product with F accumulated in Float64, so iterative solves reach the same relative residual (1e-12) as the preconditioned ones. Float32 storage changes the capsule moments by about 1e-8 relative (research notes, "Large radiation models"). RadiationModel(target, F; solver = :auto) wraps a given matrix as is.

Briareus.RadiationSolution — Type
RadiationSolution

Fields:

  • laser: absorbed laser power per element (TW), of every color with the unconverted of solve;
  • source: x-ray source flux ηP/A, plus the emission of wall elements re-emitting stored energy in a drive_history step;
  • B (radiosity) and H (incident flux); source, B and H are per element in TW/mm²;
  • albedo: per element, the albedos with which the radiosity was solved, so that B = source + albedo .* H exactly;
  • intact: the albedo that each element would have intact, which is the before albedo of a thin-foil element and the albedo elsewhere;
  • transmission: the fraction of the incident flux that each element transmits out of the enclosure, nonzero only on a burned-through BurnthroughAlbedo foil (see energy_balance);
  • time;
  • converted: the part of source converted from laser light (TW/mm²), from every color and without the emission of a re-emitting wall;
  • laser3: the 3ω part of laser (TW per element; laser itself without unconverted), from which spectral_solve forms its default M-band.

Without converted, the constructor takes it as the 3ω conversion of laser, ηP/A, and without laser3 it takes laser as all 3ω. iterations, residual and converged report the albedo fixed point of a temperature-dependent albedo (solve). They are the number of radiosity solves, the largest albedo change after the last solve (an emission change counted as the albedo change it equals), and whether that change fell below tol. An unconverged solution is the radiosity of the last iterate, with the albedos with which it was solved; it is not self-consistent. A solution without a fixed point (explicit or constant albedos, or one built directly) has iterations = 1, residual = 0 and converged = true.

Briareus.energy_balance — Method
energy_balance(sol; by = :part) -> NamedTuple

Global x-ray power accounting (TW). The terms are source (Σ ηPᵢ), absorbed by each component ((1-α)HA), escaped through each aperture, transmitted through each burned-through thin-foil component (BurnthroughAlbedo) and each open or closing gap (GapClosure, (1-s)HA while a fraction s is closed), and lost to open space, which is the power that leaves the geometry without reaching any element. The loss (1-α)HA of a thin-foil component splits into the transmitted part τHA, with τ its sol.transmission, and the absorbed part (1-α-τ)HA. With the default transmission T, τ = sT and α = (1-sT)α_before, with s the progress of the transition, so the foil absorbs (1-sT)(1-α_before)HA. With an explicit after, τ = α_before-α, and the foil absorbs (1-α_before)HA, as if intact. A capsule with a CapsuleAlbedo absorbs (1-α_c)(R/R₀)² HA, the share of its effective sphere, plus its outside share a_out (1-(R/R₀)²) HA. This is the power of $E_{cap}$, which energy_budget integrates over a drive_history. source ≈ Σabsorbed + Σescaped + Σtransmitted + lost.

The dictionaries are keyed by part: the pieces of a part split for computation (curved_components) are summed into it. by = :component keys them by component.

enforce_closure!(F, areas; cavities = nothing, tol = 1e-12, maxiter = 1000) -> F

Briareus.flux_response — Method
flux_response(model, observed; time = 0.0, albedo = nothing, weights = nothing,
              laser = nothing, unconverted = nothing) -> Matrix

Linear operator R with H[observed] = R * P, the incident x-ray flux on the observed elements (TW/mm²) per unit absorbed laser power on every element (TW). It is fixed for a given geometry and albedo, so optimizers that only change the laser source apply it as a matrix product instead of solving again. The converged sol.albedo of a reference solution, passed as albedo, linearizes about a temperature-dependent albedo state. The factorization cache of the model is shared with solve.

With weights (a matrix with one column per observed element, e.g. legendre_weights), weights * R is returned directly, and only its rows are solved for. This is far cheaper than R when only a few moments of the observed flux are wanted. On a large model (model.iterative) the rows are solved by GMRES on the transposed system, in blocks that share each product with F, and the full response costs a solve per observed element.

A time-dependent Conversion is evaluated at time. An intensity-dependent conversion makes the source nonlinear in P. The absorbed laser power laser (TW per element) then sets the state about which the response is linearized: the conversion of each element is fixed at its value for that deposition (the secant, exact at laser itself).

With unconverted (as in solve, with its colors and, per color, a Deposition or a per-element absorbed power), the result is a Dict of responses keyed by color. :omega3 is the 3ω response above. For each unconverted color c, the response to the absorbed power of that color is the 3ω response with the conversion η(t, P/A) of each element replaced by r_c η(t, P_c/A), where r_c is the conversion of the color. Then H[observed] ≈ R[:omega3] * P + Σ_c R[c] * P_c. The conversion of each color is fixed at its value for the power of that color in unconverted, a secant per color. The sum then equals the per-color result of solve within rounding at those powers, and at any powers for conversions independent of intensity. With an intensity-dependent conversion, other powers P_c′ leave the error of the source change r_c [η(P_c/A) − η(P_c′/A)] P_c′/A, carried through the radiosity solve. On the gold table of Dewald (dη/dlog₁₀I = −0.15 between 10¹⁴ and 10¹⁵ W/cm²), the secant source of the color exceeds the per-color source by 0.065 δ/η of it for a small relative rise δ of the flux of the color. The two are equal where both fluxes lie beyond the same end of the table, which is held at its end values, as for unconverted light below 10¹⁴ W/cm².

Briareus.open_fraction — Method
open_fraction(target; cavity = nothing, rays = 64, seed = 1) -> (; fraction, element)

Whether the facets of the target close an enclosure, apertures counted. The result gives the area-weighted fraction of diffuse rays leaving the elements that meet no facet at all, and that fraction per element (zero on apertures). rays cosine-distributed rays leave from uniform points on each radiating side except those of the apertures. Apertures stop the rays here, as walls would. A target is closed, and closed = true balancing (enforce_closure!) applies, when every opening has an aperture. The fraction is then zero up to cracks where meshes meet without sharing vertices, as for a disk aperture of 24 sides in a bore of 20 or 360. Such cracks give 2–7 × 10⁻⁴ on the cavities of "Closure on bored, windowed and CAD cavities" in the research notes. The holes of hohlraum meet its caps at shared vertices, and the hohlraum does not leak; the fraction was 2.7 × 10⁻³ at h = 1 when the holes had half the count of the caps. An opening left without an aperture shows as the share of the view that it takes, which may be no larger: 4 × 10⁻³ for a 40 µm waist gap, and 7 × 10⁻² for the two 3.1 mm LEHs of a CAD cavity. The fraction therefore indicates where to look (element), not whether to balance, which follows from the apertures given. Curved surfaces are ignored: cracks between the facets of a curved part and an aperture do not leak in the view factors, which integrate over the true surfaces.

cavity (element indices, or component or part names) checks one cavity of a target of several, among the cavities that RadiationModel(...; closed) balances. The rays leave only from the elements of the cavity. A ray leaks when it misses every facet, or when it first meets an element side outside the cavity or a one-sided surface from behind. An element that the declaration of the cavity misses, or one that belongs to another cavity, therefore shows as a leak where it is seen. element is zero outside the cavity.

Each element draws from a separate stream (from seed and the element), so the result does not depend on the thread count.

Briareus.solve — Method
solve(model, dep::Deposition; time = 0.0, albedo = nothing, burnthrough = nothing,
      tol = 1e-4, maxiter = 50, guess = nothing, unconverted = nothing)
solve(model, laser_power::AbstractVector; ...)

Solve the radiosity system for the given absorbed laser power per element. Albedos come from the materials at time unless an explicit per-element albedo vector is passed. Temperature-dependent albedos (WallLossAlbedo) are iterated to self-consistency with the incident-flux temperature of each element (to tol in albedo), in at most maxiter radiosity solves. The first ten iterates use the new albedos in full, and so does every later iterate until the iteration oscillates. An albedo that falls steeply enough with T overshoots, so that the albedo change reverses direction (a negative dot product with the previous change) and shrinks by less than half. Each such iterate halves the step from then on, α ← α + ω (α_new − α) with ω down to 1/64. Results that converge in ten iterates are therefore those of the plain iteration. An albedo that rises steeply with T runs away monotonically toward its range instead, and relaxation does not help there. The iterations, residual and converged of the result report the fixed point (RadiationSolution), and an unconverged solve also warns. burnthrough (ns, per element, NaN where not given) sets the burnthrough times of BurnthroughAlbedo elements. Without it, a thin-foil element uses the explicit burnthrough of its model, or is intact; drive_history tracks the burnthrough from the flux history instead.

A single solve has no history. It iterates a WallLossAlbedo to the present temperature of each element at time, as if the wall had been at that temperature since t = 0, and with no re-emission of stored energy. Partway through a shaped pulse, that wall differs from the wall found by a drive_history of the pulse. On a NIF-like mock-up the wall albedo is 0.74 against 0.67 at the picket, 0.81 against 0.86 in the trough and 0.94 against 0.87 at the peak, and the capsule drive is 15–44% off. A fixed-time scenario of such a phase takes its albedos from the history (history_state; research notes, "Drive histories: sliver steps and the albedo of a phase (2026-11-18)").

unconverted adds the x-ray source of unconverted (1ω, 2ω) light absorbed by the target. It is the result of unconverted_deposition, which contains the UnconvertedColors with which it was deposited (its colors). It may also be any collection (a NamedTuple, or a Dict keyed by Symbol) with an entry colors, the UnconvertedColors, and, under the name of each color, a Deposition or a per-element absorbed power (TW). The conversions used are those of these colors, so a deposition and the source made from it share one set of colors. The source flux of element i is then

Sᵢ = [ηᵢ(t, Pᵢ/Aᵢ) Pᵢ + Σ_c r_c ηᵢ(t, P_c,ᵢ/Aᵢ) P_c,ᵢ] / Aᵢ,

with ηᵢ the conversion_efficiency of its material, P the 3ω power, P_c the absorbed power of the color and r_c the conversion of the color relative to 3ω. The conversion of each color is evaluated at the absorbed flux of that color, an engineering default. The relative conversions come from gold disks irradiated at one wavelength at a time, and no measurement of the conversion of overlapping colors was found (research notes, "Unconverted light: absorption and conversion vs. wavelength"). With a conversion independent of intensity, this is the 3ω solve of the equivalent power P + Σ_c r_c P_c, within rounding. sol.laser is then the absorbed power of all colors, P + Σ_c P_c, and sol.converted the source above, which spectral_solve takes as the converted share. Without unconverted the source is the unchanged 3ω source. Generic in the number type of the powers of every color.

The factorization of the system for the initial albedo (that of the materials at time, or the explicit albedo) is cached on the model. Repeated solves with unchanged albedos then cost only a pair of triangular solves and a matrix–vector product. Iterates of a temperature-dependent albedo differ from call to call. They are solved by GMRES preconditioned with the factorization of a nearby system (that of the first iterate, or of the guess model), to a relative residual of 1e-12. They are factored afresh only when GMRES does not converge in a few tens of iterations, and the new factorization then preconditions later iterates. On the fine 12.5k-element hohlraum, a 50-step drive_history ran about nine times faster per step with no fresh factorization (127 GMRES solves, 819 iterations) than with a fresh factorization per iterate (127 in all). On the standard mesh it ran about 2.4 times faster. Results agree with the factored path to about 1e-12 eV in Tr and 1e-11 percentage points in P2/P0.

guess is a previous solution of the same model or of one with the same elements, such as the last step of a history or a keyframe of a moving target. The albedo iteration then starts from its incident-flux temperatures instead of the reference temperatures of the materials, and its radiosity starts the GMRES of the first iterate. The preconditioner of its model is used when this model has none. Fewer iterations are needed when the guess is close. The result then differs from a cold start by less than the tolerance of the iteration. The first albedo is not cached in that case.

A large model (model.iterative, see RadiationModel) is never factored. Every iterate is solved by unpreconditioned GMRES to the same 1e-12 relative residual, from the previous iterate or from guess. Dual-number sources are solved as one block, with the value and partials sharing each product with F.

Briareus.drive_history — Method
drive_history(model, op, beams_at, times; observe = :capsule, lmax = 6,
              wall = (:wall,), cavities = (), lpi = nothing, holes = nothing, ylm = false,
              observed_flux = false, transition = nothing, flux_change = nothing, min_step = 1e-3,
              tol = 1e-4, keep_albedo = false, record = nothing,
              wall_history = true, wall_release = true, mband = MBandSplit(),
              spectral = nothing, unconverted = nothing, colors = nif_unconverted(),
              unconverted_kw = (;), prepulse = nothing, by = :group,
              per_component = false) -> NamedTuple

Quasi-static drive history. At each time, beams_at(t) is deposited with op after the laser–plasma model lpi, evaluated at that time. The radiosity is solved at that time, with time- and temperature-dependent albedos, and the following records are kept:

  • time — the times solved (ns): times, plus any steps inserted by transition or flux_change
  • delivered — laser power (TW)
  • flux, Tr — mean incident flux on observe (TW/mm²) and its radiation temperature (eV)
  • moments — (lmax+1) × nt Legendre moments of the observed flux (aₗ/a₀ rows are moments[l+1, :] ./ moments[1, :])
  • ylm — with ylm = true, the (lmax+1)² × nt real spherical-harmonic coefficients of the observed flux (ylm_weights, in mode_index order; columns work with mode_amplitude), otherwise nothing
  • observed_flux — with observed_flux = true, the n × nt incident flux (TW/mm²) on each of the n observed elements (in the order of elements(target, observe...)), which is the state that the retune of a flat face holds (FaceFlux); otherwise nothing
  • wall_Tr, wall_albedo — area-weighted mean incident-flux temperature and albedo of the wall components
  • cavity_Tr — per cavity of cavities (pairs name => wall names, e.g. [:upper => (:upper_wall, :upper_cap_top), :lower => (:lower_wall, :lower_cap_bottom)] for two halfraums), the temperature of the area-weighted mean incident flux on the wall components of that cavity (wall_temperature of the solution of each step), keyed by cavity name; empty without cavities (the default). The walls of a cavity are named one by one, since grouping by part would also include the entrance holes of a hohlraum.
  • escaped — power (TW) through each aperture, and transmitted through each thin-foil (BurnthroughAlbedo) and closing-gap (GapClosure) component, as in energy_balance (by default the fraction s T of the flux on each burned-through element, and 1 - s on a gap a fraction s closed)
  • window_loss — power lost through diagnostic windows: transmitted plus escaped through every aperture except the entrance holes. By default the holes are the apertures not on a CylinderSurface, which are the LEHs of a hohlraum.
  • front — per thin-foil component, the area-mean heat-front areal mass as a fraction of the foil areal mass (≥ 1 once burned through), from its flux history even when the model has an explicit burnthrough
  • burnthrough — per thin-foil component, the burnthrough time of each element (ns; Inf if it has not burned through)
  • albedo — with keep_albedo = true, the converged albedo per element at each step; otherwise nothing. This is the state about which a flux response is linearized, for example for retune_drive. Where a wall re-emits stored energy, it is the effective albedo that includes the emission, so that B = ηP/A + αH. Alongside it, transmission is the fraction of the incident flux per element transmitted out of the enclosure at each step (from the RadiationSolution), otherwise nothing. history_state interpolates both to a time of the pulse.
  • setup — the power of beams_at(t) (TW, before lpi); laser — absorbed laser power per component (TW, back side included); source — x-ray source power per component (Σ ηP); absorbed — x-ray power absorbed per surface component (and per closing lip), as in energy_balance. absorbed is net of released, the power that wall components re-emit from stored energy (see below). lost — x-ray power lost to open space. energy_budget integrates these records. As in energy_balance, escaped, transmitted, laser, source and absorbed are keyed by part, and the pieces of a part split by curved_components are summed into it. front and burnthrough stay per component. With per_component = true, all of these records, released and the laser power of mband stay per component. This separates one piece of a part from the others, for example the cap of a hohlraum observed while its wall heats.
  • lip_radius — per computed entrance-hole lip (LipClosure, hohlraum(; leh_closure = :computed)), the radius of its plasma edge at each step (mm)
  • record — with record = f, the vector of f(sol) for the RadiationSolution of each step (e.g. sol -> dante(dante1(), sol) for a synthetic Dante, or a spectral_solve), otherwise nothing.
  • mband_moments, mband — the (lmax+1) × nt Legendre moments of the observed flux at photon energies above the lower M-band edge, and the laser-produced M-band power per component (TW, keyed by part like laser). The moments are those of band_flux(s, mband.emission.band[1]) for the spectral solution s of the step, which includes laser-produced and recirculating M-band and the thermal tail. Both are nothing with mband = nothing, when the target has no surface of the materials of the model, or, for a per-group model, when no beam of beams_at(times[1]) is in its groups.
  • unconverted — with unconverted, (; delivered, skipped): per color, the unconverted power delivered at each step (TW, before absorption), and the beams whose unconverted light cannot be placed (see unconverted_deposition); otherwise nothing.

Unconverted light. unconverted gives the unconverted power per color of colors (UnconvertedColors, the NIF 1ω and 2ω by default) as a fraction of the 3ω power of each beam. The fraction is a number or a function of time (ns), keyed by color name, for example (omega1 = 0.05, omega2 = 0.01) or (omega1 = PulseShape(ts, fs), omega2 = 0.0), as for the ratio of unconverted_history. There is no default, since the fraction is an input. The fraction of a color applies to every beam, or each beam has a separate fraction. A separate fraction is a vector with one entry per beam of beams_at(τ), in its order, which the pointing fixes, or it is keyed by by as prepulse is. A beam without an entry is a KeyError, as in with_pulses. An example for 1ω on the outer cones only is (omega1 = Dict(:outer44 => 0.05, :outer50 => 0.05, :inner23 => 0.0, :inner30 => 0.0), omega2 = 0.01). At each step the absorbed powers of the colors are those of unconverted_deposition for the beams of beams_at(τ), at the fractions of each beam at that step. The beam powers are those before lpi, since unconverted light does not take part in the 3ω laser–plasma model. The step is solved with these powers as the unconverted argument of solve, and each color is converted at its absorbed flux.

prepulse adds light not proportional to the 3ω pulse, as the prepulse of unconverted_history does. Per color it is optional, and it is a power per beam (TW) as a function of time: one function for all beams, or keyed by by (:group, :quad, :beam or a function, as in with_pulses). The power of a beam in color c at step τ is then r_c(τ) · P₃ω(τ) + prepulse[c](τ), with r_c its fraction in color c and P₃ω the power of the beam in beams_at(τ). A prepulse without unconverted fractions uses zero fractions (unconverted = (omega1 = 0.0, omega2 = 0.0)). The history is solved only at times, so a prepulse reaches the drive through the steps in its window. A step where only the prepulse fires (every 3ω power zero, also before the main pulse at negative times) is solved with only the prepulse light. Its x-rays heat the wall, and the memory of the wall (below) carries them into the main pulse. Between a prepulse and the main pulse, a step where nothing fires lets the wall cool and re-emit the energy it stored. Without such a step, the wall-energy integral runs across the gap at the interpolated temperature. unconverted_kw passes the offsets, lens_distance, coarse and resolution of unconverted_deposition.

The light is linear in the power of each beam, so the history deposits it once per set of beams whose powers are equal at every step and whose fractions and prepulses are the same (equal numbers or the same function). For beams on shared pulses this is once per (pulse, fraction, prepulse) schedule, as in unconverted_history. A set whose light is zero is never deposited, and the deposited patterns are scaled by the powers in time. The pointing must not change in time. laser then includes the absorbed power of each color, and setup and delivered its delivered power, so energy_budget balances. The M-band is still that of the 3ω light. Without unconverted the history is identical to the 3ω history.

M-band. mband is an MBandSplit, which contains the M-band emission model and the spectral split with which it is solved. The default is MBandSplit(), with nif_mband() (the per-cone conversions of Dewald 2020 on gold and Au-lined uranium) in the calibrated two-group split. The laser-produced M-band of each step then comes from the effective beams of that step after lpi (CBET, backscatter, the bubble), as mband_power(mband.emission, op, beams; lpi, time) gives it. It is the share of each beam group (cone) of every element, split off in the deposition pass of that step, so the M-band follows the cone balance in time. An intensity-form MBandSplit(MBandEmission()) converts the deposition of the step directly. The gray solution of the step is then split by spectral_solve with the edges, thermalize and group albedos of the split, and spectral contains the other keywords of spectral_solve (spot, tol, ...). The M-band is a diagnostic, and the gray history (every other record) is identical with or without it. It costs about 6% of a small history (600 elements), and less than the run-to-run spread on N221204 (research notes, "Per-cone M-band in the drive history (2026-10-27)"). mband = nothing turns it off.

The temperature-dependent albedos of each step are iterated to tol (see solve), starting from the temperatures of the previous step.

Wall-loss history. With wall_history = true (the default), each element whose albedo is a WallLossAlbedo loses energy as its temperature history requires. The loss is not that of a wall held at the present temperature since t = 0. The constant-T wall energy of Lindl, E_w ∝ T^a t^b (a = 4 + p = 3.3, b = 1 + q = 0.62 for the defaults), is extended to a varying T(t) as E_w ∝ [∫ (T/100 eV)^(a/b) dt]^b. This is the form of the drive integral of HeatFront, exact at constant T. Its rate is the constant-T loss at the effective time

t_eff = ∫₀ᵗ (T(t′)/T(t))^(a/b) dt′,

integrated trapezoidally between the steps from the incident-flux temperature of each element. The temperature is taken as constant from t = 0 to times[1], and no such interval is added before a first step at or before t = 0, such as that of a prepulse. A wall heated in a low foot holds little energy at the peak temperature. Its loss early in the peak is therefore several times the constant-T value at t (t_eff ≈ the time since the rise), and the drive rises through a flat peak.

A thin-foil element (BurnthroughAlbedo) whose before is a WallLossAlbedo follows the same history for as long as before enters its albedo. While intact it is a wall element with that loss, so an intact window equals the wall around it. Across its transition and after it, α_before comes from the history, and the stored energy that it re-emits (below) is that of the wall element scaled by α/α_before (1 − s f). Its heat front does not depend on this. wall_history = false evaluates the albedo at t, for walls and foils alike. This was the behavior before 2026-10-24, and for foils before 2026-11-19. See the research notes, "N221204 late-time drive (2026-10-24)" and "Intact windows follow the wall's history (2026-11-19)".

Wall re-emission. The self-similar loss never gives energy back: when T falls, t_eff grows and the loss only shrinks. With wall_release = true (the default, with wall_history), a falling drive makes the wall re-emit stored energy. The loss above is taken as the heating response to the highest temperature of the element so far, T_h = max T. A drop below it gives the deficit D = φ(T_h) − φ(T) in the wall-energy potential φ = (T/100 eV)^a, and the response to the deficit is the same self-similar kernel, superposed. This follows the principle of Duhamel for the diffusion in the wall, which is linear in φ (engineering):

F = L(T_h, t_eff) − f_r b C ∫ (t − t′)^(b−1) dD(t′),    C = L t_eff^(1−b) / (b φ(T_h)),

is the net flux into the wall. C, the coefficient of the loss, is constant for the Lindl loss of gold. f_r is the release of WallLossAlbedo, 1.0, calibrated against 1-D radiation hydrodynamics of an ablating gold wall (examples/late_time_wall.jl). A constant-T wall loses C b φ t^(b−1). A drop D at t_d gives back f_r C D (t − t_d)^b by t, quickly at first from the hot surface layer and then as the t^b of the heat wave. The effective albedo 1 − F/H exceeds 1 where the wall gives back more than it takes, and solve includes the release as an emission added to the source of the element. While no element temperature falls below its running maximum, the history is identical to that of the heating loss. At a step where nothing fires, the flux of a heated wall is its re-emission, and the albedo iteration of solve starts it at half its running maximum. An element without flux would otherwise take reference_temperature. A wall heated again after cooling takes back the energy of its surface layer, but never more than the flux on it: its radiosity is kept ≥ 0 (albedo 0, no emission, engineering). released records the re-emitted power per component, and energy_budget integrates it; absorbed is net of it. See the research notes, "Wall re-emission and the capsule radius (2026-10-24)" for the closure and "Capsule absorption, conversion and the ablating-wall release (2026-10-25)" for release 1.0.

Computed lip closure. Each entrance-hole lip with a LipClosure albedo has a closure depth d, open at times[1]. The depth is advanced over each step from the trapezoidal mean of the free speed v(T) of the lip and of the laser hold. T is the area-mean incident-flux temperature of the lip at the start of the step and, corrected, at its end. The hold is the power of the beams in beams_at, after lpi, whose ports lie on the side of the hole, over the initial area of the hole. The update is exact for the relaxation of the hold. The step is solved with each lip ring closed outside the edge r₀ − d, and solved again until the corrected depth changes by less than 1e-5 mm.

Each thin-foil element without an explicit burnthrough time tracks a separate heat front. The front is the drive integral of heat_front over the incident-flux temperature history of that element, trapezoidal between the steps from times[1], so its burnthrough is consistent with the flux that the windowed hohlraum puts on it. A step is solved with the front extended from the previous step at constant temperature. It is solved again until the temperatures of that step no longer move a burnthrough that affects its albedo.

A burnthrough transition takes the width of the foil (0.4 ns by default), and a step much longer than that jumps across it. Two optional criteria insert steps between times:

  • transition — the transition of no thin-foil element (the smooth step s of BurnthroughAlbedo, 0 intact to 1 burned through) and of no closing gap (GapClosure, 0 open to 1 closed) advances by more than this in one step. Steps are planned from the known or predicted burnthrough time of each element. A step is halved if the temperatures of that step move a burnthrough so that the advance exceeds 1.5 transition.
  • flux_change — the observed flux relative to its mean, H/⟨H⟩ on each element of observe, changes by no more than this in one step. A step that exceeds it is halved and solved again.

Neither criterion shortens a step below min_step (ns). A planned step that would leave less than min_step before the next of times is extended to that time instead. Without the criteria (the default) the steps are exactly times. With wall_release, the albedo of a cooling wall element in a step of length Δ falls as 1 − c Δ^(b−1), the implicit part of the release kernel. A step of 1e-5 ns gave albedos near −27, which the unpreconditioned GMRES of a large model does not solve, so steps much shorter than min_step are best left out of times as well.

Briareus.energy_budget — Method
energy_budget(history; from = nothing, to = nothing) -> NamedTuple

Energies (kJ = TW·ns) of a drive_history, the trapezoidal integrals of its power records over the solved times. With [from, to], the integrals run over that interval, and outside the solved times the powers count only through the trapezoids that they bound. The energies are:

  • setup — the laser power that beams_at(t) delivers (before the laser–plasma model); coupled — after it (delivered); backscattered — their difference, which is the power that Backscatter and other lossy models remove (CBET conserves power);
  • laser — absorbed laser energy per component (front elements plus back side), unabsorbed — coupled − Σ laser, the light that leaves through the holes;
  • source — x-ray energy converted per component (Σ η P); absorbed — x-ray energy absorbed per surface component ((1 − α) H A, for a CapsuleAlbedo capsule at its effective radius, $E_{cap}$); escaped per aperture; transmitted per thin-foil or closing-gap component; lost to open space;
  • heating — per surface component, the energy that stays in it, which is unconverted laser plus absorbed x-rays, laser − source + absorbed;
  • released — per component, the stored wall energy re-emitted under a falling drive (the wall_release of drive_history). absorbed is net of it: a wall component took in absorbed + released and holds absorbed.

setup ≈ backscattered + unabsorbed + Σ heating + Σ escaped + Σ transmitted + lost, to the closure of the view factors.

Briareus.MBandEmission — Type
MBandEmission(; intensity = [1e14, 1e15], fraction = [0.01, 0.20], groups = nothing,
              band = (1800, 2850), materials = (:Au, :DU))

Non-Planckian gold M-band emission from laser-heated spots, an engineering model. Elements of a material in materials emit part of their absorbed laser power P as M-band x-rays, flat in photon energy over band (eV). The default materials are gold and the Au-lined uranium of a NIF wall (:DU, uranium, nif_uranium), where the laser heats the gold liner. The fraction is

  • by default fraction(I), with I the absorbed laser flux P/A of the element (W/cm²), interpolated in log–log between the (intensity, fraction) points and held constant beyond them; or
  • with groups (a Dict from beam group to fraction, e.g. nif_mband()), fixed per beam group: an element emits Σ_g fraction_g P_g, P_g the power it absorbs from the beams of group g (mband_power deposits the groups separately).

The M-band is part of the converted x-rays (it is not added to them): the Planckian source of the element is ηP/A less the M-band (which is capped at ηP/A).

By default the conversion of laser light to M-band (> 1.8 keV) in gold at normal incidence is ~1% at 10¹⁴ and ~20% at 10¹⁵ W/cm² (Dewald et al., PoP 15, 072706, 2008, as quoted in Dewald et al., PoP 27, 2020, M; sources in the research notes, "Spectral radiosity, M-band and a synthetic Dante"). With the upper band edge at 2.85 keV, 1/3 of a flat band lies above 2.5 keV, which is Dewald 2020's share of the hohlraum M-band above the Mo edge (L). The element-averaged intensity of the default form depends on the mesh across a spot (the fraction goes as I^1.3); the per-group form does not.

Briareus.spectral_solve — Method
spectral_solve(sol::RadiationSolution; edges = [0, Inf], albedo = nothing,
               mband = nothing, mband_power = nothing, thermalize = false,
               spot = :radiation, temperature = nothing, spot_temperature = nothing,
               tol = 1e-6, maxiter = 20) -> SpectralSolution

Multigroup radiosity about a gray solution sol (same model, laser deposition and time). The groups are [edges[g], edges[g+1]] (eV; edges[1] = 0, edges[end] = Inf, e.g. spectral_edges()).

  • Sources: the gray source of an element is its converted laser x-rays ηP/A (sol.converted, from every color when solve is given unconverted) plus, on a re-emitting wall of a drive_history, the emission of its stored energy. The laser-produced M-band (mband, an MBandEmission) is part of the converted x-rays and is placed in its band. Its power per element mband_power (TW) defaults to mband_power(mband, target, sol.laser3), from only the 3ω light as in drive_history, and must be given for a per-group model. The rest is split by Planck fractions, by spot:
    • :radiation (default): the converted x-rays less the M-band are split at the radiation temperature (H/σ)^¼ of the element, which its incident flux sets. The coronal emission of a laser spot is thermal at about the cavity temperature, and its hard part is non-Planckian M-band (research notes, "Laser-spot spectrum"). The stored-energy emission, and with thermalize the thermal re-emission, is split at the wall temperature ((B − ηP/A)/σ)^¼, the brightness of the element radiosity without its converted laser part;
    • :wall: the converted x-rays also at the wall temperature;
    • :brightness: everything at the brightness temperature (B/σ)^¼ of the total radiosity (the rule before 2026-10-24's spot spectrum; a laser spot then emits as a Planckian well above the wall temperature, plus a hard tail).
    The temperatures follow the group totals when those differ from the gray ones (iterated to tol). A given temperature vector (eV) fixes the wall temperature (with :brightness, the only temperature), and spot_temperature fixes that of the converted x-rays. On elements without laser light the three rules agree.
  • Group albedos α_g: the fraction of the flux incident on an element in group g that it re-emits in group g. The gray converged albedo sol.albedo is used in every group, except for the components in albedo, a Dict (or pairs) from component name to a vector of group albedos (for a nothing entry the gray albedo is used in that group, e.g. Dict(:wall => [nothing, 0.5]); a NaN entry throws).
  • thermalize: what happens to the rest of the gray albedo, α − α_g. By default it is lost to the wall (B_g = S_g + α_g H_g, each group a gray-type solve). With thermalize = true it is re-emitted thermally, spread over the groups by the Planck fractions b_g(T) of the element at its wall temperature: B_g = S_g + α_g H_g + b_g(T) Σ_g′ (α − α_g′) H_g′ (group albedos above α are lowered to it). When thermalized, the group sums equal the gray solution whatever the group albedos; with every group albedo 0, all re-emission is thermal.

With every group albedo equal to the gray one (either way), the group sums of source, B and H equal those of the gray solution (absorbed and emitted power per element too), whatever the split; with one group and no M-band the solution is the gray one exactly. Groups at the gray albedo reuse the cached factorizations of the model. They are solved directly when that albedo is factored and otherwise by GMRES preconditioned with a cached factorization; fresh factorizations are not cached, so the gray solve path is unaffected. Groups with separate albedos are solved by GMRES (jointly when thermalized), which converges fast for the low albedos of hard groups. When the split is thermalized and a single group is at the gray albedo (e.g. a two-group split at the M-band edge, as in drive_history), that group is computed as the gray radiosity less the others, which equals its solve up to the solver tolerance.

A measured curve, interpolated linearly in its abscissa E (increasing). Tabulate a curve at the edge energies (research notes, "Dante channel responses and the unfold", interpolation of tabulated curves).

Briareus.DanteView — Type
DanteView(theta, phi; through = :leh_bottom, pixels = 400, channels = dante1_channels(),
          area = nothing, field_of_view = nothing, center = (0, 0, 0))

A broadband spectrometer at port (θ, φ) (degrees). The visible surface is found by casting pixels × pixels parallel rays toward the target, since the detector is distant. Every element then counts with its visible projected area, and partial views and occlusion by any part are included. A ray through a transmitting aperture, such as a closing lip, is weighted by its open fraction (sol.transmission). The rays cover one of two regions:

  • field_of_view = nothing (default) gives the view through one entrance hole. The rays cover a square just enclosing the aperture component through and its closing lip <through>_lip, if the target has one.
  • field_of_view = D (mm) gives the collimated view. The rays cover a disk of diameter D normal to the line of sight about center, cut to the target extent, with pixels rays across it. center is a point (mm) or a part name, which stands for the area-weighted centroid of the part. The field of view of a NIF Dante is ±30 mm at TCC in the 2004 design (research notes, "Dante-family hardware and geometry"). Every surface of a target that faces the detector therefore lies inside it, such as walls seen through windows, packages and shields.

A channel with a separate field_of_view (DanteResponse) records the signal over that field in place of the view field. That field is a disk of its diameter about center; in a view through a hole, it is the square about the hole cut to that disk. The signal of the channel comes from the footprint of that field, which is cast once per distinct field and target. Channels that share the field of the view therefore cost nothing more and record exactly what they record without a separate field. The intensity, Tr and the visible areas belong to the view.

The brightness temperature is

T = (π I / (σ A cos θ_v))^¼,

with I the power per steradian toward the detector and A cos θ_v the reference area seen from the detector. By default A is the full initial area of through, aperture plus lip, as in the Dante convention of the pre-shot LEH size. θ_v is the angle between the line of sight and the normal of through. The keyword area sets A otherwise:

  • area = A (mm²) replaces A, with the aperture θ_v, or cos θ_v = 1 without through.
  • area = :part takes the visible projected area of the part as A cos θ_v. This is the convention of Fournier et al. 2010, who used the projected wall and cap.
  • area = :open takes the open area of the hole at the solution time. That area is the aperture plus the area of each closing-lip element times its open fraction. It comes from the entrance-hole closure of the model, so Tr then equals Tr_open.
  • area = TabulatedCurve(t, A) takes a measured area history, with time in ns, area in mm² and the end values held outside. Glenzer et al. 2010 used the hole area from SXI images.

With a field of view, through = nothing and no area give Tr = NaN, though the power is still computed. The footprint is computed once per target, or once per keyframe of a moving target. dante1() and dante2() give the two NIF Dantes.

Briareus.dante — Method
dante(view::DanteView, sol::RadiationSolution) -> NamedTuple

Computes the synthetic Dante signal. The result holds:

  • intensity (TW/sr toward the detector);
  • Tr (eV), the brightness temperature over the reference area (see DanteView);
  • area (mm²) and cos of the reference area, so that area × cos is its projection toward the detector;

  • spectrum (TW/sr per group) and edges;

With DanteResponse channels, the default of dante1_channels(), channels are the voltages Ω ∫ (dI/dE) R dE (V). The voltages are then unfolded as data are, by dante_unfold with method = unfold and kwargs over the area and angle of the view. Briareus.dante_unfold — Method
dante_unfold(channels, voltages; area, cos = 1, method = :traditional,
             below = 1500, mband = (1.0, 2500.0, 600.0), λ = 1e-3, knots = 48,
             tol = 1e-4, maxiter = 50) -> NamedTuple

Unfolds Dante channel voltages (V) into a spectrum, as the data are processed. The channels are of type DanteResponse, and nonpositive or non-finite entries are dropped. area (mm²) and cos, the cosine of the angle between the line of sight and the emitter normal, turn the power per steradian into a radiation temperature, Tr = (π I / (σ A cos))^¼.

Both methods start as the Dante analysis does (Rubery et al., RSI 2023 §II.C; Harris et al., RSI 92, 033505, 2021 §II). Each channel below below eV in nominal energy is matched by a separate Planckian, which gives its channel_temperatures entry, and the start temperature is their mean.

  • method = :traditional is Harris's algorithm. The start spectrum is that Planckian plus a Gaussian M-band mband = (amplitude GW/sr/eV, center eV, FWHM eV). Harris uses amplitude 1 at 2.5 keV and a "width" of 600 eV, which dante_unfold takes as the FWHM. The response of each channel is replaced by a unit-peak Gaussian, centered between its half-maximum points with their distance as the FWHM, and the Gaussian amplitudes are adjusted until the computed voltage of every channel equals the measured one. In Harris's sweeps the amplitudes are set from the highest-energy channel down, each to match its channel, for up to maxiter sweeps and to tol. Duplicate channels (e.g. Dante 1's 1/8, 10/14 and 7/15) have identical Gaussians, and the sweep then diverges; where the sweeps do not converge, the same equations are solved directly by least squares in the relative voltages, with minimum-norm amplitudes. The solver field of the result is the method that ran. The spectrum may go negative locally, as in the original.
  • method = :smooth gives a positive, smooth unfold. The spectrum is the start Planckian, without an M-band, times a piecewise-linear factor on knots log-spaced knots over 50 eV – 20 keV. The factor is fitted by nonnegative least squares to the relative voltages with a second-difference penalty λ, so a Planckian is recovered exactly. The cubic-spline unfold of Barnak et al. (RSI 91, 073102, 2020) is similar but does not use a prior.

Returns a named tuple with fields:

  • energy (eV grid), spectrum (GW/sr/eV) and intensity (TW/sr, 50 eV – 20 keV);
  • Tr (eV) and start_temperature;
  • channel_temperatures, per channel, NaN above below;
  • fit (V), the voltages of the unfolded spectrum;
  • misfit, the largest |fit/V − 1| over the channels used;
  • solver, which is :sweep, :direct or :nnls;
  • iterations, the number of sweeps.

Briareus.Keyframes — Type
Keyframes(build, times, beams; closed = nothing, keep = 2, deposition_kw = (;),
          viewfactor_kw = (;))

A target that changes through the pulse. build(τ) makes its Target at time τ (ns), with the same elements at every time (e.g. moving_hohlraum). The view factors and the deposition operator of beams are computed at the keyframe times. Between two keyframes, model_at interpolates the exchange matrix Aᵢ Fᵢⱼ linearly in time and divides by the areas of build(τ), which preserves reciprocity. It rebalances the matrix when closed is set. By default closed is the declaration of the target, and it may be true, false or cavities of the elements of build(times[1]), as in RadiationModel. Deposition between keyframes uses an operator built at that time (deposition_at). Before the first keyframe and after the last, the nearest keyframe is used. The error against rebuilding at every time falls as the square of the spacing. On a NIF hohlraum whose wall moves at 0.1 mm/ns, keyframes 2, 1 and 0.5 ns apart keep P2/P0 within 0.035, 0.012 and 0.009 percentage points (research notes, "Moving wall").

Keyframes are built when first needed, and only the keep most recently used models are held, since the view factors of a fine hohlraum take over 1 GB each. Deposition operators are kept. A history advances forward in time, so each keyframe is built once.

drive_history(kf, beams_at, times; ...) runs a drive history on the moving target.

Briareus.WallMotion — Type
WallMotion(; speed = 0.185, start = 0.8, saturation = 0.45, knee = 0.05)

Inward motion of the radiating wall of a hohlraum. The wall cylinder lies at radius R₀ - d(t) (mm, ns), where R₀ is the initial wall radius and the displacement

d(t) = stagnate(speed · max(0, t − start), saturation, knee)

is ballistic from start and stops smoothly at saturation. It follows the straight line up to saturation − knee, then approaches saturation as saturation − knee · exp(−(x − saturation + knee)/knee), which is C¹ at the knee and has the form of bubble_depth. With saturation = Inf the wall keeps moving at speed.

The radius stands for that of the LLNL dynamic view-factor model (Young et al., PoP 27, 082702, 2020), which is the average of the laser-absorption and x-ray-emission surfaces of a 2D HYDRA simulation, as one cylinder. The defaults are fitted to the hohlraum radius of that model for N170827 (Young Fig. 3a, digitized as young2020_radii). The wall starts moving at the end of the picket, 0.8 ns after the turn-on (fit 0.78, range 0.68–0.85). It moves in at 0.185 mm/ns (0.175–0.195) and stops 0.45 mm in (0.445–0.455, ±0.03 reading), where it stays through the peak. start is therefore the turn-on of the outer cones plus 0.8 ns, and the default assumes a turn-on at 0. For HYBRID-E (N221204) the HYDRA laser-absorption edge of the wall at the waist is 0.38 ± 0.04 mm in at 6 ns (PRE 109, 025204 Fig. 1a, digitized as pre109_fig1a_wall). The emission surface lies outside it, so this value is an upper bound on the saturation of that platform. Chen et al. 2020 (G-LEH) observed the emitting wall 0.5 mm in by 5.5 ns at 0.3 mg/cc and 0.4 mm in at 0.45 mg/cc. See the research notes, "Hohlraum dynamics calibrated to published data (2026-10-24)".

Briareus.retune_drive — Method
retune_drive(quantity, op, beams, reference; target = :reference, level = nothing,
             by = :group, limits = nothing, smooth = 0.0, nominal = nothing, stay = 0.0,
             unconverted = nothing, prepulse = nothing, colors = nif_unconverted(),
             unconverted_kw = (;)) -> NamedTuple

Power histories for beams that hold a drive quantity through the times of reference, for example after a bundle is dropped. Beams are grouped by :group, :quad, :beam or a function beam -> key, with one power per beam of a group at each time. The quantity is the modes of a capsule (CapsuleModes), the x-ray flux on a face (FaceFlux) or the laser irradiance on a foil (FoilIrradiance); their docstrings list what reference must contain. op is the deposition operator of beams, for example that of the reference restricted to the remaining beams, op[keep]. Its pointing is fixed through the pulse.

Target. The vector y of the quantity (the c_lm of a capsule, the H_e of a face, the I_e of a foil) has a level L = μ'y (c_00, or the area mean) and a shape y/L. At each time the target is a shape ŝ and a level L_t. The shape is set by target:

  • target = :reference (the default): the full shape of the reference, ŝ = y_ref/L_ref. This is the modes of a capsule, or the flux distribution of a face or a foil, and so also its RMS nonuniformity and peak-to-mean ratio;
  • target = :uniform: a uniform drive (c_lm = 0 for l ≥ 1, or a flat flux), which is the least nonuniformity that the powers allow.

L_t is level (TW/mm², a number or a function of time in ns) when given, and otherwise the level L_ref of the reference. target = :uniform, level = I₀ thus gives the most uniform irradiance of a foil at the mean I₀.

Solve. At each reference time y is linear in the powers P of the groups, y = M P + y₀, where y₀ is the part of the prepulse (below). For the x-ray quantities the albedos are fixed at the converged albedos of the reference (flux_response(...; albedo)). The powers minimize

Σ_i w_i ((y_i - ŝ_i L)/L_t)² + smooth Σ_g ((P_g - P_g,prev)/P_scale)²
    + stay Σ_g ((P_g - P_g,nominal)/P_scale)²

at the level L = L_t, with the weights w_i of the quantity. For a capsule they are its modes and components. For a face or a foil they are uniformity × A_e/ΣA, and the first sum is then the square of the RMS departure from ŝ. The powers lie within the bounds limits (TW per beam), which are nothing (from 0, unbounded above), a Dict key => (lo, hi) (with (0, Inf) for missing keys), or a function (key, t) -> (lo, hi). Examples are the power capability of a quad, or (0, 0) for a dropped quad. smooth ties the powers of each step to those of the previous step, and stay ties them to nominal powers, such as those of the reference shot. nominal is a Dict key => PulseShape or a function (key, t) -> P. Among the many power sets that restore the drive, stay selects the one closest to the nominal powers, instead of one that runs many quads to their limits. The problem is a box-bounded linear least-squares problem per time. The level is held by a penalty row whose target is shifted until the level of the solution is L_t, by the secant method on that level. The level is piecewise linear in the shift, so one step suffices while the active bounds are unchanged. A penalty without the shift would pull the level down by about the square of the nonuniformity (L/L_t ≈ 1/(1 + RMS²)), a percent at an RMS of 10%. Where the bounds cannot reach L_t, the level is the nearest they allow, and drive reports it.

Unconverted light. For a reference run with unconverted (1ω, 2ω) light, the unconverted, prepulse, colors and unconverted_kw of that history are passed here (see drive_history), so that the retune uses the same per-color sources:

y = (R₃ E + Σ_c R_c E_c(t)) P + Σ_c R_c Q_c p_c(t).

R₃ and R_c are the 3ω and per-color responses, from flux_response(...; unconverted) for the x-ray quantities; for a foil, the absorbed power of each color adds to the irradiance. E is the 3ω absorbed power of each group at 1 TW per beam. E_c(t) is its absorbed power in color c at 1 TW per beam, times the fraction r_c(t) of each beam. It is the sum, over the beams of the group on each fraction schedule, of unconverted_deposition of those beams at that fraction. p_c(t) is the prepulse of the color, fixed powers deposited by Q_c. The fractions and prepulses are keyed by by, as the retuned groups are. The fraction of a color may also be a vector with one entry per beam of beams, so the beams of a retuned group may have different fractions (e.g. 1ω only on some beams of a quad). The prepulse is not retuned, and it shifts the target of the least squares. As for the 3ω response, the conversions must not depend on intensity. A reference history with unconverted light and no unconverted here is an error.

Returns (; time, keys, power, pulses, shape, target_shape, level, drive, rms, peak, residual). These are the per-beam powers (length(keys) × nt), the same powers as PulseShapes for with_pulses (with by the same key), the predicted shape y/L and the target ŝ (ny × nt), and the predicted level L (TW/mm²) and L/L_t. For a face or a foil they include the predicted RMS nonuniformity and peak-to-mean ratio max(y)/L (nothing for a capsule), and finally the weighted residual norm per time. A time whose limits fix every power (lo == hi) is not solved. An example is (0, 0) where the reference has no 3ω light, as with only a prepulse or in a gap between pulses. The powers at such a time are the limits, the target level may be zero, and the residual is NaN. For the x-ray quantities the prediction is linear about the albedos of the reference, and a drive_history of the pulses, where the albedos follow the new drive, confirms it.

Element-averaged incident x-ray flux (TW/mm²) on the receivers by Monte Carlo transport, with no view factors, for checking view_factors and solve against independent ray tracing. Sources are those of solve, ηPᵢ/Aᵢ uniform over each element, from the absorbed laser power laser_power (per element).

Paths are traced backward from the receivers, by reciprocity of the Lambertian kernel. Each path starts at a uniform point on the facets of the receiver, in a cosine-weighted direction, and finds the first facet hit (closest_hit over every triangle, apertures included), whose element contributes its source. The path continues diffusely with its weight multiplied by the albedo of the element, with Russian roulette below 1/20. It ends on an aperture (escape), on the back of a one-sided surface, or on a miss. A closing lip (aperture(...; closure), a GapClosure) is the exception among apertures. Like a surface, it re-emits diffusely the fraction albedo of the radiation that reaches it, its $α(t) = s α_{after}$ from albedos(target, time) as solve uses it, and the rest escapes. The lip has no source. Apertures stay transparent to laser rays and lines of sight elsewhere, and here the lip plane is the boundary of the enclosure. albedo defaults to the albedos at time 0. albedos(target, time) (or the albedo of a solution) gives a comparison with a solve at a later time. The sum of each path is an unbiased estimate of the incident flux, as a forward photon tally would be, but every sample is scored on a receiver.

reemit = :hit continues from the hit point. This is the transport of the continuum, with radiosity varying within elements, which is the limit to which the element model converges. reemit = :element continues from a uniform point on the hit element. This is the element model with exact view factors, visibility included, which solve should reproduce up to its view-factor error.

Geometry is the facets throughout. A CurvedSurface is ignored, so the comparison should use a target with flat elements (the same meshes without surfaces). H is the mean over batches independent batches of paths ÷ batches paths per receiver. batch, with one row per batch, gives the statistical errors of any functional of H. Batch b draws from a separate Xoshiro stream (from seed and b), and batches run on threads, so the results do not depend on the thread count.
