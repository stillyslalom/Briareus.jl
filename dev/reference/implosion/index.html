Implosion metrics

Reduced models from drive asymmetry to implosion observables: mode 1 as a hot-spot velocity, the piston model's yield factors, P2 swing metrics, and beam-error budgets in velocity. Sources and confidence: reference/research-notes.md, "Implosion metrics".

Briareus.HOTSPOT_VELOCITY_PER_PERCENT — Constant
HOTSPOT_VELOCITY_PER_PERCENT = 100.0

Hot-spot velocity (km/s, burn-off) per percent of capsule flux mode 1 (a₁/a₀, the norm of the l = 1 vector, mode_amplitude(c, 1)), applied over the rise and peak. An engineering default, not a facility criterion: Young et al., Phys. Plasmas 27, 082702 (2020) (N170601: 0.44 % → 37.6 km/s in 3D HYDRA, 44.7 km/s from view factors and their Green's function), MacGowan et al., HEDP 40, 100944 (2021) (~100 km/s per %), and Kritcher et al. 2024 (N230729: ~0.7 % → 70–75 km/s burn-off). It is platform specific and linear only below about 0.75 % (Young; Spears et al. 2014 find 120 km/s at 2 %, saturated).

Briareus.ResponseKernel — Type
ResponseKernel(times, values)

A response (Green's) function of time, piecewise linear through the table and zero outside it; callable, k(t). A time may repeat once to make a step. Units are the observable per percent of drive mode per ns (km/s per % per ns for the hot-spot velocity, Young et al. 2020; µm per % per ns for a hot-spot P2 kernel, Masse et al. 2019). Briareus ships no published kernel: those are platform specific and printed only as figures.

source
Briareus.VelocityPenalty — Type
VelocityPenalty(; scale = 10, weight = 1, per_percent, amplification = 1,
                uncertainty = nothing)

An objective term on the hot-spot velocity of an evaluation's capsule flux (hotspot_velocity): weight × |v|²/scale² (scale in km/s), the mode 1 weighted by its velocity sensitivity rather than equally with the other modes. Callable on an Evaluation; add it to an objective, e.g. obj = let J = asymmetry_objective(; modes = (2 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0)), P = VelocityPenalty(); ev -> J(ev) + P(ev) end. With uncertainty (mode_uncertainty's error keywords, as for the objectives) it adds the errors' expected |δv|². Generic in the number type (ForwardDiff).

Briareus.alpha_factor — Method
alpha_factor(f, chi; a = 3.3, q = a - 1) -> Real

The α-heating part of the yield degradation, Y_amp,3D/Y_amp,1D = exp(−q χ^1.2 f²), with χ the 1D generalized Lawson parameter (Y_amp ≈ exp(χ^1.2)). q = a − 1 is Hurricane et al. 2020 (Eq. 21 and the tolerance Eq. 26, from which their Table I comes); Casey et al., PRE 108, L053203 (2023), Eq. 5, has q = 1.2(a − 1). Generic.

source
Briareus.burn_amplification — Method
burn_amplification(Yamp) -> Float64

Factor by which α heating raises the measured (burn-on) hot-spot velocity over the burn-off one, as a function of the yield amplification Y_amp: 0.324 ln(Y_amp + 2.239) + 0.653 (Nora et al., Phys. Plasmas 30, 092705, 2023, Eq. 4, fitted for Y_amp < 40; refused above). It is about 1.8 at Y_amp = 30, against burn-on/burn-off ratios of 1.27–1.39 in Kritcher et al. 2024 (Table II): treat it as an upper estimate.

Briareus.flat_kernel — Method
flat_kernel(t0, t1; total = HOTSPOT_VELOCITY_PER_PERCENT) -> ResponseKernel

A constant kernel on [t0, t1] whose integral is total: a constant 1 % mode over the window gives total. The placeholder for Young's mode-1 Green's function (derived: it keeps the published static sensitivity and puts all weight on the window, which should be the rise and peak).

Briareus.hotspot_velocity — Method
hotspot_velocity(c; per_percent = HOTSPOT_VELOCITY_PER_PERCENT, amplification = 1) -> SVector{3}
hotspot_velocity(ev::Evaluation; ...)
hotspot_velocity(history; kernel = nothing, window = nothing, per_percent, amplification)

Hot-spot velocity (km/s, chamber x, y, z) that the capsule flux's mode 1 drives: the hot spot moves away from the high-flux side (Casey et al., PRL 126, 025002, 2021), at per_percent km/s per percent of mode1_vector (an engineering default, see HOTSPOT_VELOCITY_PER_PERCENT). amplification multiplies the burn-off velocity, e.g. by burn_amplification to compare with a burn-on nToF measurement. Generic in the number type (usable in objectives).

From a drive_history result (run with ylm = true; without it only the z component, from the Legendre moments), the velocity is the convolution of Young et al. (Phys. Plasmas 27, 082702, 2020, Eq. 9), v = −∫ G₁(t) · 100 p₁(t) dt, with G₁ in km/s per % per ns: kernel (a ResponseKernel or any function of time). The default is flat_kernel(window...; total = per_percent), a flat weight over window (default: the whole history) that gives per_percent for a constant 1 %; Young's published kernels are negative in the foot and dominated by the rise and peak, so pass window as the rise and peak, or a kernel of your own. The mode 1 is taken as linear between the history's steps and integrated exactly against a tabulated kernel (two-point Gauss on each interval between their breakpoints; a function kernel gets 16 pieces per interval); the kernel's weight outside the history's span is dropped.

Briareus.implosion_metrics — Method
implosion_metrics(v; vimp = 380, a = 3.3, c = 0.66, chi = nothing, q = a - 1, ke = nothing)
implosion_metrics(ev::Evaluation; per_percent, amplification, kwargs...)
implosion_metrics(history; kernel, window, per_percent, amplification, kwargs...)

The piston model's metrics for a hot-spot velocity v (km/s, a 3-vector; or computed with hotspot_velocity from an evaluation or a drive history). Returns (; velocity, speed, f, rke_fraction, rke, yield_factor, alpha_factor, total):

  • f = |v|/vimp, the shell asymmetry (ρR_max − ρR_min)/(ρR_max + ρR_min) (Hurricane et al. 2020, Eq. 23); vimp defaults to Hurricane's Table I value, 380 km/s (an engineering default: use the design's implosion velocity);
  • rke_fraction = c f² (rke_fraction), and rke in the units of ke when given;
  • yield_factor, the no-α yield over 1D, (1 − f²)ᵃ;
  • alpha_factor, with chi (the 1D generalized Lawson parameter) given, otherwise 1;
  • total = yield_factor × alpha_factor.

Generic in the number type.

Briareus.infer_errors — Method
infer_errors(scenario, beams, v::AbstractVector{<:Real}; sigma = ntof_covariance(v),
             per_percent, amplification = 1, thickness = 0.002,
             thickness_per_percent = 110, kwargs...) -> NamedTuple

The most probable beam errors behind a measured hot-spot velocity v (km/s, chamber x, y, z): infer_errors on the mode-1 components, with the measurement mapped to them through hotspot_velocity (per_percent, amplification; divide out a burn amplification here) and its covariance sigma (3 × 3, km²/s², or one rms number; default ntof_covariance(v)). The priors are infer_errors's keywords.

A capsule-thickness mode 1 competes with the drive (Casey et al., PRL 126, 025002, 2021): the hot spot moves toward the thicker side, at thickness_per_percent km/s per % of the shell thickness (110 simulated, 140 ± 30 measured), so the opposite sign of a flux mode 1 on that side. It enters as a nuisance error, a vector t (fractions of the thickness, toward the thicker side) with rms thickness per axis (default 0.2 %, about 0.15 µm metrology on a 78 µm shell: derived; 0 leaves it out), and is inferred with the beam errors.

Returns infer_errors's fields, with measured, predicted and residual still in the mode-1 components, plus thickness (the inferred t) and velocity = (; measured, nominal, predicted, residual, chi2, chi2_prior) in km/s: predicted is the nominal plus the inferred errors' and thickness's velocity; chi2 is MacGowan's χ²ν of the nominal (the forward prediction) against the measurement, (v − v₀)ᵀ Σₘ⁻¹ (v − v₀)/3 (Casey et al. 2023), and `chi2priorthe same with the errors' prior spread added toΣₘ` (about 1 when the measurement is what the priors expect).

Briareus.mode1_tolerance — Method
mode1_tolerance(loss; vimp = 380, a = 3.3, chi = 0.0, q = a - 1) -> NamedTuple

The largest mode-1 asymmetry that keeps the yield loss 1 − (1 − f²)ᵃ exp(−q χ^1.2 f²) (Hurricane et al. 2020, Eq. 26) at loss: (; f, velocity), the shell asymmetry and the hot-spot speed (km/s) at vimp. The inverse of implosion_metrics's total.

source
Briareus.mode1_vector — Method
mode1_vector(c) -> SVector{3}

The flux's mode-1 vector from real spherical-harmonic coefficients c (mode_index order, e.g. flux_ylm): √3 (c₁₁, c₁,₋₁, c₁₀)/c₀₀ along (x, y, z), pointing toward the high-flux side. Its norm is mode_amplitude(c, 1), the fractional amplitude ε of a dipole drive 1 + ε cos γ (not a percentage). Generic in the number type.

source
Briareus.ntof_covariance — Method
ntof_covariance(v; speed = 14, theta = 13, phi = 29) -> Matrix

Covariance (km²/s²) of an nToF hot-spot velocity measurement v (km/s): rms speed in magnitude and theta, phi degrees in direction (NIF: ±14 km/s, 13°, 29°; Young et al. 2020), along the local radial, polar and azimuthal directions of v.

source
Briareus.reactivity_exponent — Method
reactivity_exponent(T) -> Float64

The exponent a of ⟨σv⟩ ∝ Tᵃ at ion temperature T (keV): 6 − 1.5 ln T (Casey et al., PRE 108, L053203, 2023). 3.3 at 6 keV, Hurricane's 4–7 keV value.

source
Briareus.rke_fraction — Method
rke_fraction(f; c = 0.66) -> Real

Residual kinetic energy over the shell's implosion kinetic energy, RKE/KE = c f² (Hurricane et al. 2020, Eq. 23: f = v_HS/v_imp = √(RKE/(c KE)); c ≈ 0.66 in NIF-like simulations, 1 for incompressible pistons).

Briareus.swing_metrics — Method
swing_metrics(history; l = 2, window = nothing, picket = nothing, limit = 0.075,
              kernels = (;)) -> NamedTuple

Time metrics of the capsule flux's aₗ/a₀ over a drive_history result (taken as linear between its steps). Returns (; mean, max, min, swing, deviation, picket_mean, picket_max, picket_ok, response):

  • over window (default: the whole history): the time average mean, the extremes and swing = max − min, and deviation, the time average of |aₗ/a₀ − mean|;
  • over picket (default: the first 2 ns of the history), the time average and the largest |aₗ/a₀|; picket_ok compares the average with limit, Milovich et al.'s (Phys. Plasmas 23, 032701, 2016) 7.5 % early-time P2/P0 for under 10 % margin loss (whether their limit is on the average or the peak is not stated: derived);
  • response: for each name => kernel in kernels (ResponseKernels or functions, per % per ns), ∫ K(t) · 100 aₗ/a₀ dt in the kernel's units, e.g. a hot-spot or ρR P2 Green's function (Masse et al., Phys. Plasmas 26, 2019). Without kernels it is an empty NamedTuple: the flux history alone does not give a hot-spot shape. Masse's hot-spot kernel changes sign before about 4.5 ns while the ρR one does not, so a time-integrated flux P2 says neither, and Briareus reports no µm by default.

All fractions (not percent).

Briareus.velocity_uncertainty — Method
velocity_uncertainty(scenario, beams; per_percent, amplification = 1, vimp = 380,
                     a = 3.3, chi = nothing, q = a - 1, kwargs...) -> NamedTuple
velocity_uncertainty(u; ...)

The beam errors of mode_uncertainty (its keywords: pointing, power, group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning), or its result u, carried to the hot-spot velocity at per_percent km/s per % of mode 1 (hotspot_velocity). Returns (; velocity, covariance, sigma, rms, rms_by, speed_rms, yield_factor, expected_yield, total, expected_total):

  • velocity, the nominal (km/s), covariance (3 × 3, km²/s²) and sigma its per-axis rms; rms = √tr Σ, split by source in rms_by (beam, group, positioning; they add in quadrature) and speed_rms = √E|v|²;
  • yield_factor, the no-α factor at the nominal velocity, and expected_yield, its expectation over the errors, from the Gaussian closed form of E[exp(−a f²)] ((1 − f²)ᵃ ≈ exp(−a f²), within 1 % at f = 0.25); total and expected_total include the α factor (chi given) the same way, exp(−(a + q χ^1.2) f²).

Linear in the errors, like mode_uncertainty.

source
Briareus.yield_factor — Method
yield_factor(f; a = 3.3) -> Real

No-α yield relative to 1D for a mode-1 (piston) asymmetry f = v_HS/v_imp: (1 − f²)ᵃ (Hurricane et al., Phys. Plasmas 27, 062704, 2020, Eq. 20), with a the temperature exponent of ⟨σv⟩ (3.3 for 4–7 keV; reactivity_exponent). Generic.
