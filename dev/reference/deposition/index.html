Laser deposition

Two methods deposit laser power on a target. DepositionOperator with deposit is a precomputed quadrature over the f-cone directions with per-direction visibility, refined where each beam can land: each surface point receives, from each cone direction that reaches it unobstructed, the focal profile where that ray crosses the best-focus plane, weighted by the ray's own incidence angle. The result is smooth in pointing, defocus, spot size and power, so ForwardDiff gradients flow through it; 48 quads on a 3,000-element hohlraum take about 45 ms on one thread, and each beam's power is conserved to 1e-4. raytrace_deposition, an energy-conserving geometric ray trace, is the reference.

Power that clips the outside of a target, misses it or strikes the capsule is accounted for separately. The operator samples a target's outside only for its total, with Gauss rules graded toward the entrance-hole rims, where clipped light concentrates; clipped power agrees with converged ray traces to 1 % from 1e-4 to 5 % of a beam. render draws orthographic images of any per-element field from any direction or port, and line_of_sight_power gives the x-ray power toward a distant detector.

Briareus.Deposition — Type
Deposition

Absorbed laser power by element (element, TW), power striking the unmodeled back side of each component (backside, TW — e.g. the outside of a hohlraum, i.e. LEH clipping), and per-beam totals (per_beam, TW, power that landed on any opaque surface).

Briareus.DepositionOperator — Type
DepositionOperator(target, beams; level = 1, ncone = 13, resolution = 0.3,
                   reach = 2.0, maxlevel = 5, shadow = 0,
                   backside_resolution = 1.0, edge_resolution = 0.01,
                   profile = nothing)

Precomputed laser-deposition quadrature. For each beam, every opaque triangle side that faces the beam and belongs to an element is sampled at 4^level points, or more where the beam can land: a triangle within reach (mm) of the beam's footprint (2.5 spot semi-axes grown by the f-cone along the beam axis through the aim) is divided until its sub-triangles are no longer than resolution times the spot's smaller semi-axis, up to 4^maxlevel points; with shadow > 0, such a triangle crossed by a shadow edge (its points see different cone directions) is divided shadow times more. reach is the aim motion the operator stays accurate for; beyond it, deposition is resolved only at level. The defaults conserve each beam's power to 1e-4 when nothing clips and to 2e-3 when an entrance-hole lip clips several percent of it, on fine and coarse hohlraum meshes alike.

Unmodeled back sides (the outside of a hohlraum, whose power is the clipped backside loss) need only their total per component, and that is set by the open edges the light passes: an entrance-hole lip clipping 1e-4 of a beam puts the clipped light within ~1/50 of a spot semi-axis of the rim. Back sides are sampled at one point per triangle away from the beam; within reach,

  • a triangle on an open edge (an edge that no other opaque triangle shares, such as an entrance-hole rim) or touching one at a vertex gets a Gauss rule graded geometrically toward it, from resolution down to edge_resolution spot semi-axes;
  • a triangle within one (larger) spot semi-axis of an open edge, where the shoulder of a clipped spot lands, is divided down to resolution;
  • the rest are divided down to backside_resolution.

On the NIF hohlraum, clipped power then matches ray traces (averaged over nspot 241–361) to 1% from 1e-4 to 5% of a beam, 2.5% at 1e-6, and 0.6% up to 50%, and is smooth in the aim. Element values do not depend on these two keywords.

Each point stores its area weight, the normal of the lit side and a bitmask of which of the ncone f-cone directions reach it from the lens without hitting another surface. These depend only on the target and the beam directions, so the operator stays valid as pointing, defocus, spot size, and power change — deposition is then a smooth function of those parameters (see deposit).

The beam is the superposition of ncone collimated sub-beams, one per f-cone direction dⱼ (cone_directions), each carrying the best-focus profile. A point receives from each visible direction the focal profile where the ray along dⱼ through it crosses the best-focus plane, times that ray's incidence factor |n·dⱼ|/(k·dⱼ). This is the geometric-optics convolution of the focal spot with the f-cone, sampled at the cone directions as the reference ray trace samples it, so the power on any surface that intercepts the whole beam is the beam power, at any distance from focus and any incidence; and shadows cast by objects near focus — a capsule at TCC, an LEH edge — are correlated with the part of the spot they block.

profile = VisRadProfile(...) replaces this with VisRad's beam model, so that a design can be checked in VisRad and its deposition reproduced. The model keeps the super-Gaussian's shape along the beam, with semi-axes that grow on a hyperbola and an envelope cut-off. Points are then sampled along the beam axis only (ncone is ignored), and the beam axis through each point determines its shadowing. The two models agree near focus; away from it VisRad's spot keeps its shape while the f-cone convolution softens the edges, and VisRad's spot grows faster (on NIF hohlraum walls the f-cone model's peaks are 10% higher than VisRad's). Power on a surface that intercepts the whole beam is the beam's power in planes normal to the beam, and only approximately on tilted ones (per-plane normalization: 0.15% high on a foil tilted 20° across ±2.5 mm of focus, where VisRad's totals are exact).

The beams' ports must not change after construction.

Briareus.deposit — Method
deposit(op, beams; absorption = nothing) -> Deposition

Evaluate laser deposition for beams (same ports and order as at construction). The result is differentiable in every beam's aim, power, defocus and spot axes. Power that strikes no opaque surface (e.g. passes through a hohlraum and out the other LEH) is delivered - sum(per_beam). Threads share the work dynamically, and every sum is taken in a fixed order: the result is the same, bit for bit, on any number of threads.

By default every ray deposits all its power where it lands. absorption = η⊥ (0 < η⊥ ≤ 1) keeps only the fraction

η_a(ψ) = 1 - (1 - η⊥)^(cos³ψ)

of each ray, with ψ its angle of incidence (each f-cone direction's, or the beam axis's with a VisRad profile). This is inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption in a thin corona whose density falls exponentially with radius, η⊥ = 1 - exp(-8$ν_{ei}$ L/3c) being the absorption at normal incidence ($ν_{ei}$ the collision frequency at critical density, L the scale length): Murakami & Nishi, Matter Radiat. Extremes 2, 55 (2017), Eq. 29, after Kruer's text. It is a model for hard-sphere direct-drive illumination, derived for that profile, not a measured or calibrated absorption: η⊥ has no default and is the user's estimate. It holds only for thin coronas (scale length L ≪ the critical radius $r_c$): measured against ray tracing through a spherical exponential corona, an oblique ray's absorption approaches $η_a$ only as $L/r_c$ → 0 (at sin ψ = 0.9: 4 % off at $L/r_c$ = 0.0025, 13 % at 0.01, and 0.33 against $η_a$ = 0.18 at 0.1), and rays that miss the critical sphere still absorb. On a 40-beam OMEGA PDD pointing with $L/r_c$ = 0.2, $η_a$ on a hard sphere at $r_c$ gives σ = 5.7 % (l ≤ 30) where the corona gives 10.4 % (research notes, "Corona model prototype: impact-parameter kernels vs 3-D ray tracing"). Polar direct drive gets its equatorial drive from exactly those grazing rays. The unabsorbed light is dropped (no reflection is followed), so element, backside and per_beam are absorbed powers and delivered - sum(per_beam) includes it. η⊥ may be a dual number (the result is differentiable in it too). η⊥ = 1 is the default's model ($η_a ≡ 1$), equal to it to rounding; the default itself is unchanged by this option.

Briareus.raytrace_deposition — Method
raytrace_deposition(target, beams; nspot = 41, ncone = 13, extent = nothing) -> Deposition

Reference deposition by geometric ray tracing. Each spot component (core, and halo if present) is sampled with an nspot × nspot grid of best-focus points spanning ±extent 1/e semi-axes (default: out to 1e-12 of the component's peak), each launched along ncone f-cone directions and weighted by the focal intensity. Rays travel straight (no refraction) to the first opaque surface. Power is conserved exactly; defocus is exact within the sampling. Not differentiable.

Briareus.CoronaOperator — Type
CoronaOperator(profile, beams; center = (0, 0, 0), lmax = 30, ncone = 13,
               spacing = 0.1 * smallest spot semi-axis, panels = 200)

Precomputed laser absorption in the static, spherically symmetric corona profile (CoronaProfile) about center, for polar and symmetric direct drive on a bare sphere: deposit(op, beams) gives the absorbed power per steradian on the sphere about center as real spherical harmonics up to degree lmax, the primary output of direct-drive designs (reference/litreview/polar_direct_drive.md, decision 4).

Each beam is ncone collimated sub-beams along its f-cone directions (cone_directions), each carrying the best-focus profile, as in DepositionOperator. Their rays are refracted by the corona (cold-plasma geometric optics, $n^2 = 1 - n_e/n_c$) and absorbed by inverse bremsstrahlung along their paths (Kruer, The Physics of Laser Plasma Interactions, ch. 5); light that is not absorbed leaves the corona. Absorbed power is assigned to the direction from center of the point where it is absorbed (projected radially). In a spherical corona every ray stays in its plane and is fixed by its impact parameter, so the paths are integrated once, by radial quadrature (panels per pass), into harmonic kernels at the impact parameters of a square lattice of rays (spacing, mm) across each sub-beam, out to the profile's outer radius. These depend on the profile, the lattice and the ports only, so the operator serves every aim, defocus, spot and power: the beams' ports and f-numbers must not change after construction.

Scope and limits:

  • Spherical corona only. A non-spherical corona is outside the model: a P₂ of 4 % in the critical radius moves σ₂ by about 3 % of its value and the absorption by 1 point (measured by 3-D ray tracing; research notes, "Corona model prototype").
  • Bare sphere only. Nothing but the corona interacts with the light: no other target parts, stalks or shields, which would need their own shadowing; the profile must reach $n_c$ (CoronaProfile), so no light reaches the surface inside.
  • No cross-beam energy transfer (decision 8 of the review; deferred): results are CBET-free, and optimistic where CBET matters (the PDD equator).

deposit(op, beams; absorption = η⊥) on a hard sphere is this model's limit for a thin corona; oblique and grazing rays depart from it at finite scale lengths (research notes, "Corona model prototype").

Briareus.CoronaProfile — Type
CoronaProfile(r, ne_over_nc, Te; Z, lnΛ, wavelength = 0.351)
CoronaProfile(; critical_radius, scale_length, absorption,
              outer_radius = critical_radius + 14scale_length)

A static, spherically symmetric laser corona for CoronaOperator, given by the electron density as a fraction $n_e/n_c$ of the light's critical density (not in cm⁻³): that ratio and the inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption rate as functions of the radius from the target center.

The first form tabulates a snapshot, such as a 1-D hydrodynamics output: radii r (mm, increasing, not necessarily uniform), ne_over_nc ($n_e/n_c$, positive), electron temperature Te (eV), and the mean ionization Z and Coulomb logarithm lnΛ (each a number or a vector over r; no defaults). wavelength is the laser's vacuum wavelength (µm), which sets $n_c$. The absorption rate per unit length of a ray, divided by its refractive index, is (ν_ei/c)(n_e/n_c) with $ν_{ei}$ the electron–ion collision frequency of Kruer (The Physics of Laser Plasma Interactions, ch. 5), 2.91e-6 Z n_e lnΛ / T_e^{3/2} s⁻¹ ($n_e$ in cm⁻³, $T_e$ in eV). Both ln(n_e/n_c) and the logarithm of that rate are interpolated by cubic Hermite polynomials (three-point slopes), so an exponential profile is represented exactly; the profile is zero beyond r[end]. ne_over_nc[1] ≥ 1 is required: every ray must turn before the innermost radius (the light never reaches a hard surface, whose absorption the corona model does not include).

The second form is the analytic isothermal corona n_e/n_c = exp(-(r - r_c)/L) with critical_radius $r_c$ and scale_length L (mm), truncated at outer_radius, whose absorption rate is set by absorption, the fraction η⊥ that a planar profile of the same scale length absorbs at normal incidence: η⊥ = 1 - exp(-8$ν_c$ L/3c) (Kruer; the η⊥ of deposit(op, beams; absorption), Murakami & Nishi 2017, Eq. 29). It is also the exact absorption of a radial ray when the truncation is far out (e⁻¹⁴ of $n_c$ by default).

Not calibrated: a profile is the user's input (a snapshot or an engineering estimate), and Z, lnΛ and absorption have no defaults.

Briareus.deposit — Method
deposit(op::CoronaOperator, beams) -> (; ylm, per_beam, delivered)

Laser absorption in the corona of op for beams (same ports and order as at construction): ylm, the flat real-harmonic coefficients (mode_index order, l ≤ op.lmax, polar axis ẑ, about the operator's center) of the absorbed power per steradian F(Ω) = Σ ylm[mode_index(l, m)] Y_lm(Ω) (TW/sr); per_beam, each beam's absorbed power (TW); and delivered, the beams' total power. The absorbed fraction is sum(per_beam) / delivered (equal to √(4π) ylm[1] / delivered), the nonuniformity mode_rms(ylm, lmax) and its spectrum mode_power(ylm_matrix(ylm, lmax)). No cross-beam energy transfer: results are CBET-free.

Generic in the beams' number type (aims, powers, defocus and spot axes may be ForwardDiff duals); threads share the beams and the sums are taken in a fixed order, so the result does not depend on the number of threads.
