Beams and facilities

Briareus.Beam — Type
Beam(name; theta, phi, power = 1.0, spot, fnumber = 20.0, aim = (0,0,0),
     defocus = 0.0, group = :none, quad = "")

One laser beam entering from the port at polar angle theta and azimuth phi (degrees, target-chamber frame, z up). power in TW. group labels beams that share pointing parameters (e.g. a cone). wavelength is the nominal vacuum wavelength (µm) and dlambda the detuning from it (Å), used by laser–plasma models.

Briareus.SuperGaussianSpot — Type
SuperGaussianSpot(a, b = a; order = 2.0, angle = 0.0, halo = 0.0, halo_scale = 3.0,
                  halo_order = 2.0)

Elliptical super-Gaussian best-focus intensity I ∝ exp(-[(u/a)² + (v/b)²]^(order/2)) with 1/e semi-axes a, b (mm). angle (radians) rotates the a axis from the port's θ̂ toward φ̂.

Measured phase-plate spots have low-level wings beyond the super-Gaussian core fit. A fraction halo of the power can be put in a second, broader component with semi-axes halo_scale·(a, b) and order halo_order — the halo matters for clearance and clipping checks, not for drive symmetry.

Briareus.VisRadProfile — Type
VisRadProfile(; envelope = nothing, focal_length = 7695.0, growth = 0.67, measured = true)

VisRad's analytic beam model (VR/powersources/setlaserprofileprops.html), for deposition that reproduces VisRad 21's (DepositionOperator(...; profile)). The beam is not the focal spot convolved over the f-cone, as in Briareus's default model, but a super-Gaussian whose semi-axes a(z), b(z) and exponent n(z) change with the distance z from best focus. It is cut off at the elliptical radius ρ (the envelope ratio, in 1/e semi-axes a(z), b(z)) and renormalized so the envelope holds the beam's power in every transverse plane. A surface absorbs the profile times the incidence factor |n·k| of the beam axis, where the axis reaches it unobstructed.

Measured plates (measured = true, the default). For the four NIF phase plates VisRad's profile along the beam is measured (_VR_MEASURED: one beam per quad deposited on a plate normal to it at |z| = 0–15 mm, VisRad 21; reference/research-notes.md, "VisRad deposition parity"): a(z)/a₀, b(z)/b₀ and n(z)/n₀ are tabulated in |z| (symmetric about focus) and interpolated linearly, the last segment extended beyond the table. n grows by about 1.1%/mm (inner plates) and 1.6%/mm (outer), the manual's n₀(1 + 0.2|z|/z₀) with z₀ ≈ 18.5 and 12.7 mm; the semi-axes follow no hyperbola, the minor growing faster than the major. A beam takes a plate's tables when its focal exponent matches to 0.01 and its 1/e semi-axes to 1% (as visrad_profile reads them from a workspace). With VisRad's quad-relative spot rotation (read_visrad_workspace) this reproduces VisRad's hohlraum wall deposition to 0.9% rms per element.

Other spots (and measured = false) take the manual's hyperbolic semi-axes at fixed n,

a(z) = a₀ √((z/z_A)² + 1),   z_A = (γ/ρ) FL [(FL / (2a₀ f#))² − 1]^(-1/2),

and likewise b(z) with zB, for final-optic distance FL (`focallength, mm; VisRad's port radius, 769.5 cm at NIF) andgrowthγ. The manual gives z_A without the factor γ/ρ (growth = nothing` uses it so). γ = 0.67 was fitted to VisRad's hohlraum deposition before the plates were measured (2.8% element rms there).

envelope is the ratio ρ for every beam (a number), per beam name (a Dict; beams not in it take the default), or nothing for VisRad's default (ln 1000)^(1/n₀), the radius where the focal profile falls to 10⁻³ of its peak (it reproduces the NIF plates' ratios 1.53645, 1.49576, 1.27326 and 1.28955). visrad_profile takes the envelopes and focal length from a workspace.

Not modeled: the blend toward a square beam (f_sq, zero for beams circular at the final optics, as NIF's behave), the envelope's own dependence on n(z) (the cut is at ρ in 1/e semi-axes; the profile there is ≲ 10⁻³ of peak), and occultation by VisRad's ray grid (here the beam axis through each point decides it).

Briareus.intensity — Method
intensity(frame_or_beam, x)

Beam intensity (TW/mm², per unit area normal to the beam) at point x. Away from best focus the spot is the focal profile convolved with the geometric f-cone disk; this is approximated by widening the semi-axes so second moments add exactly (a(s)² = a² + s²/(16 F² m_n) with m_n = Γ(4/n)/(2Γ(2/n))) at fixed order n, for the core and halo components separately. This smooth profile serves envelope and clearance estimates; far from focus its shape departs from the convolution, so deposition (DepositionOperator, raytrace_deposition) sums over the cone directions instead.

Briareus.hybride_mock_pointing — Function
hybride_mock_pointing(beams = nif_beams(); table = HYBRIDE_MOCK_POINTING) -> Vector{Beam}

A public mock of the HYBRID-E (N221204/N230729-class) NIF pointing: beams (NIF beams or quads, with group the cone) re-aimed by cone, defocus 0; power, spot and dlambda are kept. Each cone's quads share one rule in their own frame, mirrored top to bottom: the aim sits at ±z (mm, the bottom hemisphere at -z), r along the quad's port azimuth (negative: across the axis, toward the opposite wall) and, for the outer cones, ±s along the quad's φ̂, each beam aiming to the side opposite its own port (-sign(φ_beam − φ_quad) s; the quad's beams cross). The quad azimuth is that of the mean port of its beams in beams, so a quad given as one beam (nif_quads()) gets no split.

44.5°5.700.5

Derived, not a facility pointing: the values were fitted, on a 0.1 mm grid, to the capsule-flux moments and LEH beam crossing of a non-public as-shot HYBRID-E pointing on the 11.24 × 6.4 mm hohlraum with 3.1 mm LEHs, which they reproduce to within 0.15 percentage point (P2/P0, P4/P0, P6/P0 and Y_lm/Y00 for l ≤ 6, at foot and peak cone fractions) and its reduced-model CBET transfer to 0.03 % (research notes, "Mock HYBRID-E pointing (2026-10-23)").

Briareus.nif_beams — Method
nif_beams(; power = 2.0, fnumber = 20.7, spot = nif_spot)

All 192 indirect-drive NIF beams with their individual directions from the LMF beam table (data/nif_beams.csv, transcribed from beam-port-locations-updated-10-15-2020.xlsx of the LMF Shot RI Resources). Names are the facility beam names ("B111"–"B468": bundle xy, beam z; beams 1–4 form quad QxyT, 5–8 QxyB); quad is the quad name and group the cone. The mean direction of each quad's four beams is its port direction to within 0.004°.

Briareus.omega_ports — Method
omega_ports() -> Vector{NamedTuple}

The 60 OMEGA beam directions in a hohlraum frame whose z axis is a pentagon (P6–P7) axis. They are the vertices of a truncated icosahedron with hexagon:pentagon edge ratio 1.2 (cut at 1.2/3.4 of each icosahedron edge), which gives cones at 21.415°, 42.020°, 58.852° and 81.249° and their supplements (Sci. Rep. 13, 9883, 2023). The azimuthal origin puts cone 1 at φ = 0°, 72°, …; it is not tied to chamber coordinates. Groups are :cone1–:cone4; lower-hemisphere beams use the same groups.

omega_spot(name = :EIDI300)
