Geometry and targets

Briareus — Module
Briareus

View-factor radiation and laser-pointing design for high-energy-density experiments: indirect-drive hohlraums, backlighter foils, and other laser-irradiated targets.

Units: lengths mm, time ns, power TW, flux TW/mm² (to_Wcm2 converts), radiation temperature eV, angles in degrees at the user interface.

Briareus.ConeSurface — Type
ConeSurface(half_angle; apex = (0, 0, 0), axis = (0, 0, 1))

One nappe of a right circular cone: the points seen from apex at half_angle degrees (0 < θ < 90) from axis, at distance z tan θ from the axis a distance z along it (an entrance-hole cone, a conical shield, a tapered bore). As on a cylinder, points are projected onto it radially, keeping their height along the axis, so the chords of a circle about the axis (the rims of a faceted cone) land on the circle and a faceted frustum covers the true one exactly; facets' patches have exact areas.

source
Briareus.CurvedSurface — Type
CurvedSurface

Exact surface that a faceted component approximates: SphereSurface, CylinderSurface, ConeSurface, TorusSurface or DiskSurface. When a component carries one, the radiation model integrates over the true surface (points, normals and areas of the curved patches), while ray casting and visibility keep using the flat facets. On a curved concave surface the view-factor kernel stays bounded, and flat facets would misrepresent the exchange between nearby elements.

Briareus.DiskSurface — Type
DiskSurface(; center = (0, 0, 0), normal = (0, 0, 1))

Plane through center whose elements are polar sectors about center, as produced by annulus_mesh and disk_mesh: each element's exact shape is the annular sector spanned by its vertices, with circular arcs where the mesh has chords. Giving a hohlraum's end caps and laser entrance holes this surface makes them meet a CylinderSurface wall exactly at the rim.

Briareus.TorusSurface — Type
TorusSurface(major_radius, minor_radius; center = (0, 0, 0), axis = (0, 0, 1))

Torus: the points at minor_radius r from its spine, the circle of major_radius R about axis through center (a CAD fillet: a quarter of the tube where a wall meets a cap). In its meridian half-plane a point inside the tube circle is projected to the nearest point of the circle (for a ring torus, R > r, the nearest point of the torus), and a point outside it, as on a cone, radially from the axis at its height, onto the side of the tube it is on (to the pole above or below the tube); normals point away from the spine. A faceted fillet's rims are chords of circles about the axis, which lie at the rim's height toward the axis: outside the tube for rims on the poles (where a quarter fillet meets a cap) and on the inner half, inside it and along the radius on the outer equator (where it meets a wall), so on all of these the chords land on the rim circle and a fillet's elements cover the torus band exactly. Elsewhere on the outer half (a fillet meeting a cone) the rim's chords stay off the circle by their sag, and the band is covered to second order in the facet size: no projection of points alone can do both there and keep facets near the poles second order. Facets' patches have exact areas under the projection.

Briareus.annulus_mesh — Method
annulus_mesh(r_inner, r_outer, z; nr, nphi, up = true, phi0 = 0.0, phibreaks = ())

Flat annulus (a disk when r_inner == 0) in the plane z, with nr radial by nphi azimuthal patches. Front faces point along +ẑ when up, -ẑ otherwise. Rings are equal-area so patch areas are uniform. phibreaks (radians) are azimuths the mesh must contain, as in cylinder_mesh; giving both the same nphi and phibreaks makes an end cap meet a wall vertex for vertex.

source
Briareus.conforming_grid — Method
conforming_grid(a, b, n, breaks; periodic = false) -> Vector{Float64}

Grid lines from a to b with about n cells that include every break line in breaks: each interval between consecutive breaks is divided uniformly into max(1, round(len / h)) cells, h = (b - a)/n. Breaks closer than 1e-9 (b - a) are merged. With periodic (an azimuth, b - a one period), breaks are wrapped into the period and the grid runs from the first break to the first break plus the period, so the end points a, b are not grid lines themselves. Without breaks, use range(a, b; length = n + 1).

source
Briareus.cubed_sphere_mesh — Method
cubed_sphere_mesh(radius; n, outward = true)

Sphere about the origin from the six faces of a cube, each divided into n × n equiangular cells projected onto the sphere. The four corners of a projected cell are not coplanar, so each cell is split into two triangular patches along alternating diagonals chosen to keep the faceted sphere convex (12n² planar patches). Unlike a latitude–longitude mesh it has no poles and no slivers, which suits radiation problems where every part of the sphere matters equally.

source
Briareus.cylinder_mesh — Method
cylinder_mesh(radius, z0, z1; nz, nphi, inward = true, phi0 = 0.0, zbreaks = (),
              phibreaks = ())

Open cylinder of the given radius about the z axis spanning z0 ≤ z ≤ z1, with nz axial by nphi azimuthal quadrilateral patches. Front faces point toward the axis when inward (a hohlraum wall), away from it otherwise.

zbreaks (mm) and phibreaks (radians) are lines the mesh must contain, e.g. the edges of a window cut into the wall: the grid is then uniform between consecutive breaks with about the default spacing (conforming_grid), so a region bounded by break lines is tiled exactly by whole patches. Without breaks the grid is uniform from phi0.

Briareus.icosphere_mesh — Method
icosphere_mesh(radius; n, outward = true)

Geodesic sphere about the origin: each face of an icosahedron (in its standard frame, vertices at the cyclic permutations of (0, ±1, ±ϕ): mirror-symmetric in x, y and z) divided into n × n triangles whose vertices are projected onto the sphere (20n² triangular patches). The triangles are nearly equilateral everywhere (edges within about ±20% of 1.107 radius / n), with no poles and no slivers; on a capsule they converge the flux's high Legendre moments (P4) with far fewer elements than sphere_mesh, whose polar cells are slivers. icosphere_frequency gives n for an element size.

Briareus.coarsen_mesh — Method
coarsen_mesh(mesh; size, angle = 10.0, tol = 1e-9) -> Mesh

The mesh with neighboring patches merged into larger ones (the radiosity elements of a target): each new patch grows from a seed patch over shared edges, taking patches whose normals are within angle degrees of the seed's and whose vertices lie within size / 2 mm of the seed's centroid. Triangles are unchanged; only their grouping into patches (and their order) is. Imported CAD often has hundreds of thousands of facets (fine tessellation of curved faces, slivers along edges), far more than view factors need: coarsening to a few hundred micrometres makes such parts usable for radiation.

Patches are merged only across edges where both are oriented consistently (see orient_mesh), so the front side of a merged patch is well defined, and an element stays within angle of planar: the flat-patch error of a merged element on a curved surface is that of a facet of the same size. Seeds are taken by decreasing area, so large flat faces are kept whole up to size.

source
Briareus.orient_mesh — Method
orient_mesh(mesh; toward = nothing, tol = 1e-9) -> Mesh

The mesh with its patches' windings made consistent: across every edge two patches share (vertices welded by position to tol times the mesh's extent), the patches traverse it in opposite directions, so their front sides lie on the same side of the surface. Imported CAD is often stitched from faces of either orientation (a radiating element's front is the side its winding defines).

Each connected surface then gets one orientation as a whole:

  • a closed surface (every edge shared by exactly two patches) faces out of the volume it encloses (positive signed volume), so a thick hohlraum wall, meshed as a closed solid, faces into the cavity on its inner side;
  • an open surface faces the point toward (e.g. the target center for a thin hohlraum wall) by area-weighted majority, or, without toward, keeps the orientation of the larger part of its area.

Edges shared by more than two patches (non-manifold) do not constrain the orientation, and a surface that cannot be oriented consistently (a Möbius-like stitching) keeps the first orientation found for each patch; both are reported in a warning.

source
Briareus.remesh_mesh — Method
remesh_mesh(mesh; size, angle = 10.0, tol = 1e-9) -> Mesh

The mesh's surface divided into compact, near-planar radiosity elements of about size mm, however its facets are shaped: on a smooth surface the elements average size² in area (a square element of side size has the same) and every vertex lies within size of its element's centroid. Imported CAD tessellates curved walls as long slivers (a hohlraum wall as axial strips spanning its length), which coarsen_mesh cannot shorten: it only groups whole facets. Here:

  1. vertices are welded by position (to tol times the mesh's extent), and one-sided edges with other such edges' vertices on them (T-junctions between CAD faces) are split there, so faces that meet along an edge are connected;
  2. every facet is split, in its own plane, into triangles with edges of at most size / 3; an edge gets the same points from both facets sharing it, so the refined triangulation is conforming. Parallel edges longer than that (the sides of a wall's strips: a cylinder's or an extrusion's generators, to 0.25°) and the diagonals of their strips are cut where they cross a common family of planes across them, so neighboring strips are cut at the same places and a cut across a strip is one straight chord; other edges are divided equally;
  3. the refined triangles are clustered: seeds 0.85 size apart (a Poisson-disk sample) claim triangles nearest first across shared, consistently wound edges (see orient_mesh), taking those whose normals are within angle degrees of the seed's; Lloyd iterations move each seed to its element's centroid and mean normal. On strips (facets whose edges longer than size are all cut by one family of planes), every few planes bound a band about size long, and elements grow within one band: their cuts across the strips lie on the planes, straight and shared by the elements on both sides, and a sliver strip's piece in a band goes whole to one element, so their sides run along strip edges (an element w wide crosses about w/(strip width) strips, and its outline has about two edges per strip). An element with a facet more than angle from its mean normal, a vertex farther than size from its centroid, or a centroid that does not project (along the normal) onto it, is halved until it has none;
  4. within each element, the pieces of facets in one plane are joined and re-triangulated from their outline (a point is dropped only where it lies on a straight outline in every element using it, so the mesh stays conforming).

The surface is unchanged: every output triangle lies in the plane of an input facet, and the elements tile the input facets exactly (areas agree to round-off). Facets of zero area (height below 1e-12 of their longest edge) and triangles outside every patch are dropped; slivers thinner than 1e-6 of their length have no orientation to keep and join any neighboring element. The input's patches do not constrain the elements; its windings do (orient it first). Faces narrower than size (a wall's end, a fillet) give elements as narrow, and elements on a faceted curved wall keep its faceting (a facet of width w on radius R lies up to w²/8R off the true surface).

Briareus.BurnthroughAlbedo — Type
BurnthroughAlbedo(; thickness, density = 19.3, before = WallLossAlbedo(),
                  transmission = eddington_transmission(τ), after = nothing,
                  width = 0.4, burnthrough = nothing, front = HeatFront())

Albedo of a thin wall layer (a thin-foil diagnostic window: thickness µm of a material of density g/cm³, gold by default) that re-emits like the wall until the Marshak heat front (front, a HeatFront) has heated its whole areal mass ρ × thickness, and then lets part of the flux that reaches it through.

Once burned through, a fraction T = transmission of the incident flux passes through the foil and leaves the hohlraum (a number, or RosselandTransmission() for a fraction that follows the radiation temperature). The rest meets the foil as before and is re-emitted with the albedo of before. Across the transition

α(t) = α_before (1 - s T),    transmitted fraction s T,

with s = s((t - t_b)/width) the smooth step 3x² - 2x³ on [0, 1] and t_b the burnthrough time, so the burned-through albedo is (1 - T) α_before. Of the flux H A on the foil, s T H A is transmitted and (1 - s T)(1 - α_before) H A absorbed (energy_balance, drive_history). before is an albedo model or a number, by default the wall's Marshak loss (WallLossAlbedo).

The default T is the gray-foil estimate of R. S. Lester et al., Phys. Rev. E 114, L023201 (2026), doi:10.1103/k8dz-pjdx: T = 4/(3τ + 2) (their eq. 2, eddington_transmission). The optical depth is their eq. 1, τ = L/λ_Au(T_h) at a fixed Rosseland mean free path λAu, scaled linearly in areal mass (`opticaldepth) from their pointτ ∼ 5atL = 2.5 µm`:

τ = 5 × ρ thickness / (19.3 g/cm³ × 2.5 µm),

so T ≈ 0.33, 0.235 and 0.17 for 1.7, 2.5 and 3.6 µm of gold. Limitations:

  • λAu is held at their evaluation, for a hohlraum radiation temperature `Thof typically 300–350 eV and an assumed gold density of about 1 g/cm³ (the expanded foil), soTis constant once burned through.transmission = RosselandTransmission()` lets λ_Au follow the temperature of the flux on the foil instead; its density dependence is not modeled.
  • Pairing that density with the 2.5 µm as-built thickness is theirs. Taken literally, eq. 1 at 1 g/cm³ describes a layer of 0.25 mg/cm², not the foil's 4.8. The scaling with thickness (areal mass) is ours, from eq. 1.
  • The estimate is gray, planar and quasi-static, and for gold: pass transmission for another material. Below τ = 2/3 (0.33 µm of gold) it is capped at 1.

transmission = 1 makes a burned-through foil an open hole whose whole incident flux is transmitted.

An explicit after (an albedo model or number; exclusive with transmission) sets the burned-through albedo directly instead,

α(t) = α_before + s (α_after - α_before),

and counts as transmitted only the loss beyond the intact foil's, (α_before - α) H A; the intact foil's own (1 - α_before) H A stays absorbed. after = 0 is the open-hole bound on the loss with this split (the default before transmission existed).

The burnthrough time t_b is, in order:

  • the per-element time passed to solve/albedos (burnthrough = ...), which drive_history integrates from each element's own incident-flux temperature history (heat_front);
  • the explicit burnthrough (ns), when given;
  • otherwise the foil is intact (t_b = ∞): a single solve has no history to estimate it from. burnthrough_time gives the estimate for a temperature history.

Before t_b the foil loses exactly what a thick wall loses and transmits nothing: in the diffusion picture the cold material ahead of a nonlinear heat front does not change the flux into it, so no early loss is modeled. Measured burnthrough of 0.8–3 µm gold foils on a 260 eV hohlraum wall (Nova; Lindl et al., Phys. Plasmas 11, 339 (2004), Fig. 4-6) reaches half signal 0.1–0.25 ns after the computed front arrival, 0.19 ns on average, which sets the default width (half transmission at t_b + width/2; see the diagnostic-windows tutorial).

Briareus.CapsuleAlbedo — Type
CapsuleAlbedo(; albedo = 0.2, radius = nothing, outside = 0.0)

Albedo of a capsule that absorbs as a sphere of effective radius R(t) inside its fixed mesh of radius R₀, plus a share outside of what crosses the mesh outside that sphere:

α_eff(t) = 1 - (1 - α_c) s² - a_out (1 - s²),    s = R(t)/R₀,

so that the capsule mesh absorbs (1 - α_c) H 4πR(t)², the absorption of the smaller sphere at the same incident flux, plus a_out H 4π(R₀² - R(t)²), and passes the rest on (re-emitted from the mesh, an approximation of light crossing the shrunken capsule's surroundings). albedo αc is a number or an AlbedoModel (TabulatedAlbedo in time, TemperatureAlbedo in the incident radiation temperature); radius is nothing (R = R₀), a (times, R/R₀) table (ns; linear, held at its ends) or a function t -> R/R₀. outside aout (0 ≤ aout ≤ 1, default 0) is the absorptance of the blow-off between the ablation front and R₀ for the flux that misses the front: an explicit term for absorption outside the front (W-doped ablator plasma), unpublished, so 0 unless a study sets it; with the default the model is the front's alone, and without a radius the term vanishes. `energybalancethen books the capsule's absorbed power at the effective radius plus the outside term, andenergybudget` its integral Ecap.

The default αc = 0.2 is the 0-D value fcap = 1 − αcap ≈ 0.8 of Swadling et al., PoP 32, 052707 (2025). For a NIF HDC capsule in a drive history, Young et al.'s quasi-1D HYDRA ablator albedo (PoP 27, 082702 (2020) Fig. 3a, `young2020albedos()) is the published α_c(t);examples/n221204.jl` uses it on N221204's time axis.

Briareus.Component — Type
Component(name, mesh, material; kind = :surface, two_sided = false, surface = nothing,
          part = name)

A named piece of a target. kind = :surface components are opaque: they block laser rays and lines of sight, absorb laser light, and radiate from their front side (and back side when two_sided). kind = :aperture components (e.g. laser entrance holes) are transparent diagnostic surfaces that record the x-ray flux crossing them and re-emit nothing, or, with a GapClosure or LipClosure albedo, re-emit what a closing lip of plasma would (see aperture). surface is the exact CurvedSurface the mesh approximates, if any; the radiation model then uses the curved geometry (see view_factors).

part names the user's part the component belongs to: its own name, unless it is a piece of a part split for computation (curved_components names the pieces of a part p p/cyl1, …, p/rest, each with part = p). Name-taking functions (elements, Scenario(...; observe), keep-outs, clearance hardware) accept a part's name for all its pieces, and reports (energy_balance, component_power, write_results, report) are keyed by part; see parts.

Briareus.Conversion — Type
Conversion(; efficiency = 0.75, time_exponent = 0.0, reference_time = 1.0, start = 0.0,
           t0 = 0.1, intensities = nothing, efficiencies = nothing, range = (0.0, 1.0))

Laser → x-ray conversion efficiency that varies in time and with the absorbed laser intensity, for Material(...; conversion = Conversion(...)):

η(t, I) = clamp(η₀(I) ((t - start)/reference_time)^s, range...),

with s = time_exponent, t - start clamped below at t0 (ns), and η₀(I) either the constant efficiency or, with intensities (W/cm²) and efficiencies, the table interpolated linearly in log₁₀ I and held at its ends. I is each element's absorbed laser flux P/A (×1e14 W/cm²): a property of the element mesh too (coarse elements average a spot over more area). Evaluate with conversion_efficiency.

Published forms (see nif_conversion for the preset):

  • in time, η ∝ t^0.12–0.2: 0.7 t^0.12 for Nova's 1 ns hohlraums, which makes the measured T ∝ t^0.15 (Lindl et al., PoP 11, 339 (2004), Fig. 4-4 caption); 0.7 t^0.2 (Rosen 2005, Nova LASNEX); 0.85 t^0.2 (Schein 2007, Omega);
  • in intensity, the backscatter-corrected plateau on Au, U and cocktail spheres: 0.95 at 10¹⁴ and 0.80 at 10¹⁵ W/cm² (Dewald et al., PoP 15, 072706 (2008), abstract), Conversion(intensities = (1e14, 1e15), efficiencies = (0.95, 0.80)). A hohlraum's η exceeds a disk's at the same intensity (Lindl 2004, Fig. 4-10).

Generic in the number type of the laser power (the intensity table is differentiable); a material with a plain number keeps that number, bit for bit.

source
Briareus.GapClosure — Type
GapClosure(; closure, width = 0.4, after = WallLossAlbedo())

Albedo of an opening that closes during the pulse, such as the assembly gap around a diagnostic window, WallWindow(; gap, gap_material = Material(:Au; albedo = GapClosure(closure = 3.0))). Until closure (ns) the gap is open: all the flux that reaches it leaves the hohlraum, as through an aperture. Across the next width ns it closes, and then it re-emits like the wall with the albedo of after (a model or a number):

α(t) = s α_after,    transmitted fraction 1 - s,

with s = s((t - closure)/width) the smooth step 3x² - 2x³ on [0, 1], as for BurnthroughAlbedo. energy_balance books (1 - s) H A as transmitted, and drive_history counts it in window_loss. The closure time is an input: a gap tens of micrometres wide closes as plasma blown off its walls fills it, which this model does not compute. Unlike an aperture, the gap is opaque to laser light and to view-factor visibility rays, which matters only for gaps a beam would reach.

source
Briareus.HeatFront — Type
HeatFront(; coefficient = 9.51e-4, temperature_exponent = 1.914, time_exponent = 0.5156)

Areal mass (g/cm²) behind the ablative (subsonic) Marshak heat front that radiation of temperature T drives into a gold wall: at constant T,

m_F = c (T/100 eV)^a (t/1 ns)^b,

with the defaults from the self-similar solution of Hammer & Rosen, Phys. Plasmas 10, 1829 (2003), eq. 112 for a constant drive (q₀ = 0), built on their power-law fits to gold's opacity and equation of state. For a drive T(t) the front is integrated as

m_F(t) = c [∫₀ᵗ (T(t′)/100 eV)^(a/b) dt′]^b,

which is exact at constant T, depends only on the drive history, and for a ramp T ∝ tᵏ gives m_F = c (1 + k a/b)^(-b) T(t)^a t^b: within 2% of Hammer & Rosen's exact coefficient for k = 0.182 and within 1% of Shussman & Heizler's (Phys. Plasmas 22, 082109 (2015)) for k = 0.123 (see heat_front and the diagnostic-windows tutorial). Lindl et al. (Phys. Plasmas 11, 339 (2004), eq. 4-24) give c = 1.20e-3, a = 1.86, b = 0.54, the scaling the WallLossAlbedo defaults come from; it heats about 20% more mass at 300 eV, 1 ns. T is the incident-flux radiation temperature (Lindl's convention; Hammer & Rosen drive with the surface temperature, a few percent lower for gold late in a pulse).

Briareus.LipClosure — Type
LipClosure(; speed = 0.022, exponent = 0.5, depth = 0.24, hold_time = 0.4,
           hold = (6.0, 17.0), after = WallLossAlbedo())

An entrance-hole lip whose closure is computed from the local conditions (hohlraum(; leh_closure = :computed)). The gold blown off the lip fills the hole from its rim inward; the plasma edge (the radius at which the blow-off turns opaque) sits a depth d(t) inside the initial radius r₀ and moves as

dd/dt = (1 − h) v(T) + h (depth − d)/hold_time,    v(T) = speed (T/100 eV)^exponent,

with T the radiation temperature incident on the lip (the sound speed of x-ray-ablated gold, c_s ∝ T^½, sets the free expansion) and h ∈ [0, 1] the laser's hold: the smooth step 3x² − 2x³ of the laser intensity through the hole (power of the beams entering it over the hole's initial area, TW/mm²) between hold[1] and hold[2]. While the beams pass at high power the blow-off stagnates against the laser-heated plasma in a dense ridge depth inside the rim (Chen et al., PoP 27, 022702 (2020): the ridge forms on the rise to peak where the outer beams cross the lip blow-off, then holds; "heating of the plasma inside the LEH region by the lasers keeps the LEH open"); a lip that closed further in a long foot is pushed back to it (Schneider et al. 2015: closes in the low-power part, reopens at peak). Below hold[1] (the foot, and after the laser turns off) the edge moves in freely at v(T).

drive_history integrates d(t) per lip from times[1] (open there) and maps it onto the lip's elements: a lip meshed in rings between its inner radius and r₀ (the floor of the closure) has each ring the closed fraction s of its area outside the edge, so the open area is exact at any radial resolution. A closed part re-emits with the albedo of after, α = s α_after, and 1 − s escapes (as for GapClosure). A single solve treats the lip as open unless given its closed fraction per element as burnthrough. lip_depth integrates the closure for given histories.

Defaults (calibrated, research notes "Computed LEH closure (2026-10-24)"): fitted to the measured effective LEH radius of five HDC basecamp shots (Chen 2020 Fig. 27, 3.37 mm LEHs, chen2020_leh()) with the lip temperature of the model's own basecamp history: speed, depth and hold_time; the hold intensities bracket the rise (free closure at 110 TW, held from ~300 TW of 400); rms 0.033 mm over 31 points, about the shot-to-shot scatter. depth 0.24 mm matches the closure at peak of Young et al. 2020 (SXI: 1.83 → 1.57 mm, 0.26) and Schneider et al. 2015 (0.84 of 1.55 mm, 0.25); on 3.1–3.64 mm LEHs a common depth and a common fraction of r₀ cannot be told apart, and the model takes the depth (the ridge stands where the beams meet the blow-off). After the laser turns off the measured edge moves in ≤ 0.02 mm/ns over 1.7 ns (LASNEX 0.07–0.11), as the fitted free speed gives at the falling temperature.

Briareus.RosselandTransmission — Type
RosselandTransmission(; reference_temperature = 325.0, exponent = 1.5)

Post-burnthrough transmission of a gold foil that follows the radiation temperature T (eV) of the flux on it, for BurnthroughAlbedo(; transmission = RosselandTransmission()). The foil's optical depth scales with gold's Rosseland opacity,

τ(T) = τ_ref (T / reference_temperature)^(-exponent),

and the transmission is eddington_transmission(τ(T)), where τ_ref is the foil's optical_depth (5 at 2.5 µm). The exponent is that of Hammer & Rosen's power-law fit to gold's Rosseland opacity, 1/K = g T^1.5 ρ^-0.2 (Phys. Plasmas 10, 1829 (2003), eq. 31, fitted over 100–200 eV), so the mean free path grows as T^1.5. The reference temperature is the middle of the 300–350 eV at which Lester et al. (Phys. Rev. E 114, L023201 (2026)) evaluate τ ∼ 5: at the reference the transmission equals the constant default. Hammer & Rosen's fit also reproduces that point: with the as-built areal mass of 2.5 µm of gold at Lester's expanded density of 1 g/cm³ it gives τ = 5.9 at 325 eV (derived, extrapolating the fit). The density is held at that expansion (K ∝ ρ^0.2: solid gold would double τ). Where no flux is known (T = nothing) the reference temperature is used.

Briareus.Target — Type
Target(components; closed = false, visibility = :centroid)

Assembled target geometry. Each patch side that radiates becomes an element (the unknowns of the radiation model): one element per patch for one-sided surfaces and apertures, two for two-sided surfaces. The back side of a one-sided surface absorbs laser light as a loss but is not modeled radiatively.

closed = true declares that the elements (apertures included) form a closed enclosure, so RadiationModel balances its view factors by default (enforce_closure!). hohlraum sets it; a target assembled from its components does not, since added parts (a foil, a sombrero) may lie outside the enclosure. Declare it (here, or cad_target(...; closed = true)) when apertures cover every opening (open_fraction shows where facets leak). On bored entrance holes, waist gaps, windows and CAD cavities so closed, balancing brings the loss through the apertures within 0.2 % of a continuum Monte Carlo with :sampled visibility (raw factors up to 0.8 % off) and the capsule drive within 0.07 %, and moves the capsule's a₂/a₀ by at most 0.08 pp (toward the reference where it moves it most). With :centroid, rims leave the raw loss 2–3 % high and balancing brings it within 0.4 %, but moves a₂/a₀ by 0.1–0.3 pp, toward the reference or away from it. Flat facets must be watertight for it: balancing a cracked mesh adds the exchange the cracks lose (research notes, "Closure on bored, windowed and CAD cavities").

visibility is the view_factors visibility rule RadiationModel uses by default: :centroid (one ray per pair) or :sampled (the visible fraction of pairs partly shadowed by a rim). Targets with rims set :sampled: cad_target and visrad_target (bored entrance holes, gaps, CAD walls), where one centroid ray leaves row sums up to 1% off on walls and 6% on LEH apertures; hohlraum keeps :centroid (flush apertures, row-sum errors ~0.1% that balancing removes; research notes, "Partial visibility").

Briareus.WallLossAlbedo — Type
WallLossAlbedo(; coefficient = 0.32, temperature_exponent = -0.7, time_exponent = -0.38,
               reference_temperature = 250.0, t0 = 0.1, range = (0.0, 0.95),
               factor = 1.0, factor_exponent = 0.0, factor_temperature = 300.0,
               liner = 0.0, front = HeatFront(), release = 1.0)

Marshak-wave wall-loss albedo, 1 - α = c (T/100 eV)^p (t/1 ns)^q, with defaults for gold from Lindl's scaling (T in heV, t in ns). t is clamped below at t0, and α to range. The radiation temperature is each element's incident-flux temperature, so solve iterates it to self-consistency; reference_temperature (eV) is used where no flux is known. The form is Lindl's for a wall held at T since t = 0; drive_history evaluates it at each element's effective time instead, from its own temperature history (wall_history).

Wall material. factor f and factor_exponent δ scale gold's loss to another wall material's,

1 - α = r(T) c (T/100 eV)^p (t/1 ns)^q,    r(T) = f (T / factor_temperature)^δ,

applied to gold's loss after its range clamp (so a DU wall's albedo may exceed gold's cap), the ratio of the two materials' Marshak wall losses (Rosen 2005: for U₀.₆Dy₀.₂Au₀.₂ against Au, E/A ∝ T^3.1 t^0.57 against T^3.3 t^0.6, a ratio ∝ T^-0.22 t^-0.05). du_wall_loss() is the preset for an Au-lined depleted-uranium wall.

Liner. liner (g/cm², 0 for none) of gold on the inside of such a wall: until the heat front has crossed it the wall loses like gold. The front's areal mass is the constant-temperature similarity form of front (a HeatFront) at the element's own T and t, m_F = c_F (T/100 eV)^a t^b (no history: exact at constant T, within a few percent of the drive integral for slow ramps), and the ratio becomes

r_eff = w + (1 - w) r(T),    w = min(1, liner / m_F),

the gold liner's share of the heated mass (an energy-content blend, engineering: continuous in T and t, so the albedo iteration stays smooth; a sharp front would switch the loss rate to the substrate's as soon as m_F passes the liner). With the defaults (factor = 1, no liner) the albedo is gold's, bit for bit.

Re-emission. release scales the stored energy the wall gives back under a falling drive in a drive_history (wall_release): the linear (Duhamel) response of the wall's own self-similar loss, times release. 1.0 is calibrated against 1-D Lagrangian radiation hydrodynamics of an ablating gold wall (examples/late_time_wall.jl: Hammer & Rosen 2003 power laws, P = 0.25 e/V, flux-limited diffusion, Marshak boundary), with the closure given that wall's own constant-T scaling: drops of 10, 30 and 50 % in the drive temperature after a NIF-like peak need 1.07, 0.99 and 0.91 for the energy the wall takes in over the next 1.5 ns, and 0-D hohlraums on three NIF shots' pulses 0.92–1.06 (range 0.9–1.07). That wall reproduces Hammer & Rosen's ablative self-similar solution (Rosen, OSTI 885380 (2005), Lecture 1) to 1–3 % in stored energy and 3–7 % in heated mass at constant and ramped temperature, within the solution's own first-order accuracy. A static (non-ablating) wall needs 0.77 (the value before 2026-10-25): its cooled surface throttles the release more, while Lindl's loss, which this model uses, is the ablative one. Research notes, "Wall re-emission and the capsule radius (2026-10-24)" and "Capsule absorption, conversion and the ablating-wall release (2026-10-25)".

Briareus.hohlraum — Method
hohlraum(; length = 9.4, radius = 2.875, leh_radius = 1.55, capsule_radius = 0.9,
         nz = 36, nphi = 48, nr_cap = 6, nr_leh = 4, capsule_ntheta = 24, capsule_nphi = 32,
         h = nothing, capsule_h = nothing, capsule_mesh = :compact, wall = gold(),
         capsule = ablator(), capsule_center = (0,0,0), windows = (), corner_radius = 0,
         nr_corner = nothing, leh_closure = nothing, leh_open_radius = nothing,
         nr_lip = nothing, sombrero = nothing)

Cylindrical hohlraum along z, centered on TCC (dimensions in mm), with components

  • :wall — inner cylinder wall (radiates inward)
  • :cap_top, :cap_bottom — end-cap washers between the LEH and the wall
  • :leh_top, :leh_bottom — laser entrance holes (apertures; their incident flux is the x-ray loss through the LEH)
  • :leh_top_lip, :leh_bottom_lip — with leh_closure, each hole's closing lip (after its hole)
  • :capsule — spherical capsule (omit with capsule_radius = 0)
  • :window_1, … — diagnostic windows in the wall, one component per WallWindow in windows (or the window's own name): closed windows are opaque wall patches with their own material, open ones apertures. A window with a gap is followed by its gap component :window_1_gap, ….
  • :sombrero_top, :sombrero_bottom — with sombrero, the entrance-hole shields (last)

Laser light that strikes the outside of the wall or caps (clipping on the LEH edge) is reported as the _outside back-side loss of those components; light that leaves through an open window is :missed. The wall, caps, LEHs, windows and capsule carry their exact CylinderSurface, DiskSurface and SphereSurface, so the radiation model uses the true geometry. The target is declared closed, so RadiationModel balances its view factors (enforce_closure!) unless given closed = false: windows, their gaps and closing lips are apertures, the sombreros see nothing of the cavity, and with open windows and gaps the balanced loss and capsule drive agree with a continuum Monte Carlo within 0.1 % (research notes, "Closure on bored, windowed and CAD cavities").

With windows, the wall and end-cap meshes contain the windows' edges as grid lines (conforming_grid; the wall's axial grid gets their z edges, wall and caps share the azimuthal grid with their φ edges), so each rectangular window (and gap frame) is tiled exactly by whole patches, and the caps still meet the wall vertex for vertex. A circular window is cut the same way as a square box around it and its gap, half a wall cell wider, which is re-meshed to conform to the circles (_circular_window) and stitched to the surrounding grid at the box's own vertices. Without windows the mesh is the uniform nz × nphi grid.

Mesh size: give h (mm) to set the wall, cap and LEH counts from an element size instead (hohlraum_counts), and capsule_h for the capsule (capsule_counts); they replace the corresponding counts.

corner_radius (mm) rounds the wall–cap corners into quarter-circle fillets of that radius (a hohlraum machined with curved corners): the wall stops corner_radius short of each end and the caps corner_radius short of the wall radius. Each fillet is nr_corner bands (default: about one wall cell of arc each, at least 2), components :corner_top_1, … (from the wall to the cap) and :corner_bottom_1, …, of the wall material. Each band is the exact frustum through its two rim circles (ConeSurface), so the fillet is the chord polygon of the arc (sagitta corner_radius (1 − cos(π/4k)), 20 µm for 1 mm in 4 bands) and the bands, wall and caps meet vertex for vertex.

leh_closure, a GapClosure, closes each entrance hole to x-rays from leh_radius down to leh_open_radius during the pulse: the annulus between them is a closing lip (aperture(...; closure)), transparent to laser light and lines of sight, which lets its incident flux escape until closure.closure (ns) and across the next closure.width ns turns into plasma that re-emits with the albedo of closure.after (the wall's, e.g. WallLossAlbedo()). Engineering default for low-fill NIF hohlraums: open through the foot, closing over ~0.5 ns at the rise to peak, to leh_open_radius = 0.84 leh_radius (Schneider et al. 2015: ~0.84 r₀ through the peak; Young et al. 2020 use 85 % static radii; research notes, "Closing entrance holes"). Beams pass the lip unclipped: the laser keeps its own path open (Chen et al. 2020).

leh_closure = :computed (or a LipClosure) computes the closure instead: each lip's plasma edge moves in from leh_radius at the sound speed of the gold blown off the lip and stands a fixed depth inside the rim while the beams pass at high power (LipClosure, calibrated on measured LEH radii; drive_history integrates it per hole from the lip's own temperature and the beams entering that hole). The lip then reaches down to leh_open_radius (default 0.6 leh_radius, the floor of the closure) in nr_lip rings (default 6; 1 for a GapClosure).

sombrero (true for the measured Sombrero(), or a Sombrero) adds an annular shield around each end, one-sided and facing away from the waist: opaque to beams (clipping, deposition: power on its waist side is booked as sombrero_top_outside) and lines of sight, hardware for clearance. It lies outside the cavity: its elements see none of the hohlraum's (their view-factor rows and columns are zero), so the enclosure stays closed, closed = true balancing leaves them alone, and the hohlraum's solution does not change. Off by default.

capsule_mesh picks the capsule mesh: :compact (the default), a geodesic icosphere_mesh of nearly equilateral triangles with sides of about capsule_h (icosphere_frequency; without capsule_h, the lat-long meridional size π capsule_radius / capsule_ntheta), or :latlong (sphere_mesh, capsule_ntheta × capsule_nphi). The capsule mesh sets the flux's P4: lat-long cells are slivers near the poles, and the compact mesh reaches the same P4 error with several times fewer elements (research notes, "Compact capsule mesh"). The default was :latlong until 2026-10-22; pass it to reproduce earlier baselines.

Briareus.SurfaceRegion — Type
SurfaceRegion

Patches of a mesh lying on one analytic surface, as found by fit_surfaces:

  • kind: :plane, :cylinder, :cone, :sphere or :torus
  • surface: the fitted CylinderSurface, ConeSurface, SphereSurface or TorusSurface (nothing for a plane)

Briareus.curved_components — Method
curved_components(component; kinds = (:cylinder, :cone, :sphere, :torus), regions = nothing,
                  maxradius = Inf, minfraction = 1e-3, kwargs...) -> Vector{Component}
curved_components(components; kwargs...) -> Vector{Component}
curved_components(target; kwargs...) -> Target

The component split into one component per cylinder, cone, sphere or torus that fit_surfaces finds in its mesh (other keywords are passed on to it; or give its regions), each carrying its fitted CylinderSurface, ConeSurface, SphereSurface or TorusSurface, and the rest: <name>/cyl1, <name>/cyl2, …, <name>/cone1, …, <name>/sph1, …, <name>/tor1, …, by decreasing area, then <name>/rest with the other patches (planes and unrecognized faces). Material, kind, sidedness and part are the component's. A component that one fitted surface covers whole keeps its name (and its mesh) and only gains the surface. For a remeshed CAD part pass the facets it was remeshed from as reference (see fit_surfaces). A component that already has a surface, or in which no curved region is found, is returned unchanged.

maxradius and minfraction leave regions in the rest: those whose radius of curvature (R/cos θ on a cone of half-angle θ, the minor radius on a torus) exceeds maxradius times the part's extent (its bounding-box diagonal), and those of less than minfraction of the part's area. Gently curved CAD faces that are neither planar nor free-form within the fit's tolerance (radii of tens to hundreds of mm on a hohlraum half) otherwise each become a component of their own; their flat elements are close to exact (the sagitta of an element of size h is h²/8R). minfraction defaults to 1e-3: small holes and edge faces of CAD fit as tiny cylinders, cones and spheres (and, before tori, the fillet pieces), and on a NIF CAD cavity (remeshed at 0.6 and 0.3 mm) the default keeps 68 of 158–159 components, with the capsule's incident flux within 3e-4 and its Legendre moments within 0.04 percentage points of fitting everything (research notes, "Fitting CAD by default"). Pass minfraction = 0 to fit every region. maxradius is off by default.

The radiation model then integrates over the true curved surface, as it does for the primitive hohlraum: each element's sub-triangle points are projected onto the surface with exact curved-patch areas, whatever the element's shape (the compact, many-triangle elements of remesh_mesh included), and the facets of a cylinder, cone or sphere no longer occlude pairs of points inside it (a torus is not convex: its facets still do). Ray casting keeps the facets. Planar regions are not given a surface: flat elements are already exact there (DiskSurface describes polar-sector elements of primitive end caps, not general planar CAD faces).

The pieces remember their part (part), so the split is computational only: elements(target, :part), the other name-taking functions and the reports (energy_balance, component_power, write_results, report) see the part, and each piece stays reachable by its own name (see parts).

Briareus.fit_surfaces — Method
fit_surfaces(mesh; reference = nothing, kinds = (:plane, :cylinder, :cone, :sphere, :torus),
             rtol = 1e-3, atol = 1e-4, angle = 30.0, minpatches = 6, minarea = 0.0,
             tol = 1e-9) -> Vector{SurfaceRegion}

Regions of mesh whose patches lie on one plane, cylinder (any axis), cone, sphere or torus, by decreasing area; patches in no region are on none of them (free-form faces, patches straddling two faces). Use it on imported CAD (raw facets or after remesh_mesh), then curved_components to give the curved regions their exact surface for the radiation model.

A patch belongs to a surface when every one of its vertices lies within max(atol, rtol min(R, L)) mm of it (R the radius of curvature, R/cos θ on a cone of half-angle θ; L the region's extent; atol for a plane: CAD planes are flat to round-off) and its normal is within angle degrees of the surface normal at its centroid (either side: windings do not matter). Regions grow from seed patches, by decreasing area, over patches that share a vertex (welded by position to tol times the mesh's extent), nearest first, refitting by least squares as they grow: planes by principal components, spheres by an algebraic fit, cylinders by the axis the patch normals are perpendicular to and a circle fit across it, cones by the axis the normals make a constant angle with (or the cylinder's, for a small patch) and an algebraic fit of the radius linear along it, all refined by Gauss–Newton on the vertex distances; tori by the axis the normals all meet (or, on a small patch, axes through the patch's centres of curvature) and a circle fit in the meridian plane, refined by Levenberg–Marquardt. A cone whose apex lies over 1000 extents away is left to the cylinder, as a surface whose radius of curvature does is left to the plane. From each seed all kinds are grown and the largest region kept, the earlier of plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus when within 1% of the largest: a narrow band with vertices only on its two rims fits a sphere as well, and a cone too when its rims differ in radius (the bore of an entrance hole is a cylinder, a conical shield tessellated as one band a cone: a tessellator puts rings of vertices inside a spherical zone that bulges beyond its chord tolerance). Only regions with at least minpatches patches and minarea mm² whose rms distance is under a quarter of the tolerance count: on a true surface the residual is the faceting, well inside the tolerance, while a fit to a free-form face (a variable-radius fillet; a torus without :torus) grows until the tolerance stops it (rms about 0.4 of it), so such patches stay unfitted. Regions of one kind whose patches all lie on each other's fits (one surface interrupted by another face) are then merged.

Tori (CAD fillets: a quarter of the tube where a wall meets a cap) are held to CAD round-off, since with three more parameters than a sphere a torus also follows free-form faces and spans neighbouring faces within the relative tolerance: their tolerance is atol, their rms must be under atol/16, their vertices must fall on at least four evenly spaced points of the tube circle (bands of strips with vertices only on their rims lie on a circle through any three of them), and patches that a plane, cylinder, cone or sphere fits are left to it. A fillet then becomes one region in place of a patchwork of small cones and spheres. Pass kinds without :torus for the fits before tori (2026-10-24); on the NIF CAD parts that changes only fillet regions (research notes, "Tori").

On a finely faceted face of none of the kinds (a tapered tube without :cone, a fine fillet without :torus) every patch would seed such a growth, at a cost quadratic in the facet count. A rejected growth of at least 512 patches therefore covers the patches near its seed (within half its radius, while all its patches stay available): a growth of the same kind from a covered seed is given up once, at 128, 256, … members, its rms reaches half that of the covering growth at the same size, as it does when it is retracing the same face. A true surface fits its patches to the faceting and keeps growing.

The tolerance must exceed the faceting several times over. Facet vertices of exported CAD lie on the true surface, but points remesh_mesh adds on a facet lie up to w²/8R inside a curved one (w the facet's width: 0.1 µm for 50 µm strips on a 2.9 mm wall, 5 µm for 0.3 mm strips), more than the tolerance allows once w exceeds ~0.08 R. So give the facets the mesh was remeshed from as reference: the regions are then fitted on them, and each patch of mesh joins the region whose surface all its vertices lie within that tolerance plus the largest distance of the region's facets from it (and whose bounding box holds them), with its normal within angle. The regions returned index the patches of mesh, with their rms and largest distances from the (reference) fit.
