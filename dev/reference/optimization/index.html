Optimization

GroupPointing maps parameters to beams, per cone, quad or beam. The parameters are the axial, radial and tangential aim (in the port azimuth of the quad by default), the split of a quad about its aim point, defocus, power, spot scale and Δλ, with top–bottom mirroring. parameters builds its parameter vector from values named by group and field, and Scenario precomputes everything an evaluation needs. The objectives are:

  • symmetry_objective and asymmetry_objective, for capsule drive symmetry (axisymmetric and three-dimensional);
  • face_flux_objective, for the uniformity of the x-ray drive on a flat observed face, such as a halfraum package or an opacity sample;
  • irradiance_objective, for foil uniformity.

optimize_pointing runs bounded L-BFGS-B on forward-mode automatic-differentiation gradients, with a callback for progress and cancellation. influence_matrix gives the mode response of each beam. HeldParameters holds some parameters of a parameterization at given values, and PowerScales adds a power scale per group of beams (for example per cone) beside its parameters; both compose with GroupPointing, FramePointing and each other.

Limits that a design must meet go to optimize_pointing as Constraints, which an augmented Lagrangian around the same L-BFGS-B handles. check_constraints reports them on any evaluation. The PointingResult records the value of each constraint against its limit and what ended the run, and, for each parameter held by the box, the derivative of the optimum with respect to that bound. from = result continues a run with its multipliers and penalty, and time_limit bounds its wall time. On VisRadProfile with its hard cut, a run optimizes the profile tapered below the cut and reports the design under the hard cut (smooth_cut). Phase 1 (phase1, on by default) first searches for a design within the limits and ends with :infeasible when the search finds none; relaxation = true then reports how far each conflicting constraint must be relaxed by itself (Relaxation).

The ready-made constraints are:

  • irradiance_ceiling, a ceiling on the laser irradiance over named parts. By default the exact value, peak_irradiance, is constrained; with exact = false only the smooth stand-in irradiance_norm is.
  • mode_gate, a gate per degree on the mode_departure from a reference drive shape, which goes with drive_objective for the largest drive within the gates. Keywords of mode_gate set the gates at a fraction of the pointing floor, set physical gates with the floor as their lower bound, or shift the target shape from that of the reference by shift_modes.
  • drive_floor, a lower bound on the capsule drive as a fraction of that of a reference, and drive_ceiling, the matching upper bound, for a band.
  • clearance_floor, a lower bound on the worst-case clearance of each beam from a keep-out at its minimum.
  • light_ceiling and light_floor, an upper and a lower bound on the laser light on named parts (light_fraction).
  • picket_ceiling (Laser–plasma models), a dedicated ceiling on the irradiance of the outer cones in a phase such as the picket, trough or foot, the quantity of the published gold-bubble scalings.

mode_sensitivity gives the exact sensitivity of the drive shape to the pointing and power errors of each beam. mode_uncertainty gives the asymmetry that independent errors leave by linear propagation (sample_modes is the Monte Carlo check), with its floor, the spread per degree in the units of mode_departure and the lower bound of a gate. Errors can be shared by a group of beams or be the positioning error of the target. The uncertainty keyword of the objectives optimizes the expected symmetry over them, and infer_errors finds the most probable errors behind a measured asymmetry.

Briareus.ConstraintRow — Type
ConstraintRow

One component of a Constraint at a design, from check_constraints or in PointingResult.constraints. Fields:

  • name — the name of the constraint, with the label of the component for a vector constraint, e.g. "mode_gate[l = 2]".
  • value, limit — in the units of the constraint.
  • upper — true for a ceiling value ≤ limit, false for a floor.
  • violation — the normalized excess ±(value - limit)/scale, positive when violated and negative inside.
  • multiplier — the Lagrange multiplier of the component at the design, in the normalized units. In the result of a constrained run, it is the nonnegative least-squares solution of the stationarity condition on the parameters inside the box (PointingResult). The fit covers the components within feasibility of their limits or beyond them, and the other components get zero. It is 0 when not computed, as in check_constraints.
  • status — :violated (violation > feasibility), :active (within feasibility of the limit) or :satisfied.

Briareus.GroupPointing — Type
GroupPointing(beams; by = :group, fields = (:z, :r, :defocus), mirror = true,
              relative = false, unit = :quad, radius = nothing, center = (0, 0, 0))

Maps a parameter vector to beams. Beams are grouped by by: :group (e.g. a cone), :quad, :beam, or a function beam -> key. Each group gets one value per field:

  • :z — axial aim coordinate (mm). With mirror, beams from the lower hemisphere (port z < 0) aim at -z, so one value describes a top/bottom-symmetric cone.
  • :r — radial aim offset (mm) toward the quad port azimuth. With unit = :beam (below), the offset is toward the beam port azimuth.
  • :phi — tangential aim offset (mm), counterclockwise about z.
  • :split — tangential split of the beams of a quad about its aim point (mm), along the quad φ̂. Each beam aim moves by ±split, with the sign of the beam port azimuth measured from the mean port of the quad; beams counterclockwise of it take +split. A positive split therefore opens the aims of a quad in the sense of its ports, and a negative one crosses them. The s of hybride_mock_pointing is split = -s. The field sets the tangential offset of each beam from the mean template aim of its quad, or shifts that offset with relative. A quad held as one beam is not split. The field needs unit = :quad and quad names.
  • :defocus — focal-plane offset along the beam (mm, see Beam).
  • :power — power per beam (TW).
  • :spot — multiplier on the best-focus spot semi-axes.
  • :dlambda — wavelength detuning (Å), used by laser–plasma models.

Fields not listed keep the template value of each beam.

Pointing on a sphere (polar direct drive). With radius = R (mm), the aim can instead be a point on the sphere of radius R about center. Direct-drive pointings are published on this target surface (Barlow et al., PRL 133, 175101, 2024, Table I; Yeamans et al., Nucl. Fusion 61, 046031, 2021, Table 1; Hohenberger et al., PoP 22, 056308, 2015, Table I). The spherical fields are:

  • :theta_s — polar angle of the aim point (degrees from +z). With mirror, beams from the lower hemisphere aim at 180° - theta_s, so one value describes a top/bottom-symmetric cone pair, and a larger value moves both toward the equator.
  • :dphi_s — azimuth of the aim point relative to the beam port azimuth, in degrees counterclockwise about z and the same for both hemispheres.
  • :split_theta, :split_phi — split of the beams of a quad (degrees). Each beam aim moves by ±split_theta in polar angle and ±split_phi in azimuth, with the sign of the beam port offset from the mean port of the quad. Beams at a larger polar angle than the quad port take +split_theta, with the angle counted from the pole of their hemisphere when mirrored. Beams counterclockwise of the quad port take +split_phi. Positive values therefore open the aims of a quad in the sense of its ports. This is the split-quad pointing of Cok et al. (PoP 15, 082705, 2008) and Yeamans et al. 2021 (left/right Δφ) as angles on the sphere. A quad given as one beam, or a beam level with the mean port of its quad, is not split. The fields need quad names; other per-beam aims come from by, e.g. one group per sub-ring.

The aim is the surface point itself, aim = center + R * port_vector(θ, φ), which the axis meets first when it faces the port (Beam). Best focus is then at aim + defocus * k, so defocus is measured from the surface aim point, as VisRad measures the focus offset from its reference point (research notes, "Pointing and focus conventions"). The angles of each template beam are those of the first point where its axis meets the sphere; an ArgumentError is thrown if the axis misses. Listed spherical values are shared by the members of a group. For an angle not set by a listed field, the value of each beam is kept. The aim moves to the surface along the template axis, so the focus moves with it unless the template aim was already on the sphere. The spherical fields and the hohlraum fields :z, :r, :phi exclude each other.

By default (unit = :quad), the unit of pointing for the hohlraum fields is a quad. :r, :phi and :split are then measured in the azimuth of the quad port, which is the mean port of the beams that share its quad name, instead of the port of each beam. The beams of a quad that share their values then share one aim point, whether the table contains the quad as one beam or as four. A NIF pointing given per cone or per quad thus stays one aim per quad while the deposition is computed per beam. The beams of a quad are pointed apart about its aim point as follows:

p = GroupPointing(nif_beams(); by = :quad, fields = (:z, :r, :phi, :split))
beams = apply(p, parameters(p, "Q31T" => (z = 5.8, split = 0.5)))

by = :beam gives each beam a separate aim, with its offsets still in the quad azimuth. With relative = true (below), the differences between the beams of a per-beam table are kept under one shift per quad. An as-shot SST export, read_visrad_sst(path; template = nif_beams(quads)), is such a table. unit = :beam measures :r and :phi of each beam in the beam port azimuth instead. The four beams of a quad with the same r then aim r sin Δφ apart, where Δφ is the azimuth of a beam from that of its quad, up to 5.7° on the NIF 23.5° cone. A quad held as one beam, and a beam without a quad name, give identical results either way.

relative = true makes the aim fields (:z, :r, :phi, :split, or the spherical ones) shifts added to the template aim of each beam, instead of values shared by the beams of a group. A group then moves rigidly, and the differences between its members are kept. This re-points an as-shot table, whose beams within a quad aim at different points (the per-beam offsets of a facility), by one shift per quad. The initial parameters are then zero; the other fields stay absolute.

Briareus.PointingResult — Type
PointingResult

Result of optimize_pointing. Fields:

  • x — the best parameters.
  • beams — apply(scenario.param, x), a PerPhase of beam vectors for a phased scenario.
  • value, initial_value — the objective at x and at the clamped x0.
  • iterations — the L-BFGS-B iterations completed.
  • evaluations — the objective evaluations, each gradient counting as one.
  • reason — :converged, :iterations, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback.
  • stopped — true for the last three reasons, which return the best design evaluated before the stop.
  • result — the result of Optim, nothing when stopped.
  • names — the parameter_names of the parameterization, or x[i] for one without a method.
  • lower, upper — the box.
  • at_bounds — the names of the parameters at a bound, within 1e-8 of the box width. Parameters fixed by lower == upper are excluded.

The display lists the parameters at a bound with their values and bounds.

What ended the run is recorded in two more fields. constraints holds one ConstraintRow per constraint component at x, and it is empty without constraints. The display shows a constraint of more than eight components, such as a keep-out per beam, as its worst row and a count by status. outcome is one of:

  • :interior: the run converged with every constraint met and no parameter at a bound;
  • :box: the run converged with every constraint met and parameters at a bound. The box is active, and a wider box may lower the objective;
  • :infeasible_box: a constraint is still violated and parameters are at a bound, so the constraint cannot be met within the box;
  • :infeasible: a constraint is still violated and no parameter is at a bound. The design is a local point where the constraints cannot be met, and another start may help;
  • :iterations, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback: the budget, the time limit or the callback ended the run first. Its constraints, if any, are in constraints.

reason is :converged for the first four. outer is the number of outer (multiplier) iterations of a constrained run, and 0 without constraints. iterations and evaluations are then totals over them, and value and initial_value are the values of the objective without the constraint terms.

state is what optimize_pointing(...; from = result) continues from. It is nothing without constraints. For a constrained run, it is a named tuple of:

  • the multipliers λ, in normalized units, one per constraint component in the order of constraints;
  • the penalty parameter μ and its ceiling μmax;
  • the tolerance tol of the inner runs;
  • the last infeasibility–complementarity measure Vold;
  • the working limits limits, which contain the lowered stand-in limit of an exact ceiling (see irradiance_ceiling);
  • the iterate x they belong to. It is the x of the result unless the run returned its iterate of least violation (below).

For a run ended by its budget, time limit or callback, the state of the interrupted inner problem is kept, so that the restart resumes it.

Multipliers. The multipliers in constraints are those that best satisfy stationarity at x, which differ from the iterated state.λ. They are the nonnegative least-squares solution of ∂f/∂xᵢ + Σⱼ λⱼ ∂gⱼ/∂xᵢ = 0 over the parameters inside the box. Here gⱼ are the normalized violations, with the stand-in of an exact ceiling at its working limit. The fit covers the components within feasibility of their limits or beyond them, and the other components get zero (complementarity). The iterated estimate λ ← max(0, λ + μ g) after each inner run is exact only when the inner run ended at a stationary point. The inner runs of a drive objective end on their value tolerance instead. At a large penalty μ, the update then zeroes the multipliers of active limits at which the design ends a few 1e-5 inside (research notes, "Multipliers from the converged active set").

The fit costs one Jacobian of the objective and the constraints after the run, the cost of a gradient, which is not counted in evaluations. Several sets of multipliers can fit equally, as for components with the same gradient, such as the clearances of beams related by the port symmetry. The fit then returns the one of least norm. stationarity is the residual of the fit: the largest |∂L/∂xᵢ| over the parameters inside the box, with L = f + Σⱼ λⱼ gⱼ. It is in objective units per parameter unit, the measure that the inner runs compare with g_tol, and it is NaN without constraints or when phase 1 ended the run. Near zero, the design is a Karush–Kuhn–Tucker point with these multipliers. A drive run whose inner runs ended on their value tolerance can end with a stationarity that is a sizable fraction of the objective gradient. The multipliers and bound sensitivities are then estimates at a design short of stationarity. On the case of the dropped-bundle tutorial, the stationarity is 1.2e-3, against 3.0e-3 per mm for the largest derivative of the drive objective.

An exact ceiling, such as irradiance_ceiling or picket_ceiling with exact = true, reports the exact value in its row. It is included in the fit through its smooth stand-in at the working limit. Its row can therefore show :satisfied, with the exact value inside the limit by more than feasibility, together with a positive multiplier from the active stand-in. The display notes such rows; the multiplier is that of the stand-in.

Bound sensitivities. For each parameter in at_bounds, in the same order, bound_gradient contains the derivative ∂f/∂xᵢ of the objective at x. bound_sensitivity contains that of the Lagrangian, ∂L/∂xᵢ with L = f + Σⱼ λⱼ gⱼ, using the multipliers of constraints above. Without constraints the two are equal. By the envelope theorem, bound_sensitivity is the derivative of the optimal objective with respect to the bound value, with the active set held. When the run has converged, it is positive at a lower bound and negative at an upper bound. Widening the box by δ in that parameter, by lowering a lower bound or raising an upper one, then lowers the optimum by about |bound_sensitivity| δ, to first order. The unit is objective units per parameter unit. bound_gradient is the gain of the objective without the constraint terms, before the constraints take their share.

Without constraints, the sensitivities cost one gradient after the run. With constraints, they come from the Jacobian of the multiplier fit. Neither cost is counted in evaluations. The sensitivities are meaningful at convergence. The active constraints and bounds can leave a flat family of designs, as for a drive objective under several active gates, and the run can end short of stationarity when its inner runs end on their value tolerance. In both cases the derivative can differ from the gain of a wider box by its whole size. A sign that would move the parameter inward marks a bound that does not hold it (research notes, "Multipliers from the converged active set"). A restart with from in the wider box measures the gain.

VisRad's hard cut. When optimize_pointing optimized a scenario with the hard cut on a profile tapered below its cut (smooth_cut, its default), tapered_value is the objective that the tapered run reached. value, initial_value, constraints and outcome are then those of the scenario under its hard cut. Otherwise tapered_value is NaN.

Phase 1 and relaxations (phase1 = true, relaxation = true). phase1 is nothing when phase 1 did not run. Otherwise it is a named tuple of:

  • evaluations and iterations, which are included in the totals;
  • feasible, true when phase 1 reached a design within the limits, where the main run started;
  • verdict, true when phase 1 converged or stalled above feasibility. The run then ended at that design, with outer 0;
  • its design x;
  • reason, the cause of the end of phase 1. It is :feasible, :converged or :stalled for a verdict or a design within the limits. Otherwise it is the stop that came first, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback. No verdict is reached on such a stop, and the run ends with its design of least violation.

With relaxation = true, relaxations contains one Relaxation per constraint violated or active at the design of a verdict; otherwise it is empty. Their evaluations count in evaluations. The display lists them under the constraints.

Least violation and the last design within the limits. These fields apply to constrained runs and are nothing without constraints. The run records the largest normalized violation of each iterate over the reported rows, max(0, maxⱼ violation) of ConstraintRow. For an exact ceiling, the row contains the exact value against its limit, and the violation is zero when the constraint is met for every component. The record is taken at the start, at the design of phase 1 and after every inner run. least_violation is the iterate of least violation, with violations within feasibility counted as equal and the lower objective then preferred. last_feasible is the latest iterate with every violation within feasibility, or nothing when none had. Each is a named tuple (; x, violation, value, outer) that contains the parameters, the largest violation, the objective and the outer iteration of that iterate. The outer iteration is 0 for the start and for the design of phase 1.

A run can end with a violation above feasibility: with outcome :infeasible or :infeasible_box, or after a budget, time-limit or callback stop above the limits. It then returns least_violation as x, with its beams, value and rows. The last iterate is not returned, since it can be worse than the start on the limits that the start met. state.x is then the last iterate, to which the multipliers of state belong and from which a restart with from continues. When an earlier iterate met the limits, the limits are met at the returned design, and a run that ended at the penalty ceiling has reason and outcome :iterations. Under VisRad's hard cut (tapered_value finite), the records are those of the tapered run.

capped counts the outer iterations whose inner run ended on its cap (iterations or inner_evaluations) instead of converging. After each such run that ended above the limits, the multipliers were updated, and the penalty, the inner tolerance and the reference of the penalty test were held (optimize_pointing). The count is shown in the display.

Briareus.Relaxation — Type
Relaxation

How far a single constraint must be relaxed for the others to be met. A relaxation is an entry of PointingResult.relaxations after a run that optimize_pointing found infeasible with relaxation = true. Fields:

  • name — the name of the constraint.
  • violation — the largest normalized violation of its components at a design where every other constraint is met. This is the relaxation in units of its scale; where the scale is the limit (the default), the relaxed limit is 1 + violation times the limit.
  • limit, relaxed_limit — one per component, in the units of the constraint. The relaxed limit is a ceiling raised, or a floor lowered, by violation × scale.
  • upper — true for a ceiling.
  • status — :relaxed, :others_conflict or :stopped (below).
  • x — that design.
  • constraints — the ConstraintRow of every constraint at x.
  • evaluations — the objective evaluations of the search.

status is :relaxed when the others are met at x. It is :others_conflict when the other constraints cannot all be met either, so relaxing only this one does not suffice; violation is then NaN. It is :stopped when the budget, the time limit or the callback ended the search first; violation is then that of the best design, and the others are possibly unmet.

The relaxation is achievable, since the others are met at the design x with this one at relaxed_limit. It is the least one only locally. The run that finds it minimizes a smooth stand-in for the largest violation, a 64-norm of the positive violations, from the infeasible design, which is a local search.

Scenario(target, beams; kwargs...)
Scenario(target, parameterization; kwargs...)

A scenario contains everything precomputed for fast repeated evaluation of a pointing design. Its parameterization (scenario.param) maps the parameter vectors of evaluate and optimize_pointing to beams. The three forms differ in the parameterization:

  • The first form builds the deposition operator for the template beams of pointing, which may also be a FramePointing.
  • The second form is Scenario(target, GroupPointing(beams)), with fields :z, :r and :defocus per group.
  • The third form takes any other parameterization with methods of apply(parameterization, x) and initial_parameters(parameterization), and builds the operator for the beams at its initial parameters.

The operator resolves the beam footprints where those beams aim (reach, DepositionOperator). Besides the DepositionOperator, the scenario contains the view factors and the linear flux response of the observed elements when radiation and observe are given. observe names the components whose incident x-ray flux is the figure of merit, e.g. :capsule. lpi is a laser–plasma model (Backscatter, CalibratedTransfer, CBET, or a tuple of them), applied at time between the setup beams and deposition.

The flux response is linear in the laser source at fixed albedos. By default these are the albedos of the materials at time, with temperature-dependent ones at their reference temperatures and thin foils intact. An explicit per-element albedo replaces them, such as the converged sol.albedo of a solution at the design point (e.g. the peak of a drive history, with windows burned through). For a time partway through a shaped pulse (a phase), the material albedos at time differ from those of the wall. The albedo of a WallLossAlbedo depends on the temperature history of each element, and neither the reference temperature nor a standalone solve at time includes that history. Pass the history albedos as st.time, st.albedo, with st = history_state(target, history, τ) (history_state).

A scenario with a response (observe and a model) warns at construction when it is built without albedo on a target whose materials include a WallLossAlbedo, or a BurnthroughAlbedo whose before is one. The loss is then taken at the reference temperature (250 eV by default). Mid-pulse, this overstated the drive against a drive history by up to 1.64 times on a public mock-up (research notes, "Drive histories: sliver steps and the albedo of a phase"). albedo = :reference marks the reference temperature as intended and builds the same scenario without the warning.

Objectives need only the moments of the observed flux, so the response is stored contracted with the Legendre (lmax = 12) and real-harmonic (lmax = 6) weights. Each evaluation then costs a 13 × n and a 49 × n product, and the setup costs a 62-column solve. The full observed × n response scenario.response, and with it the flux of an evaluation, is computed when first read. On a large (iterative) model, the flux of an evaluation is one solve instead, until the response has been read.

Options of the operators pass through two named tuples:

  • deposition_kw is passed to DepositionOperator. Its options are:
    • profile, a VisRadProfile that selects the VisRad beam model instead of the f-cone superposition;
    • ncone, the number of f-cone directions (13);
    • reach, the aim motion in mm over which the operator stays accurate (2.0);
    • resolution, level, maxlevel and shadow, the quadrature refinement.
  • viewfactor_kw is passed to RadiationModel and through it to view_factors. Its options are:
    • closed, the closure correction, by default the declaration of the target that hohlraum makes;
    • F, given view factors, e.g. those of a target that differs only in its materials;
    • storage, one of Float64, Float32 or :hmatrix;
    • solver, visibility (:centroid or :sampled), visibility_grid, level and near.

model, a RadiationModel of the target, is used instead of building one. It excludes viewfactor_kw, and giving both throws an ArgumentError.

remake(scenario; ...) replaces the laser–plasma model, the parameterization, the deposition options or the observed elements, time and albedos of the response, and rebuilds only what depends on them.

Briareus.asymmetry_objective — Method
asymmetry_objective(; modes = (1 => 1.0, 2 => 1.0, 3 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0), components = (),
                    clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0, direct_names = nothing,
                    drive = nothing, drive_weight = 1.0, keepout = nothing,
                    reference = nothing, uncertainty = nothing)

Objective for three-dimensional drive symmetry. For each l => w in modes it adds w × relative_mode_power(c, l) — the rotation-invariant power of all 2l+1 real harmonics of degree l over the mean ((aₗ/a₀)²/(2l+1) for an axisymmetric field) — and for each (l, m) => w in components adds w × (c_lm/c_00)², so a particular azimuthal mode (e.g. the l = 1, m = ±1 drift of a dropped bundle) can be weighted separately. Penalties for clipping, missing and direct capsule illumination and the drive target and keepout are as in symmetry_objective, with drive compared to the mean flux c_00/√(4π).

With reference (a coefficient vector from flux_ylm, e.g. of a tuned design before a change to the target or the beams), the modes and components measure the departure of c_lm/c_00 from c_lm/c_00 of the reference instead of from zero, so the objective restores a known-good drive shape.

With uncertainty = (; pointing, power) the mode and component terms are their expected values over independent beam errors of those rms sizes (linearized; see mode_uncertainty and symmetry_objective): a robust design. Shared errors (group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning) are accepted as in mode_uncertainty.

Briareus.bounds — Function
bounds(p::GroupPointing, x0; z = 1.0, r = 1.0, phi = 1.0, split = 1.0, defocus = 2.0,
       power = 0.5, spot = 0.5, dlambda = 2.0, theta_s = 5.0, dphi_s = 10.0,
       split_theta = 5.0, split_phi = 5.0, power_min = 0.0, power_max = Inf)
       -> (lower, upper)

Box bounds around x0: ±z, ±r, ±phi, ±split, ±defocus mm, relative ±power, ±spot fractions, ±dlambda Å, and ±theta_s, ±dphi_s, ±split_theta, ±split_phi degrees (engineering defaults that are not facility limits). Power bounds are further clipped to the absolute limits power_min ≤ P ≤ power_max (TW per beam, e.g. the per-quad capability of a facility). A group at zero power in x0 (a dropped quad) gets ±power of the mean of the groups' nonzero powers, [0, power · P̄] before the clipping, so that it can come back; with every group at zero the upper bound is power_max, which must then be finite.

Briareus.face_flux_objective — Method
face_flux_objective(; uniformity = 1.0, rms_scale = nothing, flux = nothing,
                    flux_weight = 1.0, clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0,
                    direct_names = nothing, keepout = nothing)

Objective for the x-ray drive of a flat observed face, such as a package face in a halfraum or an opacity sample, which has no capsule modes. The shape term is uniformity × (σ/σ₀)². Here σ = rms_nonuniformity(H, A) is the area-weighted RMS nonuniformity of the incident flux on the observed elements of the scenario (face_nonuniformity). It is computed through the linear flux response of the scenario, so it is differentiable, and it is the shape term of FaceFlux for a uniform target. σ₀ = rms_scale, and nothing sets it to 1, which gives the absolute σ². With flux (TW/mm²) given, the objective adds flux_weight × (H̄/flux - 1)², with H̄ the face_mean_flux. The clip, missed, direct and keepout penalties are as in symmetry_objective. Here direct counts laser light on the face itself, or with direct_names on the named parts or components.

rms_scale = face_nonuniformity(evaluate(scenario, x0)) makes the term equal to uniformity at the design x0, so uniformity = 1 balances the shape against the penalties on any face. Without it, the term is the absolute σ², which is as small as the face is uniform. On a uniform 1 mm sample, σ ~ 1e-4 and σ² ~ 1e-8, so the penalties (10 × a squared light fraction, ~1e-2) set the optimum. The modes of a capsule, by contrast, have (Pₗ/P₀)² ~ 1e-2 before tuning, the same size as the penalties. The scale is a number fixed at construction, so the objective remains a function of only the beams, thread-safe and differentiable.

Briareus.optimize_pointing — Method
optimize_pointing(scenario, objective; from = nothing,
                  x0 = initial_parameters(scenario.param),
                  lower = bounds(scenario.param, x0)[1], upper = bounds(scenario.param, x0)[2],
                  iterations = 200, evaluations = 20 * iterations, show_trace = false,
                  g_tol = 1e-8, callback = nothing, constraints = (),
                  feasibility = 1e-3, outer = 30, time_limit = Inf, phase1 = true,
                  relaxation = false, inner_reltol = 1e-10,
                  inner_evaluations = nothing, smooth_cut = true) -> PointingResult

Minimize objective(evaluate(scenario, x)) within the box lower ≤ x ≤ upper by L-BFGS-B with forward-mode automatic-differentiation gradients, from x0 (clamped to the box). Returns a PointingResult, whose display lists the parameters that end at a bound.

The default box is bounds(scenario.param, x0) with its default widths. These are aims within ±1 mm of x0 (z, r, phi), defocus ±2 mm, power and spot scale ±50%, Δλ ±2 Å and the spherical angles ±5–10°. They are engineering defaults and are not facility limits. A parameter at its bound usually means that the box set its value and the objective without the box would have moved it further. To widen the box, pass keywords of bounds, e.g. lower, upper = bounds(pointing, x0; z = 1.5, power = 0.4, power_max = 12.0), or pass any lower and upper vectors. A parameterization other than GroupPointing and FramePointing has no bounds method and needs lower and upper.

scenario may be a PhasedScenario, which contains one pointing over several phases of a pulse with power scales per (group, phase). The objective and every constraint then receive the PerPhase of the evaluations of the phases at each parameter vector, evaluated once. The beams of the result are a PerPhase of beam vectors. Everything else is the same, including constraints, from, phase1, relaxation and the time limit.

The run ends after iterations L-BFGS-B iterations or evaluations objective evaluations, whichever comes first; each gradient counts as one evaluation. A line search may take many evaluations in one iteration, and dozens on a kinked objective such as keep-out hinges or a coarse mesh, so the iteration limit alone does not bound the run time. time_limit, in seconds of wall time (default none), ends the run at the first evaluation after it has passed. reason is one of:

  • :converged: the projected gradient fell below g_tol, or the run made no further progress;
  • :iterations, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback: that limit or the callback ended the run.

On :evaluations, :time_limit and :callback, the result is the best design evaluated before the stop, with stopped = true and result = nothing. iterations is the number of iterations completed in every case.

callback, if given, is called after every (non-derivative) objective evaluation with (; evaluations, iteration, value, best_value, best_x); returning true stops the optimization. Use it for progress reports and cancellation.

Constraints. constraints is a tuple or vector of Constraints: ceilings f(ev) ≤ limit and floors f(ev) ≥ limit, such as irradiance_ceiling and mode_gate. They are handled as constraints by an augmented Lagrangian; they are not penalty terms of the objective. Its inner problems are box-constrained L-BFGS-B runs on objective + Σ μ/2 [max(0, gᵢ + λᵢ/μ)² - (λᵢ/μ)²], where gᵢ are the normalized violations ±(value - limit)/scale. Each inner run starts from the last, and the multipliers λ and the penalty μ are updated between them by the rules of ALGENCAN (Andreani et al., SIAM J. Optim. 18, 1286, 2008).

The run converges when three conditions hold:

  • every violation is at most feasibility, in the scaled units of the constraints (1e-3, or 0.1% of a limit, by default);
  • the multipliers are complementary to the same tolerance;
  • the last inner run has converged at the final tolerance.

The tolerance of the inner runs on the projected gradient tightens tenfold per outer iteration, from 1e-3 to g_tol. The run ends only once an inner run at g_tol has converged. An inner run converges when its projected gradient falls below the tolerance, or when its value changes by less than inner_reltol relative per iteration, since a drive objective is flat along the limits. A start already within the limits, as on a restart or a hand-off to a finer parameterization, is therefore still optimized. A constraint that cannot be met ends the run at the verdict of phase 1 (below). With phase1 = false, it ends the run once the penalty has grown by 10⁸ without progress.

outer caps the outer iterations, iterations caps each inner run, and evaluations is the budget of the whole run. The iterations and evaluations of the result count over all inner runs. The multipliers change only between inner runs, so the cap sets the number of iterations spent on each set of multipliers. An inner run ended by its cap has not solved its subproblem, and the penalty test (has the infeasibility–complementarity measure halved?) requires a solved one. After a capped run that ends above the limits, the multipliers are updated, while the penalty, the inner tolerance and the reference of the test are held. A cap therefore never grows the penalty toward the infeasible verdict. The usual test is applied to a capped run that ends within the limits. capped in the result counts both kinds (research notes, "Least violation, capped inner runs and phase 1's budget"). A run that ends above its limits, infeasible or stopped by a budget, the time limit or the callback, returns its iterate of least violation instead of its last (the least_violation and last_feasible fields of PointingResult).

The public cases of up to about a hundred parameters converge with a large cap in about a thousand evaluations; the dropped-bundle tutorial passes 1,000. Small early caps were measured as an alternative: they saved evaluations on some of these cases and cost drive on others. A problem with hundreds of parameters, whose evaluations take seconds and whose start is far from the limits, gains from a cap of about 100, which updates the multipliers sooner. A 276-parameter study completed one outer iteration in an hour with iterations = 1000, and six in 40 minutes with 100 (research notes, "The inner budget").

The cap on iterations does not bound the evaluations of an inner run, which line searches consume. An inner run can spend hundreds of them on 1e-4 of the objective while limits stay violated. inner_evaluations (default none) caps the evaluations of each inner run. An inner run that reaches it ends at its best design, as one ended by iterations does, and the outer loop updates the multipliers and the penalty from there. Phase 1 is not capped; the runs of the relaxations are. A capped inner run never counts as converged, so the run still ends only after an inner run converges within the cap at the final tolerance, and a small cap needs a larger outer. A coarse version of the run within limits in the dropped-bundle tutorial has 92 parameters, and its default run spent 795 of its 1,044 evaluations in one inner run. With a cap of 100, it converged in 10 outer iterations and 766 evaluations at 0.06% less drive. With a cap of 30, it ended at outer = 30 unconverged (research notes, "Inner-run options, a wall-loss scenario warning and laser delivery figures").

inner_reltol (default 1e-10; 0 turns it off) is the relative value tolerance of the inner runs above, also used by phase 1. The default ends the inner runs of a drive objective where the objective is flat along the limits, short of stationarity; the distance is recorded in the stationarity of the result. A tighter tolerance costs evaluations. On the run within limits in the dropped-bundle tutorial, a tolerance of 1e-12 gives a stationarity of 1.9e-5 against 1.2e-3 for 2.1 times the evaluations, with the drive the same within the 1e-4 spread of the flat family (research notes, "Multipliers from the converged active set"). When the multipliers or bound sensitivities of a result matter, continue it tighter through from with a smaller inner_reltol.

The constraints and outcome of the result report each constraint against its limit, with the multipliers that best satisfy stationarity at the final design. They also report whether the box, the constraints or the budget ended the run (PointingResult). The named tuple of the callback then also contains outer, and its value is that of the inner problem, the objective with the constraint terms. Without constraints, the run is the plain L-BFGS-B above.

Infeasible constraints. With phase1 = true (the default), a start that violates a constraint first goes through phase 1. Phase 1 minimizes the infeasibility ½ Σ max(0, gᵢ)² over the normalized violation of every component, without the objective, by the same L-BFGS-B. This infeasibility is the limit of the augmented Lagrangian as its penalty grows. Phase 1 stops at the first design where every violation is within feasibility, and the main run starts at that design. Phase 1 can instead converge or stall with a violation above feasibility; it stalls when its least infeasibility falls by less than 1% over 100 evaluations, an engineering default. The run then ends there with outcome :infeasible, or :infeasible_box when parameters are at a bound, and no outer iteration. That design is the least infeasible one the search reached from this start, a local verdict, and its constraints rows show the violation of each constraint at that design.

relaxation = true implies phase1. After such a verdict, it adds one constrained run per constraint violated or active at that design. Each run computes the smallest largest violation of the components of its constraint with every other constraint met, which is how far that one constraint must be relaxed (Relaxation, in PointingResult.relaxations). These runs share the budget and the time limit.

phase1 is on by default and relaxation off. A run whose start violates a constraint starts its main run from the design of phase 1, and it can end at another design of a flat family than a run started at x0. phase1 = false starts the main run at x0. A start at which every constraint is met, and a restart with from, skip phase 1. The outer of the callback is 0 during phase 1. Phase 1's L-BFGS-B run is not capped by iterations, which caps the subproblems of the augmented Lagrangian. It ends on its convergence, its stall rule, the evaluations of the run, the time limit or the callback. A stop before a verdict is named in the result (phase1.reason) and its display.

On the dropped-bundle test case made infeasible, phase 1 reached the verdict in 577 evaluations, where the augmented Lagrangian had none after 2,195. On feasible starts, phase 1 cost 8% fewer to 29% more evaluations (research notes, "A verdict on conflicting constraints"). On the constrained cases of the test suite that start outside their limits, it cost −23% to +54%, with drive designs 3e-4 apart on a flat family (research notes, "Defaults changed (2026-11-19)").

VisRad's hard cut. A scenario can deposit with a VisRadProfile at its hard cut (taper = 0, its default). Every deposition-derived quantity then jumps where an operator point crosses the cut, and line searches stall on the jumps. With smooth_cut = true (the default), the run optimizes the same scenario with the profile tapered over one e-fold below the cut (taper = 1). The points and screens of the operator and the responses of the scenario are shared, and nothing is rebuilt. The run then reports the design under the hard cut of the scenario. The value, initial_value, constraints and outcome of the result are those of the hard cut at the design and start of the run, and tapered_value contains the objective the tapered run reached.

The taper moves wall elements by at most 7e-5 of the wall peak on NIF hohlraums, well inside the default feasibility of 1e-3. A design within its limits under the taper is therefore normally within them under the cut. Where it is not, its row shows :violated and outcome is :infeasible. The multipliers (fitted under the taper), stationarity, bound sensitivities, phase 1, relaxations and state are those of the tapered run, and a restart with from continues it. smooth_cut = false optimizes the scenario profile as it is. Other scenarios, such as the f-cone model or a profile already tapered, are optimized as they are. Each phase of a PhasedScenario is tapered separately.

Restarts. from, the PointingResult of a previous run, continues that run. x0, lower and upper default to its last iterate and box. The last iterate is state.x of a constrained run, and x otherwise or whenever the run returned its last iterate. A constrained run starts from the multipliers, penalty parameter, inner tolerance and working limits of the previous run (result.state) instead of starting them again from zero. A run cut short by evaluations or by the callback and continued with from resumes the interrupted inner problem. The counts of the new result (iterations, evaluations, outer) cover only that run, and its initial_value is the objective at its start. The constraints must have the same components as those of the previous run, or a DimensionMismatch is thrown. An unconstrained from result only sets the start and the box.

Compared with penalty terms at fixed weights, the weights of the constraints are carried by the multipliers from one inner run to the next. The limits are met without stiff weights, which stall the line search, and without stages of loosened limits set by hand. The design also ends at its limits. With penalty terms, it ends where the terms balance the objective. On the dropped-bundle tutorial, this gave 0.35% more drive in a comparable number of evaluations, 997 against 1,072 for the penalty form (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing").

Briareus.parameters — Method
parameters(p::GroupPointing, beams::AbstractVector{<:Beam}) -> Vector{Float64}

The parameter vector of p that reproduces beams, such as the result of an optimization in another parameterization of the same beams. Each template beam of p is matched to the beam of its name in beams. An ArgumentError is thrown for a missing name, and extra beams are ignored. The value of a field for each group is the mean over its members of what apply would need for that beam:

  • For the aim fields, it is the axial coordinate of the aim (:z, mirrored as in apply) and the components of the aim along the radial and tangential directions of the beam (:r, :phi, in the frame of unit). For a relative parameterization, it is the same components of the shift from the template aim.
  • For :split, it is the group mean of the signed tangential offset of each beam from the mean of its quad, as in initial_parameters.
  • For :defocus, :power and :dlambda, it is the value of the beam. For :spot, it is the ratio of the spot semi-axis a of the beam to that of the template.

Spherical fields are not supported and throw an ArgumentError.

apply(p, parameters(p, beams)) reproduces the beams exactly when p can express them, as when the groups of p split those of the parameterization that produced beams and share its aim frame: a re-pointing by quad handed to a per-beam polish on the same template,

quad = GroupPointing(beams; by = :quad, fields = (:z, :phi), relative = true)
s = remake(scenario; parameterization = quad)
r = optimize_pointing(s, objective; ...)
per_beam = GroupPointing(beams; by = :beam, fields = (:z, :phi), relative = true)
x0 = parameters(per_beam, r.beams)       # the box `bounds(per_beam)` stays about `beams`
polish = optimize_pointing(remake(s; parameterization = per_beam), objective; x0, ...)

Otherwise the result is the group means of those values: per-beam aims read by quad give each quad its mean shift.

Briareus.remake — Method
remake(scenario; parameterization, lpi, deposition_kw, observe, time, albedo, model,
       history) -> Scenario

Copy of scenario with the given parts replaced. What depends on a replaced part is rebuilt, and everything else is shared:

  • lpi (a laser–plasma model or nothing): nothing is rebuilt.
  • parameterization: the deposition operator is kept when the beams of the new parameterization enter through the ports of the operator in the same order with the same f-cone directions, and, under a VisRadProfile, with the same envelope ratios. These beams are the template of a GroupPointing or FramePointing, or the beams of any other parameterization at its initial parameters. Otherwise the change is refused with an ArgumentError, unless deposition_kw is given too. A kept operator resolves the footprints where the original beams aimed (its reach), as for subset.
  • deposition_kw: the operator is rebuilt for the beams of the parameterization with these options. Options not given take their defaults, because a scenario does not record the options its operator was built with. Pass all options that differ from the defaults, such as profile, reach and resolution.
  • observe, time, albedo and model (a RadiationModel of the same target, or nothing for none): the mode weights and moment responses are recomputed with one flux-response solve, and the full response is computed again when read. With albedo = nothing or :reference, the material albedos at time are used without a warning; any warning was issued when the scenario was constructed.

The target, radiation and viewfactor_kw do not change through remake, and giving them throws an ArgumentError. They need a new Scenario, or a model built for the purpose.

history, a drive_history of the scenario target run with keep_albedo = true, sets with time the albedos and time of the history state at time. remake(scenario; history = h, time = τ) is exactly st =history_state(scenario.target, h, τ) followed by remake(scenario; st.time, st.albedo). Remade so, a phase of a design and its reference at the same time have the same time, which the reference checks of mode_gate and drive_floor compare.

Print a power accounting and flux-moment summary of an evaluation. The summary lists:

  • the setup and effective power per group, when the scenario has a laser–plasma model;

Briareus.symmetry_objective — Method
symmetry_objective(; modes = (2 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0), odd = 1.0, lmax = 6,
                   clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0, direct_names = nothing,
                   drive = nothing, drive_weight = 1.0, center = nothing, keepout = nothing,
                   uncertainty = nothing)

Objective for indirect-drive symmetry. The objective is the sum of these terms:

  • Σ wₗ (aₗ/a₀)² over modes;
  • odd × the sum of squared odd moments, which measures the top/bottom imbalance;
  • clip, missed and direct times the squared fractions of laser power clipped on the outside of the target, missing it, or hitting the observed elements directly. The observed elements are those named by the scenario observe, the capsule whose drive is measured. With direct_names, the direct term counts the named parts or components instead, and a name that is not in the target is an error;
  • with drive (TW/mm²) given, drive_weight × (a₀/drive - 1)², with a₀ the mean_flux. With drive = mean_flux(evaluate(scenario, x0)), the objective holds the drive of the design x0;
  • with a keepout, its penalty on the worst-case clearance of the beams. A keep-out is an ApertureKeepOut for the entrance-hole rims, a SphereKeepOut for the capsule, or a tuple of them.

The direct penalty counts only power already on the capsule, so keep a margin with a SphereKeepOut.

With uncertainty = (; pointing, power), the mode terms are their expected values over those errors, linearized. The errors are the rms beam-to-beam pointing in mm and the relative power, as in mode_uncertainty. Each (aₗ/a₀)² gains the variance due to the errors, so the optimum also makes the drive shape insensitive to them (robust design). This costs about three gradients per evaluation. The shared errors of mode_uncertainty (group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning) are accepted too. They add a fourth per-beam sensitivity, for about four gradients per evaluation.

Briareus.Constraint — Type
Constraint(name, f; upper = nothing, lower = nothing, scale = nothing, labels = nothing)

An inequality constraint for optimize_pointing: f(ev) ≤ upper (a ceiling) or f(ev) ≥ lower (a floor), with f any function of an Evaluation (its beams ev.beams included), generic in the number type so that the automatic-differentiation gradients of the optimizer pass through it. Give exactly one of upper and lower.

f may return a number or a vector; a vector constraint has one component per entry, each with a separate multiplier and row in the report, against a limit that is a number (shared) or a vector of the same length. labels name the components in reports (default "1", "2", …): a collection of names, or a function of the evaluation that returns them, for a constraint whose components follow the evaluation (one per beam, as in clearance_floor). Within one run the number of components is fixed; a constraint with a shared limit and labels from the evaluation can be used for scenarios with different numbers of beams, such as a stage on quads and then one on their beams.

scale (a number or one per component, positive) sets the units of the violation: the optimizer works on ±(f(ev) - limit)/scale and its feasibility tolerance is in those units. By default scale = |limit|, so a violation is relative (1e-3: 0.1% of the limit); a zero limit needs a scale.

Ready-made constraints: irradiance_ceiling, mode_gate, clearance_floor (the clearance from a keep-out per beam), light_ceiling and light_floor (the laser light on named parts), pulse_ceiling (the limit of the laser on the pulses of a phased design). Any other quantity of the beams only is Constraint(:name, ev -> quantity(ev.beams); lower = minimum).

clearance_floor(keepout; minimum = keepout.minimum, scale = 1.0, name = :clearance) -> Constraint

A floor on the worst-case clearance (mm) of each beam from a keep-out: the Constraint keepout_clearance(keepout, ev) .≥ minimum, one component per beam of the evaluation, labeled by the beam name. The keep-out is any with a keepout_clearance method and a minimum field: an ApertureKeepOut, a SphereKeepOut, a HardwareKeepOut. A beam the keep-out does not apply to (clearance Inf) gives a component that is always met; a clearance of -Inf or NaN throws an ArgumentError naming the keep-out and the beam, as in keepout_penalty. The weight of the keep-out is not used: the constraint replaces its penalty (keepout_penalty), so pass the keep-out to the keepout keyword of the objective or as a constraint, not both.

scale (mm) is the unit of the violation: with the default 1 mm and feasibility = 1e-3, a clearance within 1 µm of minimum is :active (an engineering default, finer than the keep-out accuracy against clearance, a few micrometers). Give each of several keep-outs a distinct name.

Where the limits conflict, scale is also the weight of the keep-out against the other constraints, because phase 1, the augmented Lagrangian and the least-violation design of PointingResult all trade normalized violations. At 1 mm, a beam 0.1 mm inside the keep-out weighs as much as a mode gate exceeded by 10% of itself or a ceiling 10% over its limit, with one component per beam. A smaller scale, e.g. a pointing tolerance of 0.05 mm, makes the same shortfall weigh twenty times more. On a re-pointing study whose gates conflicted with the keep-out and a ceiling, that change moved the violation from the keep-out onto the gates and the ceiling, and each of them ended several times further over (research notes, "Least violation, capped inner runs and phase 1's budget", § 6). The scale should be chosen for the trade the design accepts.

The floor applies to each beam separately. In the augmented Lagrangian each beam component has a separate multiplier, which stays zero for a beam clear of its minimum, and the term of that beam then vanishes from the inner problem. Each term is as smooth as the clearance of its beam. The smallest clearance over the beams would instead have a kink wherever the closest beam changes (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing"). The components follow the beams of the evaluation, so the same constraint can be used for a stage on quads and a stage on their beams.

On a PhasedScenario the constraint takes the PerPhase evaluation directly, without in_phase: the component of each beam is its least clearance over the phases, and its rows carry no phase in their names (leh[B211]). The phases share the aims, so a keep-out that does not read the beam power (an ApertureKeepOut at a peak_fraction) gives every phase the same clearance, identical to that of any one phase; a keep-out at an absolute intensity widens with the power, and the brightest phase sets the clearance.

Briareus.irradiance_ceiling — Method
irradiance_ceiling(reference, names...; limit = peak_irradiance(reference, names...),
                   p = 64, weight = nothing, scale = limit, exact = true) -> Constraint

A ceiling on the absorbed laser irradiance over the elements of the named parts or components. The Constraint is named :irradiance, and by default it bounds the peak_irradiance by limit (see exact below). Its function is the smooth stand-in the optimizer works on, the irradiance_norm scaled to equal the peak at the reference evaluation, ‖I‖ₚ × peak_ref/‖I‖ₚ,ref. With the default limit, the peak of the reference, the irradiance is held at that of the reference design, for example the pointing before a bundle was dropped; a number in TW/mm² sets another, measured on the same scale. The reference evaluation must be on the same target as the optimized scenario (a subset of it shares the target), with the same laser–plasma model (lpi, compared on every field, so that a model built alike passes) at the same time; otherwise evaluating the ceiling throws an ArgumentError. A reference evaluated without the CBET model of the scenario gives the peak and the stand-in scale of another quantity, and the ceiling would hold the design to a limit on that quantity, so the mismatch throws an error. Evaluate the reference in a scenario with the same model, e.g. evaluate(remake(reference_scenario; lpi = scenario.lpi), beams). weight selects and weighs the beams that count, as in peak_irradiance (the outer cones only, or picket energies for a fluence ceiling), for the reference and the design alike.

The ceiling is on an absolute irradiance: where the power can fall (power scales, PowerScales, or powers among the parameters) the optimizer can meet it by cutting power, at a loss of drive (on a re-pointing study with a power scale per cone at most 1, a large one). With a drive_floor beside it, the drive is held.

A ceiling on the peak wall irradiance at the value of the reference is an engineering limit and is not a published criterion. The published scalings of the growth of the gold bubble are written in terms of the picket fluence of the outer cones on the wall (research notes), and a weight computes that fluence on the same machinery.

With exact = true (the default) the peak itself is held at the limit. The optimizer works on the norm, but once the constrained run has converged it compares the peak_irradiance with the limit; while the peak exceeds it by more than the feasibility of the run (in units of scale), the working limit of the norm is scaled by limit/peak and the run continues from the same design, multipliers and penalty. The reported value (check_constraints, PointingResult.constraints) is the peak against the limit, and a converged run ends with the peak at or below the limit within feasibility. The cost is the further outer iterations, when the peak ends above the limit (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing").

exact = false bounds only the norm, and the norm is the reported value. At the limit the hot spots of the field are as hot, in the p-norm mean, as those of the reference, but the peak over the elements may exceed the limit by a margin that depends on where the run ends. On the tutorial mesh the margin is 0.1% at p = 64 and 3% at p = 16. On the dropped-bundle test case (0.45 mm wall elements) it is 3% at p = 64 for one of two designs that meet the same gates, under another floating-point rounding of the same run (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing").

Across meshes the ceiling is a ratio to the reference on the same mesh, which changes less with the element size than the ratio of the peaks does (rms difference 1.0% against 1.3% between 0.2 and 0.45 mm elements near the limit), while the peak itself falls on a coarser mesh.

Briareus.mode_gate — Method
mode_gate(reference::Evaluation, gates; fraction = 1, floor = nothing, shift = nothing)
    -> Constraint
mode_gate(reference::AbstractVector, gates; fraction = 1, floor = nothing, shift = nothing)
    -> Constraint

A gate per degree on the departure of the capsule drive shape from a reference shape. The Constraint mode_departure(ev, reference; lmax) ≤ gates is named :mode_gate and has one component per degree l = 1:lmax, labeled l = 1, …, each in units of its gate.

gates is either a vector of departures or the result of mode_uncertainty. In a vector, gates[l] is the gate for degree l in the units of mode_departure, which for an axisymmetric difference are |Δaₗ/a₀|; so 0.005 at l = 2 is 0.5% in P2/P0. For a mode_uncertainty result the gate is its floor, √(2l+1) × rms_mode[l], the departure that random errors of that size (e.g. NIF's 50 µm rms pointing) give each degree. That gate is a lower bound, because it is what pointing error alone produces. Laser delivery and the hohlraum plasma move the shape from shot to shot by more, in ways a view-factor model does not carry. A tolerance per degree in physical units, chosen for the design, is therefore the usual gate. At the pointing floor the gates can cost re-pointing and drive that a tolerance within that shot-to-shot spread does not (research notes, "Mode gates in physical units"). The share of laser delivery can be included in the floor through mode_uncertainty's power, an rms fraction per pointing unit. On the case of the dropped-bundle tutorial, 3% per quad raises the floor by 40% at l = 1 and 21% at l = 2, and 5% raises it by 92% and 51%. NIF's published beam-to-beam balance is better than 5% (research notes, "Inner-run options, a wall-loss scenario warning and laser delivery figures", § 7). With drive_objective the optimizer finds the largest drive within the gates; with shift_objective it finds the least re-pointing that meets them.

With a reference Evaluation (e.g. the pointing before a bundle was dropped, in a scenario that observes the capsule), the shape is its flux_ylm coefficients, and the gate records the laser–plasma model (lpi) and time of the reference scenario: evaluating the gate on a scenario with another model, or the same model at another time, throws an ArgumentError, as for irradiance_ceiling (the shape of a drive without the CBET model of the scenario is another shape, and the gates would hold the design to it). The reference may be on another target (a hohlraum without the windows of the design). A coefficient vector does not record a model, so that method cannot check it: the vector must come from an evaluation with the lpi and time of the scenario.

Keywords.

  • fraction (positive, default 1) scales a pointing floor. With gates a mode_uncertainty result, the gate is fraction × √(2l+1) × rms_mode[l], that is fraction × u.floor[l]; fraction = 0.8 gives 0.8 of the floor. A vector as long as the floor gives one multiple per degree; fraction = [1, 2, 2, 2] gives the floor at l = 1 and twice the floor at l = 2–4.
  • floor (a mode_uncertainty result, or a vector of departures per degree) makes the floor a lower bound of physical gates: the gate per degree is the larger of gates[l] and fraction × floor[l], so that a tolerance in physical units is never tighter than the departure pointing error alone produces (research notes, "Mode gates in physical units"). gates and floor cover the same degrees.
  • shift moves the target shape away from the reference shape: the departure is measured from the reference coefficients with each listed mode shifted, the laser–plasma model and time of the reference still checked. A shift is a pair l => Δ (the axisymmetric mode (l, 0)) or (l, m) => Δ, or a collection of them, with Δ in the units of mode_departure: for m = 0, Δ is the change of aₗ/a₀ in the Legendre expansion of the capsule flux Σ aₗ Pₗ(cos θ) (2 => 0.024 sets the target P2/P0 0.024 above that of the reference; with the orthonormal coefficients of flux_ylm, c_l0 → c_l0 + Δ c_00/√(2l+1)), and for m ≠ 0 the same change of the real coefficient c_lm, so that the shifted shape departs from the reference shape by |Δ| in degree l (shift_modes). The drive c_00 is unchanged.

Briareus.infer_errors — Method
infer_errors(scenario, beams, measured; sigma, pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0,
             group_pointing = 0.0, group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad,
             positioning = 0.0) -> NamedTuple

Infers the most probable beam errors behind a measured drive-shape departure. This function is the inverse of mode_uncertainty and takes the same error priors. A measured low mode can trend from shot to shot; MacGowan et al. (HEDP 2021) trend mode 1 of NIF implosions against beam and target data. Such a trend raises the question of which errors could have caused it, and infer_errors gives a linear answer for one shot.

measured contains the departures Δc = (c_lm/c_00)_measured − (c_lm/c_00)_nominal as pairs. A pair (l, m) => Δc gives one component, and l => [Δc_l,-l, …, Δc_l,l] gives a whole degree in mode_index order. sigma is the rms error of the measurement, one number or one per component in the order that measured expands to. The nominal shape is that of the scenario for beams. Components are allowed through l = 6, the limit of the cached $Y_{lm}$ weights.

The model is Δc = J x + ε, with x the errors and J their exact sensitivities (mode_sensitivity, with the shared columns). The errors are zero-mean normal with the variances of mode_uncertainty: per beam, its aim along the spot axes a, b and its relative power; per group, a shared displacement and power; and the target positioning. The posterior mean, which is also the most probable x, and its covariance come from the data-space (Woodbury) form, a solve the size of the measurement:

x̂ = Σₚ Jᵀ (J Σₚ Jᵀ + Σₘ)⁻¹ Δc,   Σ = Σₚ − Σₚ Jᵀ (J Σₚ Jᵀ + Σₘ)⁻¹ J Σₚ.

Returns (; beam, group, positioning, x, sigma, prior_sigma, covariance, names, components, measured, predicted, residual, sources, resolved, beams, J, c):

  • beam: nb × 3, the inferred a, b displacement (mm) and relative power of each beam;
  • group: a Dict from group key to (; displacement, power), with the displacement a Vec3 in mm;
  • positioning: the inferred common displacement of every aim relative to the target. The target therefore lies at minus this vector from where the beams are aimed.
  • x, sigma, prior_sigma, covariance, names: all errors as one vector, with their posterior and prior rms, the posterior covariance and labels. The vector holds the beam a, b and power, then the group errors, then x, y and z.
  • predicted = J x̂ and residual = Δc − J x̂, over the (l, m) list components;
  • sources: the fraction of Δc reproduced by the share of J x̂ from each error source, dot(J_s x̂_s, Δc)/|Δc|², sorted descending. A source is the pointing or power of a beam or of a group, or the positioning. The fractions sum to dot(predicted, Δc)/|Δc|², and the first entries are the sources that best explain the measurement.
  • resolved: Σⱼ (1 − Σⱼⱼ/σⱼ²), the number of error combinations that the data determine, at most the number of components;
  • beams: beams with x̂ applied, for a full evaluate as the nonlinear check. The errors are applied before a laser–plasma model, as in the sensitivities.

The name describes the result: the errors that are most probable under a prior, which are not a unique explanation. Many errors map to a few modes, so a handful of combinations are determined and every other direction stays at its prior. sigma ./ prior_sigma therefore stays near 1 for most errors, and resolved gives the number of determined combinations. The result is only as good as the prior. An error left out of it, such as group_pointing = 0, cannot be blamed, and its effect is attributed to the sources that are allowed. The inference is linear in the errors. infer_velocity_errors is the same inverse for a measured hot-spot velocity.

Briareus.mode_uncertainty — Method
mode_uncertainty(scenario, beams; pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0, lmax = 4,
                 group_pointing = 0.0, group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad,
                 positioning = 0.0) -> NamedTuple

Computes the expected spread of the observed drive shape from beam errors. The errors are propagated linearly through exact per-beam sensitivities (mode_sensitivity).

Independent beam-to-beam errors displace the aim of each beam at random transverse to the beam, with rms pointing mm. That rms is of the displacement length, so each axis gets pointing/√2; NIF specifies 50 µm. The power of each beam is scaled by an rms fraction power.

Correlated errors are zero by default:

  • group_pointing displaces every beam of a group by one shared vector transverse to the mean beam direction of the group, with rms length group_pointing mm. groups is a Beam field such as :quad or a function of the beam, which can select bundles or the cone in one hemisphere, b -> (b.group, b.port[3] > 0). Beams at an angle to that mean also move along their axes, with the focus moving with the aim. A group whose beams have no common direction, such as :group for a cone over both hemispheres, is refused.
  • group_power gives one shared relative power error per group, with rms group_power.
  • positioning displaces the target from chamber center, which moves every aim by the same vector relative to the target. It is an isotropic rms length in mm or a 3-tuple of rms values along x, y and z.

Each source adds to the covariance independently. Returns (; c, sigma, covariance, rms_mode, floor, rms_by, J):

  • c is the nominal c_lm/c_00, sigma the rms of each coefficient and covariance their covariance.
  • rms_mode[l], for each degree l = 1:lmax, is the rms of the mode amplitude over the errors, √E[Σ_m (δc_lm/c_00)²]. It is the expected mode-l budget used by the errors. mode_amplitude gives √(2l+1) times the nominal amplitude for comparison.
  • floor[l] is √(2l+1) × rms_mode[l], the same spread in the units of mode_departure, which is |Δaₗ/a₀| for an axisymmetric change. It is the noise floor of a drive shape under these errors. mode_gate takes its gate from this result, with fraction a multiple of the floor, one number or one per degree.
  • rms_by splits rms_mode by source into beam, group and positioning, which add in quadrature.
  • J is the matrix of mode_sensitivity.

The propagation is linear in the errors. Large errors can be checked with sample_modes, which takes the same keywords.

An objective for optimize_pointing, the weighted mean squared aim shift (mm²) of the beams of an evaluation from the reference beams, matched by name, Σ w_b |aim_b - aim_ref(b)|² / Σ w_b. Several phases under one pointing

A PhasedScenario holds one pointing over several phases of a pulse, such as the picket, the foot, and the peak before and after windows burn through. Each Phase has a separate target, radiation model, options and beam powers, given per beam or from pulses at a time. The parameter vector (PhasedPointing) is that of the shared pointing, which sets the aims of every phase, followed by a power scale per (group, phase) for the phases not fixed. evaluate gives one evaluation per phase in a PerPhase, which optimize_pointing passes to the objective and to every constraint. in_phase applies any constraint or objective of one evaluation to one phase, with the phase in its reported name, while the clearance_floor of a keep-out takes every phase at once. The albedos and time of a phase come from a drive history with Phase(name, scenario; history, time), and a reference at the same time from remake(scenario; history, time).

Further tools for phased designs are:

  • bounds(scenario.param; power_max) caps every beam in every phase at the limit of the laser, and pulse_ceiling constrains the pulses written for a design (scaled_pulse) to stay within it between the phase samples.
  • phase_beams puts reference beams at the powers of each phase.
  • power_scales returns the scales inside an objective (differentiable), power_scale_objective penalizes their departure from a reference drive (each scale weighted by the beams it governs), and power_scale_table tabulates them per key and phase.
  • remake(scenario; deposition_kw) rebuilds the deposition operator of every phase, for example to check a finished design on a finer one.
  • pointing_summary reports each phase against its gates and pointing floor, with the departure per degree split into m = 0 and m ≠ 0 and the quads moved past given shifts.

Briareus.bounds — Function
bounds(p::PhasedPointing, x0 = initial_parameters(p); scale = 0.5, scale_min = 0.0,
       scale_max = Inf, power_max = Inf, pulses = nothing, times = nothing,
       peak = nothing, kwargs...) -> (lower, upper)

The box of the pointing (its bounds about x0[1:n] with kwargs) for the first parameters, and a range for each power scale. Each scale lies within x0 (1 ± scale), where scale = Inf removes the relative limit, and is clipped to [scale_min, scale_max] and to the laser limit. power_max (TW per beam, a number or a function of a beam) caps every beam in every scaled phase, so a scale is at most the least power_max / P over the beams of its group in its phase. P is the phase power, or the pointing power at x0 in a phase without powers. A fixed phase with a beam above power_max is an ArgumentError, and so is an empty box (scale_min above the cap of a scale). The defaults are engineering defaults; no facility limit sets them.

Between the phases. The scale bounds limit the beams only at the times of the phases. A design is delivered as the pulse scaled_pulse (or shape_pulses) of the base pulse of each beam, with the scales of its key as multipliers at the times of the scaled phases. The multipliers are interpolated between those times, so a base pulse that rises between two phase samples can exceed power_max where neither sample does. Given pulses, times and peak, the box also includes every condition of pulse_ceiling that bounds only one scale. pulses is the base pulse of each beam, a PulseShape for every beam or a function of a beam. times is the time of each phase in ns by name, as a named tuple or dictionary, or a vector in the order of the phases. peak is the value given to scaled_pulse. The conditions on one scale are:

  • the pulse before the first scaled phase, with its multiplier held at the first scale;
  • the pulse after the last scaled phase, on the peak ramp to 1, or held at the last scale without peak;
  • on each interval between two scaled phases, the cap that follows with the neighboring scale at its least value in the box.

No design whose pulses stay within power_max is excluded by these caps. The conditions that couple two neighboring scales, for the multiplier between their times, are linear in both, and no box can express them; pulse_ceiling imposes them as a constraint. The power of each scaled phase must be the base pulse at the phase time. Otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown, since the multipliers would scale another pulse. Without pulses the caps are those at the phase times, and scaled_pulse(...; power_max) checks a pulse table.

An objective term for optimize_pointing on a phased scenario. The term is the beam-weighted mean squared relative change of the power scales from reference,

Σ_j W_j (s_j / s0_j - 1)² / Σ_j W_j,

over the scales s_j of power_scales, one per (key, phase) of scenario.param.keys. W_j is the summed weight of the beams that the scale applies to, by default their number. With unit weights the term is the mean, over every beam and scaled phase, of the squared relative change of the beam scale. A scale shared by a cone therefore counts as many times as the cone has beams, and a scale per quad as many times as the quad has beams. The term regularizes a re-pointing whose powers should stay near a reference drive. It is added to an objective, ev -> J(ev) + w * reg(ev) with reg = power_scale_objective(ps; reference = s0).

reference is one scale for every key, a vector in the order of the keys (for example power_scales(ps, x0) of a start), or a function (key, phase) -> scale. Its values must be positive and finite. weight is a function of a beam of the pointing (of its group, quad or name), evaluated once at construction on the beams of the pointing. The weights must be finite and non-negative with a positive sum. The scales are taken from the evaluations as in power_scales, so every scaled phase needs explicit powers (a power or pulses in its Phase). Generic in the number type and thread-safe, since it does not modify its tables.

Briareus.power_scales — Method
power_scales(p, ev::PerPhase) -> Vector
power_scales(p, beams::PerPhase) -> Vector
power_scales(p, x::AbstractVector) -> Vector

The power scales of a phased design, one per (key, phase) of p.param.keys in that order, where p is a PhasedScenario or its PhasedPointing. They are the parameters x[n+1:end] by which apply multiplies the powers of the phases. From a PerPhase of evaluations (their setup beams, ev[phase].beams) or of beam vectors (in the order of the pointing, as apply and evaluate give them), each scale is the power of a beam over its phase power (phase_powers). The beam used is the first beam of the key with positive phase power. A key whose beams are all at zero power in the phase has no scale to recover and gives 1, as in parameters. A scaled phase whose powers are the pointing powers (no power or pulses in its Phase) has no phase power to divide by, and an ArgumentError is thrown; the scales of such a phase come from x.

Generic in the number type and thread-safe. Inside an objective of the PerPhase evaluation the scales are duals of the optimizer, for example in a regularization of the scales toward s0 (a vector in the order of the keys), ev -> J(ev) + w * sum(abs2, power_scales(ps, ev) ./ s0 .- 1) / length(s0). power_scale_table gives them per key and phase for a report.

Briareus.pulse_ceiling — Method
pulse_ceiling(p; pulses, times, power_max, peak = nothing, name = :pulse) -> Constraint

A ceiling on the pulses of a phased design between the phase samples as well as at them. The Constraint requires every point of the scaled_pulse of each beam to stay at or below power_max. That pulse is the base pulse of the beam, with the power scales of its key as multipliers at the times of the scaled phases, ramped to 1 at peak. p is a PhasedScenario or its PhasedPointing. pulses is the base pulse of each beam, a PulseShape for every beam or a function of a beam. times is the time of each phase in ns by name, as a named tuple or dictionary, or a vector in the order of the phases. power_max is the limit in TW per beam (a number, or a function of a beam), and peak the time given to scaled_pulse. The power of each scaled phase must be the base pulse at the phase time, or an ArgumentError is thrown. Every scaled phase needs powers, a power or pulses in the Phase, since the constraint takes the scales from the beams of the evaluation (power_scales). The constraint is generic in the number type.

The written pulse is linear between its points, which are the points of the base pulse and the phase times, so its largest power is at a point. At a point τ between the scaled phases at tᵢ and tᵢ₊₁, the multiplier is (1 − w) sᵢ + w sᵢ₊₁ with w = (τ − tᵢ)/(tᵢ₊₁ − tᵢ). The condition P(τ) ((1 − w) sᵢ + w sᵢ₊₁) ≤ power_max is therefore linear in the two scales. Before the first phase and after the last, the condition bounds one scale; on the peak ramp the multiplier runs to 1. Of the points between two phases, only those on the lower convex hull of (w, power_max/P(τ)) are kept, since their conditions imply those at the other points. The constraint is therefore exact, with a few components per key and interval: a design meets it if and only if its written pulses stay within power_max. The pulse from peak on is the base pulse, which lies outside the constraint; scaled_pulse(...; power_max) checks it.

The value of a component is the written power at its point (TW), and its limit is power_max. The label gives the key and the time (pulse[outer, 4.35 ns]), and the default scale makes the violation relative to the limit. bounds(p.param; power_max, pulses, times, peak) imposes exactly, as a box, the components that bound only one scale. With both, the augmented Lagrangian includes only the coupled components, each to within the feasibility of the run (1e-3 of the limit by default), so a written pulse can end that far over the limit. With a limit lowered by the feasibility, power_max = (1 - 1e-3) * L, the pulse stays within L.

Briareus.remake — Method
remake(scenario::PhasedScenario; pointing, by, scaled, deposition_kw) -> PhasedScenario

Copy of a phased scenario with another pointing, another grouping by of the power scales, or other scaled phases (by name). The new pointing is a parameterization of the same beams, whose ports the deposition operators of the phases serve, as when staging per quad, then per beam. A function that gives several quads one key ties their scales. The operators, radiation model and response of every phase are kept, and the powers of the phases are set again on the beams of the new pointing.

With deposition_kw, the deposition operator of every phase is rebuilt with these options for the beams of the pointing, as remake(scenario; deposition_kw) does for one Scenario. Options not given take their defaults, including those in the deposition_kw of a phase. The target, radiation model, response, laser–plasma model and time of each phase are kept. With pointing as well, the operators are built for the beams of the new pointing, so its ports need not be the old ones. Phases that shared one operator share the rebuilt one. A phase on a target with the same mesh as an earlier phase (a target that differs only in its materials) is given the rebuilt operator of that phase on its target, so that phases on one mesh are still deposited in one pass.

Pulse shapes

Each distinct pulse shape needs facility validation. pulse_shape_groups groups per-key power multipliers over the phases of a pulse (one per quad and phase, from power scales) into a given number of shapes. It runs k-means on the log multipliers per class (for example the inner and the outer cones) and splits the shapes between the classes by fit. shape_pulses and scaled_pulse write the shapes out as pulses for write_pulse_table, and the assignment of the grouping ties the power scales of a re-optimization per shape (PowerScales).

PulseShapeGroups

Multipliers per key and phase grouped into shapes, from pulse_shape_groups. Fields:

  • class: the class of each shape;
  • multipliers: shapes × phases, the multiplier of each shape per phase, the geometric mean of those of its members;
  • members: the keys of each shape, in input order;
  • assignment: a Dict from each key to the name of its shape;
  • rms: the root-mean-square residual of the fit in the natural log of the multipliers, over every key and phase;
  • split: the number of shapes per class, as class => n pairs in order of first appearance; splits: every admissible split of the chosen number of shapes (counts per class in that order) and its rms;
  • counts: each number of shapes tried and the rms of its best split, in increasing order (only shapes when no tolerance is given);
  • exact: true when the grouping of every class is the exact least-squares optimum (by enumeration or, with one phase, by dynamic programming), false where seeded restarts found it.

Briareus.pulse_shape_groups — Method
pulse_shape_groups(multipliers::AbstractMatrix, keys; shapes, tolerance = nothing,
                   class = nothing, seed = 1, restarts = 100,
                   exact_limit = 10^6) -> PulseShapeGroups
pulse_shape_groups(multipliers::AbstractDict; phases = nothing, kwargs...)

Group per-key power multipliers over the phases of a pulse into shapes distinct pulse shapes. The grouping is k-means on the natural log of the multipliers, with one coordinate per phase for the point of each key, so that the multiplier of a shape per phase is the geometric mean of those of its members and the fit is least squares in the relative error. Rows of the matrix are keys (e.g. quad names) and columns phases; a dictionary maps each key to its vector of multipliers per phase, or, with phases (the phases in order), each (key, phase) pair to a multiplier, the layout of power scales keyed per quad and phase. Keys of a dictionary are sorted (by their string) for a deterministic order. Every multiplier must be positive.

class is a function of the key, a dictionary or a vector aligned with the keys, and nothing means one class. It separates keys that never share a shape, such as the quads of the inner and the outer cones, which follow different base pulses. Each class is grouped separately, with at least one shape and at most one per key, and the shapes are split between the classes by the smallest total squared error over every admissible split (ties to the first in order).

tolerance (an rms in the natural log of the multipliers; nothing, the default, for a fixed count) makes shapes an upper bound: the result is the smallest number of shapes, from one per class up to shapes, whose best split fits with an rms at or below tolerance, or shapes when none does (counts lists the rms of each number tried). Shapes that differ by less than the laser delivers distinctly need not be separate pulses. The following figures give a scale only; the default tolerance is nothing. NIF's laser performance model predicts total energies "within 2–3 percent" and gives "beam-to-beam energy balance better than 5 percent for full system shots" (NIF User Guide, section 4.8, Laser Performance Operations Model, https://nifuserguide.llnl.gov/home/4-laser-system/48-laser-performance-operations-model). The page does not give a statistic for the balance. A relative error ε is about ε in the log, so 0.02–0.05 spans those figures. The tolerance is left to the study.

Each class of n keys is grouped into k groups exactly where the size allows. Every partition into k groups is enumerated when there are at most exact_limit of them (the Stirling number S(n, k)), and dynamic programming over the sorted values is used when there is a single phase (an optimal one-dimensional grouping is contiguous). Otherwise the best of restarts k-means++ seedings is taken, each refined by single-key transfers until no transfer lowers the squared error (Hartigan's rule, which reaches a local optimum at least as good as Lloyd's). The seedings are drawn from a SplitMix64 stream started at seed, so the result is the same on every Julia version and machine. Restarts are a heuristic: the result is the global optimum only with high probability (it matched exhaustive enumeration on every small case tested).

shape_pulses writes the shapes out as pulses, and the assignment is used to tie the power scales of a re-optimization per shape (PowerScales with by = b -> groups.assignment[b.quad]).

inner = Set(b.quad for b in beams if b.group in (:inner23, :inner30))
g = pulse_shape_groups(m, quad_names; shapes = 6, class = q -> q in inner ? :inner : :outer)
g.split, g.rms, g.assignment["Q15T"]
# the fewest shapes, up to 8, that fit within 3% rms
g3 = pulse_shape_groups(m, quad_names; shapes = 8, tolerance = 0.03,
                        class = q -> q in inner ? :inner : :outer)
length(g3.names), g3.counts

Briareus.scaled_pulse — Method
scaled_pulse(base::PulseShape, times, multipliers; peak = nothing, power_max = nothing)
    -> PulseShape

The pulse base with its power multiplied by a multiplier given per phase. The multiplier is multipliers[i] at times[i] (ns, increasing), interpolated linearly between the phase times and held at the first and last values outside them. The result is tabulated at the union of base's times and the phase times (inside base's span), and each tabulated power is the power of the base times the multiplier at that point. Between those points the result is linear, as every PulseShape is. The exact product of two linear pieces is quadratic, so the table contains the multiplier applied at the points; the exact product is not tabulated.

With peak, a time after the last phase, the pulse is left unscaled from that time on: the multiplier then varies linearly from its value at the last phase to 1 between the last phase time and peak, and is 1 after it. This is the treatment for a peak at the laser limit, which is left unscaled. The pulse is sampled only at the phase times, and the ramp between the last phase and the peak is an assumption of this function (it is not fitted).

power_max (TW per beam) checks the result against the laser limit: a pulse above it anywhere (at the table points, where its maximum lies) is an ArgumentError naming the time, the power and the excess. Multipliers within the limit at the phase times can still exceed it between them, where the base pulse rises. pulse_ceiling(scenario; pulses, times, power_max, peak) holds this pulse within the limit as a constraint of a phased design, and bounds(scenario.param; power_max, pulses, times, peak) gives the conditions on a single scale as a box. A table is not clipped: clipping would change the energy of the pulse and the drive the design was optimized for.
