Optimization

GroupPointing maps parameters to beams, per cone, quad or beam: axial, radial and tangential aim (in the quad's port azimuth by default) and a quad's split about its aim point, defocus, power, spot scale and Δλ, with top–bottom mirroring; parameters builds its parameter vector from values named by group and field, and Scenario precomputes everything an evaluation needs. symmetry_objective and asymmetry_objective target capsule drive symmetry (axisymmetric and three-dimensional), face_flux_objective the uniformity of a flat observed face's x-ray drive (a halfraum's package, an opacity sample), and irradiance_objective foil uniformity. optimize_pointing runs bounded L-BFGS-B on forward-mode automatic-differentiation gradients, with a callback for progress and cancellation. influence_matrix gives each beam's mode response. HeldParameters holds some parameters of a parameterization at given values, and PowerScales adds a power scale per group of beams (e.g. per cone) beside its parameters; both compose with GroupPointing, FramePointing and each other.

Limits that a design must meet go to optimize_pointing as Constraints, which an augmented Lagrangian around the same L-BFGS-B handles; check_constraints reports them on any evaluation, and the PointingResult records each one's value against its limit, what ended the run and, for each parameter held by the box, the optimum's derivative with respect to that bound. from = result continues a run with its multipliers and penalty, and time_limit bounds its wall time. phase1 = true first looks for a design within the limits and ends with :infeasible when the search finds none, and relaxation = true then reports how far each conflicting constraint must be relaxed alone (Relaxation). Ready-made constraints: irradiance_ceiling, a ceiling on the laser irradiance over named parts (its exact value peak_irradiance, held itself by default, its smooth stand-in irradiance_norm, held alone with exact = false), and mode_gate, a gate per degree on the mode_departure from a reference drive shape, which goes with drive_objective for the largest drive within the gates, and drive_floor, the capsule's drive at least a fraction of a reference's; clearance_floor, each beam's worst-case clearance from a keep-out at or above its minimum; light_ceiling and light_floor, the laser light on named parts (light_fraction) at most or at least a limit. The laser irradiance during the picket, the quantity of the gold bubble's published scalings, has its own ceiling, picket_ceiling (Laser–plasma models).

mode_sensitivity gives the exact sensitivity of the drive shape to each beam's pointing and power errors, and mode_uncertainty the asymmetry independent errors leave by linear propagation (sample_modes is the Monte Carlo check). Errors can be shared by a group of beams or be the target's positioning. The objectives' uncertainty keyword optimizes the expected symmetry over them, and infer_errors finds the most probable errors behind a measured asymmetry.

Briareus.ConstraintRow — Type
ConstraintRow

One component of a Constraint at a design, from check_constraints or in PointingResult.constraints. Fields: name (the constraint's name, with the component's label for a vector constraint, e.g. "mode_gate[l = 2]"), value and limit (in the constraint's units), upper (true for a ceiling value ≤ limit, false for a floor), violation (±(value - limit)/scale, the normalized excess: positive when violated, negative inside), multiplier (the augmented Lagrangian's multiplier estimate in the normalized units, 0 when not computed) and status: :violated (violation > feasibility), :active (within feasibility of the limit) or :satisfied.

Briareus.GroupPointing — Type
GroupPointing(beams; by = :group, fields = (:z, :r, :defocus), mirror = true,
              relative = false, unit = :quad, radius = nothing, center = (0, 0, 0))

Maps a parameter vector to beams. Beams are grouped by by (:group — e.g. cone —, :quad, :beam, or a function beam -> key); each group gets one value per field:

  • :z — axial aim coordinate (mm). With mirror, beams from the lower hemisphere (port z < 0) aim at -z, so one value describes a top/bottom-symmetric cone.
  • :r — radial aim offset (mm) toward the port azimuth of the beam's quad (its own with unit = :beam, below).
  • :phi — tangential aim offset (mm), counterclockwise about z.
  • :split — tangential split of a quad's beams about its aim point (mm), along the quad's φ̂: each beam's aim moves by ±split, with the sign of its own port's azimuth from its quad's mean port (beams counterclockwise of it +split), so a positive split opens a quad's aims in the sense of its ports and a negative one crosses them (the s of hybride_mock_pointing is split = -s). It sets each beam's tangential offset from its quad's mean template aim (a shift of it with relative); a quad held as one beam gets none. Needs unit = :quad and quad names.
  • :defocus — focal-plane offset along the beam (mm, see Beam).
  • :power — power per beam (TW).
  • :spot — multiplier on the best-focus spot semi-axes.
  • :dlambda — wavelength detuning (Å), used by laser–plasma models.

Fields not listed keep each beam's template value.

Pointing on a sphere (polar direct drive). With radius = R (mm) the aim can instead be a point on the sphere of radius R about center, the target surface on which direct-drive pointings are published (Barlow et al., PRL 133, 175101, 2024, Table I; Yeamans et al., Nucl. Fusion 61, 046031, 2021, Table 1; Hohenberger et al., PoP 22, 056308, 2015, Table I):

  • :theta_s — polar angle of the aim point (degrees from +z). With mirror, beams from the lower hemisphere aim at 180° - theta_s, so one value describes a top/bottom-symmetric cone pair and a larger value moves both toward the equator.
  • :dphi_s — azimuth of the aim point relative to the beam's own port azimuth (degrees, counterclockwise about z; the same for both hemispheres).
  • :split_theta, :split_phi — split of a quad's beams (degrees): each beam's aim moves by ±split_theta in polar angle and ±split_phi in azimuth, with the sign of its own port's offset from its quad's mean port (beams at a larger polar angle than their quad's, counted from the beam's own pole when mirrored, take +split_theta; beams counterclockwise of it +split_phi), so positive values open a quad's aims in the sense of its ports. This is the split-quad pointing of Cok et al. (PoP 15, 082705, 2008) and Yeamans et al. 2021 (left/right Δφ) as angles on the sphere. A quad given as one beam, or a beam level with its quad's mean, gets no split. Needs quad names; other per-beam aims come from by (e.g. one group per sub-ring).

The aim is the surface point itself, aim = center + R * port_vector(θ, φ), which the axis meets first when it faces the port (Beam). Best focus is then at aim + defocus * k: defocus is measured from the surface aim point, as VisRad measures the focus offset from its reference point (research notes, "Pointing and focus conventions"). Each template beam's angles are those of the first point where its axis meets the sphere (an ArgumentError if it misses); listed spherical values are shared by a group's members, and an angle that no listed field governs keeps each beam's own value. The aim moves to the surface along the template's axis, so the focus moves with it unless the template aim was already on the sphere. The spherical fields and the hohlraum fields :z, :r, :phi exclude each other.

A quad is the unit of the hohlraum fields' pointing by default (unit = :quad): :r, :phi and :split are measured in the azimuth of the quad's port, the mean port of the beams that share its quad name, instead of each beam's own. The beams of a quad that share their values then share one aim point, whether the table holds the quad as one beam or as its four, so a NIF pointing given per cone or per quad stays one aim per quad while the deposition is computed per beam. A quad's beams pointed apart about its aim point:

p = GroupPointing(nif_beams(); by = :quad, fields = (:z, :r, :phi, :split))
beams = apply(p, parameters(p, "Q31T" => (z = 5.8, split = 0.5)))

by = :beam gives each beam its own aim (its offsets still in its quad's azimuth), and a per-beam table (an as-shot SST export, read_visrad_sst(path; template = nif_beams(quads))) keeps its beams' differences under one shift per quad with relative = true (below). unit = :beam measures each beam's :r and :phi in its own port azimuth instead, so a quad's four beams with the same r aim r sin Δφ apart (Δφ: a beam's azimuth from its quad's, up to 5.7° on NIF's 23.5° cone). A quad held as one beam, and a beam without a quad name, are the same either way, bit for bit.

relative = true makes the aim fields (:z, :r, :phi, :split, or the spherical ones) shifts added to each beam's own template aim instead of values the group's beams share, so a group moves rigidly and keeps its members' differences: re-pointing an as-shot table, whose beams within a quad aim at different points (a facility's per-beam offsets), by one shift per quad. Its initial parameters are then zero; the other fields stay absolute.

Briareus.PointingResult — Type
PointingResult

Result of optimize_pointing. Fields: x (the best parameters), beams (apply(scenario.param, x)), value and initial_value (the objective at x and at the clamped x0), iterations (L-BFGS-B iterations completed), evaluations (objective evaluations, each gradient counting one), reason (:converged, :iterations, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback), stopped (true for the last three, which return the best design evaluated so far), result (Optim's result, nothing when stopped), names (parameter_names of the parameterization, x[i] for one without a method), the box lower, upper, and at_bounds: the names of the parameters at a bound (within 1e-8 of the box width; parameters fixed by lower == upper excluded). Its display lists those parameters with their values and bounds.

Two more fields record what ended the run. constraints holds one ConstraintRow per constraint component at x (empty without constraints; the display shows a constraint of more than eight components, such as a keep-out per beam, as its worst row and a count by status), and outcome is one of

  • :interior: converged with every constraint met and no parameter at a bound;
  • :box: converged with every constraint met and parameters at a bound (the box is active: a wider box may lower the objective);
  • :infeasible_box: a constraint is still violated and parameters are at a bound (the box keeps the run from meeting it);
  • :infeasible: a constraint is still violated and no parameter is at a bound (a local point where the constraints cannot be met; another start may help);
  • :iterations, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback: the budget, the time limit or the callback ended the run first (its constraints, if any, are in constraints).

reason reads :converged for the first four. outer is the number of outer (multiplier) iterations of a constrained run, 0 without constraints; iterations and evaluations are then totals over them, and value and initial_value are the objective's own values, without the constraint terms.

state is what optimize_pointing(...; from = result) continues from: for a constrained run, a named tuple of the multipliers λ (normalized units, one per constraint component in the order of constraints), the penalty parameter μ and its ceiling μmax, the inner runs' tolerance tol, the last infeasibility–complementarity measure Vold and the working limits limits (an exact ceiling's lowered stand-in limit; see irradiance_ceiling); nothing without constraints. A run ended by its budget, time limit or callback keeps the state of the interrupted inner problem, so that the restart resumes it.

Bound sensitivities. For each parameter in at_bounds (same order), bound_gradient holds the objective's derivative ∂f/∂xᵢ at x and bound_sensitivity the Lagrangian's, ∂L/∂xᵢ with L = f + Σⱼ λⱼ gⱼ (the multipliers of constraints on the normalized violations; without constraints the two are equal). By the envelope theorem bound_sensitivity is the derivative of the optimal objective with respect to the bound's value, the active set held: positive at a lower bound and negative at an upper bound when the run has converged, so widening the box by δ in that parameter (lowering a lower bound, raising an upper one) lowers the optimum by about |bound_sensitivity| δ (to first order, in the objective's units per parameter unit). bound_gradient is what the objective alone would gain, before the constraints take their share. Both cost one gradient each after the run (not counted in evaluations), only when a parameter is at a bound; they are meaningful at convergence. Where the active constraints and bounds leave a flat family of designs (a drive objective under several active gates), the multipliers are not unique and the derivative can overstate what a wider box gains by an order of magnitude or more (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing"); a restart with from in the wider box measures it.

Phase 1 and relaxations (phase1 = true, relaxation = true). phase1 is nothing when phase 1 did not run, else a named tuple of its evaluations and iterations (included in the totals), feasible (it reached a design within the limits, where the main run started), verdict (it converged above feasibility: the run ended there, outer 0) and its design x. relaxations holds one Relaxation per constraint violated or active at a verdict's design, with relaxation = true (empty otherwise); their evaluations count in evaluations. The display lists them under the constraints.

Briareus.Relaxation — Type
Relaxation

How far one constraint must be relaxed, alone, for the others to hold: an entry of PointingResult.relaxations after a run that optimize_pointing found infeasible with relaxation = true. Fields: name (the constraint's), violation (the largest normalized violation of its components at a design that meets every other constraint: the relaxation in units of its scale, so a multiple 1 + violation of the limit where the scale is the limit, the default), limit and relaxed_limit (one per component, in the constraint's units: a ceiling raised and a floor lowered by violation × scale), upper, status, x (that design), constraints (every constraint's ConstraintRow at x) and evaluations. status is :relaxed (the others met at x), :others_conflict (the other constraints cannot all be met either, so relaxing this one alone does not suffice; violation is NaN) or :stopped (the budget, the time limit or the callback ended the search first; violation is the best design's, the others possibly unmet).

The relaxation is achievable (the design x meets the others with this one at relaxed_limit) and the least one only locally: the run that finds it minimizes a smooth stand-in for the largest violation (a 64-norm of the positive violations) from the infeasible design, a local search.

Briareus.Scenario — Type
Scenario(target, pointing::GroupPointing; observe = nothing,
         radiation = observe !== nothing, time = 0.0, albedo = nothing, lpi = nothing,
         deposition_kw = (;), viewfactor_kw = (;), model = nothing)
Scenario(target, beams; kwargs...)
Scenario(target, parameterization; kwargs...)

The parameterization (scenario.param) maps the parameter vectors of evaluate and optimize_pointing to beams. The first form builds the deposition operator for the template beams of pointing (a FramePointing too); the second is Scenario(target, GroupPointing(beams)) (fields :z, :r, :defocus per group). The third takes any other parameterization with methods of apply(parameterization, x) and initial_parameters(parameterization), and builds the operator for the beams at its initial parameters. The operator resolves the beams' footprints where those beams aim (reach, DepositionOperator).

Everything precomputed for fast repeated evaluation of a pointing design: the DepositionOperator, and — when radiation and observe (component names whose incident x-ray flux is the figure of merit, e.g. :capsule) are given — the view factors and the linear flux response of the observed elements. lpi is a laser–plasma model (Backscatter, CalibratedTransfer, CBET, or a tuple of them) applied at time between the setup beams and deposition.

The flux response is linear in the laser source at fixed albedos: the materials' at time (temperature-dependent ones at their reference temperatures, thin foils intact), or an explicit per-element albedo, such as the converged sol.albedo of a solution at the design point (e.g. a drive history's peak, windows burned through).

Objectives need only the moments of the observed flux, so the scenario keeps the response contracted with the Legendre (lmax = 12) and real-harmonic (lmax = 6) weights: per evaluation a 13 × n and a 49 × n product, and a 62-column solve at setup. The full observed × n response scenario.response (and an evaluation's flux) is computed when first read; on a large (iterative) model an evaluation's flux is one solve instead, until the response has been read.

The operators' own options pass through two named tuples:

  • deposition_kw to DepositionOperator: profile (a VisRadProfile: VisRad's beam model instead of the f-cone superposition), ncone (f-cone directions, 13), reach (the aim motion, in mm, that the operator stays accurate for, 2.0), resolution, level, maxlevel and shadow (the quadrature's refinement);
  • viewfactor_kw to RadiationModel and through it to view_factors: closed (the closure correction; by default the target's own declaration, which hohlraum makes), F (given view factors, e.g. those of a target that differs only in its materials), storage (Float64, Float32 or :hmatrix), solver, visibility (:centroid or :sampled), visibility_grid, level and near.

model, a RadiationModel of the target, is used instead of building one; it excludes viewfactor_kw (an ArgumentError).

remake(scenario; ...) replaces the laser–plasma model, the parameterization, the deposition options or the response's observed elements, time and albedos, and rebuilds only what depends on them.

Briareus.asymmetry_objective — Method
asymmetry_objective(; modes = (1 => 1.0, 2 => 1.0, 3 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0), components = (),
                    clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0, direct_names = nothing,
                    drive = nothing, drive_weight = 1.0, keepout = nothing,
                    reference = nothing, uncertainty = nothing)

Objective for three-dimensional drive symmetry. For each l => w in modes it adds w × relative_mode_power(c, l) — the rotation-invariant power of all 2l+1 real harmonics of degree l over the mean ((aₗ/a₀)²/(2l+1) for an axisymmetric field) — and for each (l, m) => w in components adds w × (c_lm/c_00)², so a particular azimuthal mode (e.g. the l = 1, m = ±1 drift of a dropped bundle) can be weighted separately. Penalties for clipping, missing and direct capsule illumination and the drive target and keepout are as in symmetry_objective, with drive compared to the mean flux c_00/√(4π).

With reference (a coefficient vector from flux_ylm, e.g. of a tuned design before a change to the target or the beams), the modes and components measure the departure of c_lm/c_00 from the reference's c_lm/c_00 instead of from zero, so the objective restores a known-good drive shape.

With uncertainty = (; pointing, power) the mode and component terms are their expected values over independent beam errors of those rms sizes (linearized; see mode_uncertainty and symmetry_objective): a robust design. Shared errors (group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning) are accepted as in mode_uncertainty.

Briareus.bounds — Function
bounds(p::GroupPointing, x0; z = 1.0, r = 1.0, phi = 1.0, split = 1.0, defocus = 2.0,
       power = 0.5, spot = 0.5, dlambda = 2.0, theta_s = 5.0, dphi_s = 10.0,
       split_theta = 5.0, split_phi = 5.0, power_min = 0.0, power_max = Inf)
       -> (lower, upper)

Box bounds around x0: ±z, ±r, ±phi, ±split, ±defocus mm, relative ±power, ±spot fractions, ±dlambda Å, and ±theta_s, ±dphi_s, ±split_theta, ±split_phi degrees (engineering defaults, not facility limits). Power bounds are further clipped to the absolute limits power_min ≤ P ≤ power_max (TW per beam, e.g. a facility's per-quad capability). A group at zero power in x0 (a dropped quad) gets ±power of the mean of the groups' nonzero powers, [0, power · P̄] before the clipping, so that it can come back; with every group at zero the upper bound is power_max, which must then be finite.

face_flux(ev) -> Vector

Incident x-ray flux H_e (TW/mm²) on each of the scenario's observed elements: the full linear flux response (scenario.response, computed when first read) times the absorbed laser power, generic in the number type (the optimizer's duals). Briareus.face_flux_objective — Method
face_flux_objective(; uniformity = 1.0, rms_scale = nothing, flux = nothing,
                    flux_weight = 1.0, clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0,
                    direct_names = nothing, keepout = nothing)

Objective for the x-ray drive of a flat observed face (a package's face in a halfraum, an opacity sample), which has no capsule modes: uniformity × (σ/σ₀)² with σ = rms_nonuniformity(H, A) the area-weighted RMS nonuniformity of the incident flux on the scenario's observed elements (face_nonuniformity, through the scenario's linear flux response, so differentiable), the shape term of FaceFlux for a uniform target, and σ₀ = rms_scale (nothing: 1, the absolute σ²). When flux (TW/mm²) is given, adds flux_weight × (H̄/flux - 1)² with H̄ the face_mean_flux. The clip, missed and direct penalties (direct: laser light on the face itself, or with direct_names on the named parts or components) and the keepout penalty are as in symmetry_objective.

rms_scale = face_nonuniformity(evaluate(scenario, x0)) makes the term 1 at the design x0 (times uniformity), so uniformity = 1 balances the shape against the penalties on any face. Without it the term is the absolute σ², which is as small as the face is uniform: on a uniform 1 mm sample (σ ~ 1e-4, σ² ~ 1e-8) the penalties (10 × a squared light fraction, ~1e-2) set the optimum, while a capsule's modes ((Pₗ/P₀)² ~ 1e-2 before tuning) are of their size. The scale is a number fixed at construction, so the objective stays a function of the beams alone (thread-safe, differentiable).

Briareus.influence_matrix — Method
influence_matrix(scenario, beams; lmax = 6) -> Matrix

Mode response of the observed flux to each beam: column b is the real-harmonic coefficient vector (mode_index order) produced by beam b alone at 1 TW, with the beam's pointing, spot and focus. Briareus.optimize_pointing — Method
optimize_pointing(scenario, objective; from = nothing,
                  x0 = initial_parameters(scenario.param),
                  lower = bounds(scenario.param, x0)[1], upper = bounds(scenario.param, x0)[2],
                  iterations = 200, evaluations = 20 * iterations, show_trace = false,
                  g_tol = 1e-8, callback = nothing, constraints = (),
                  feasibility = 1e-3, outer = 30, time_limit = Inf, phase1 = false,
                  relaxation = false) -> PointingResult

Minimize objective(evaluate(scenario, x)) within the box lower ≤ x ≤ upper by L-BFGS-B with forward-mode automatic-differentiation gradients, from x0 (clamped to the box). Returns a PointingResult, whose display lists the parameters that end at a bound.

The default box is bounds(scenario.param, x0) with its default widths: aims within ±1 mm of x0 (z, r, phi), defocus ±2 mm, power and spot scale ±50 %, Δλ ±2 Å and the spherical angles ±5–10° (engineering defaults, not facility limits). A parameter at its bound usually means the box, not the objective, set it: widen it with keywords of bounds, e.g. lower, upper = bounds(pointing, x0; z = 1.5, power = 0.4, power_max = 12.0), or pass any lower and upper vectors. A parameterization other than GroupPointing and FramePointing has no bounds method and needs lower and upper.

The run ends after iterations L-BFGS-B iterations or evaluations objective evaluations (each gradient counts one), whichever comes first: a line search may take many evaluations in one iteration, on a kinked objective (keep-out hinges, a coarse mesh) dozens, so the iteration limit alone does not bound the run time. time_limit (seconds of wall time, default none) ends the run at the first evaluation after it has passed. reason is :converged (projected gradient below g_tol, or no further progress), :iterations, :evaluations, :time_limit or :callback. On :evaluations, :time_limit and :callback the result is the best design evaluated so far, with stopped = true and result = nothing; iterations is the number of iterations completed in every case.

callback, if given, is called after every (non-derivative) objective evaluation with (; evaluations, iteration, value, best_value, best_x); returning true stops the optimization. Use it for progress reports and cancellation.

Constraints. constraints, a tuple or vector of Constraints (ceilings f(ev) ≤ limit and floors f(ev) ≥ limit, such as irradiance_ceiling and mode_gate), are handled as constraints rather than as penalty terms of the objective: by an augmented Lagrangian, whose inner problems are box-constrained L-BFGS-B runs on objective + Σ μ/2 [max(0, gᵢ + λᵢ/μ)² - (λᵢ/μ)²] (gᵢ the normalized violations, ±(value - limit)/scale), each started from the last, with the multipliers λ and the penalty μ updated between them (ALGENCAN's rules, Andreani et al., SIAM J. Optim. 18, 1286, 2008). The run converges when every violation is at most feasibility (in the constraints' scaled units: 1e-3 is 0.1 % of a limit by default), the multipliers are complementary to the same tolerance, and the last inner run has converged at the final tolerance: the inner runs' tolerance on the projected gradient tightens tenfold per outer iteration from 1e-3 to g_tol, and the run ends only once an inner run at g_tol has converged (its projected gradient below it, or its value changing by less than 1e-10 relative per iteration: a drive objective is flat along the limits), so a start that already meets the limits, as on a restart or a hand-off to a finer parameterization, is still optimized. A constraint that cannot be met ends the run once the penalty has grown by 10⁸ without progress (see phase1 below for a faster verdict). outer caps the outer iterations, iterations caps each inner run and evaluations is the budget of the whole run (the result counts both over all inner runs). The result's constraints and outcome report each constraint against its limit and whether the box, the constraints or the budget ended the run (PointingResult). The callback's named tuple then also carries outer, and its value is the inner problem's (the objective with the constraint terms). Without constraints the run is the plain L-BFGS-B above.

Infeasible constraints. phase1 = true first minimizes, from a start that violates a constraint, the infeasibility ½ Σ max(0, gᵢ)² over every component's normalized violation (the augmented Lagrangian's limit as its penalty grows), ignoring the objective, by the same L-BFGS-B. It stops at the first design where every violation is within feasibility, and the main run starts there. If it converges, or stalls (its least infeasibility falls by less than 1 % over 100 evaluations, an engineering default), with a violation above feasibility instead, the run ends there with outcome :infeasible (or :infeasible_box, parameters at a bound) and no outer iteration: that design is the least infeasible one the search reached from this start (a local verdict), and its constraints rows show each constraint's violation there. relaxation = true (which implies phase1) adds, after such a verdict, one constrained run per constraint violated or active there: the smallest largest violation of its components with every other constraint met, which is how far that constraint alone must be relaxed (Relaxation, in PointingResult.relaxations); these runs share the budget and the time limit. Both are off by default: with phase1, a feasible run starts its main run from another design and can end at another design of a flat family. A start that already meets every constraint, and a restart with from, skip phase 1. The callback's outer is 0 during phase 1. On the dropped-bundle test case made infeasible, phase 1 reached the verdict in 577 evaluations where the augmented Lagrangian had none after 2,195, and on feasible starts it cost 8 % fewer to 29 % more evaluations (research notes, "A verdict on conflicting constraints").

Restarts. from, a previous run's PointingResult, continues that run: x0, lower and upper default to its x and box, and a constrained run starts from its multipliers, penalty parameter, inner tolerance and working limits (result.state) instead of learning them again from zero. A run cut short by evaluations (or by the callback) and continued with from resumes the interrupted inner problem; the counts of the new result (iterations, evaluations, outer) are its own, and its initial_value is the objective at its start. The constraints must have the same components as the previous run's (a DimensionMismatch otherwise). An unconstrained from result only sets the start and the box.

Compared with penalty terms at fixed weights, the multipliers carry the constraints' weights from one inner run to the next: the limits are met without stiff weights (which stall the line search), without stages of loosened limits set by hand, and the design ends at its limits rather than where the penalty terms balance the objective (on the dropped-bundle tutorial, 0.35 % more drive in a comparable number of evaluations, 997 against the penalty form's 1,072: research notes, "Constrained re-pointing").

Briareus.parameters — Method
parameters(p::GroupPointing, beams::AbstractVector{<:Beam}) -> Vector{Float64}

Briareus.remake — Method
remake(scenario; parameterization, lpi, deposition_kw, observe, time, albedo, model)
    -> Scenario

Copy of scenario with the given parts replaced. What depends on a replaced part is rebuilt and everything else is shared:

  • lpi (a laser–plasma model or nothing): nothing is rebuilt.
  • parameterization: the deposition operator is kept when the new parameterization's beams (a GroupPointing's or FramePointing's template, another's beams at its initial parameters) enter through the operator's ports in the same order with the same f-cone directions (and, under a VisRadProfile, the same envelope ratios); otherwise the change is refused with an ArgumentError, unless deposition_kw is given too. The target, radiation and viewfactor_kw do not change through remake (an ArgumentError): they need a new Scenario, or a model built for the purpose.

Briareus.symmetry_objective — Method
symmetry_objective(; modes = (2 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0), odd = 1.0, lmax = 6,
                   clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0, direct_names = nothing,
                   drive = nothing, drive_weight = 1.0, center = nothing, keepout = nothing,
                   uncertainty = nothing)

Objective for indirect-drive symmetry: Σ wₗ (aₗ/a₀)² over modes, plus odd × the sum of squared odd moments (top/bottom imbalance), plus clip, missed and direct times the squared fractions of laser power clipped on the outside of the target, missing it, or hitting the observed elements directly (the scenario's observe, the capsule whose drive is measured), or with direct_names the named parts or components instead (a name that is not in the target is an error). The direct penalty only sees power already on the capsule; keep a margin with a SphereKeepOut.

This costs about three gradients per evaluation. The shared errors of mode_uncertainty (group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning) are accepted too; they add a fourth per-beam sensitivity (about four gradients per evaluation).

Briareus.Constraint — Type
Constraint(name, f; upper = nothing, lower = nothing, scale = nothing, labels = nothing)

An inequality constraint for optimize_pointing: f(ev) ≤ upper (a ceiling) or f(ev) ≥ lower (a floor), with f any function of an Evaluation (its beams ev.beams included), generic in the number type so that the optimizer's automatic-differentiation gradients pass through it. Give exactly one of upper and lower.

f may return a number or a vector; a vector constraint has one component per entry, each with its own multiplier and row in the report, against a limit that is a number (shared) or a vector of the same length. labels name the components in reports (default "1", "2", …): a collection of names, or a function of the evaluation that returns them, for a constraint whose components follow the evaluation (one per beam, as in clearance_floor). Within one run the number of components is fixed; a constraint with a shared limit and labels from the evaluation serves scenarios with different numbers of beams (a stage on quads, then one on their beams).

scale (a number or one per component, positive) sets the units of the violation: the optimizer works on ±(f(ev) - limit)/scale and its feasibility tolerance is in those units. By default scale = |limit|, so a violation is relative (1e-3: 0.1 % of the limit); a zero limit needs a scale.

Ready-made constraints: irradiance_ceiling, mode_gate, clearance_floor (a keep-out's clearance per beam), light_ceiling and light_floor (the laser light on named parts). Any other quantity of the beams alone is Constraint(:name, ev -> quantity(ev.beams); lower = minimum).

Briareus.clearance_floor — Method
clearance_floor(keepout; minimum = keepout.minimum, scale = 1.0, name = :clearance) -> Constraint

A floor on each beam's worst-case clearance (mm) from a keep-out: the Constraint keepout_clearance(keepout, ev) .≥ minimum, one component per beam of the evaluation, labeled by the beam's name. The keep-out is any with a keepout_clearance method and a minimum field: an ApertureKeepOut, a SphereKeepOut, a HardwareKeepOut. A beam the keep-out does not apply to (clearance Inf) gives a component that always holds; a clearance of -Inf or NaN throws an ArgumentError naming the keep-out and the beam, as in keepout_penalty. The keep-out's weight is not used: the constraint replaces its penalty (keepout_penalty), so pass the keep-out to the objective's keepout keyword or as a constraint, not both.

scale (mm) is the unit of the violation: with the default 1 mm and feasibility = 1e-3, a clearance within 1 µm of minimum is :active (an engineering default, finer than the keep-out's own accuracy against clearance, a few micrometers). Give each of several keep-outs its own name.

The floor is per beam, not on the smallest clearance over the beams: in the augmented Lagrangian each beam's component has its own multiplier, which stays zero for a beam clear of its minimum (its term then vanishes from the inner problem), and each term is as smooth as that beam's clearance, while the smallest clearance over the beams has a kink wherever the closest beam changes (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing"). The components follow the evaluation's beams, so the same constraint serves a stage on quads and a stage on their beams.

Briareus.irradiance_ceiling — Method
irradiance_ceiling(reference, names...; limit = peak_irradiance(reference, names...),
                   p = 64, weight = nothing, scale = limit, exact = true) -> Constraint

A ceiling on the absorbed laser irradiance over the elements of the named parts or components: a Constraint named :irradiance that holds the peak_irradiance at or below limit (by default; exact, below). Its function is the smooth stand-in the optimizer works on, the irradiance_norm scaled to equal the peak at the reference evaluation, ‖I‖ₚ × peak_ref/‖I‖ₚ,ref. With the default limit (the reference's peak) the ceiling holds the irradiance at the reference design's, the reference being, for example, the pointing before a bundle was dropped; a number in TW/mm² sets another, measured on the same scale. The reference evaluation must be on the same target as the optimized scenario (a subset of it shares the target), with the same laser–plasma model (lpi, compared field by field, so that a model built alike passes) at the same time; otherwise evaluating the ceiling throws an ArgumentError. A reference evaluated without the scenario's CBET model gives the peak and the stand-in's scale of another quantity, and the ceiling would hold the design to a limit it does not share, so the mismatch is an error rather than a warning: evaluate the reference in a scenario with the same model, e.g. evaluate(remake(reference_scenario; lpi = scenario.lpi), beams). weight selects and weighs the beams that count, as in peak_irradiance (the outer cones only, or picket energies for a fluence ceiling), for the reference and the design alike.

The ceiling is on an absolute irradiance: where the power can fall (power scales, PowerScales, or powers among the parameters) the optimizer can meet it by cutting power, at a loss of drive (on a re-pointing study with a power scale per cone at most 1, a large one). A drive_floor beside it holds the drive.

A ceiling on the wall's peak irradiance at the reference's value is an engineering limit, not a published criterion: the published scalings of the gold bubble's growth use the outer cones' picket fluence on the wall (research notes), which weight expresses on the same machinery.

With exact = true (the default) the ceiling holds the peak itself. The optimizer works on the norm, but once the constrained run has converged it compares the peak_irradiance with the limit; while the peak exceeds it by more than the run's feasibility (in units of scale), the norm's working limit is scaled by limit/peak and the run continues from the same design, multipliers and penalty. The reported value (check_constraints, PointingResult.constraints) is the peak against the limit, and a converged run ends with the peak at or below the limit within feasibility. The cost is the further outer iterations, when the peak ends above the limit (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing").

exact = false bounds the norm alone, and the norm is the reported value: at the limit the field's hot spots are as hot, in the p-norm mean, as the reference's, while the peak over the elements may exceed the limit, by a margin that depends on where the run ends: on the tutorial's mesh 0.1 % at p = 64 and 3 % at p = 16, and on the dropped-bundle test case (0.45 mm wall elements) 3 % at p = 64 for one of two designs that meet the same gates, under another floating-point rounding of the same run (research notes, "Constrained re-pointing").

Across meshes the ceiling is a ratio to the reference on the same mesh, which changes less with the element size than the peak's ratio does (rms difference 1.0 % against 1.3 % between 0.2 and 0.45 mm elements near the limit), while the peak itself falls on a coarser mesh.

Briareus.infer_errors — Method
infer_errors(scenario, beams, measured; sigma, pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0,
             group_pointing = 0.0, group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad,
             positioning = 0.0) -> NamedTuple

The most probable beam errors behind a measured drive-shape departure: the inverse of mode_uncertainty, which takes the same error priors. A measured low mode that trends from shot to shot (MacGowan et al., HEDP 2021, trend mode 1 of NIF implosions against beam and target data) raises the question of which errors could have caused it; this answers it for one shot, linearly.

measured holds the departures Δc = (c_lm/c_00)_measured − (c_lm/c_00)_nominal as pairs: (l, m) => Δc for a component, l => [Δc_l,-l, …, Δc_l,l] for a whole degree (mode_index order). sigma is the measurement's rms error, one number or one per component (in the order measured expands to). The nominal shape is the scenario's for beams; components through l = 6 (the cached $Y_{lm}$ weights).

Model: Δc = J x + ε, with x the errors (zero-mean normal, the variances of mode_uncertainty: per beam its aim along the spot axes a, b and its relative power; per group a shared displacement and power; the target positioning) and J their exact sensitivities (mode_sensitivity, with the shared columns). The posterior mean, which is also the most probable x, and its covariance come from the data-space (Woodbury) form, a solve the size of the measurement:

x̂ = Σₚ Jᵀ (J Σₚ Jᵀ + Σₘ)⁻¹ Δc,   Σ = Σₚ − Σₚ Jᵀ (J Σₚ Jᵀ + Σₘ)⁻¹ J Σₚ.

Returns (; beam, group, positioning, x, sigma, prior_sigma, covariance, names, components, measured, predicted, residual, sources, resolved, beams, J, c):

  • beam: nb × 3, each beam's inferred a, b displacement (mm) and relative power;
  • group: a Dict from group key to (; displacement, power) (a Vec3 in mm);
  • positioning: the inferred common displacement of every aim relative to the target (the target sits at minus this from where the beams are aimed);
  • x, sigma, prior_sigma, covariance, names: all errors as one vector (beams' a, b, power, then the groups', then x, y, z), their posterior and prior rms, the posterior covariance, and labels;
  • predicted = J x̂ and residual = Δc − J x̂, over components (the (l, m) list);
  • sources: per error source (a beam's pointing, a beam's power, a group's pointing or power, the positioning), the fraction of Δc reproduced by its share of J x̂, dot(J_s x̂_s, Δc)/|Δc|², sorted descending (the fractions sum to dot(predicted, Δc)/|Δc|²): the few sources that best explain the measurement;
  • resolved: Σⱼ (1 − Σⱼⱼ/σⱼ²), the number of error combinations the data determine (at most the number of components);
  • beams: beams with x̂ applied, for a full evaluate (the nonlinear check; the errors are applied before a laser–plasma model, which the sensitivities follow).

The name reflects what comes back: errors, under a prior, not a unique explanation. Many errors map to a few modes, so a handful of combinations are determined and every other direction keeps its prior: sigma ./ prior_sigma stays near 1 for most errors, and resolved counts what was learned. What the inverse reports is only as good as the prior: an error left out of it (group_pointing = 0, say) cannot be blamed, and its effect is attributed to the sources that are allowed. Linear in the errors. infer_velocity_errors is the same inverse for a measured hot-spot velocity.

Briareus.mode_sensitivity — Method
mode_sensitivity(scenario, beams; lmax = 4) -> NamedTuple

Sensitivity of the observed flux's real spherical-harmonic shape c_lm/c_00 (mode_index order, through lmax ≤ 6) to each beam's own errors: a pointing displacement of its aim transverse to the beam (mm, along the spot's a and b axes) and a relative power error. Returns (; J, c, names): J is (lmax+1)² × 3nb, column 3(b-1) + k the derivative with respect to error k of beam b (k = 1, 2 the two transverse directions, 3 the relative power), c the nominal c_lm/c_00, and names the column labels.

Each beam's deposition depends on its own errors only, so the derivatives come from one forward-mode pass per beam with three dual partials: exact (to round-off), and about the cost of one gradient of an objective. The beams are the scenario's setup beams; a laser–plasma model is applied before the errors (the errors are in the delivered beams). Errors move the aim; the beam direction (the port) is fixed.

Briareus.mode_uncertainty — Method
mode_uncertainty(scenario, beams; pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0, lmax = 4,
                 group_pointing = 0.0, group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad,
                 positioning = 0.0) -> NamedTuple

Expected spread of the observed drive shape from beam errors, by linear propagation through exact per-beam sensitivities (mode_sensitivity).

Independent beam-to-beam errors: each beam's aim is displaced at random transverse to the beam with rms pointing mm (the rms of the displacement's length, pointing/√2 along each axis; NIF specifies 50 µm), and its power by an rms fraction power.

Correlated errors (zero by default):

  • group_pointing: every beam of a group (groups: a Beam field such as :quad, or a function of the beam, e.g. its bundle, or its cone in one hemisphere, b -> (b.group, b.port[3] > 0)) is displaced by one shared vector transverse to the group's mean beam direction, rms length group_pointing mm. Beams at an angle to that mean also move along themselves (their focus moves with the aim). A group whose beams have no common direction (:group, a cone over both hemispheres) is refused.
  • group_power: one shared relative power error per group, rms group_power.
  • positioning: the target is displaced from chamber center, which moves every aim by the same vector relative to the target: rms length positioning mm, isotropic, or a 3-tuple of rms values along x, y, z.

Each source adds to the covariance independently. Returns (; c, sigma, covariance, rms_mode, rms_by, J): the nominal c_lm/c_00, the rms of each coefficient, their covariance, and for each degree l = 1:lmax the rms of the mode's amplitude over the errors, √E[Σ_m (δc_lm/c_00)²] (the expected mode-l budget the errors use: compare mode_amplitude, which is √(2l+1) times the nominal one). rms_by splits rms_mode by source (beam, group, positioning; they add in quadrature), and J is mode_sensitivity's. Linear in the errors: check large errors with sample_modes, which takes the same keywords.

Briareus.HeldParameters — Type
HeldParameters(p, held; values = initial_parameters(p))

The parameterization p with the parameters held fixed at values and the rest free: its parameter vector holds the free parameters of p, in their order, and apply(h, y) is apply(p, x) with x = values except at the free indices, where x = y. held selects parameters of p by

  • indices (a vector of integers) or a mask (a Bool per parameter);
  • names (a vector of parameter_names(p) entries, e.g. "Q31B.z");
  • a predicate on the name, e.g. n -> startswith(n, "inner") for the inner cones of a GroupPointing grouped by cone, or n -> first(split(n, '.')) in inner_quads for a set of quad names when grouped by quad.

An unknown name is an ArgumentError. p may itself be a composition, such as a PowerScales; p needs a parameter_names method to select by name.

The full parameterization interface follows p: initial_parameters are values at the free indices, parameter_names and nparams those of the free parameters, and bounds(h, y0; kwargs...) the box of p about the held values and y0 (the keywords of p's bounds), restricted to the free parameters. apply is generic in the number type: the held values enter as constants of the element type of y, so ForwardDiff duals flow through the free parameters only. Named values pass through to p (parameters), and a subset of a scenario keeps the kept parameters' names, values and holds.

pointing = GroupPointing(quads; by = :quad, fields = (:z, :phi), relative = true)
inner = Set(b.quad for b in quads if b.group in (:inner23, :inner30))
outers_only = HeldParameters(pointing, n -> first(split(n, '.')) in inner)

Briareus.PowerScales — Type
PowerScales(p; by = :group)

The parameterization p with a power scale per group of beams after its own parameters: the parameter vector is [x; s], and apply(ps, [x; s]) is apply(p, x) with each beam's power multiplied by the scale s of its group. Groups are keyed by by on the beams of p at its initial parameters: :group (e.g. the cone), :quad, :beam, or a function beam -> key, such as two keys for the inner and the outer cones,

cones = PowerScales(pointing; by = b -> b.group in (:inner23, :inner30) ? :inner : :outer)

which varies the cone fraction (and the total power) beside the pointing. The scales start at 1 and are named "<key>.power_scale". They multiply whatever power p gives, a :power field of p included, so the two are redundant together; holding one of them (HeldParameters) removes the redundancy.

bounds(ps, y0; scale = 0.5, scale_min = 0.0, scale_max = Inf, kwargs...) puts each scale within y0 (1 ± scale), clipped to [scale_min, scale_max] (engineering defaults, not facility limits; scale_max = 1 holds each group at or below the power of p), and the other parameters in p's box with kwargs. apply is generic in the number type. A scale is set by name as the field power_scale of its key (parameters), and a subset of a scenario keeps the scales of the groups that keep a beam.

Briareus.PointingSummary — Type
PointingSummary

A pointing (e.g. the beams of a re-pointing result) against reference beams, from pointing_summary. Fields:

  • nbeams; shift_mean and shift_max (mm): the mean and largest aim shift |aim - reference aim| over the beams, matched by name;
  • power (TW): the total setup power, and cone_fraction: the inner groups' share of it;
  • clearance and reference_clearance (mm): per keep-out, the least clearance of the beams and of the reference beams, leaving out +Inf (a beam the keep-out does not apply to; Inf when every beam is such) but keeping -Inf (a beam the rule cannot place, e.g. a CaptureKeepIn beam whose axis misses every capture part) and NaN, which then are the least and show as such; and margin (mm): the clearance less the keep-out's minimum (negative: inside the keep-out);
  • clipped and missed: clipped_fraction and missed_fraction of the beams in the scenario;
  • with a drive_reference: drive_ratio, the capsule drive's mean over the reference's (c₀₀/r₀₀ of the flux_ylm coefficients), and departure, the mode_departure per degree l = 1:lmax from the reference's shape; NaN and empty without one.

Its display is one line, suited to a log of a study's runs.
