Optimization

GroupPointing maps parameters to beams, per cone, quad or beam: axial, radial and tangential aim, defocus, power, spot scale and Δλ, with top–bottom mirroring; parameters builds its parameter vector from values named by group and field, and Scenario precomputes everything an evaluation needs. symmetry_objective and asymmetry_objective target capsule drive symmetry (axisymmetric and three-dimensional), and irradiance_objective foil uniformity. optimize_pointing runs bounded L-BFGS-B on forward-mode automatic-differentiation gradients, with a callback for progress and cancellation. influence_matrix gives each beam's mode response.

mode_sensitivity gives the exact sensitivity of the drive shape to each beam's pointing and power errors, and mode_uncertainty the asymmetry independent errors leave by linear propagation (sample_modes is the Monte Carlo check). Errors can be shared by a group of beams or be the target's positioning. The objectives' uncertainty keyword optimizes the expected symmetry over them, and infer_errors finds the most probable errors behind a measured asymmetry.

Briareus.Evaluation — Type
Evaluation

Result of evaluate: the setup beams, the effective beams after the scenario's laser–plasma model, their deposition, and flux — incident x-ray flux on the scenario's observed elements (TW/mm²; nothing without radiation). Deposition fractions are relative to effective (post-LPI) power. The flux is computed when first read; flux_moments and flux_ylm do not need it.

Briareus.GroupPointing — Type
GroupPointing(beams; by = :group, fields = (:z, :r, :defocus), mirror = true,
              relative = false, radius = nothing, center = (0, 0, 0))

Maps a parameter vector to beams. Beams are grouped by by (:group — e.g. cone —, :quad, :beam, or a function beam -> key); each group gets one value per field:

  • :z — axial aim coordinate (mm). With mirror, beams from the lower hemisphere (port z < 0) aim at -z, so one value describes a top/bottom-symmetric cone.
  • :r — radial aim offset (mm) toward the beam's own port azimuth.
  • :phi — tangential aim offset (mm), counterclockwise about z.
  • :defocus — focal-plane offset along the beam (mm, see Beam).
  • :power — power per beam (TW).
  • :spot — multiplier on the best-focus spot semi-axes.
  • :dlambda — wavelength detuning (Å), used by laser–plasma models.

Fields not listed keep each beam's template value.

Pointing on a sphere (polar direct drive). With radius = R (mm) the aim can instead be a point on the sphere of radius R about center, the target surface on which direct-drive pointings are published (Barlow et al., PRL 133, 175101, 2024, Table I; Yeamans et al., Nucl. Fusion 61, 046031, 2021, Table 1; Hohenberger et al., PoP 22, 056308, 2015, Table I):

  • :theta_s — polar angle of the aim point (degrees from +z). With mirror, beams from the lower hemisphere aim at 180° - theta_s, so one value describes a top/bottom-symmetric cone pair and a larger value moves both toward the equator.
  • :dphi_s — azimuth of the aim point relative to the beam's own port azimuth (degrees, counterclockwise about z; the same for both hemispheres).
  • :split_theta, :split_phi — split of a quad's beams (degrees): each beam's aim moves by ±split_theta in polar angle and ±split_phi in azimuth, with the sign of its own port's offset from its quad's mean port (beams at a larger polar angle than their quad's, counted from the beam's own pole when mirrored, take +split_theta; beams counterclockwise of it +split_phi), so positive values open a quad's aims in the sense of its ports. This is the split-quad pointing of Cok et al. (PoP 15, 082705, 2008) and Yeamans et al. 2021 (left/right Δφ) as angles on the sphere. A quad given as one beam, or a beam level with its quad's mean, gets no split. Needs quad names; other per-beam aims come from by (e.g. one group per sub-ring).

The aim is the surface point itself, aim = center + R * port_vector(θ, φ), which the axis meets first when it faces the port (Beam). Best focus is then at aim + defocus * k: defocus is measured from the surface aim point, as VisRad measures the focus offset from its reference point (research notes, "Pointing and focus conventions"). Each template beam's angles are those of the first point where its axis meets the sphere (an ArgumentError if it misses); listed spherical values are shared by a group's members, and an angle that no listed field governs keeps each beam's own value. The aim moves to the surface along the template's axis, so the focus moves with it unless the template aim was already on the sphere. The spherical fields and the hohlraum fields :z, :r, :phi exclude each other.

relative = true makes the aim fields (:z, :r, :phi, or the spherical ones) shifts added to each beam's own template aim instead of values the group's beams share, so a group moves rigidly and keeps its members' differences: re-pointing an as-shot table, whose beams within a quad aim at different points (a facility's per-beam offsets), by one shift per quad. Its initial parameters are then zero; the other fields stay absolute.

Briareus.PointingResult — Type
PointingResult

Result of optimize_pointing. Fields: x (the best parameters), beams (apply(scenario.param, x)), value and initial_value (the objective at x and at the clamped x0), iterations (L-BFGS-B iterations completed), evaluations (objective evaluations, each gradient counting one), reason (:converged, :iterations, :evaluations or :callback), stopped (true for the last two, which return the best design evaluated so far), result (Optim's result, nothing when stopped), names (parameter_names of the parameterization, x[i] for one without a method), the box lower, upper, and at_bounds: the names of the parameters at a bound (within 1e-8 of the box width; parameters fixed by lower == upper excluded). Its display lists those parameters with their values and bounds.

Briareus.Scenario — Type
Scenario(target, pointing::GroupPointing; observe = nothing,
         radiation = observe !== nothing, time = 0.0, albedo = nothing, lpi = nothing,
         deposition_kw = (;), viewfactor_kw = (;), model = nothing)
Scenario(target, beams; kwargs...)
Scenario(target, parameterization; kwargs...)

The parameterization (scenario.param) maps the parameter vectors of evaluate and optimize_pointing to beams. The first form builds the deposition operator for the template beams of pointing; the second is Scenario(target, GroupPointing(beams)) (fields :z, :r, :defocus per group). The third takes any other parameterization with methods of apply(parameterization, x) and initial_parameters(parameterization), and builds the operator for the beams at its initial parameters. The operator resolves the beams' footprints where those beams aim (reach, DepositionOperator).

Everything precomputed for fast repeated evaluation of a pointing design: the DepositionOperator, and — when radiation and observe (component names whose incident x-ray flux is the figure of merit, e.g. :capsule) are given — the view factors and the linear flux response of the observed elements. lpi is a laser–plasma model (Backscatter, CalibratedTransfer, CBET, or a tuple of them) applied at time between the setup beams and deposition.

The flux response is linear in the laser source at fixed albedos: the materials' at time (temperature-dependent ones at their reference temperatures, thin foils intact), or an explicit per-element albedo, such as the converged sol.albedo of a solution at the design point (e.g. a drive history's peak, windows burned through).

Objectives need only the moments of the observed flux, so the scenario keeps the response contracted with the Legendre (lmax = 12) and real-harmonic (lmax = 6) weights: per evaluation a 13 × n and a 49 × n product, and a 62-column solve at setup. The full observed × n response scenario.response (and an evaluation's flux) is computed when first read; on a large (iterative) model an evaluation's flux is one solve instead, until the response has been read.

The operators' own options pass through two named tuples:

  • deposition_kw to DepositionOperator: profile (a VisRadProfile: VisRad's beam model instead of the f-cone superposition), ncone (f-cone directions, 13), reach (the aim motion, in mm, that the operator stays accurate for, 2.0), resolution, level, maxlevel and shadow (the quadrature's refinement);
  • viewfactor_kw to RadiationModel and through it to view_factors: closed (the closure correction; by default the target's own declaration, which hohlraum makes), F (given view factors, e.g. those of a target that differs only in its materials), storage (Float64, Float32 or :hmatrix), solver, visibility (:centroid or :sampled), visibility_grid, level and near.

model, a RadiationModel of the target, is used instead of building one; it excludes viewfactor_kw (an ArgumentError).

Briareus.asymmetry_objective — Method
asymmetry_objective(; modes = (1 => 1.0, 2 => 1.0, 3 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0), components = (),
                    clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0, direct_names = nothing,
                    drive = nothing, drive_weight = 1.0, keepout = nothing,
                    reference = nothing, uncertainty = nothing)

Objective for three-dimensional drive symmetry. For each l => w in modes it adds w × relative_mode_power(c, l) — the rotation-invariant power of all 2l+1 real harmonics of degree l over the mean ((aₗ/a₀)²/(2l+1) for an axisymmetric field) — and for each (l, m) => w in components adds w × (c_lm/c_00)², so a particular azimuthal mode (e.g. the l = 1, m = ±1 drift of a dropped bundle) can be weighted separately. Penalties for clipping, missing and direct capsule illumination and the drive target and keepout are as in symmetry_objective, with drive compared to the mean flux c_00/√(4π).

With reference (a coefficient vector from flux_ylm, e.g. of a tuned design before a change to the target or the beams), the modes and components measure the departure of c_lm/c_00 from the reference's c_lm/c_00 instead of from zero, so the objective restores a known-good drive shape.

With uncertainty = (; pointing, power) the mode and component terms are their expected values over independent beam errors of those rms sizes (linearized; see mode_uncertainty and symmetry_objective): a robust design. Shared errors (group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning) are accepted as in mode_uncertainty.

Briareus.influence_matrix — Method
influence_matrix(scenario, beams; lmax = 6) -> Matrix

Mode response of the observed flux to each beam: column b is the real-harmonic coefficient vector (mode_index order) produced by beam b alone at 1 TW, with the beam's pointing, spot and focus. Without a laser–plasma model the flux is linear in beam powers, so the coefficients of any power assignment P are M * P — the basis of power-rebalancing solves and of tables of what losing each beam costs. beams are the effective beams (after apply_lpi); a laser–plasma model that deposits light itself (the ridge stop of BubbleAbsorption, linear in each beam's power) is included through lpi_deposit at the scenario's time.

Briareus.optimize_pointing — Method
optimize_pointing(scenario, objective; x0 = initial_parameters(scenario.param),
                  lower = bounds(scenario.param, x0)[1], upper = bounds(scenario.param, x0)[2],
                  iterations = 200, evaluations = 20 * iterations, show_trace = false,
                  g_tol = 1e-8, callback = nothing) -> PointingResult

Minimize objective(evaluate(scenario, x)) within the box lower ≤ x ≤ upper by L-BFGS-B with forward-mode automatic-differentiation gradients, from x0 (clamped to the box). Returns a PointingResult, whose display lists the parameters that end at a bound.

The default box is bounds(scenario.param, x0) with its default widths: aims within ±1 mm of x0 (z, r, phi), defocus ±2 mm, power and spot scale ±50 %, Δλ ±2 Å and the spherical angles ±5–10° (engineering defaults, not facility limits). A parameter at its bound usually means the box, not the objective, set it: widen it with keywords of bounds, e.g. lower, upper = bounds(pointing, x0; z = 1.5, power = 0.4, power_max = 12.0), or pass any lower and upper vectors. A parameterization other than GroupPointing has no bounds method and needs lower and upper.

The run ends after iterations L-BFGS-B iterations or evaluations objective evaluations (each gradient counts one), whichever comes first: a line search may take many evaluations in one iteration, on a kinked objective (keep-out hinges, a coarse mesh) dozens, so the iteration limit alone does not bound the run time. reason is :converged (projected gradient below g_tol, or no further progress), :iterations, :evaluations or :callback. On :evaluations and :callback the result is the best design evaluated so far, with stopped = true and result = nothing; iterations is the number of iterations completed in every case.

callback, if given, is called after every (non-derivative) objective evaluation with (; evaluations, iteration, value, best_value, best_x); returning true stops the optimization. Use it for progress reports and cancellation.

Briareus.report — Method
report([io = stdout,] ev; lmax = 6, minor = 1e-3) -> nothing

Print a power accounting and flux-moment summary of an evaluation: the setup and effective power per group when the scenario has a laser–plasma model; the delivered laser power and its split over the parts (component_power), with the parts below minor × the delivered power summed on one line; the residual, if not below that fraction; and the observed flux's mean (mean_flux), radiation temperature and Legendre moments Pₗ/P₀ up to lmax.

The residual is the delivered power minus the power absorbed and clipped: light that strikes no surface plus the deposition quadrature's error (DepositionOperator conserves each beam's power to 1e-4 when nothing clips). It prints as missed when positive and as quadrature excess when negative (the quadrature counts more power than the beams deliver).

Briareus.symmetry_objective — Method
symmetry_objective(; modes = (2 => 1.0, 4 => 1.0), odd = 1.0, lmax = 6,
                   clip = 10.0, missed = 10.0, direct = 10.0, direct_names = nothing,
                   drive = nothing, drive_weight = 1.0, center = nothing, keepout = nothing,
                   uncertainty = nothing)

Objective for indirect-drive symmetry: Σ wₗ (aₗ/a₀)² over modes, plus odd × the sum of squared odd moments (top/bottom imbalance), plus clip, missed and direct times the squared fractions of laser power clipped on the outside of the target, missing it, or hitting the observed elements directly (the scenario's observe, the capsule whose drive is measured), or with direct_names the named parts or components instead (a name that is not in the target is an error). When drive (TW/mm²) is given, adds drive_weight × (a₀/drive - 1)², with a₀ the mean_flux: drive = mean_flux(evaluate(scenario, x0)) holds the drive of the design x0. A keepout (an ApertureKeepOut for the entrance-hole rims, a SphereKeepOut for the capsule, or a tuple of them) adds its penalty on the beams' worst-case clearance. The direct penalty only sees power already on the capsule; keep a margin with a SphereKeepOut.

With uncertainty = (; pointing, power) (rms beam-to-beam pointing in mm and relative power, as in mode_uncertainty) the mode terms are their expected values over those errors, linearized: each (aₗ/a₀)² gains the variance the errors give it, so the optimum also makes the drive shape insensitive to them (robust design). This costs about three gradients per evaluation. The shared errors of mode_uncertainty (group_pointing, group_power, groups, positioning) are accepted too; they add a fourth per-beam sensitivity (about four gradients per evaluation).

Briareus.infer_errors — Method
infer_errors(scenario, beams, measured; sigma, pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0,
             group_pointing = 0.0, group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad,
             positioning = 0.0) -> NamedTuple

The most probable beam errors behind a measured drive-shape departure: the inverse of mode_uncertainty, which takes the same error priors. A measured low mode that trends from shot to shot (MacGowan et al., HEDP 2021, trend mode 1 of NIF implosions against beam and target data) raises the question of which errors could have caused it; this answers it for one shot, linearly.

measured holds the departures Δc = (c_lm/c_00)_measured − (c_lm/c_00)_nominal as pairs: (l, m) => Δc for a component, l => [Δc_l,-l, …, Δc_l,l] for a whole degree (mode_index order). sigma is the measurement's rms error, one number or one per component (in the order measured expands to). The nominal shape is the scenario's for beams; components through l = 6 (the cached $Y_{lm}$ weights).

Model: Δc = J x + ε, with x the errors (zero-mean normal, the variances of mode_uncertainty: per beam its aim along the spot axes a, b and its relative power; per group a shared displacement and power; the target positioning) and J their exact sensitivities (mode_sensitivity, with the shared columns). The posterior mean, which is also the most probable x, and its covariance come from the data-space (Woodbury) form, a solve the size of the measurement:

x̂ = Σₚ Jᵀ (J Σₚ Jᵀ + Σₘ)⁻¹ Δc,   Σ = Σₚ − Σₚ Jᵀ (J Σₚ Jᵀ + Σₘ)⁻¹ J Σₚ.

Returns (; beam, group, positioning, x, sigma, prior_sigma, covariance, names, components, measured, predicted, residual, sources, resolved, beams, J, c):

  • beam: nb × 3, each beam's inferred a, b displacement (mm) and relative power;
  • group: a Dict from group key to (; displacement, power) (a Vec3 in mm);
  • positioning: the inferred common displacement of every aim relative to the target (the target sits at minus this from where the beams are aimed);
  • x, sigma, prior_sigma, covariance, names: all errors as one vector (beams' a, b, power, then the groups', then x, y, z), their posterior and prior rms, the posterior covariance, and labels;
  • predicted = J x̂ and residual = Δc − J x̂, over components (the (l, m) list);
  • sources: per error source (a beam's pointing, a beam's power, a group's pointing or power, the positioning), the fraction of Δc reproduced by its share of J x̂, dot(J_s x̂_s, Δc)/|Δc|², sorted descending (the fractions sum to dot(predicted, Δc)/|Δc|²): the few sources that best explain the measurement;
  • resolved: Σⱼ (1 − Σⱼⱼ/σⱼ²), the number of error combinations the data determine (at most the number of components);
  • beams: beams with x̂ applied, for a full evaluate (the nonlinear check; the errors are applied before a laser–plasma model, which the sensitivities follow).

The name reflects what comes back: errors, under a prior, not a unique explanation. Many errors map to a few modes, so a handful of combinations are determined and every other direction keeps its prior: sigma ./ prior_sigma stays near 1 for most errors, and resolved counts what was learned. What the inverse reports is only as good as the prior: an error left out of it (group_pointing = 0, say) cannot be blamed, and its effect is attributed to the sources that are allowed. Linear in the errors. infer_velocity_errors is the same inverse for a measured hot-spot velocity.

Briareus.mode_sensitivity — Method
mode_sensitivity(scenario, beams; lmax = 4) -> NamedTuple

Sensitivity of the observed flux's real spherical-harmonic shape c_lm/c_00 (mode_index order, through lmax ≤ 6) to each beam's own errors: a pointing displacement of its aim transverse to the beam (mm, along the spot's a and b axes) and a relative power error. Returns (; J, c, names): J is (lmax+1)² × 3nb, column 3(b-1) + k the derivative with respect to error k of beam b (k = 1, 2 the two transverse directions, 3 the relative power), c the nominal c_lm/c_00, and names the column labels.

Each beam's deposition depends on its own errors only, so the derivatives come from one forward-mode pass per beam with three dual partials: exact (to round-off), and about the cost of one gradient of an objective. The beams are the scenario's setup beams; a laser–plasma model is applied before the errors (the errors are in the delivered beams). Errors move the aim; the beam direction (the port) is fixed.

Briareus.mode_uncertainty — Method
mode_uncertainty(scenario, beams; pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0, lmax = 4,
                 group_pointing = 0.0, group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad,
                 positioning = 0.0) -> NamedTuple

Expected spread of the observed drive shape from beam errors, by linear propagation through exact per-beam sensitivities (mode_sensitivity).

Independent beam-to-beam errors: each beam's aim is displaced at random transverse to the beam with rms pointing mm (the rms of the displacement's length, pointing/√2 along each axis; NIF specifies 50 µm), and its power by an rms fraction power.

Correlated errors (zero by default):

  • group_pointing: every beam of a group (groups: a Beam field such as :quad, or a function of the beam, e.g. its bundle, or its cone in one hemisphere, b -> (b.group, b.port[3] > 0)) is displaced by one shared vector transverse to the group's mean beam direction, rms length group_pointing mm. Beams at an angle to that mean also move along themselves (their focus moves with the aim). A group whose beams have no common direction (:group, a cone over both hemispheres) is refused.
  • group_power: one shared relative power error per group, rms group_power.
  • positioning: the target is displaced from chamber center, which moves every aim by the same vector relative to the target: rms length positioning mm, isotropic, or a 3-tuple of rms values along x, y, z.

Each source adds to the covariance independently. Returns (; c, sigma, covariance, rms_mode, rms_by, J): the nominal c_lm/c_00, the rms of each coefficient, their covariance, and for each degree l = 1:lmax the rms of the mode's amplitude over the errors, √E[Σ_m (δc_lm/c_00)²] (the expected mode-l budget the errors use: compare mode_amplitude, which is √(2l+1) times the nominal one). rms_by splits rms_mode by source (beam, group, positioning; they add in quadrature), and J is mode_sensitivity's. Linear in the errors: check large errors with sample_modes, which takes the same keywords.

Briareus.sample_modes — Method
sample_modes(scenario, beams; pointing = 0.05, power = 0.0, n = 100,
             rng = Random.default_rng(), lmax = 4, group_pointing = 0.0,
             group_power = 0.0, groups = :quad, positioning = 0.0) -> Matrix

Monte Carlo of the drive shape under the errors of mode_uncertainty (same keywords): n samples of c_lm/c_00 (columns), each from a full evaluation with every beam's aim displaced along its spot axes by normal errors of rms pointing/√2 each and its power by a normal fraction of rms power, plus the shared group and target-positioning errors. The independent check of the linear propagation, and the answer when errors are large enough for the drive to respond nonlinearly.
