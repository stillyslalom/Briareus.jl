Shot review

Every check is judged on the worst case over pointing, target positioning, hardware tolerance and focus errors, never on the nominal beams. clearance gives the worst-case beam-to-hardware clearance, power and intensity, with intensity-contour envelopes, clearance-only spot wings (nif_wings) and pass, review and fail tiers; its thresholds and wing models are engineering defaults, which the review requirements and measured far fields of a facility should replace. talis_check applies NIF's published target-configuration rules (3ω capture and incidence, 3ω and 1ω clearance, unconverted-light management, the TAS stay-in zone), and unconverted_light_check covers the 1ω and 2ω footprints and the 32 mm zone, and unconverted_deposition puts their power on the target (a uniform square beam per beamlet and color, with shadowing; the unconverted fraction is an input). unconverted_history follows that light through the pulses, with a fraction and a prepulse that may vary in time, and irradiance_history the 3ω light; specular_check and preplasma_check compare them with the NIF User Guide's 10⁸ W/cm² plasma-onset guideline for metals. ApertureKeepOut puts the worst-case clearance of each beam from its entrance hole into an optimization objective, SphereKeepOut its clearance from the capsule and HardwareKeepOut its clearance from named hardware; each clearance is also a separate quantity (keepout_clearance) for a hand-written objective or constraint, as are the fractions of the laser power on named parts (clipped_fraction beside power_fraction). The TaLIS rules that carry a margin are such quantities too, each checked against talis_check: the 3ω clearance is a HardwareKeepOut with the rule tolerance, CaptureKeepIn gives the 3ω capture margin (capture_margin), IncidenceRule the 3ω incidence (incidence_angle) and Omega1KeepOut the 1ω clearance (omega1_clearance). backlighter_foil and line_of_sight place a foil on a diagnostic line of sight and check it against shields and beams, and backlighter_signal follows the deposition through a He-α conversion efficiency fitted to published foil data (kshell_ce) to the photons at the detector.

Briareus.DEFAULT_CLEARANCE_THRESHOLDS — Constant
DEFAULT_CLEARANCE_THRESHOLDS

The status tiers of clearance. They are engineering defaults, and no facility criterion sets them:

  • :fail above 1e12 W/cm², for plasma formation, or above 1e-3 of the beam power on a component;
  • :review above 1e9 W/cm², for the onset of ablation and debris, or above 1e-5.

Briareus.BeamEnvelope — Type
BeamEnvelope(beam; intensity, peak_fraction, enclosed, margin = 0.0)

A geometric beam envelope. At axial distance s from best focus, the envelope is the best-focus contour ellipse (see envelope_radius) grown by the f-cone radius |s|/(2F) and a uniform margin (mm). In geometric optics with a phase plate, every lens point fills the whole spot. The intensity at a point is then the mean of the focal intensity over a disk of radius |s|/(2F), so outside the envelope it is below the contour level at every s. Clearance is the signed distance to the envelope; negative means inside.

ClearanceReport

The result of clearance. Briareus.ClearanceRow — Type
ClearanceRow

One pair of a beam and a hardware component. "Worst" values are taken over the error set of pointing, target offset, hardware tolerance and focus:

  • clearance — worst-case signed distance to the beam envelope (mm), negative for overlap
  • nominal — the same at nominal pointing and focus
  • point, s — hardware point of minimum clearance and its axial position from best focus (mm)
  • power, intercepted — worst-case power (TW) on the component (core + wings) and its fraction of the beam power
  • peak_intensity — worst-case beam intensity (W/cm², normal to the beam, core + wings) at the upstream surface points of the component, ignoring shadowing
  • status — :fail, :review or :pass against the thresholds of the report

Briareus.PowerLawWing — Type
PowerLawWing(fraction; rh = :r95, rmax = 1.5)

Halo with I ∝ [1 + (r/rh)²]^(-3/2) for r ≤ rmax, carrying fraction of the beam power. rh = :r95 uses the radius enclosing 95% of the core, the geometric mean of the axes. rmax is the spatial-filter cutoff, ≈ ±200 µrad × 7.7 m ≈ 1.5 mm on NIF.

Briareus.clearance — Method
clearance(target, beams; targets, hardware = all other surfaces,
          intensity = nothing, peak_fraction = 1e-4, enclosed = nothing,
          wings = nif_wings(), pointing = 0.05, target_offset = 0.025,
          hardware_tolerance = 0.0, focus = 1.0, margin = 0.0, ssd = 0.0,
          thresholds = DEFAULT_CLEARANCE_THRESHOLDS, nangles = 16, level = 2,
          resolution = 1e-4, rtol = 0.01,
          op = nothing, nspot = 61, callback = nothing,
          batch = Threads.nthreads()) -> ClearanceReport

Checks beam-to-hardware clearance, judged on the worst case over an error set. targets and hardware name parts or components (see elements). A part split for computation stands for all its pieces and gets one row per beam, the worst case over its pieces.

Spot. The spot is the SuperGaussianSpot core of each beam, with its optional super-Gaussian halo, plus clearance-only circular wings. The default wings, nif_wings(), are a 1% power-law halo and a 10⁻³ scatter floor. They are engineering defaults, to be overridden with measured far fields. ssd (mm) is the half-width of the SSD smear along the dispersion axis, added isotropically to the envelope as a conservative choice.

Envelope. The envelope is the best-focus contour grown by the f-cone, ssd and margin. The contour is set by one of an absolute intensity (W/cm²), a peak_fraction of the core peak, or an enclosed core fraction. With wings, the contour of each spot component is taken at the threshold divided by the number of components, and the envelope is their union, so the total intensity outside it is below the threshold.

Error set. Transverse errors add linearly, not in quadrature, as R = pointing + target_offset + hardware_tolerance (mm). The three terms are:

  • the per-beam pointing, with a NIF spec of 50 µm rms; pass ≈ 0.12 for a 3σ statistical radius;
  • the rigid target offset, with a NIF mean beams-to-target offset of ≈ 25 µm;
  • the as-built hardware tolerance, from metrology, or 0.1–0.25 mm without it.

focus is the ± focal-position error in mm, ≤ 1 mm in the 1994 NIF spec. The worst-case clearance is exact, nominal − R − focus/(2F). Worst-case power and intensity are maxima over aims displaced to nangles points on the radius-R circle, plus the nominal aim, at focus offsets 0, ±focus.

Status. thresholds are in W/cm² and in fractions of the beam power. A pair is :fail above fail_intensity (1e12, plasma formation) or fail_fraction (1e-3). It is :review above review_intensity (1e9, onset of ablation and debris) or review_fraction (1e-5). These are engineering defaults, and no facility review criterion sets them; talis_check applies the NIF rules.

Reach. Only hardware upstream of the landing point of each beam is checked. The landing point is where the beam axis first meets one of the targets components, the surfaces that the beam should hit, or the aim plane if the axis misses them. Hardware is split into 4^level sub-triangles per triangle. A point counts only if beam light can reach it past the opaque surfaces of the target. Apertures are transparent, and the triangles of the component itself never block it. Beam light reaches a point in one of three cases:

  • the straight segment to the point from the beam axis at the same axial position is unobstructed;
  • the point is outside the envelope, and the segment to it from the nearest envelope point along the same transverse ray is unobstructed, provided that envelope point is lit. An envelope point is lit when some f-cone direction reaches it from upstream, so envelope behind a surface that the beam edge already struck is dark.
  • the point is inside the envelope and is itself lit.

Hardware outside a hohlraum wall is therefore not judged against a beam inside it, while hardware beside the beam path before an entrance hole still is. A sub-triangle counts whole when any of its vertices or its centroid is reached. This choice is conservative, since a sub-triangle is dropped only when every path to each of those points is blocked.

Minimum. The clearance is minimized over the whole of each counted sub-triangle, and not only at its vertices. The envelope distance is Lipschitz in the point, with constant √(1 + cone²), so a branch-and-bound subdivision bounds the minimum from below. The bound is within resolution (mm) or rtol times its distance from zero worst-case clearance, whichever is larger. A plate crossed by a beam between its vertices is therefore found. The reported clearance is that lower bound. The peak intensity is the largest over the reached vertices and the point of least clearance. The power on hardware comes from the reference ray trace, with an nspot grid per core component and equal-power polar grids for the wings. When op, a DepositionOperator for these ports, is given, the core power comes from it.

Progress and cancel. With a callback, beams are checked in batches of batch in input order. callback((; done, total)) is called with the number of beams completed, once with done = 0 before any work and then after each batch. Returning true cancels the check. clearance then returns at once with the rows of the completed beams and canceled = true in the report. A true returned after the last batch is ignored. The callback always runs on the calling task, never on a worker thread, so it may touch task-local state; it must not block for long, since no work runs while it does. Rows do not depend on callback or batch.

Briareus.backlighter_foil — Method
backlighter_foil(los; standoff, width = 2.0, height = width, tilt = 0.0,
                 tilt_toward = 0.0, rotation = 0.0, center = (0, 0, 0), n = 8,
                 name = :backlighter, material = foil_material(),
                 two_sided = true) -> Component

Builds a rectangular backlighter foil for the diagnostic line of sight los = (θ, φ) in degrees, the direction from the object toward the detector. The object is at center, TCC by default. The foil is placed on the far side, centered standoff mm from center at center − standoff · port_vector(θ, φ). Its front face is heated by the backlighter beams, and its x rays cross the object to the detector, which the front face is turned toward. The placement is foil_frame with the same keywords. The mesh lies in the x–y plane of that frame, with width along its x axis.

By default the foil normal lies along the line of sight. tilt (degrees) rotates the whole foil about its center, off the line of sight toward cos(ψ) θ̂ + sin(ψ) φ̂. The angle ψ = tilt_toward is in degrees from θ̂ of the line of sight toward φ̂, like the angle of a spot. Before the tilt, the width edge of the foil lies along θ̂ and height along φ̂, and rotation (degrees) turns both about the normal from θ̂ toward φ̂. A foil tilted by β about its height axis (tilt_toward = 0) has a projected width width · cos β across the line of sight.

The defaults (a 2 mm square gold foil) are generic engineering values that are not taken from a facility backlighter design, and real backlighter dimensions and standoffs are shot-specific. n sets the mesh (n × n patches for a square foil). The default material is foil_material(), directly irradiated gold (albedo 0.8, conversion 0.8); foil_material(:Ti) and the other mid-Z elements give a suggested point inside a suggested range, listed in the table of foil_material, and none of these is a measurement. The x-ray emission of the foil set by this conversion is broadband radiosity; the He-α line signal comes from kshell_ce, fitted to separate data.

Briareus.line_of_sight — Method
line_of_sight(target, los; foil = :backlighter, object = :capsule,
              center = (0, 0, 0), radius = the object's projected radius,
              hardware = nothing, level = 0, nrings = 4,
              detector = nothing) -> LineOfSightReport

Checks whether the diagnostic line of sight los is unobstructed from the backlighter foil through the object to the detector. los is θ, φ in degrees from center toward the detector, or a direction vector. The object is represented by its disk normal to the line of sight through center, of radius radius. The default radius is the projected radius of the object, the largest distance of its vertices from the line of sight; give radius when there is no object (object = nothing). The foil and object parts never obstruct.

hardware names the parts or components that may obstruct, and nothing takes every opaque part of the target except the foil and object. Apertures such as entrance holes and open windows are transparent, as for rays everywhere; a closed window is opaque.

The bundle. With detector = nothing (the default), the bundle consists of every ray from a foil sample point through a disk sample point, continued without end past the disk. These rays are the x-ray paths from the whole emitting area of the foil through the object toward any detector in their cone, a conservative superset of what one imager accepts. The samples are taken as follows:

  • Foil samples are the vertices of the foil mesh plus 4^level sub-triangle points per triangle. The vertices include the corners and edges, where the extreme rays of the bundle start.
  • Disk samples are the center and nrings rings of 8k points, starting along θ̂ of the line of sight.

A ray counts as obstructed if it meets an opaque triangle of the hardware anywhere past the foil. A ray that would leave the foil through its back face also counts as obstructed, under the foil name.

detector = L takes the imaging rays instead, with L in mm from center along the line of sight, or Inf for a distant imager. Each imaging ray is traced from the detector point through a disk point back to the foil, or along the line of sight for Inf. The segment from the foil to the detector is then checked, unbounded for Inf.

Coverage. covered is the fraction of disk points whose image ray meets the foil front. The image ray is parallel to the line of sight, or directed toward the detector point when detector is finite. Below 1 the foil does not backlight the whole object. clear requires that no ray is obstructed and that covered == 1.

The result is exact for obstructions whose edges meet the bundle at its sampled extremes, such as straight edges parallel to a foil edge and rims crossing the θ̂ or φ̂ extreme of the bundle. Elsewhere it is a sampled answer, and raising level and nrings resolves a near-grazing obstruction.

Briareus.backlighter_signal — Method
backlighter_signal(target, deposition, los; line, pulse, distance, foil = :backlighter,
                   prepulse = false, pinhole = nothing, pinhole_distance = nothing,
                   pixel = nothing, filters = (), angular = :isotropic,
                   ce = nothing, irradiance = :mean, object = :capsule,
                   center = (0, 0, 0), radius = nothing, nrings = 4,
                   model = kshell_ce_model()) -> BacklighterSignal

Computes the photometric chain from the laser power on a backlighter foil to He-α photons per unit area at a detector. The detector is distance mm from center on the line of sight los, given as (θ, φ) in degrees or as a direction vector from center toward the detector. The chain has six steps:

  1. Laser energy. The energy of each foil element is its power in deposition (TW) over a flat-top pulse of pulse ns. Briareus deposits all of the incident power.

  2. Conversion. The CE comes from kshell_ce for line (an element Symbol or keV). With irradiance = :mean, it is evaluated at the power-weighted mean irradiance of the foil. With :element, each element gets the CE at its irradiance, but σ is still that of the mean. ce = value overrides the model, with σ = 0. The He-α energy into 4π is E = CE · E_laser.

  3. Angular distribution. The distribution is an assumption:

    • :isotropic (the default): the CE data were converted to 4π this way, each from one view angle.
    • :lambertian: an optically thick emitting face, 4 cos θ, toward the front only.
    • A function f(cos θ) normalized to ∫ f dΩ = 4π.

    Each element radiates E_e f(cos θ)/4π per steradian. An element contributes nothing if its front faces away from the detector or if its path to the detector crosses hardware. Every surface part except the foil and object counts as hardware.

  4. Geometry.

    • With pinhole = nothing, a point projection, the fluence at the detector point is Σ_e E_e f(cos θ_e) / (4π r_e²), where r_e is the element–detector distance.
    • With a pinhole of that diameter (mm) at pinhole_distance p mm from center, the setup is an imager of the object. The detector is at distance, so q = distance − p behind the pinhole. The fluence at each image point is R · A_pinhole / q², where R = E_e f / (4π A_e cos θ) is the radiance of the foil element seen through the pinhole and the object disk. The disk has radius radius, by default the projected radius of object, and is sampled as in line_of_sight. fluence is the mean over the image points of the disk.

  5. Filters. The fluence is multiplied by the filter_transmission of filters, given as material => mm pairs, at the line energy and normal incidence.

  6. Photons. The photon count is fluence / hν per mm², and per pixel (mm, in the detector plane) if given.

Attenuation by the object is not included, so the result is the open-field level. Only the He-α line is counted, without the continuum, Ly-α or a detector response. The lognormal 1σ of the CE propagates to photons_lo and photons_hi; filter and geometry uncertainties are not included.

Briareus.kshell_ce — Method
kshell_ce(line; irradiance, prepulse = false, pulse = nothing,
          model = kshell_ce_model()) -> NamedTuple

Returns the He-α conversion efficiency of a planar foil from the fit kshell_ce_model. The CE is the fraction of the incident laser energy emitted in the line into 4π. line is an element Symbol (see kshell_line_energy) or a line energy in keV, and irradiance (W/cm²) is the spot-average incident value.

Returns (ce, sigma, lo, hi, optimum, peak, extrapolated):

  • ce: the median CE.
  • sigma: the 1σ uncertainty of ln CE, which combines the model scatter and the coefficient uncertainty of the fit at this point in quadrature. CE is lognormal in this model, uncertain by a factor e^σ either way.
  • lo = ce·e^-σ and hi = ce·e^σ.
  • optimum and peak: the irradiance at which the CE of this line peaks, and that CE.
  • extrapolated: whether the point lies outside the data of its class in line energy or irradiance, or in pulse length when pulse (ns) is given. pulse changes nothing else.

Briareus.kshell_ce_model — Method
kshell_ce_model() -> KShellCEModel

Returns the weighted least-squares fit of ln CE to the rows of kshell_ce_data with fit = 1 (68 points). CE is the He-α line with its satellites, into 4π, over the incident laser energy, for planar foils driven at 351 nm:

\[\ln \mathrm{CE} = a_c + b\,\max(L - L_s, 0) + c_c\,Y + d\,Y^2,\qquad Y = X - s\,L,\]

with $L = \ln(h\nu/10\ \mathrm{keV})$ and $X = \ln(I/10^{15}\ \mathrm{W/cm^2})$. The CE of each line is a parabola in ln I of shared curvature d. It peaks at the optimum irradiance $I^* \propto (h\nu)^s$, where Y* = −c_c/2d. The peak CE falls as $(h\nu)^b$ above the saturation energy $h\nu_s = 10\ \mathrm{keV}\, e^{L_s}$ and is flat below it. The class c is :foil for one main pulse or :prepulsed for a pre-pulse or picket ahead of the main drive. The amplitude a_c and the linear coefficient c_c differ by class, so each class has a separate peak and optimum; b, d, s and hνₛ are shared. The fitted values are:

  • b = −3.33 ± 0.25, where Workman & Kyrala report hν^-(2.5–4) and Barrios et al. report hν^-(3–4);
  • s = 2.5 ± 0.4;
  • hνₛ = 6.1 ± 0.4 keV, between Cr and Fe;
  • a foil optimum of 1.2e15 W/cm² for Cr, 1.8e15 for Fe and 5.2e15 for Ge, with a peak of 3.2% at and below hνₛ;
  • a prepulsed optimum of 2.0e15 for Ge.

Why this form. The reasons are recorded in the research notes, "He-α CE: saturating peak (2026-10-26)". The earlier form, a_c + bL + c_c X + dX² + eXL, has a peak a_c + bL + (c_c + eL)²/(4|d|) that is convex in L. Its peak therefore rose steeply toward low hν, to 4.1% for Cr and 9.2% for Ti, against the Cr data (3.0% at 0.93e15) and the held-out Ti foil. Writing the parabola about its optimum Y removes that convexity at the same number of parameters. It also lowers the cross-validated error, from 0.55 to 0.44 rms in ln CE with one source left out at a time. The saturation adds one parameter. The Cr points require it, since they lie 0.24 below the power law through Fe–Mo, and with it the fit predicts the held-out Ti foil within 1.1σ. Inside the data the saturation is weakly determined, since the likelihood changes by only 1.1 in −2 ln L with it. With Cr left out, hνₛ is not identified. Two plausible reasons for a plateau below Fe are not modeled. Absorption, and the fraction of it radiated, cannot grow without bound. At the lower temperatures that suffice for low-Z lines, the energy also goes increasingly into lower ionization stages and the continuum.

Weights. Each point has the variance σᵢ² = sᵢ² + τ². The term sᵢ = u/ce is the published error bar as a relative error in ln CE. Where a row has none, as for the digitized points of Workman & Kyrala, sᵢ = √(0.1² + 0.2²) combines their stated relative error and shot-to-shot reproducibility. τ is the scatter that the stated errors do not cover, from calibrations, spot definitions, bands and the model form. It is set to 0.23, so that the weighted χ² per degree of freedom is 1 (random effects). For given (s, Lₛ) the other six parameters are linear, and (s, Lₛ) maximize the likelihood on a refined grid. The covariance of all eight is (JᵀWJ)⁻¹, with J the gradient of ln CE at the data. The scatter of the model is the spread of a measured CE about the fit, √(τ² + s̄²) with s̄ the rms sᵢ. It is 0.35 in ln CE, a factor of 1.43. Outside the data the parameter covariance adds to it (see kshell_ce).

Pulse length and spot size do not enter. The foil data are 1 ns (OMEGA), except the Fe shots of Coppari et al. at 0.6 and 2 ns. A ln τ term on the foil class fits CE ∝ τ^(0.6 ± 0.2), but only through the 2 ns Fe of Coppari against the 1 ns Fe of Workman. Within the shots of Coppari, the 1 ns Fe point lies above the 2 ns ones. The pulse length therefore stays confounded with the source and, in the prepulsed class, with the picket. Every source varies the irradiance through the spot size, so the spot size is confounded with the irradiance.

Below Cr the plateau is an extrapolation. The measured CE of the one held-out Ti foil (OMEGA, 1 ns, 1.3e15 W/cm², fit = 0) is 1.6%, where the fit gives 3.0% ×/÷ 1.48. That 1.6% is the He-α share of a 4–6 keV band, inferred; the whole band has 2.3%. The difference is −0.63 in ln CE, 1.1σ with the 42% error bar of that point. On Gekko XII and Shenguang II, Ti foils had CEs of 1.4–1.8% in the band, so the CE may fall below Cr, where the fitted plateau may not hold. The fit does not separate the two cases. Ti is flagged extrapolated; pass a measured ce for Ti backlighters when one exists. The fitted Fe peak (2.3%) lies below the two-sided Fe of Coppari et al. (2.8–3.25%) and the 3.0% of Workman at 3.5e15. It agrees with the Fe of Workman near 1e15 (2.4%), so the sources disagree by about the two-sided factor.

Engineering model: confidence medium inside the data, low beyond it. Facilities, spectrometer calibrations, spot definitions, one- and two-sided irradiation and bands are mixed in the compilation; the 550 eV band of Workman may include part of Ly-α. The fit runs once per session, from the CSV.

Briareus.ApertureKeepOut — Type
ApertureKeepOut(target; apertures = planar aperture components, peak_fraction = 0.01,
                intensity = nothing, pointing = 0.05, target_offset = 0.025,
                hardware_tolerance = 0.0, focus = 1.0, margin = 0.0,
                minimum = 0.0, weight = 100.0, nrim = 96, softness = 1e-4)

Keep-out constraint for beams entering through planar apertures of any shape, such as laser entrance holes and slots. aperture_clearance(keepout, beams) gives the worst-case clearance (mm) of each beam from the rim of its entrance aperture, which is the aperture on the port side of the beam. The clearance is judged as clearance judges it. The beam envelope is the best-focus contour at peak_fraction of the peak (or at intensity, W/cm²), grown by the f-cone radius |s|/(2F) and margin. The clearance is the smallest distance from the rim to the envelope, less the transverse error radius pointing + target_offset + hardware_tolerance and focus/(2F) for the focus error.

The rim is the boundary of the hole. It consists of the edges of the aperture triangles that no other triangle of the aperture continues across; a closing lip in the plane of the aperture about the same center is part of the union. On a DiskSurface aperture, an edge whose ends lie at one radius from the center of the surface is the circular arc between them, the exact shape of the element. The rim of a circular hole meshed as a polygon is therefore its circle. Every other edge is straight, as for rectangular, slotted or elliptical openings and CAD apertures, where the mesh is the geometry. The rim is sampled at about nrim points, with every straight edge sampled at both ends. The least distance on each sample interval is found exactly, at an end or where the slope of the distance along the rim rises through zero. That point is located from the slopes at the samples to 1e-12 of the interval. An interval is first split at these points:

  • where the distance has a kink, which is where the rim crosses the plane of best focus and the f-cone term turns;
  • where the rim crosses an axis of an elongated envelope, since the first-order distance below turns sharply there;
  • inside the envelope, where the rim passes nearest the beam axis, since the first-order distance dips steeply there;
  • where the cubic through the distances and slopes at the ends turns twice.

The rim of a dual beam is searched on its values. The partials of the result follow from its sensitivities to the focus and the semi-axes of the envelope, so their cost does not grow with the number of partials.

The clearance is a smooth minimum of these interval minima, −softness · log Σ σₖ exp(−dₖ/softness) (softness in mm). The weight σₖ of each interval is 1 within 10 softness of the least and falls smoothly to 0 at 20 softness, where a term weighs at most e⁻¹⁰ of the least. The exact minimum over the rim has kinks where the worst rim point jumps between separate local minima of the distance, and the smooth minimum rounds them off. It never exceeds the exact minimum, lies below it by at most softness times the log of the number of intervals counted, and is the exact minimum for softness = 0. The default, 1e-4 mm, is an engineering default well below the agreement of the keep-out with clearance, which is a few micrometers. On NIF quads through the entrance holes of a hohlraum, the smooth minimum lies 0 to 0.18 µm below the exact one. Over 144 scans of the aim, the largest change of slope in a step falls from 0.21 (a kink) to 0.002, which remains where one interval contains two local minima (research notes, "Defaults changed (2026-11-19)").

The distance to the envelope ellipse is first order (Sampson). It is exact on the envelope and for round spots, and smooth in aim, defocus and spot size. A design should be confirmed with clearance, which also accounts for wings and power on hardware. The keep-out is passed as keepout to symmetry_objective or asymmetry_objective, which then add weight × Σᵢ max(0, minimum − cᵢ)².

Briareus.SphereKeepOut — Type
SphereKeepOut(target; spheres = parts on a `SphereSurface`, peak_fraction = 0.01,

Briareus.talis_check — Method
talis_check(target, beams; capture, hardware = all surfaces, leh = (),
            omega1_components = all surfaces, diagnostics = (), tolerance = 0.5, pivot = :tcc,
            pointing = 0.05, target_offset = 0.025, hardware_tolerance = 0.0,
            focus = 1.0, ssd = 0.0, offsets = nothing, open_ports = :nif,
            curvature = Dict(), level = 2, resolution = 1e-4, rtol = 0.01, ...) -> TaLISReport

NIF TaLIS target-configuration checks from the "TCC Configuration Requirements" (NIF User Forum, 27 Oct 2018, LMF Shot RI Resources). Every distance is measured orthogonal to the beam, as the document states, and judged on the worst case over the error set (and, except for incidence, the rule tolerance), never at nominal.

capture lists the components that beams are meant to land on, such as backlighter foils, capture surfaces and hohlraum walls. The landing component of a beam is the first of them its nominal axis meets. leh lists the components that form the edge of a laser entrance hole the beams pass through (the end caps or LEH lips of a hohlraum, e.g. (:cap_top, :cap_bottom) for hohlraum); they are checked for 3ω clearance like hardware, whether or not hardware names them. Here and in hardware, leh, omega1_components, diagnostics and curvature, a component may be named by its part: a part split for computation (curved_components) is checked as one, and its rows are listed under the part name.

Rules

  • :capture_3w — the capture_contour (10%)-of-peak contour must lie ≥ capture_margin (0.5 mm) inside the substrate edge of the landing component: the minimum over the edge of the transverse distance to the beam envelope (best-focus contour grown by the f-cone at the axial position of each point, see BeamEnvelope). Substrate edges are the free edges of the opaque mesh (edges with a single triangle; edges shared with another component are not edges). A beam whose axis misses every capture component fails with component :none.
  • :incidence_3w — incidence < max_incidence (70°) to the target normal, the maximum over the surface of the landing component inside the worst-case 10% footprint (visible from the beam; exact normals on SphereSurface/CylinderSurface components). The angle is that of the steepest ray of the f-cone, the angle of the beam axis plus the cone half-angle atan(1/(2f#)) (1.38° at NIF), as VisRad's capture report measures it (VisRad 21 reports tilt + 1.384–1.390° on plates tilted 68–69.7° toward either spot axis). The incidence check includes the error set but not the rule tolerance; the capture margin and the clearances include both.
  • :clearance_3w — the clearance_contour (1%)-of-peak contour must clear each hardware component other than the landing component by > clearance_margin (0.5 mm); hardware downstream of the landing point is skipped, and everything along the beam is checked if the beam misses every capture component. Capture and clearance failures have the TaLIS consequence, an assessment of blow-by by the backscatter working group. On an leh component the rule is an LEH-clearance assessment: not clearing it is a :review (on other hardware, a :fail; see "Entrance-hole edges").
  • :clearance_1w — the 10% contour of the 1ω footprint must clear each omega1_components component by > omega1_margin (0.2 mm). Not clearing is a :review (the TaLIS outcome is to evaluate unconverted-light management) and is resolved by the next rule.
  • :management_1w — for each surface the 1ω footprint does not clear: pass if its radius of curvature is < cylinder_radius (2 mm, cylindrical) or < sphere_radius (5 mm, spherical); otherwise the specular 1ω reflection from every uncleared facet must stay > reflection_angle (20°) from every open quad (open_ports), less twice the tolerance angle (a tilt by α turns a reflection by 2α). Curvature comes from curvature[name] when given, and otherwise from the CylinderSurface/SphereSurface of the component or a planar mesh. The entry is (:cylinder, r), (:sphere, r), :flat, or (:dimpled, r) / (:dimpled, r, half_side) for a surface covered with spherical dimples of radius of curvature r, e.g. the dimpled 1ω "sombrero" shield around the LEH of an ignition hohlraum, modeled as a flat annulus_mesh. A dimpled surface is judged like a sphere against sphere_radius. The NIF User Guide (§5.6.2) also requires a dimple half-side/radius ≥ 0.25, so a dimpled surface passes only with half_side given and that ratio met, and is a :review otherwise. For other meshes the curvature is unknown and a failing reflection check is a :review. A flat component without a curvature entry is given the reflection check, which a horizontal plate usually fails (it mirrors each beam into the conjugate port of the same hemisphere).
  • :diagnostic_1w — the diagnostics components (diagnostic hardware modeled in the target) must stay ≥ 10 mm from 1ω light at TCC, growing linearly to 25 mm at 1 m (10 + 0.015 r mm, r the distance from TCC; interpretation of "a cone of 10 mm at TCC extruded to 25 mm at 1 m"). With no diagnostics the rule is listed in the notes of the report as not evaluated. Diagnostic hardware given only as hardware is checked for the 3ω and 0.2 mm 1ω clearances but not for this rule.
  • :tas_stay_in — (NIF beams) no component may extend more than stay_in (19 mm) above the lowest aim point of an upper beam or below the highest aim point of a lower beam (TAS alignment stay-in zone; violating it may require a different alignment sequence or beam selection). hardware_tolerance is added to the extent.

The remaining requirements of the document are listed in TALIS_OUT_OF_SCOPE.

1ω footprint

The 1ω rules apply to beams whose quad is a NIF quad name (or that have an offsets entry). A NIF quad-level beam (port within 0.1° of its quad port, as from nif_quads) is expanded into its four beamlets (LMF beam table) sharing its aim and defocus. The 1ω footprint of each beamlet at its 3ω best-focus plane is a square of side omega1_size whose nearest edge is omega1_edge = 4.45 mm (the TaLIS 10% contour) from the 3ω axis, displaced in φ̂ toward the beamlet side of its quad (the side of the quad axis its port lies on; NIF User Guide Fig. 4-12 — derived, confidence medium). The default side is the User Guide's 22 mm footprint grown by 4.8 − 4.45 = 0.35 mm per side, so its near edge is the TaLIS 10% contour (22.7 mm). Away from that plane the footprint is the cone converging to the 1ω focus omega1_focus (default 460 mm, from nif_unconverted) beyond the 3ω focus, with its center on the chief ray from the lens center lens_distance (7.7 m) upstream: at axial position s the near edge is 4.45 + 0.027 s mm, i.e. closer to the 3ω axis upstream of focus (derived). Pass omega1_focus = Inf, lens_distance = Inf for a prism (the footprint fixed in the transverse plane). offsets maps beam names to a footprint direction (degrees from the port θ̂ toward φ̂, or a vector) and overrides the NIF convention for that beam. The focus error moves the footprint with the 3ω focal plane; shadowing is ignored (conservative).

Entrance-hole edges

The TaLIS document states 3ω clearance "to target features" without distinguishing the entrance holes of a target from hardware, and gives every 3ω violation the same consequence: the configuration "must be assessed for blow-by by the backscatter working group". The consequence is an assessment and does not prohibit the configuration. Reporting LEH edges as :review while hardware proper, such as shields, stalks, diagnostics and closed windows, stays :fail is an assumption of Briareus; the TaLIS document does not state it. It is based on the fact that no NIF hohlraum with 3.1 mm-diameter LEHs meets the rule on its LEH edge. The best-focus 1% contour of an inner (23.5°) quad has a semi-axis of 1.145 mm along φ̂, which is horizontal and orthogonal to the beam. With the axis through the LEH center, 1.55 − 1.145 = 0.405 mm remains to the lip before the f-cone, any error or the tolerance is counted; the rule requires 0.5 mm. Light that reaches the lip is LEH clipping, which the deposition computes as such.

Tolerance and error set

The document adds "up to 0.5 degrees metrology/alignment/positioning tolerance" to the 3ω and 1ω clearances; Briareus also applies it to the 3ω capture margin. The angle is an insertion or assembly error of the target component: the beams must still clear with the component rotated by up to 0.5° off nominal in the least favorable direction, whether the whole target is inserted rotated or the component is assembled off-square (user, from facility practice, 2026-10-27; the document does not give a formula). It enters as a lateral allowance r·tan(tolerance), with r the distance of the hardware point from the pivot of the rotation: the largest displacement of the point that such a rotation causes, taken in full toward the beam (conservative). The default pivot = :tcc is the target inserted rotated about TCC; a component assembled off-square rotates about its mount, given as a point (pivot = (0, 0, 12.0)). :aim (rotation about the beam aim point) and :max (the larger of the TCC and aim distances) are alternatives; with tolerance = 0 the allowance is dropped.

It is combined with the error set of clearance by linear addition (no RSS):

worst = nominal − (pointing + target_offset + hardware_tolerance) − focus/(2F) − r·tan(tolerance)

evaluated at each point and minimized over the component (for the 1ω footprint the focus term is the footprint shift over ±focus instead of the f-cone). The target_offset and hardware_tolerance partly overlap what the tolerance is meant to cover, so this double counts conservatively; set them to 0 to rely only on the tolerance. ssd (mm) widens the 3ω envelopes as in clearance.

Distances are minimized over the whole of each triangle (and each substrate edge), not at sample points. Every distance in these rules is Lipschitz in the point, so a branch-and-bound subdivision bounds the minimum from below. The Lipschitz constant is √(1

  • cone²) for the beam envelope, 1 plus its taper for the 1ω footprint and tan(tolerance)

for the tolerance allowance. The reported worst and nominal values are those lower bounds, never above the exact minimum and within resolution (mm) or rtol times the distance of the value from the rule threshold, whichever is larger: a bound never crosses the threshold, so the status is exact (up to resolution), and only large margins are reported coarser (1% by default). A plate crossed by a beam between its vertices fails. The incidence and specular-reflection rules include each of the 4^level sub-triangles of a component (with its normal) that has any point inside the footprint, and whether a sub-triangle reaches the footprint is determined in the same way. Clearance-only far-field wings (nif_wings) are not used: the TaLIS rules refer to contours of the beam spot.

Briareus.HardwareKeepOut — Type
HardwareKeepOut(target, parts; targets = every other opaque surface,

                target_offset = 0.025, hardware_tolerance = 0.0, focus = 1.0,
                margin = 0.0, tolerance = 0.0, pivot = :tcc, miss = :aim,
                minimum = 0.0, weight = 100.0)

Keep-out constraint for named hardware such as shields, stalks, diagnostic snouts and closed windows. hardware_clearance(keepout, beams) gives the worst-case clearance (mm) of each beam from parts, judged as clearance judges it with wings = []. parts is a name or a collection of part or component names, and a part stands for all its pieces. The beam envelope is the best-focus contour at peak_fraction of the peak, or at intensity (W/cm²), grown by the f-cone radius |s|/(2F) and margin. The clearance is the smallest signed distance from the surface of the parts to the envelope, transverse to the beam and negative inside it, less the transverse error radius pointing + target_offset + hardware_tolerance and focus/(2F) for the focus error.

Only the surface upstream of the landing of the beam counts. As in clearance, the landing is the plane normal to the beam where its nominal axis first meets one of targets, by default every opaque surface not among parts. A beam whose axis meets none of them is judged up to its aim plane, or along its whole length with miss = :all. A beam whose landing is on one of parts itself (when targets names it) gets Inf. The defaults are those of ApertureKeepOut. They are engineering defaults and are not set by a facility criterion; the 1% contour is the level of the TaLIS 3ω clearance rule.

tolerance (degrees) is the rule tolerance of talis_check: the parts may sit rotated off nominal by up to that angle about pivot (:tcc, :aim the beam aim point, :max the farther of the two, or a point), which costs each point r·tan α, r its distance from the pivot, subtracted at each point inside the minimization (so the clearance stays continuous where the closest point jumps). With targets the capture components, miss = :all, tolerance = 0.5 and minimum = 0.5 (all other defaults equal) the keep-out is the TaLIS 3ω clearance rule of talis_check for each beam:

HardwareKeepOut(t, hardware; targets = capture, tolerance = 0.5, miss = :all,
                minimum = 0.5)

a surrogate checked against talis_check in this package (its :clearance_3w worst values; see the research notes), not against the facility tool. talis_check reports each hardware part on a separate row, where the keep-out takes the minimum over its parts (one keep-out per part gives the rows), and it passes a clearance strictly above 0.5 mm; on an leh component its failure is a review, which a keep-out does not distinguish.

The distance to the envelope ellipse is exact (the point–ellipse distance clearance uses), and the minimum over the parts is found, not sampled. On a triangle not crossing best focus the distance is convex (a convex function of the transverse position less a linear one of the axial), and it has no stationary point inside the triangle except on the medial strip, where it is not differentiable: the segment of the major axis between ±(A² − B²)/A (the axis itself for a round spot) swept along the beam, at least B²/A inside the envelope. So the minimum lies on the triangle edges, their crossings with the medial strip, or their cuts by the landing plane, each split at best focus: line segments on which the distance is convex. The triangles are grouped in spatially compact clusters of up to 16; clusters and segments are taken best first by a lower bound (the circumscribed circle and the homothetic ellipse through the center of a cluster or the midpoint of a segment, less the Lipschitz constant √(1 + 1/(2F)²) times its radius or half length), and each segment is minimized by a safeguarded root search on the derivative, until the bound reaches the smallest value found. The result is the exact minimum to about 1e-12 mm.

The tolerance term −r·tan α is concave in the point. With it the minimum still lies off the triangle interiors, because off the medial strip the Hessian of the distance has rank one, so in every plane the sum curves down along some direction. The term does spoil the convexity along a segment. Each segment is therefore minimized with the term replaced by its chord, a lower bound equal to it at the ends, and split at the minimizer of the chord until the two agree to 1e-14 mm; the bounds of clusters and segments subtract tan α (r + radius).

The clearance is continuous in the aim, defocus and spot size of the beams (a minimum over a fixed surface), and its derivatives are those of the distance at the closest point (Danskin's theorem); on the medial strip, where two points of the ellipse are equally near, they are the mixture of theirs that leaves the distance stationary at the minimum, as at the saddle of a minimax. They are one-sided only where two separate points of the parts are equally close (a kink of the minimum itself). The landing is found on the nominal beam: the clearance jumps where the landing of the axis moves from one component to another while the parts reach past the landing plane, and the cut by the landing plane does not move with the beam in the derivatives.

The keep-out departs from clearance in two ways, both conservative. Every point of the parts upstream of the landing counts, also where opaque surfaces hide it from the beam; clearance skips points the beam cannot reach, and talis_check judges 3ω clearance as this keep-out does. The clearance-only far-field wings are not modeled. Pass the keep-out as keepout to the objectives, by itself or in a tuple with other keep-outs; the objectives then add weight × Σᵢ max(0, minimum − cᵢ)² (keepout_penalty).

omega1_clearance(keepout, beams) -> Vector

Worst-case clearance (mm) of the parts of an Omega1KeepOut from the 1ω footprint of each beam, the rule tolerance included; Inf for a beam without a 1ω footprint. The clearance is differentiable in the aim and defocus of the beams and has their number type.
