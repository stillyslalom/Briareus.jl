Frames and placement

A Frame is a rigid frame in the chamber: its axes as the columns of a rotation and its origin, so that local coordinates map to the chamber by to_chamber and back by to_frame. compose places one frame in another, and inv inverts one. Frames come from closed-form constructors: euler_rotation and euler_frame (VisRad's polar, azimuthal and rotation angles), port_frame (a port's axis), foil_frame (the frame in which backlighter_foil places its foil) and component_frame (a part of a scripted target, about its part_centroid). coords_of and point_of convert between chamber points and Cartesian, cylindrical or spherical coordinates in a frame.

FrameDef places a named frame in a parent frame by an offset and three angles, and resolve_frames resolves a set of them, depth first, from base frames such as target-chamber center. A definition's PlacementSteps then move it relative to other frames, in order, each in closed form: relations (Coincident, OnAxis, Concentric, Distance, Facing, Parallel, Roll) fix degrees of freedom, and relative moves (Offset, Rotate) fix none. A relation that undoes an earlier one is an error naming both; placement_report gives the degrees of freedom each step fixes, redundant steps, what stays free, and the residuals left by relative moves. The steps run in sequence rather than through a simultaneous solver: every relation has a closed form, the result is deterministic, and an error names one step.

FramePointing parameterizes beam aims as local coordinates (u, v, w) of a frame per beam, with the non-aim fields of GroupPointing: a backlighter's beams optimized across the foil's face, or a hohlraum's cones in the frames of their entrance holes, one local aim giving mirrored chamber aims. It serves Scenario, subset and optimize_pointing.

placement_sweep steps a design through a sequence of placements, such as a foil moved along its line of sight with its beams following, and checks each step for beam clearance, an unobstructed line of sight and beam capture, optionally re-optimizing the pointing with a warm start. SweepResult holds one SweepRow per step, and write_sweep_csv writes them. The Backlighter placement sweep tutorial runs the whole sequence.

CHAMBER

The chamber frame: identity axes at target-chamber center.

source
Briareus.Frame — Type
Frame(R, origin)
Frame(origin; z, x = θ̂ of z)

A right-handed rigid frame in the chamber: R holds the frame's unit axes as columns and origin is its origin (mm), so a point with local coordinates x lies at R * x + origin in the chamber (to_chamber), and a chamber point p has local coordinates R' * (p - origin) (to_frame). CHAMBER is the identity frame at target-chamber center (TCC).

The keyword form builds the frame from its z axis (any length) and a vector x toward which its x axis points: x minus its component along z, normalized; by default the polar unit vector θ̂ of z (port_basis; along chamber x when z is the pole). y = z × x completes the right-handed triad. An x parallel to z is an ArgumentError.

A frame is Float64 and constant; the points mapped through it may carry ForwardDiff duals (the aims of FramePointing).

source
Briareus.FrameDef — Type
FrameDef(parent = :tcc; offset = (0, 0, 0), rotation = (0, 0, 0), steps = [])

A frame placed in another: its origin at offset (mm) in the frame named parent, and its axes turned by rotation = (polar, azimuth, rotation) in degrees in the parent (euler_rotation), so its chamber frame is compose(parent's frame, euler_frame(offset, rotation...)); then the PlacementSteps steps apply in order (relations to other frames, relative moves). Degrees of freedom no step fixes keep the values of offset and rotation. A port's frame is FrameDef(:tcc; rotation = (θ, φ, roll)) (port_frame). Resolve a set of named definitions with resolve_frames.

Briareus.component_frame — Method
component_frame(target, part) -> Frame

A frame for a part of a target built in a script (the GUI's parts have their own placement frames): origin at its part_centroid, and z axis, in order of preference,

  1. the axis of its exact surface: a CylinderSurface, ConeSurface or TorusSurface axis or a DiskSurface normal, when every piece with such a surface has a parallel one (the first piece's, with its sign);
  2. else the area-weighted mean front normal of its triangles, when that mean is at least 0.1 in length (a flat foil: its front normal; a hemispherical cup: 0.5);
  3. else the principal axis of its triangles' area moments whose moment differs most from the other two (the symmetry axis of a body of revolution), signed toward chamber +z (else +x, else +y); when the three moments agree within 1e-6 (a sphere, a cube) chamber z.

The x axis is θ̂ of z (see Frame). The choice is ambiguous for shapes with no axis (a sphere or a closed box) and for the sign of a principal axis; the 0.1 threshold of rule 2 is an engineering default. Give the frame explicitly when it matters.

Briareus.compose — Method
compose(parent, child) -> Frame

The chamber frame of child, a frame given in parent's local coordinates: to_chamber(compose(parent, child), x) == to_chamber(parent, to_chamber(child, x)) (to round-off).

Briareus.coords_of — Function
coords_of(p, system = :cartesian; frame = CHAMBER) -> NTuple{3,Float64}

Coordinates of the chamber point p in frame: :cartesian (x, y, z), :cylindrical (r, φ, z: distance from the frame's z axis, azimuth in degrees in [0, 360) from x toward y, height) or :spherical (r, θ, φ: distance from the origin, polar angle from z and azimuth, degrees). At r = 0 the angles are 0. point_of is the inverse.

Briareus.euler_frame — Function
euler_frame(origin, polar, azimuth, rotation = 0) -> Frame

The frame at origin with axes euler_rotation(polar, azimuth, rotation).

source
Briareus.euler_rotation — Function
euler_rotation(polar, azimuth, rotation = 0) -> SMatrix{3,3}

The rotation Rz(azimuth) · Ry(polar) · Rz(rotation) (degrees, right-handed): turn by rotation about z, tilt by polar about y, then turn by azimuth about z. Its z column is port_vector(polar, azimuth), and at rotation = 0 its x and y columns are the polar and azimuthal unit vectors θ̂ and φ̂ there (port_basis), so a frame with this rotation has its z axis toward the direction (polar, azimuth) and rotation turns its x axis from θ̂ toward φ̂. This is the order of VisRad's orientation angles (Polar, Azimuthal, Rotation; the GUI's part placement uses the same).

source
Briareus.foil_frame — Method
foil_frame(los; standoff, tilt = 0.0, tilt_toward = 0.0, rotation = 0.0,
           center = (0, 0, 0)) -> Frame

The frame of a backlighter foil on the line of sight los = (θ, φ) in degrees, as backlighter_foil places it: origin at the foil's center, center − standoff · port_vector(θ, φ); x along the foil's width edge, y along its height edge and z along its front normal (toward the detector). Before the tilt, x is the line of sight's θ̂ turned by rotation toward φ̂; tilt turns the whole frame off the line of sight toward cos(ψ) θ̂ + sin(ψ) φ̂, ψ = tilt_toward (degrees). Local (u, v, 0) is a point on the foil's face, u across its width.

source
Briareus.part_centroid — Method
part_centroid(target, part) -> Vec3
part_centroid(target) -> Dict{Symbol,Vec3}

The area-weighted centroid of the triangles of part (all its pieces, see part_pieces): the center of a foil, a disk or an entrance hole, a reference point to aim beams at. The second form gives it for every part of the target.

source
Briareus.point_of — Function
point_of(c, system = :cartesian; frame = CHAMBER) -> Vec3

The chamber point with coordinates c in frame, in the order of coords_of: :cartesian (x, y, z), :cylindrical (r, φ, z) or :spherical (r, θ, φ), angles in degrees. A negative radius is an ArgumentError.

source
Briareus.port_frame — Method
port_frame(theta, phi; roll = 0, origin = (0, 0, 0)) -> Frame

The frame of the port at polar angle theta and azimuth phi (degrees): z toward the port (port_vector(theta, phi), so a beam from it travels along -z), x the port's θ̂ turned by roll (degrees) toward φ̂, at origin (TCC by default). It equals euler_frame(origin, theta, phi, roll). The x axis along θ̂ is an assumption at low confidence (research notes, "Frames and placement (2026-10-28)"): VisRad's port frames have not been seen in a sample workspace.

source
Briareus.resolve_frames — Function
resolve_frames(defs, base = Dict(:tcc => CHAMBER)) -> Dict{Symbol,Frame}

Chamber frames of the named definitions defs (a dictionary or a collection of name => FrameDef pairs), each placed in its parent, which is another definition or one of the base frames (analytic frames: TCC, a template's entrance holes, a foil; :tcc is added as CHAMBER when absent), then moved by its placement steps. The result holds the base frames too. Definitions resolve depth first, the frames a definition's steps name before it, so a frame follows every ancestor and every frame its steps refer to exactly. A definition that reuses a base frame's name, a name given twice, an unknown parent or step frame, frames that depend on each other in a loop (the error names the loop, e.g. a → b → a), a step that cannot apply (a facing point at the origin) and a step that undoes an earlier one (see placement_report) are each an ArgumentError.

Briareus.to_chamber — Method
to_chamber(frame, x; direction = false) -> SVector{3}

Chamber coordinates of the local point x of frame, R * x + origin; with direction = true, of the local vector x, R * x (no translation). The element type follows x, so duals pass through.

source
Briareus.to_frame — Method
to_frame(frame, p; direction = false) -> SVector{3}

Local coordinates in frame of the chamber point p, R' * (p - origin); with direction = true, of the chamber vector p, R' * p. The inverse of to_chamber.

Briareus.Coincident — Type
Coincident(ref; point = (0, 0, 0))

Move the frame's origin onto the local point of the frame ref (mm). Fixes the three translations.

source
Briareus.Concentric — Type
Concentric(ref; axis = :z, flip = false)

Turn the frame so its z axis lies along ref's axis (against it with flip), by the smallest rotation, and move its origin onto that axis line at the nearest point. Fixes the two translations and the two rotations across the axis; the position along it and the roll about it stay free.

source
Briareus.Distance — Type
Distance(ref, distance; point = (0, 0, 0))

Move the frame's origin along the line from ref's local point through it, to distance (mm, ≥ 0) from the point. Fixes the translation along that line. An origin at the point is an ArgumentError (no line).

source
Briareus.Facing — Type
Facing(ref; point = (0, 0, 0), side = :front)

Turn the frame by the smallest rotation so its z axis points at ref's local point (side = :front) or away from it (side = :back). Fixes the two rotations across that direction; the roll about it stays free. A point at the frame's origin is an ArgumentError.

source
Briareus.Offset — Type
Offset(dx, dy, dz; frame = nothing)

Move the frame by (dx, dy, dz) (mm) along its own axes, or along the axes of the frame named frame. A relative move: it fixes nothing.

source
Briareus.OnAxis — Type
OnAxis(ref; axis = :z, distance = nothing)

Move the frame's origin onto the line through ref's origin along its axis (:x, :y, :z or a vector in ref's coordinates): with distance (mm), to that signed distance from ref's origin along the axis, fixing the three translations; without, to the nearest point of the line, fixing the two translations across it.

source
Briareus.Parallel — Type
Parallel(ref; axis = :z, flip = false)

Turn the frame by the smallest rotation so its z axis is parallel to ref's axis (antiparallel with flip). Fixes the two rotations across the axis.

Briareus.PlacementReport — Type
PlacementReport

What a frame's placement steps fix (placement_report). Fields: frame (its name), steps, fixed (per step, the translations and rotations it fixes that no earlier step fixed, as (translations, rotations)), redundant (indices of relations that fix nothing new), free_translation and free_rotation (orthonormal chamber directions and rotation axes no step fixes; they keep the values of the frame's offset and rotation) and residuals (per step, how far the final frame is from it, (mm, degrees): zero for a relation no later relative move disturbs, and for every relative move).

The rotations a step fixes are counted at the frame's orientation after the step (the axes across a z axis set by Facing, Parallel or Concentric, and that z axis for Roll).

source
Briareus.PlacementStep — Type
PlacementStep

A step of a frame's placement (FrameDef's steps), applied in order after the frame's offset and rotation in its parent. Relations fix degrees of freedom: Coincident, OnAxis, Concentric, Distance, Facing, Parallel and Roll with a reference direction. Relative moves fix none: Offset, Rotate and Roll without one. Each step has a closed form; a rotation turns the frame about its own origin. Frames named by steps (ref) are resolved first, so a frame follows them as it follows its parent. placement_report gives what each step fixes.

source
Briareus.Roll — Type
Roll(angle; from = nothing)

Turn the frame about its own z axis. With from (a frame name, its x axis, or (name, axis)), set the frame's x axis to angle degrees from that direction's projection across z, turning toward y; this fixes the roll. A direction along z has no projection and is an ArgumentError. Without from, turn by angle from the current roll, a relative move.

source
Briareus.Rotate — Type
Rotate(axis, angle)

Turn the frame by angle degrees (right-handed) about its own axis (:x, :y, :z or a vector in its coordinates) through its origin. A relative move: it fixes nothing.

placement_report(defs, base = Dict(:tcc => CHAMBER)) -> Dict{Symbol,PlacementReport}

A PlacementReport for every definition in defs (as in resolve_frames, which raises the same errors): the degrees of freedom each placement step fixes, redundant steps, what stays free, and the residuals.

Briareus.FramePointing — Type
FramePointing(beams, frames; by = :group, fields = (:u, :v, :defocus),
              relative = false)

Maps a parameter vector to beams whose aims are local coordinates of a Frame: beam i aims at to_chamber(frame_i, (u, v, w)), R * SVector(u, v, w) + origin. frames is one Frame for every beam, a vector of frames (one per beam), or a function beam -> Frame. Beams are grouped by by (:group, :quad, :beam, or a function beam -> key), and each group gets one value per field:

  • :u, :v, :w — the aim's local coordinates (mm): on a foil's foil_frame, u and v across its face and w along its front normal;
  • :defocus, :power, :spot, :dlambda — as for GroupPointing.

A group shares local coordinates, not chamber aims: beams of one group in different frames aim at the same local point of each. Two entrance holes' frames, the bottom one the top one mirrored through the equator, so give a mirrored pointing with no mirror flag. Aim coordinates that are not parameters keep each beam's own (its template aim in its frame); with no aim field at all the template aims are kept exactly.

relative = true makes the aim fields shifts added to each beam's own local coordinates, so a group moves rigidly in its frame; its initial aim parameters are then zero. The other fields stay absolute.

apply is generic in the number type (frames are Float64, parameters may be ForwardDiff duals), so the parameterization serves Scenario, optimize_pointing and subset as a GroupPointing does.

Briareus.SweepRow — Type
SweepRow

One step of a placement_sweep:

  • value — the swept quantity (the convenience method: the shift along the axis, mm);
  • beams — the step's beams (after re-optimization, when on);
  • min_clearance, worst_beam, worst_component, fails, reviews — the least worst-case clearance of the clearance check (mm), its beam and hardware part, and the numbers of :fail and :review pairs (NaN, "", :none, 0 when not checked);
  • los_clear, blocked_fraction, covered — the line_of_sight check: clear, the fraction of its rays obstructed, the fraction of the object covered (missing, NaN when not checked);
  • min_captured — the least fraction of a beam's power absorbed by the capture parts (NaN when not checked);
  • objective_before, objective_after, x — the objective at the step's starting parameters and after re-optimization, and the optimized parameters (NaN, empty when not optimized);
  • clearance, los — the full reports (nothing when not checked).

Briareus.placement_sweep — Method
placement_sweep(design, values; clearance_kw = nothing, los = nothing, los_kw = (;),
                capture = nothing, capture_kw = (;), optimize = nothing,
                callback = nothing) -> SweepResult
placement_sweep(target, beams, parts, values; frame = CHAMBER, axis = :z,
                follow = false, kwargs...) -> SweepResult

Steps a design through values and checks each step. design(value) returns a named tuple with the step's target and beams, and with pointing, the parameterization whose initial parameters give those beams, when the step is re-optimized.

Each check runs when its keyword is given:

  • clearance_kw, the keywords of clearance (targets among them): the least worst-case clearance, its beam and part, and the numbers of failing and review pairs;
  • los, a line of sight (θ, φ in degrees or a direction) for line_of_sight with keywords los_kw: whether it is clear, the fraction of its rays blocked and the object's coverage;
  • capture, a part name or a tuple of them (a backlighter foil): the least fraction of a beam's power those parts absorb, from beam_component_power on a DepositionOperator built for the step with keywords capture_kw.

optimize, a named tuple, re-optimizes the pointing at each step before the checks: objective (required), scenario_kw (keywords of Scenario), bounds_kw (keywords of bounds, a box about the step's own initial parameters), warm (default true: start from the previous step's optimum when it has the same number of parameters) and any keywords of optimize_pointing. With a FramePointing in a frame that moves with the part, the parameters keep their meaning from step to step, which is what makes the warm start useful.

callback((; done, total, row)) is called once with done = 0 and row = nothing before any work, then after each step with that step's row; returning true cancels, and the result holds the completed rows with canceled = true (a true after the last step is ignored).

Moving parts along a frame axis. The second form moves the named parts (a Symbol or a collection) of target by value · a at each step, a the unit axis of frame in the chamber (:x, :y, :z, or a local vector). Beams selected by follow (true for all, false for none, beam indices, or a predicate beam -> Bool) move with them: their aims shift by the same vector. beams may be a FramePointing instead: its template beams are swept, followed beams' frames move with them, and it is the pointing that optimize re-optimizes. value is then the shift (mm) and every other keyword passes to the first form.

Each step costs a target rebuild, the clearance check and a deposition operator for the capture check, and the optimization when on: give these checks their quick settings (clearance_kw = (; nangles = 8, nspot = 31, ...), a coarse capture_kw) for long sweeps.

source
Briareus.write_sweep_csv — Method
write_sweep_csv(path, result) -> path

Write the rows of a SweepResult as CSV, one line per step: the value, the least clearance (mm), its beam and part, the failing and review pairs, the line of sight (clear, blocked fraction, covered fraction), the least captured fraction and the objective before and after re-optimization. A check that did not run leaves its cells empty.
