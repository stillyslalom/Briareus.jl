Frames and placement

A Frame is a rigid frame in the chamber: its axes as the columns of a rotation and its origin, so that local coordinates map to the chamber by to_chamber and back by to_frame. compose places one frame in another, and inv inverts one. Frames come from closed-form constructors: euler_rotation and euler_frame (VisRad's polar, azimuthal and rotation angles), port_frame (the axis of a port), foil_frame (the frame in which backlighter_foil places its foil) and component_frame (a part of a scripted target, about its part_centroid). coords_of and point_of convert between chamber points and Cartesian, cylindrical or spherical coordinates in a frame.

FrameDef places a named frame in a parent frame by an offset and three angles, and resolve_frames resolves a set of them, depth first, from base frames such as target-chamber center. The PlacementSteps of a definition then move it relative to other frames, in order, each in closed form: relations (Coincident, OnAxis, Concentric, Distance, Facing, Parallel, Roll) fix degrees of freedom, and relative moves (Offset, Rotate) fix none. A relation that undoes an earlier one is an error naming both; placement_report gives the degrees of freedom each step fixes, redundant steps, what stays free, and the residuals left by relative moves. The steps run in sequence, without a simultaneous solver: every relation has a closed form, the result is deterministic, and an error names one step.

FramePointing parameterizes beam aims as local coordinates (u, v, w) of a frame per beam, with the non-aim fields of GroupPointing. Examples are the beams of a backlighter optimized across the foil face, and the cones of a hohlraum in the frames of their entrance holes, where one local aim gives mirrored chamber aims. It works with Scenario, subset and optimize_pointing.

placement_sweep steps a design through a sequence of placements, such as a foil moved along its line of sight with its beams following, and checks each step for beam clearance, an unobstructed line of sight and beam capture, optionally re-optimizing the pointing with a warm start. SweepResult contains one SweepRow per step, and write_sweep_csv writes them. The Backlighter placement sweep tutorial runs the whole sequence.

Briareus.Frame — Type
Frame(R, origin)
Frame(origin; z, x = θ̂ of z)

A right-handed rigid frame in the chamber. The columns of R are the unit axes of the frame, and origin is its origin (mm). A point with local coordinates x therefore lies at R * x + origin in the chamber (to_chamber), and a chamber point p has local coordinates R' * (p - origin) (to_frame). CHAMBER is the identity frame at target-chamber center (TCC).

The keyword form builds the frame from its z axis, of any length, and a vector x that sets the direction of its x axis. The x axis is x minus its component along z, normalized. By default x is the polar unit vector θ̂ of z (port_basis), which lies along chamber x when z is the pole. y = z × x completes the right-handed triad. An x parallel to z throws an ArgumentError.

A frame is Float64 and constant; the points mapped through it may carry ForwardDiff duals (the aims of FramePointing).

source
Briareus.FrameDef — Type
FrameDef(parent = :tcc; offset = (0, 0, 0), rotation = (0, 0, 0), steps = [])

A frame placed in another frame. Its origin lies at offset (mm) in the frame named parent, and its axes are turned by rotation = (polar, azimuth, rotation) in degrees in the parent (euler_rotation). Its chamber frame is therefore compose(parent's frame, euler_frame(offset, rotation...)). The PlacementSteps steps then apply in order, as relations to other frames and relative moves. Degrees of freedom that no step fixes keep the values of offset and rotation. The frame of a port is FrameDef(:tcc; rotation = (θ, φ, roll)) (port_frame). Resolve a set of named definitions with resolve_frames.

Briareus.component_frame — Method
component_frame(target, part) -> Frame

Returns a frame for a part of a target built in a script. Parts made in the GUI have separate placement frames. The origin is at the part_centroid of the part, and the z axis is the first of these that applies:

  1. The axis of its exact surface, such as the axis of a CylinderSurface, ConeSurface or TorusSurface or the normal of a DiskSurface. This rule applies when every piece with such a surface has a parallel one, and the axis of the first piece sets the sign.
  2. The area-weighted mean front normal of its triangles, when that mean is at least 0.1 in length. For a flat foil this is its front normal; for a hemispherical cup the mean is 0.5 long.
  3. The principal axis of its triangle area moments whose moment differs most from the other two, which is the symmetry axis of a body of revolution. The axis is signed toward chamber +z, else +x, else +y. When the three moments agree within 1e-6, as for spheres and cubes, the axis is chamber z.

The x axis is θ̂ of z (see Frame). The choice is ambiguous for shapes with no axis, such as spheres and closed boxes, and for the sign of a principal axis. The 0.1 threshold of rule 2 is an engineering default. Give the frame explicitly when it matters.

Briareus.compose — Method
compose(parent, child) -> Frame

Returns the chamber frame of child, a frame given in the local coordinates of parent, so that to_chamber(compose(parent, child), x) == to_chamber(parent, to_chamber(child, x)) within round-off.

Briareus.coords_of — Function
coords_of(p, system = :cartesian; frame = CHAMBER) -> NTuple{3,Float64}

Returns the coordinates of the chamber point p in frame, in one of three systems:

  • :cartesian (the default) gives (x, y, z).
  • :cylindrical gives (r, φ, z): the distance from the frame z axis, the azimuth in degrees in [0, 360) from x toward y, and the height.
  • :spherical gives (r, θ, φ): the distance from the origin, the polar angle from z and the azimuth, in degrees.

At r = 0 the angles are 0. point_of is the inverse.

Briareus.euler_frame — Function
euler_frame(origin, polar, azimuth, rotation = 0) -> Frame

Returns the frame at origin with axes euler_rotation(polar, azimuth, rotation).

source
Briareus.euler_rotation — Function
euler_rotation(polar, azimuth, rotation = 0) -> SMatrix{3,3}

Returns the rotation Rz(azimuth) · Ry(polar) · Rz(rotation) (degrees, right-handed). The rotation is a turn by rotation about z, a tilt by polar about y, and then a turn by azimuth about z. Its z column is port_vector(polar, azimuth). At rotation = 0 its x and y columns are the polar and azimuthal unit vectors θ̂ and φ̂ at that direction (port_basis). A frame with this rotation therefore has its z axis along the direction (polar, azimuth), and rotation turns its x axis from θ̂ toward φ̂. This is the order of the VisRad orientation angles Polar, Azimuthal and Rotation, which part placement in the GUI also uses.

source
Briareus.foil_frame — Method
foil_frame(los; standoff, tilt = 0.0, tilt_toward = 0.0, rotation = 0.0,
           center = (0, 0, 0)) -> Frame

Returns the frame of a backlighter foil on the line of sight los = (θ, φ) in degrees, as backlighter_foil places it:

  • The origin is at the foil center, center − standoff · port_vector(θ, φ).
  • x lies along the width edge of the foil, y along its height edge, and z along its front normal, toward the detector.

Before the tilt, x is θ̂ of the line of sight turned by rotation toward φ̂. tilt then turns the whole frame off the line of sight toward cos(ψ) θ̂ + sin(ψ) φ̂, with ψ = tilt_toward in degrees. Local (u, v, 0) is a point on the foil face, with u across its width.

Briareus.part_centroid — Method
part_centroid(target, part) -> Vec3
part_centroid(target) -> Dict{Symbol,Vec3}

Returns the area-weighted centroid of the triangles of part, over all its pieces (see part_pieces). For parts such as foils, disks and entrance holes the centroid is the center, a reference point to aim beams at. The second form gives the centroid of every part of the target.

Briareus.point_of — Function
point_of(c, system = :cartesian; frame = CHAMBER) -> Vec3

Returns the chamber point with coordinates c in frame, in the order of coords_of: :cartesian (x, y, z), :cylindrical (r, φ, z) or :spherical (r, θ, φ), with angles in degrees. A negative radius throws an ArgumentError.

Briareus.port_angles — Method
port_angles(facility::Symbol, port) -> (theta, phi)

Returns the polar angle and azimuth (degrees) of a facility port from the facility table:

  • :nif: an indirect-drive quad port, by its number (NIF_PORTS, NIF User Guide Table 4-1) or its quad name such as "Q31B" (nif_port), or a diagnostic port by name such as "P143-274" (NIF_DIAGNOSTIC_PORTS, Table 5-1);
  • :omega: a beam port by name, "B01"–"B60", in the hohlraum frame of omega_ports.

An unknown port throws a KeyError, and another facility throws an ArgumentError.

source
Briareus.port_frame — Method
port_frame(theta, phi; roll = 0, origin = (0, 0, 0)) -> Frame

port_frame(beam::Beam; roll = 0, origin = (0, 0, 0)) -> Frame
port_frame(facility::Symbol, port; roll = 0, origin = (0, 0, 0)) -> Frame

Returns the frame of the port at polar angle theta and azimuth phi (degrees), with its origin at origin (TCC by default):

  • z lies along port_vector(theta, phi), toward the port. A beam from the port therefore travels along -z, as the beam convention k = -port requires.
  • x is the port θ̂ turned by roll (degrees) toward φ̂.

The frame equals euler_frame(origin, theta, phi, roll). The second form takes the port of a beam (port_angles), and the third takes a facility port by name (port_angles(facility, port)). According to the documentation of the VisRad coordinate transform tool, its diagnostic-port frames share the origin and z. The x axis along θ̂ is an assumption at low confidence, because VisRad port frames have not been seen in a sample workspace (research notes, "Frames and placement (2026-10-28)").

Briareus.resolve_frames — Function
resolve_frames(defs, base = Dict(:tcc => CHAMBER)) -> Dict{Symbol,Frame}

Returns the chamber frames of the named definitions defs, given as a dictionary or a collection of name => FrameDef pairs. Each frame is placed in its parent and then moved by its placement steps. The parent is another definition or one of the base frames, which are analytic frames such as TCC, the entrance holes of a template and foils. :tcc is added as CHAMBER when absent, and the result also contains the base frames too. Definitions are resolved depth first, and the frames named by the steps of a definition are resolved before it. A frame therefore moves exactly with every ancestor and with every frame that its steps refer to. Each of these is an ArgumentError:

  • a definition that reuses the name of a base frame, or a name given twice;
  • an unknown parent or step frame;
  • frames that depend on each other in a loop, where the error names the loop, e.g. a → b → a;
  • a step that cannot apply, such as a facing point at the origin;
  • a step that undoes an earlier one (see placement_report).

Briareus.to_chamber — Method
to_chamber(frame, x; direction = false) -> SVector{3}

Returns the chamber coordinates R * x + origin of the local point x of frame. With direction = true, it returns those of the local vector x, R * x, without translation. The element type follows x, so duals pass through.

source
Briareus.to_frame — Method
to_frame(frame, p; direction = false) -> SVector{3}

Returns the local coordinates R' * (p - origin) in frame of the chamber point p. With direction = true, it returns those of the chamber vector p, R' * p. It is the inverse of to_chamber.

Briareus.transform_point — Method
transform_point(from::Frame, to::Frame, c; input = :cartesian, output = :cartesian,
                direction = false) -> NTuple{3,Float64}
transform_point(frames, from::Symbol, to::Symbol, c; …)

Returns the coordinates in frame to of the point with coordinates c in frame from, after the VisRad coordinate transform tool. c is given in the system input and the result in output, each :cartesian, :cylindrical or :spherical (coords_of, point_of), in mm and degrees. With direction the three numbers are a vector, which is turned by the frame rotations and not moved by the frame origins. Its cylindrical or spherical coordinates are those of the vector tip from the origin. The second form looks the frames up by name in frames, a dictionary such as resolve_frames returns, and a missing name throws an ArgumentError.

Coincident(ref; point = (0, 0, 0))

Move the origin of the frame onto the local point of the frame ref (mm). Fixes the three translations.

Briareus.Concentric — Type
Concentric(ref; axis = :z, flip = false)

Turn the frame by the smallest rotation so that its z axis lies along the axis of ref (against it with flip), and move its origin onto that axis line at the nearest point. Fixes the two translations and the two rotations across the axis. The position along the axis and the roll about it stay free.

source
Briareus.Distance — Type
Distance(ref, distance; point = (0, 0, 0))

Move the origin of the frame along the line from the local point of ref through the origin, to distance (mm, ≥ 0) from the point. Fixes the translation along that line. An origin at the point is an ArgumentError, since the line is then undefined.

source
Briareus.Facing — Type
Facing(ref; point = (0, 0, 0), side = :front)

Turn the frame by the smallest rotation so that its z axis points at the local point of ref (side = :front) or away from it (side = :back). Fixes the two rotations across that direction, and the roll about it stays free. A point at the origin of the frame is an ArgumentError.

Briareus.Offset — Type
Offset(dx, dy, dz; frame = nothing)

Move the frame by (dx, dy, dz) (mm) along its axes, or along the axes of the frame named frame. This is a relative move and does not fix a degree of freedom.

source
Briareus.OnAxis — Type
OnAxis(ref; axis = :z, distance = nothing)

Move the origin of the frame onto the line through the origin of ref along its axis (:x, :y, :z or a vector in the coordinates of ref). With distance (mm), the origin moves to that signed distance from the origin of ref along the axis, which fixes the three translations. Without it, the origin moves to the nearest point of the line, which fixes the two translations across it.

Briareus.Parallel — Type
Parallel(ref; axis = :z, flip = false)

Turn the frame by the smallest rotation so that its z axis is parallel to the axis of ref (antiparallel with flip). Fixes the two rotations across the axis.

Briareus.PlacementReport — Type
PlacementReport

The degrees of freedom that the placement steps of a frame fix (placement_report). Fields:

  • frame: the name of the frame;
  • steps;
  • fixed: per step, the translations and rotations that it fixes and no earlier step fixed, as (translations, rotations);
  • redundant: indices of relations that fix nothing new;
  • free_translation and free_rotation: orthonormal chamber directions and rotation axes that no step fixes. They keep the values of the offset and rotation of the frame;
  • residuals: per step, how far the final frame is from it, as (mm, degrees). The residual is zero for a relation that no later relative move disturbs, and for every relative move.

The rotations that a step fixes are counted at the orientation of the frame after the step. They are the axes across a z axis set by Facing, Parallel or Concentric, and that z axis for Roll.

source
Briareus.PlacementStep — Type
PlacementStep

A step of the placement of a frame (the steps of a FrameDef), applied in order after the offset and rotation of the frame in its parent. Relations fix degrees of freedom: Coincident, OnAxis, Concentric, Distance, Facing, Parallel and Roll with a reference direction. Relative moves do not fix any: Offset, Rotate and Roll without a reference direction. Each step has a closed form, and a rotation turns the frame about its origin. Frames named by steps (ref) are resolved first, so a frame follows them as it follows its parent. placement_report gives the degrees of freedom that each step fixes.

source
Briareus.Roll — Type
Roll(angle; from = nothing)

Turn the frame about its z axis. With from (a frame name for its x axis, or (name, axis)), the x axis of the frame is set to angle degrees from the projection of that direction across z, turning toward y, which fixes the roll. A direction along z has no projection and is an ArgumentError. Without from, the frame turns by angle from the current roll, a relative move.

source
Briareus.Rotate — Type
Rotate(axis, angle)

Turn the frame by angle degrees (right-handed) about its axis (:x, :y, :z or a vector in its coordinates) through its origin. This is a relative move and does not fix a degree of freedom.

placement_report(defs, base = Dict(:tcc => CHAMBER)) -> Dict{Symbol,PlacementReport}

A PlacementReport for every definition in defs, as in resolve_frames, which raises the same errors. The report gives the degrees of freedom that each placement step fixes, the redundant steps, the free degrees of freedom and the residuals.

Briareus.FramePointing — Type
FramePointing(beams, frames; by = :group, fields = (:u, :v, :defocus),
              relative = false)

Maps a parameter vector to beams whose aims are local coordinates of a Frame. Beam i aims at to_chamber(frame_i, (u, v, w)), which is R * SVector(u, v, w) + origin. frames is one Frame for every beam, a vector of frames (one per beam), or a function beam -> Frame. Beams are grouped by by (:group, :quad, :beam, or a function beam -> key), and each group gets one value per field:

  • :u, :v, :w — the local coordinates of the aim (mm). On the foil_frame of a foil, u and v run across its face and w along its front normal.
  • :defocus, :power, :spot, :dlambda — as for GroupPointing.

A group shares local coordinates. Beams of one group in different frames aim at the same local point of each frame, so their chamber aims differ. With two entrance-hole frames, the bottom one the top one mirrored through the equator, a mirrored pointing is given without a mirror flag. For aim coordinates that are not parameters, the value of each beam is kept, which is its template aim in its frame. With no aim field at all, the template aims are kept exactly.

relative = true makes the aim fields shifts added to the local coordinates of each beam, so a group moves rigidly in its frame; its initial aim parameters are then zero. The other fields stay absolute.

apply is generic in the number type (frames are Float64, parameters may be ForwardDiff duals), so the parameterization can be used with Scenario, optimize_pointing and subset in the same way as a GroupPointing.

source

apply(p::FramePointing, x) -> Vector{Beam}

Beams for parameter vector x. Each aim is R * SVector(u, v, w) + origin in the frame of its beam. The element type is that of x, so the map is differentiable.

SweepResult

The result of placement_sweep, with fields:

Briareus.SweepRow — Type
SweepRow

One step of a placement_sweep:

  • value — the swept quantity, which for the convenience method is the shift along the axis in mm;
  • beams — the beams of the step, after re-optimization when it is on;
  • min_clearance, worst_beam, worst_component, fails, reviews — the least worst-case clearance of the clearance check (mm), its beam and hardware part, and the numbers of :fail and :review pairs (NaN, "", :none, 0 when not checked);
  • los_clear, blocked_fraction, covered — the results of the line_of_sight check, which are whether it is clear, the fraction of its rays obstructed and the fraction of the object covered (missing, NaN when not checked);
  • min_captured — the least fraction of the power of a beam absorbed by the capture parts (NaN when not checked);
  • objective_before, objective_after, x — the objective at the starting parameters of the step and after re-optimization, and the optimized parameters (NaN, empty when not optimized);
  • clearance, los — the full reports (nothing when not checked).

Briareus.placement_sweep — Method
placement_sweep(design, values; clearance_kw = nothing, los = nothing, los_kw = (;),
                capture = nothing, capture_kw = (;), optimize = nothing,
                callback = nothing) -> SweepResult
placement_sweep(target, beams, parts, values; frame = CHAMBER, axis = :z,
                follow = false, kwargs...) -> SweepResult

Steps a design through values and checks each step. design(value) returns a named tuple with the target and beams of the step. When the step is re-optimized, the tuple also holds pointing, the parameterization whose initial parameters give those beams. It may add scenario_kw, keywords of Scenario for only that step, which are applied after optimize.scenario_kw. For instance, viewfactor_kw = (; closed = cavities) balances the cavities of the step, whose elements move with its target.

Each check runs when its keyword is given:

  • clearance_kw, the keywords of clearance (targets among them): the least worst-case clearance, its beam and part, and the numbers of failing and review pairs;
  • los, a line of sight (θ, φ in degrees or a direction) for line_of_sight with keywords los_kw: whether it is clear, the fraction of its rays blocked and the coverage of the object;
  • capture, a part name or a tuple of them, such as a backlighter foil: the least fraction of the power of a beam that those parts absorb, from beam_component_power on a DepositionOperator built for the step with keywords capture_kw.

With optimize, a named tuple, the pointing is re-optimized at each step before the checks. Its fields are:

  • objective (required);
  • scenario_kw, keywords of Scenario;
  • bounds_kw, keywords of bounds, a box about the initial parameters of the step;
  • warm, which defaults to true and starts from the optimum of the previous step when it has the same number of parameters;
  • any keywords of optimize_pointing.

With a FramePointing in a frame that moves with the part, the parameters keep their meaning from step to step, which is what makes the warm start useful.

callback((; done, total, row)) is called once with done = 0 and row = nothing before any work, and then after each step with the row of that step. Returning true cancels the sweep, and the result contains the completed rows with canceled = true. A true returned after the last step is ignored.

Moving parts along a frame axis. The second form moves the named parts, a Symbol or a collection, of target by value · a at each step. The vector a is the unit vector of axis in frame, expressed in the chamber; axis is :x, :y, :z or a local vector. The beams selected by follow move with the parts, and their aims shift by the same vector. follow is true for all beams, false for none, beam indices, or a predicate beam -> Bool. beams may be a FramePointing instead: its template beams are swept, the frames of followed beams move with them, and it is the pointing that optimize re-optimizes. value is then the shift (mm) and every other keyword passes to the first form.

Each step costs a target rebuild, the clearance check, a deposition operator for the capture check, and the optimization when it is on. For long sweeps, give these checks quick settings, such as clearance_kw = (; nangles = 8, nspot = 31, ...) and a coarse capture_kw.

source
Briareus.write_sweep_csv — Method
write_sweep_csv(path, result) -> path

Writes the rows of a SweepResult as CSV, one line per step. Each line contains the value, the least clearance (mm) with its beam and part, and the failing and review pairs. It also contains the line-of-sight results, which are the clear flag and the blocked and covered fractions, the least captured fraction, and the objective before and after re-optimization. A check that did not run leaves its cells empty.
