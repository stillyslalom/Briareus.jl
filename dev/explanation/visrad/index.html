For VisRad users

VisRad (Prism Computational Sciences; MacFarlane, J. Quant. Spectrosc. Radiat. Transfer 81, 287 (2003)) is the view-factor code most hohlraum and backlighter pointing work is done in. Briareus covers the same core calculation (laser deposition, 3D view-factor radiosity and capsule drive symmetry), reads and writes VisRad's files, and has been compared with VisRad 21 on NIF hohlraums. This page maps VisRad's concepts to Briareus's, lists the files each reader and writer handles, summarizes how the two codes' results compare, and states where they differ, including what VisRad does that Briareus does not. It ends with a walkthrough of porting a VisRad study. The Physics background page covers the physics both codes compute, and VisRad interchange documents every reader and writer.

How the results compare

VisRad 21 computed the laser deposition on two NIF hohlraum workspaces built from Briareus geometry, and Briareus read each one, built the same target and beams, and compared its deposition element by element with VisRad's. With VisRadProfile, wall deposition differs from VisRad's by 0.45–0.7 % rms per element and peaks agree within 0.1 %. With the earlier, tabulated profile, the capsule's P2 and P4, computed from the two depositions through the same Briareus radiosity, agreed to 0.014 and 0.004 percentage points. Verification and validation gives the setup and all the numbers. On a foil workspace with four beams, the totals agree to the table's precision (3.6000 TW), elements above 20 TW/cm² within 1.4 %, and the faint wings near focus (2–30 TW/cm²) differ by 8–15 % (research notes, "VisRad workspace files (2026-10-17)").

write_visrad_kit and compare_visrad set up the same comparison for any case. The comparison covers deposition only: VisRad's own radiosity, absolute drive levels and mode amplitudes have not yet been compared with Briareus's (ROADMAP, "Benchmark against VisRad").

Modes. VisRad's Surface Element Statistics fit Legendre polynomials and real spherical harmonics by least squares to the element values; the weighting is not documented. Briareus integrates the harmonics over each element's exact solid angle. The Legendre coefficients mean the same in both ($A_l$ and $a_l$, with $A_2/A_0 = a_2/a_0$ = P2/P0), but the two estimates differ on a coarse mesh; apply visrad_legendre_fit to both codes' element values for a like-for-like comparison. VisRad's spherical harmonics are normalized so that $Y_{00} = 1$: they are Briareus's orthonormal ylm_moments times $\sqrt{4\pi}$. Whether VisRad includes the Condon–Shortley phase is not stated, so the signs of odd-$M$ coefficients may differ; per-$L$ amplitudes do not.

Where the codes differ

The beam away from focus

Near best focus both codes deposit the same super-Gaussian. Away from focus they differ. Briareus's default model is the geometric-optics one: the best-focus profile summed over a quadrature of the f-cone's directions, which is the focal profile convolved with the disk of focal points. It conserves power at any distance and matches an exact convolution (Laser spot on a foil). VisRad's model is analytic. It is a super-Gaussian whose semi-axes $a(z)$, $b(z)$ and order $n(z)$ grow with the distance $z$ from focus, blended toward a square of growing weight. It is cut off at 10⁻³ of its local peak and renormalized in each transverse plane. The two shapes differ in kind: away from focus VisRad's spot turns square-shouldered, while the f-cone sum softens the focal profile, to about half its focal order at 15 mm.

On a hohlraum wall, 3–8 mm past focus, the f-cone sum gave 10 % rms per element against VisRad's deposition, with peaks 10 % higher. VisRadProfile is VisRad's law. The semi-axes, order, square weight and square width follow the parameters VisRad itself exports, and two constants were fitted to its deposition; the docstring gives the forms. visrad_profile reads the envelope ratios from a workspace. Neither model is a measurement of the real beams: the default is the geometric-optics convolution of the focal profile, and VisRadProfile is VisRad's model. Use VisRadProfile to reproduce a VisRad calculation. The law was identified on NIF beams at f/20.755 and VisRad's default lens ratio, and checked at envelope ratios 1.2 and the default.

Smaller differences of the beam model:

  • Spot rotation. VisRad's rotation 0 is the quad's horizontal, which is turned from the beam's own port frame by 1.6–5.3° depending on the cone; the readers and writers convert. A positive rotation turns the spot clockwise as seen looking along the beam (rotation_sense = :beam, the default): VisRad's deposition at ±30°, 60° and 90° on ten beams matches it within 0.04°, and the other sense is off by 60°.
  • The beam axis. VisRad's axis runs from the port on the chamber wall through the Reference Point; Briareus's is parallel to the port direction. The difference at focus is under 1 µm for millimeter-scale aims.
  • Occultation. Briareus tests each f-cone direction at each point, or, with VisRadProfile, the beam axis through each point. VisRad's shadows are magnified: a point's shadow ray leans away from the axis in proportion to its position in the spot, as if from a source 12–19 mm upstream of focus, and each beam is then rescaled to its full power. Behind a washer or a sphere 5 mm upstream of a foil, the axis test leaves 30 % rms per element against VisRad. VisRad also puts up to 7 × 10⁻⁴ TW/cm² on LEH washers that Briareus leaves dark.
  • Beam totals. VisRad rescales each beam's deposited total: to its full power on surfaces that catch the whole beam, at any incidence, and to a share within ±2.5 % of the geometric one when part of the beam hits nothing. VisRadProfile normalizes each transverse plane instead, which puts 0.2 % too much power on a foil at 40° incidence and 2.2 % at 78°.
  • Capture/clearance report. VisRad's report applies the TaLIS margins as fixed widths around its 1 % and 10 % cones (0.5 mm), with no angular allowance. Its cones match Briareus's envelope (best-focus contour grown by the f-cone) to within 0.05 mm, up to 10 mm from focus. Its incident angle is that of the steepest ray of the f-cone (tilt + 1.38°), as in talis_check. talis_check adds the 0.5° insertion and assembly tolerance to the clearances and the capture margin, where the facility applies it.
  • Laser reflectivity. VisRad can reflect laser light from a surface; Briareus absorbs all of it by default and warns when a workspace object reflects.
  • Unconverted light. Under VisRad's "Fraction of Total Beam Power" model, the power column is the total of the 3ω, 2ω and 1ω cones; the readers take the 3ω share, $P(1 - f_G - f_R)$. Briareus's unconverted-light check (unconverted_light_check) treats the 2ω and 1ω light separately.

Radiation on curved surfaces

Briareus's radiation model integrates over the exact surface a component carries: the cylinder and spheres of hohlraum, and the cylinders, cones and spheres that visrad_target fits to remeshed CAD (fit). On flat facets, the exchange between neighboring elements is misstated by an amount of order one, which shows in the flux next to a laser spot; on the curved surface an integrating sphere is uniform to round-off (Integrating sphere). The research notes do not record VisRad's view-factor quadrature, so how much of this difference appears in a VisRad calculation is not known. When rebuilding a VisRad primitive by hand, note that VisRad's grid size of a sphere, cylinder or cylindrical hohlraum is that of its outer wall, with the inner wall inward by the wall thickness.

Closure and visibility

A discretized view-factor matrix closes only to its quadrature error, and at high albedo the cavity multiplies that error. For a target declared closed (apertures over every opening), Briareus rescales the view factors so that every row sums to one while reciprocity holds (enforce_closure!). hohlraum declares its targets closed; visrad_target does not, and takes closed = true once the apertures close the cavity (open_fraction checks for leaks). Whether VisRad applies a comparable correction is not recorded in the research notes.

Visibility between two elements is determined from their centroids by default (visibility = :centroid); :sampled traces a grid of rays where the centroid rays and probe rays disagree, and is the default for CAD and VisRad targets, whose LEH washers and rims partly shadow elements.

Element values

VisRad's direct laser power source is an element average: centroid values differ from it by up to 13 % next to a spot's peak. Briareus deposits the integral over each element. visrad_deposition multiplies VisRad's values by the element areas, so the two codes compare in TW per element.

What VisRad has that Briareus does not

From the import warnings and ROADMAP ("VisRad import: what is skipped", "CAD", "GUI requests"):

  • STEP CAD import (Briareus reads STL and the CAD bundled in workspaces), and the NIF CMT XML.
  • Most of VisRad's primitives: cylinders, cones, disks, boxes, polygons, halfraums, rugby and slotted hohlraums, surfaces of revolution, multi-element objects, wall thickness and partial spheres. Briareus has its own mesh builders for most of these shapes, but read_visrad_workspace skips them with a warning.
  • From workspaces: unconverted 2ω and 1ω beams, time-dependent beam powers and energy-normalized beams, DPR (split OMEGA beams), pointing toward a diagnostic port or in target-mount frames, and the time-dependent tables of albedo, conversion, lengths and power sources. Briareus has its own time-dependent models (above), but does not import VisRad's.
  • Laser reflection, and power self-emission.
  • The blend toward a square beam far from focus (zero for NIF's beams).

What Briareus adds (gradient-based optimization, laser–plasma corrections, the moving wall and closing holes, a synthetic Dante, worst-case clearance and NIF's target-configuration rules) is summarized on the home page.

Porting a VisRad study

The code below is a sketch of the usual sequence, not run by the documentation build: it needs a workspace. File names and component names are placeholders.

1. Read the workspace. With a template beam set, the workspace's pointing, power and spots are applied to the NIF beams by name, which keeps their cones as group:

using Briareus

ws = read_visrad_workspace("study.vrw"; template = nif_beams())
ws.objects                  # components: name, kind, folder, albedo, conversion, flags
beams = ws.beams            # 3ω power, aim, defocus and spot per beam
profile = visrad_profile(ws)   # VisRad's beam model, with this workspace's envelopes

2. Check the deposition against VisRad's own. On a workspace that VisRad has computed, a target on VisRad's own elements (no remeshing) takes VisRad's per-element deposition. visrad_deposition maps only onto components on those elements, so this target serves the comparison only; step 3 builds the radiation target:

t0 = visrad_target(ws)
dep = deposit(DepositionOperator(t0, beams; profile), beams)
P = visrad_deposition(ws, t0)          # TW per element, from VisRad's table
wall = elements(t0, :Hohlraum_wall)    # the wall object's name in the workspace
sum(dep.element[wall]), sum(P[wall])

3. Build the radiation target. CAD can hold far more triangles than a view-factor matrix should. Select the parts that bound the cavity, repair their normals, remesh them and keep what bounds the free space around a point in the gas:

t = visrad_target(ws; select = o -> o.folder == "Hohlraum",
                  orient = true, remesh = 0.5, inside = (0.0, 0.0, 2.0),
                  materials = Dict("Hohlraum_wall" => nif_gold()))
open_fraction(t).fraction    # pass closed = true above only if apertures close the cavity
model = RadiationModel(t)

4. Solve and analyze.

op = DepositionOperator(t, beams; profile)    # or the default f-cone model
sol = solve(model, deposit(op, beams))
capsule = elements(t, :Capsule)
a = legendre_moments(sol.H, t, capsule)
a[3] / a[1]                                   # P2/P0

5. Time-dependent powers. VisRad's power file becomes a pulse per beam, keyed by beam name, and the drive history evaluates the beams at each time (every beam needs a column in the file):

pulses = read_visrad_powers("powers.csv"; template = beams)
beams_at(time) = with_pulses(beams, pulses, time; by = :beam)
history = drive_history(model, op, beams_at, 0.0:0.25:9.0; observe = :Capsule)
history.moments[3, :] ./ history.moments[1, :]   # P2/P0 against time

6. Optimize and write back. Re-point and re-balance by cone, then write the design into a copy of the original workspace, whose components, spots and settings stay VisRad's, and as an SST file:

pointing = GroupPointing(beams; fields = (:z, :power), relative = true)
scenario = Scenario(t, pointing; observe = :Capsule, deposition_kw = (; profile))
x0 = initial_parameters(pointing)
drive = mean_flux(evaluate(scenario, x0))
objective = symmetry_objective(; drive)   # the direct penalty is on `observe`
lower, upper = bounds(pointing, x0; power = 0.3)
result = optimize_pointing(scenario, objective; x0, lower, upper)
design = apply(pointing, result.x)
write_visrad_workspace("study_repointed.vrw", "study.vrw", design)
write_visrad_sst("study_repointed_sst.csv", design)

7. Benchmark a case in both codes. For a target built in Briareus (target, here a hohlraum with a component named capsule), the kit holds what VisRad needs to rebuild the case and Briareus's results; VisRad's element and power summary exports are then compared quantity by quantity:

kit = write_visrad_kit("kit", target, design; observe = :capsule)
# ... build and run the case in VisRad from kit/README.txt, export its tables ...
compare_visrad("kit"; elements = "capsule_elements.txt",
               power_summary = "power_summary.psd",
               names = Dict("hohlraum_barrel" => "wall"), io = stdout)

The research notes ("VisRad interchange formats" and the sections after it) record how each format detail was established, its confidence, and the items still to confirm on real files ("To verify with real files at work").
