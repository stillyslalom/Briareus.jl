Laser–plasma models

Corrections applied between the beam setup and the deposition, differentiable in power and Δλ: per-cone, time-dependent Backscatter; an empirical CalibratedTransfer (transfer as a function of Δλ); a reduced kinetic CBET model at the LEHs (multi-species Maxwellian ion-acoustic response, flow Doppler shift, polarization smoothing and a smooth δn/n saturation clamp), whose multiplier, flow and clamp calibrate_cbet fits to transfer data; TwoZoneCBET, which separates the flowing plasma outside the LEH from the stagnant one inside, with calibrations to published HYDRA transfer for HYBRID-E shots; and inner-beam absorption in the gold bubble (BubbleAbsorption), whose picket fluence ratio picket_ratio can take from the outer picket fluence of a pointing on the wall (picket_fluence). PicketLinkedBubble takes it from the beams inside every evaluation, so that the bubble follows a re-pointed design, and bubble_prediction reports what a re-pointing does to the picket, the bubble and the P2 swing. picket_irradiance gives the irradiance of the outer cones in a phase, and picket_ceiling bounds it in a constrained optimization. The irradiance is the peak, the energy-weighted mean, the spatially averaged value of the published scalings, or the mean near the centerlines of the inner beams. picket_hot_elements lists the hottest elements. lpi_deposit applies them together. The CBET model assumes uniform LEH plasma and full pairwise overlap, and its unsaturated coupling overpredicts transfer: its magnitudes hold only after calibration against shot data or reference transfer points.

Briareus.CalibratedTransfer — Type
CalibratedTransfer(donors, receivers, fraction)

Empirical cross-beam transfer. A fraction of the total power of the donors groups is moved to the receivers groups and shared in proportion to the power of each receiving beam. fraction(Δλ, t) is a calibration, e.g. a fit to shot data, where Δλ = ⟨dlambda⟩_receivers − ⟨dlambda⟩_donors (Å, power-weighted means), so a positive Δλ means the receivers are red-shifted relative to the donors, the direction that drives transfer toward them. Total power is conserved.

At zero power the transfer is evaluated at its limit. Nothing is transferred from donors without power (f · 0), and their ⟨dlambda⟩ is then the unweighted mean, so the derivatives with respect to their powers are finite. Nothing is transferred to receivers without power, since the light is shared in proportion to their powers, and the beams are then returned unchanged.

Build fraction with linear_transfer or tabulated_transfer, or pass any differentiable function.

Briareus.BubbleAbsorption — Type
BubbleAbsorption(; radius, turn_on = 0.0, fill = 0.3, picket_ratio = 1.0, kwargs...)

Inner-beam absorption in the wall bubble (a LaserPlasmaModel). The picket of the outer cones launches a bubble of wall plasma from the wall under their footprint. Its tip has moved

x_bal(t) = v_b · max(0, t − t_L),   t_L = turn_on + delay,
v_b = speed · E^0.2 · (fill / 0.3)^-0.2

in from the initial wall radius R (mm, ns) while it is ballistic. E = picket_ratio is the picket fluence of the outer cones over that of the calibration platform, the HDC basecamp of Izumi 2021, and is dimensionless. fill is the gas fill in mg/cc. E is set by hand by default. picket_ratio computes it from the picket fluence of a pointing on the wall (picket_fluence), for remake(m; picket_ratio), and PicketLinkedBubble computes it for the beams of every evaluation; both are opt-in. The tip stagnates smoothly at the depth stagnation $x_{max}$ (bubble_depth). It is ballistic up to $x_{max} - w$ (w = stagnation_width), then at $x_{max} - w e^{-(x_{bal} - x_{max} + w)/w}$. In the plane z_b of the outer footprint, at the axial position where the beam axes of the footprint cones reach the wall, the bubble fills the annulus from R in to radius R − $x_{bub}$. Each inner beam loses

absorbed = f · (1 − T_b),
T_b = exp(−τ_b (fill/0.3)^q (x_bub/x_ref)^p (R/(R − x_bub))²),

where f is the fraction of its power crossing z_b inside the bubble and T_b the transmission of the bubble. f is computed from the beam itself, with its best-focus spot carried along its ncone f-cone directions, as in the deposition. T_b uses the opacity $τ_b$, the exponent q = density_exponent of the fill density and the exponent p = depth_exponent of the depth. The absorbed power is conducted to the wall under the outer footprint (Ralph et al. 2018). It is shared equally by the footprint beams in the hemisphere of the inner beam, those whose port is on the same side, so it is deposited where those beams land. Beam powers are conserved, since the model moves power between beams and loses none. The fraction of the footprint beams that misses the wall, a few 1e-4 through the entrance holes, applies to the moved power as well.

Ridge stop. After the ablator–wall collision at collision $t_c$, the inner power that leaves the bubble stops at the stagnation ridge, at radius ridge·R about the axis (Izumi 2023), and does not reach the wall. A fraction g(t) of it (ridge_fraction, rising over ±ridge_rise ns about $t_c$) is deposited by lpi_deposit on an axisymmetric band of the ridge_components (the hohlraum wall). The band is centered on the height where the beam axis crosses that radius, Gaussian in z with rms ridge_width (mm), and area-weighted. The heated ridge radiates at the conversion of the wall, from the wall just outside it. The rest of the beam power is deposited as usual. Power is conserved, and the ridge power is booked to the per_beam of the beam. The ridge stop acts only where the deposition goes through lpi_deposit: in drive_history, in Scenario evaluations, in the beam-error sensitivities (mode_sensitivity and the rest of uncertainty.jl) and in influence_matrix.

The edge of the bubble is smoothed over edge (mm, a logistic step), which keeps f smooth in time, in the bubble depth and in the pointing of the beams. Everything is generic in the number type. speed, opacity, stagnation, collision and picket_ratio, and the beams, can carry ForwardDiff duals (remake), so the model can be used in drive_history(...; lpi), Scenario(...; lpi) and calibrations. The model composes with CBET as lpi = (cbet, bubble). The transfer acts first, and the bubble then takes its share of the power in the inner beams.

Keywords, by role (defaults in parentheses):

  • Shot and geometry: radius R (required; the initial wall radius, mm), turn_on (0.0; the turn-on of the outer cones, ns), fill (0.3 mg/cc), picket_ratio E (1.0), capsule_radius $r_{cap}$ (0.908 mm), inner ((:inner23, :inner30)) and footprint ((:outer50,)), the beam groups that lose and receive the power; wall (nothing), a WallMotion that moves the footprint plane with the radiating wall. The axes of the outer beams reach the moved wall nearer the entrance hole, and the bubble tip and the ridge are measured from the initial wall in either case. footprint_z (nothing) fixes |$z_b$| instead.
  • Calibration: speed (0.36 mm/ns), delay (0.25 ns), opacity $τ_b$ (0.111), density_exponent q (1.8), depth_exponent p (0.5) and its reference depth x_ref (1.0 mm).
  • Tip stagnation: stagnation (0.80 R) and stagnation_width (0.08 R), both defaulting to fractions of radius; with stagnation = Inf the tip stays ballistic.
  • Ridge sub-model: collision $t_c$ (derived from the other keywords, below; collision = Inf turns the ridge stop off), ridge (0.73), ridge_width (0.5 mm), ridge_rise (0.2 ns), ridge_components ((:wall,)).
  • Numerics: edge (0.05 mm); spot points, nr (12) equal-power radii by nangle (24) angles per f-cone direction, over ncone (13) directions.

Defaults (research notes, "Hohlraum dynamics calibrated to published data (2026-10-24)"; the data are izumi2021_bubble and izumi2021_absorption):

  • speed 0.36 mm/ns at 0.3 mg/cc and the basecamp picket, ∝ E^0.2 ρ₀^-0.2, and delay 0.25 ns after the outer turn-on are fitted to the 28 measured tip positions of Izumi et al., PoP 28, 022706 (2021) Fig. 8a. The data cover seven HDC SymCap shots at 0.15–0.3 mg/cc with pickets of 0.73–1.5×, and the fit has χ²/dof 0.89 with the ±40 µm of the paper. It gives 0.360 (0.347–0.372) mm/ns and a delay of 0.25 (0.07–0.42) ns, with the two correlated. Meezan et al. 2020 measured 321–461 µm/ns on the same shots.
  • density_exponent q = 1.8: inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption goes as $ρ_{bub}^2$, and Eq. 7 of Izumi has $ρ_{bub}$ = S ρ₀ R/(R − x) with the shock compression S ∝ ρ₀^-0.1, so $ρ_{bub}^2$ ∝ ρ₀^1.8 (R/(R − x))². depth_exponent 0.5 is the chord ($x_{bub}$/1 mm)^0.5 of Eq. 6.
  • opacity $τ_b$ 0.111 is fitted to the inner-power fraction absorbed in the bubble in the 2D HYDRA simulation by Izumi of the 0.3 mg/cc basecamp (Fig. 10a: 0.02, 0.23, 0.55 at 4, 5, 6 ns). The fit uses the clipping that this model computes for NIF beams pointed for a 5.75 mm hohlraum, with the bubble plane at z = ±3 mm as in Izumi (footprint_z = 3.0). The fitted model absorbs 0.03, 0.20, 0.36 and 0.56 at 4, 5, 5.5 and 6 ns, with χ² 1.1 at ±0.03. The statistical range is 0.100–0.124. The plane (2.8–3.2 mm) and the launch move $τ_b$ over 0.085–0.158 but leave the absorbed fraction, the calibrated quantity, within 0.01. $T_b$ at 5.5 ns is then 0.36 at 0.3 mg/cc and 0.58 at 0.15 mg/cc; the model of Izumi gives 0.29 and 0.54, with an annulus for f.
  • stagnation 0.80 R and stagnation_width 0.08 R are engineering values. The tip is exactly ballistic to 0.72 R (2.07 mm in a 2.88 mm hohlraum), past the measured depths at 0.3 mg/cc (2.07 mm at 6.2 ns) and near those at 0.15 mg/cc (up to 2.28 mm, 0.79 R). In Izumi 2023 the tip slows on back-pressure near the axis. A depth of R − 0.73 R, the depth of the ridge at the waist, would stop the tip before it reaches the inner beams at $z_b$, 1.3 mm from the wall.
  • collision $t_c$ = turn_on + 5.5 ns · (R − $r_{cap}$)/(2.875 − 0.908 mm) · E^-0.16 is fitted to Izumi et al., RSI 89, 10K111, 2018. There the inner spots separate at 5.8 ns for the 0.3 ns turn-on of the basecamp, at 5.45 ns with a 1.5× picket, and about 0.4 ns earlier with the 1.18× capsule. The gap between the outer radius of the capsule capsule_radius $r_{cap}$ and the wall gives 0.44 ns for that capsule, so a constant closing speed of ablator and wall plasma, 0.36 mm/ns, accounts for the capsule shot. ridge 0.73 corresponds to the ridge of Izumi 2023 at r ≈ 2.1 mm in a 2.88 mm hohlraum. ridge_width 0.5 mm and ridge_rise 0.2 ns are engineering choices and were not measured.

Briareus.PicketFluence — Type
PicketFluence{T}

The picket fluence of a set of beams on the wall of a target, from picket_fluence:

  • fluence: per element of the target (kJ/mm²; ×1e5 → J/cm²), zero outside elements;
  • elements: the elements counted (those of the named components);
  • energy: the picket energy deposited on them, ∫F dA (kJ). Over a fixed spot area it is the spatially averaged fluence of the published bubble scalings, whose ratio between designs is the energy ratio (the :average of picket_irradiance);
  • mean: the energy-weighted mean fluence ∫F² dA / ∫F dA (kJ/mm²), the summary that picket_ratio uses;
  • peak: the largest element fluence (kJ/mm²), for information;
  • area: the effective footprint area energy / mean = (∫F dA)² / ∫F² dA (mm²), over all counted elements. These include both hemispheres of a hohlraum, so a symmetric pointing has the mean of either side and twice its area.

Briareus.PicketLinkedBubble — Type
PicketLinkedBubble(bubble, target, reference; pulses = nothing, window = nothing,
                   by = :group, power = nothing, groups = (:outer44, :outer50),
                   components = (:wall,), deposition_kw = (;))

A BubbleAbsorption whose picket fluence ratio E follows the beams it is applied to. The model is an opt-in LaserPlasmaModel. For beams bs,

E(bs) = bubble.picket_ratio × F̄(bs) / F̄(reference),

with F̄ the energy-weighted mean outer picket fluence on the components of target (picket_fluence). The bubble is remake(bubble; picket_ratio = E(bs)), with tip speed ∝ E^0.2 and collision time − turn_on ∝ E^-0.16. bubble.picket_ratio is the E of the reference pointing, set by hand or from picket_ratio against the calibration platform. At the reference the model is therefore identical to bubble. A re-pointed design is given the bubble that its picket grows, inside evaluate(scenario, beams), the constraints and objectives built on it (mode_gate) and drive_history(...; lpi). The model composes as bubble does, for example lpi = (cbet, linked).

The picket map is computed on target. It is the hohlraum on a mesh fine enough for the map (h ≤ 0.2 mm), a separate target used only for the deposition; the radiation mesh of a drive history is usually too coarse (research notes). A deposition operator is built on it for the reference beams in groups (with deposition_kw). The beams that the model is applied to are matched to those beams by name, and they must enter through the same ports. A beam of groups not among them is an error, and a missing one, as in a dropped bundle, deposits nothing. The picket powers are those of picket_irradiance: pulses over a window, for example the mean power over the first ns after the outer turn-on, or power, a function of the beam. One of the two is required. It must not use the power of the beam, which at the time of the model is not the picket power; in a chain after CBET it is not even the setup power. E depends only on the pointing, spots and focus of the beams, so it is constant through a drive history.

Cost: two picket depositions per evaluation, one in apply_lpi and one in lpi_deposit. Generic in the number type. E and the bubble carry the ForwardDiff duals of the beams, so a pointing gradient includes the response of the bubble to the picket fluence. Thread-safe, since the model has no state. The per-beam sensitivities of the uncertainty analysis (mode_sensitivity, mode_uncertainty) deposit beams one at a time and cannot resolve E, so they throw an ArgumentError. The resolved bubble linked_bubble(model, beams) is passed to them instead.

Derived (confidence low). The exponents are published for picket energy and quad splitting on one platform: E^0.2 from Izumi et al., PoP 28, 022706 (2021), on seven shots, and E^-0.16 from Izumi et al., RSI 89, 10K111 (2018). Their use for a fluence changed by re-pointing at fixed energy has no published test. F̄ counts the overlap of the spots, as the split-quad shot of Izumi 2021 does. The energy over a fixed area used in the papers (the :average of picket_irradiance) does not, and it differs from F̄ on uneven powers. See the research notes, "The swing and the bubble without new data (2026-11-18)" §5, and "The published picket fluence, the trough and the inner-beam corridor (2026-11-18)" §5, which measures the link through the fixed area instead. No facility criterion sets the model, and no default uses it.

Briareus.bubble_prediction — Method
bubble_prediction(model::PicketLinkedBubble, design, reference; history = nothing,
                  reference_history = nothing, fixed = false, swing = (4.5, 9.0))
    -> NamedTuple

The prediction of the gold bubble model for a re-pointed design (beams) against a reference pointing. Both are mapped by model, a PicketLinkedBubble with its picket powers, target and bubble. Usually reference is the pointing on which the model was built. The fields are:

  • peak_ratio: the maximum picket fluence (equivalently irradiance) over the elements of the model, design over reference (the peak of picket_fluence);
  • fluence_ratio: the energy-weighted mean picket fluence, design over reference;
  • picket_ratio, reference_picket_ratio: E of each (linked_bubble);
  • speed_ratio: the tip speed of the bubble, design over reference, fluence_ratio^0.2;
  • collision, reference_collision: the ablator–wall collision times (ns);
  • bubble, reference_bubble: the two BubbleAbsorptions; fluence, reference_fluence: the two PicketFluence maps;
  • history: nothing, or, with history, the P2/P0 of the capsule drive through time with the bubble linked.

history(beams, bubble) runs a drive history of beams (the result of drive_history, with lmax ≥ 2) with the laser–plasma chain built on bubble, for example (bs, bub) -> drive_history(model, τ -> with_pulses(bs, pulses, τ), times; lpi = (cbet, bub)). It is run for the design with its bubble and for the reference with a separate bubble. reference_history, a result of the same call for the reference, replaces that second run. With fixed = true it is also run for the design with the bubble of the reference, which splits the effect of the pointing into the parts through the deposition and through the bubble. history then contains time, p2, reference_p2 and fixed_p2 (or nothing). For each run (design, reference, fixed) it gives the minimum of P2/P0 in the swing over the swing window (ns), the time at which it is reached (at), and the upward zero crossing after it (linear between steps; NaN if none). It also gives shift, the value of the design minus that of the reference, and with fixed, through_bubble (design − fixed) and through_deposition (fixed − reference).

Derived (confidence low). The response of the bubble to the pointing rests on the published exponents (tip speed ∝ E^0.2, Izumi 2021; collision ∝ E^-0.16, Izumi 2018) applied to a fluence changed by re-pointing, which has no published test (PicketLinkedBubble). Ratios between pointings on one mesh converge to < 1% (research notes, "The swing and the bubble without new data (2026-11-18)" §5).

Briareus.picket_ceiling — Method
picket_ceiling(reference, names...; pulses = nothing, window = nothing, by = :group,
               power = nothing, groups = (:outer44, :outer50), lpi = false,
               measure = :peak, area = 53.0, corridor = 1.3, edge = 0.3,
               inner = (:inner23, :inner30),

               scale = limit, exact = true, reference_names = names) -> Constraint

A ceiling on the laser irradiance of the beams in groups over the elements of the named parts or components, as a Constraint for optimize_pointing named :picket_<measure>. The irradiance is that of picket_irradiance, whose keywords set the powers, the beams and the measure, the same for the reference and the design. By default the limit is the value of the reference evaluation; a number in TW/mm² sets another limit.

  • measure = :peak: the maximum irradiance over the elements. The optimizer works on the smooth area-weighted p-norm of the irradiance, scaled to the peak of the reference, as irradiance_ceiling does at the time of the scenario. With exact = true (the default) the run enforces the peak itself. The working limit of the stand-in is lowered while the peak exceeds the limit, and the peak is reported against the limit (check_constraints, PointingResult.constraints).
  • measure = :weighted: the energy-weighted mean irradiance, the quantity that picket_ratio carries into the tip speed of BubbleAbsorption (∝ ratio^0.2). It is smooth and enforced directly.
  • measure = :average: the spatially averaged irradiance over a fixed area, the quantity of the published bubble scalings. Against the reference it is the absorbed power on the parts over that of the reference. It is linear in the powers and enforced directly.
  • measure = :corridor: the mean irradiance near the centerlines of the inner beams of the design, which move with its aims. It is smooth and enforced directly.

A reference on another target. For :peak the reference evaluation must be on the same target as the optimized scenario; a subset of the scenario shares the target. Otherwise evaluating the constraint throws an ArgumentError. The reason is that the peak follows one element and the mesh, and the scale of the stand-in is the ratio of peak to norm on the reference mesh. The three integral measures are valid across meshes and accept a reference on another target, such as a hohlraum without the windows of the design or a separate mesh for the reference. They are:

  • :average, the absorbed power over a fixed area, which is a quantity of the field and does not depend on the mesh;
  • :weighted, an integral that converges as O(h²) with the element size h. Targets should be meshed at the same h, where the bias of about 1% at 0.2 mm cancels in the ratio;
  • :corridor, an area mean.

On an evaluation of another target, the constraint measures the parts names of that target, and the limit is the value of the reference over reference_names (by default names). A design with windows in its mesh can then hold the light on its wall and windows at the light of the reference on its wall, with picket_ceiling(reference, :wall, :window; reference_names = (:wall,), measure = :average).

The laser–plasma model is used only with lpi = true. The reference, on the same target or another, must then have the model and time of the scenario, as for irradiance_ceiling and mode_gate. Evaluating the constraint with another model, or at another time with a model, throws an ArgumentError. The constraint performs one deposition per evaluation, in the number type of the evaluation. Inside optimize_pointing, with the beams at their present powers (no pulses or power), no deposition is needed. The element powers of the groups are then kept from the same pass of the deposition in the evaluation, with identical sums. The exceptions are a laser–plasma model of the scenario that deposits light itself, and one that changes the beams with lpi = false. For a phase other than that of the optimized scenario, such as a trough or a foot, the powers of the phase are given by pulses and window or by power, or the constraint is built on an evaluation of that phase.

Derived; no facility criterion sets the limit. The picket fluence is the measure of the published bubble scalings, and holding it at the value of a reference was chosen for a re-pointing study (user, 2026-11-18). That study limits first its peak, as an engineering limit on the hottest element of the picket, then the published :average in the picket, the trough and the second step, with the :corridor reported. The model predicts the bubble of a design from the ratio of the means to the 0.2 power (PicketLinkedBubble, bubble_prediction), with low confidence (research notes, "The swing and the bubble without new data (2026-11-18)" §5, and "The published picket fluence, the trough and the inner-beam corridor (2026-11-18)").

Briareus.picket_fluence — Method
picket_fluence(op, beams; groups = (:outer44, :outer50), components = (:wall,),
               duration = 1.0) -> PicketFluence
picket_fluence(op, beams, pulses, window; by = :group, groups, components)

The picket fluence F (energy per area, kJ/mm²) of the beams in groups (the outer cones) on the elements of components, and its summaries (PicketFluence). components are part or component names, as for elements. The first form takes the power of each beam as its picket power (TW) over a picket of duration ns, or, with duration = 1, as its picket energy (kJ). The second form integrates the pulse of each beam (pulses keyed as for with_pulses, with by) over window = (t0, t1) ns. An example window is the first ns after the outer turn-on, the convention of the research notes for the pickets of the basecamp and N221204; the same window should be used in a design and its reference. The other beams are deposited at zero power. Deposition is linear in power, so F is the deposition of those beams at their picket energies over the area of each element. It is the fluence on the wall, with the incidence and the overlap of the spots. Backscatter or transfer in the picket can be taken off the beams first; Ralph et al. 2018 use the picket energy net of outer backscatter.

The summaries. Every published bubble scaling is written in the picket fluence of the outer cones, as a nominal energy over a nominal area:

  • Ralph et al., PoP 25, 082701 (2018) use the outer picket energy net of backscatter over the outer phase-plate spot area (speed ∝ I^0.36).
  • Callahan et al., PoP 25, 056305 (2018) use the outer picket energy over the one-side outer spot area, 53 mm² (size ∝ (E/A)^0.5).
  • Izumi et al., PoP 28, 022706 (2021) use the spatially averaged outer picket fluence over the first ns (tip position ∝ E^0.2). It is 27% lower when the quads and cones are split at the same energy.

Over a fixed area that quantity is energy, whose ratios between designs are the energy ratios (the :average of picket_irradiance). mean = ∫F² dA / ∫F dA is the energy over the effective area (∫F dA)²/∫F² dA, and it also counts the overlap of the spots. One isolated spot gives its energy over its effective area. For a flat top that is exactly E/A; for a super-Gaussian of order n it is 2^(-2/n) of the peak fluence, and half the peak for a Gaussian. k coincident spots give k times the value of one, and separated spots the same value as one. mean therefore includes the split-quad change of Izumi 2021, which a fixed area does not. As an integral, it is smooth in the pointing and nearly independent of the mesh. The two measures differ when the picket energies of the beams are uneven or their spots move onto one another (research notes, "The published picket fluence, the trough and the inner-beam corridor (2026-11-18)"). peak follows one element and the mesh, and it measures neither quantity.

Mesh. An element average lowers ∫F² dA by about h²/(6a²) for a Gaussian spot of 1/e radius a on elements of size h. mean therefore converges as O(h²) and is low on a coarse mesh. With the NIF outer spots (0.67 × 0.39 mm 1/e semi-axes) on NIF hohlraums, it is about 1% low at h = 0.2 mm and 0.3% low at 0.1 mm, and its ratio between two hohlraums is 0.35% high at 0.2 mm (research notes). A design and its reference should be compared on the same mesh size, where most of the error cancels in the ratio. A radiation mesh of 0.8 mm is too coarse for the map, and a separate target used only for the deposition is cheap.

Generic in the number type of the beams (ForwardDiff duals through the pointing). Derived: a link built on the published definitions and exponents. No facility criterion sets it.

Briareus.picket_irradiance — Method
picket_irradiance(ev, names...; pulses = nothing, window = nothing, by = :group,
                  power = nothing, groups = (:outer44, :outer50), lpi = false,
                  measure = :peak, area = 53.0, corridor = 1.3, edge = 0.3,
                  inner = (:inner23, :inner30)) -> Real

The laser irradiance (TW/mm²) of the beams of an Evaluation in groups (the outer cones by default) on the elements of the named parts or components. measure selects the summary:

  • :peak: the largest over the elements;
  • :weighted: the energy-weighted mean ∫I² dA / ∫I dA, the summary that picket_ratio and PicketLinkedBubble use (picket_fluence);
  • :average: the spatially averaged irradiance over a fixed spot area, which is the power absorbed on the elements over area (mm²) and the quantity of the published bubble scalings (below);
  • :corridor: the mean irradiance on the elements near the centerlines of the beams in inner, ∫w I dA / ∫w dA with a weight w that is 1 within corridor − edge/2 of a centerline and 0 beyond corridor + edge/2 (below).

The irradiance times the length of the phase is its fluence, so the ratios between designs are the fluence ratios. The powers are those of whatever phase the evaluation represents: the picket, the trough or the foot.

The powers. With pulses (keyed as for with_pulses, with by) and window = (t0, t1) (ns), the power of each beam is its mean power over the window. With power, a function of the beam that returns its power (TW), the power can be set, for example, by cone from a picket table. With neither, the power of each beam is used, as in an evaluation run at the powers of a phase, or with power = b -> r * b.power to scale them. The other beams are deposited at zero power, by the deposition operator of the scenario with the setup beams of the evaluation (ev.beams, which set the pointing, spots and focus). With lpi = true the beams are instead the effective beams of the evaluation (ev.effective), after the laser–plasma model of the scenario at its time, for example net of a Backscatter and after a transfer. They are used at their present powers, and pulses and power are then an error. Otherwise the laser–plasma model is not used, and backscatter or transfer is taken off the powers given. Each call performs one deposition. On an evaluation inside optimize_pointing that already contains the element powers of the groups at their present powers (picket_ceiling), no deposition is needed. Generic in the number type (ForwardDiff duals through the pointing and powers).

The published quantity (:average). The published scalings use these definitions:

  • Callahan et al., PoP 25, 056305 (2018) divide the picket energy of the outer cones by the one-side outer spot area of the NIF phase plates, 53 mm². That area is the default area, so that the irradiance times the picket length in ns is their E/A in kJ/mm².
  • Ralph et al., PoP 25, 082701 (2018) take the outer picket energy net of backscatter over the outer spot area.
  • Izumi et al., PoP 28, 022706 (2021) take the spatially averaged outer picket fluence over the first ns.

In Briareus terms, the quantity is the power absorbed on the named parts, over a fixed area. For the wall, light clipped on the outside of the entrance holes, light landing on the capsule and light leaving the target do not grow the bubble on the wall (L). The power is net of backscatter when the powers are, either with lpi = true and a backscatter model or with powers net of backscatter. A ratio to a reference does not depend on the area, and an absolute value uses the convention of Callahan. A fixed area does not count the overlap of the spots, which :weighted does. The two measures differ for uneven powers between beams and for spots moved onto one another (research notes, "The published picket fluence, the trough and the inner-beam corridor (2026-11-18)").

The inner-beam corridor (:corridor). Izumi et al., PoP 30, 032706 (2023) found that outer bubble spikes in line with the 23.5° beams clip those beams and make the core more oblate. The corridor measures the outer light where the inner beams pass. A centerline is the line through the aim of a beam along its direction, for each beam of inner with nonzero power in the evaluation. The distance is that from the element centroid, in 3-D, to the nearest centerline. The weight is one minus the product over the centerlines of (1 − s), where s rises from 0 to 1 across the edge as a quintic smoothstep. The weight is therefore smooth (C²) in the aims of the inner beams as they move, and exactly 0 or 1 away from the edge; elements beyond every edge are skipped. corridor = 1.3 mm and edge = 0.3 mm are engineering defaults. The value 1.3 mm was chosen for a re-pointing study (user, 2026-11-18). The edge is wider than an element of a 0.2 mm deposition map, so that the mean changes smoothly as centroids cross it. Neither value is a published criterion.

The peak follows one element and the mesh, since on a coarser mesh the hottest spot is averaged over more area. The other three measures are integrals, nearly independent of the mesh (picket_fluence). picket_hot_elements lists the hottest elements with their distance to the nearest inner centerline.

Briareus.picket_ratio — Method
picket_ratio(design::PicketFluence, reference::PicketFluence; at = 1.0) -> E

The picket_ratio E of BubbleAbsorption for design. E is at times the ratio of the energy-weighted mean picket fluences of the two designs (mean, picket_fluence), so that E follows the pointing (overlap, incidence, spot sizes) and the picket energy.

Relative to what. The tip speed of the bubble (0.36 mm/ns, ∝ E^0.2, Izumi 2021) was fitted at E = 1, on the HDC basecamp of Izumi et al., PoP 28, 022706 (2021). That platform is a 5.75 mm Au hohlraum with unsplit outer quads (research notes, "Hohlraum dynamics calibrated to published data (2026-10-24)"). With reference = basecamp_picket_fluence() and at = 1, E is relative to that calibration. Its pointing is a proxy, since the facility pointing is unpublished, and its picket energy was read from a figure by eye. The absolute E is therefore uncertain by more than its changes between designs. A re-pointing study is better referred to a design whose E is already set: picket_ratio(new, old; at = E_old) changes E through the pointing only. E_old is 1.1 in examples/n221204.jl, from the picket energy and the split quads. Both maps should be computed on the same mesh size.

With remake(bubble; picket_ratio = E), the design then enters the tip speed (∝ E^0.2) and the collision time ($t_c$ − turn_on ∝ E^-0.16). The link is opt-in, and no default uses it; the exponents and calibrated constants are those of the model. Generic in the number type. Derived; no facility criterion sets it. The exponents are published (Izumi 2021: 0.2 ± 0.03 on seven shots), but their application to a re-pointing at fixed energy is not. No re-pointing study is published, and the nearest test is the split-quad shot of Izumi 2021 (research notes).

Briareus.CBET — Type
CBET(; leh_z, ne_nc = 0.06, Te = 5000, Ti = 1500, ions = CH_IONS, mach = 1.0,
     flow = nothing, length = 1.5, polarization = :smoothed, multiplier = 1.0,
     nsteps = 20)

Reduced steady-state CBET among the beams that share each laser entrance hole, applied as a laser–plasma model (apply_lpi, Scenario(...; lpi)).

  • leh_z — z (mm) of the LEH planes; beams whose ports have z > 0 cross at +leh_z, the rest at -leh_z.
  • Plasma in the crossing region: ne_nc (electron density over the critical density at the nominal wavelength), Te, Ti (eV), and ion species ions ((fraction, Z, A) number fractions; default fully ionized CH). Defaults are the NIF LEH conditions of Michel et al. 2009; Ti is not given in that paper and is an assumption.
  • flow — outflow speed along the hohlraum axis (km/s, away from the hohlraum), or mach times the ion sound speed √((Z̄ Te + 3 Ti)/(Ā m_p)) when flow is nothing. The value is signed: a negative mach or flow points into the hohlraum. With outflow the inner (steeper) cones get the larger blue shift, so flow alone moves power from the inner to the outer cones; red-detuned inner cones (dlambda > 0) take power back. TwoZoneCBET puts a supersonic zone outside the LEH and a slow one inside.
  • length — maximum interaction length (mm). A pair crossing at angle θ interacts over min(length, w/sin θ), with w the mean beam width at the LEH plane.
  • polarization — :smoothed for NIF polarization smoothing with uncorrelated speckle, which multiplies the gain by (1+cos²θ)/4, or :parallel for co-polarized beams, which leave the gain unchanged.
  • multiplier — calibration factor on all gains. Fit it to shot data: published NIF designs tune CBET with saturation clamps and power multipliers.
  • clamp — ion-wave saturation level δn/n (nothing = linear theory, which grossly overpredicts transfer at NIF intensities). The coupling of each pair is multiplied by 1/√(1 + (δn/clamp)²), a smooth version of capping the linear δn/n at clamp. Published NIF calibrations range from 3×10⁻⁴ (2011) to 0.002 (foot) / 0.0075 (peak) for N221204 (Kritcher et al., PRE 109, 025204, 2024); the default is 0.002. cbet_clamp_schedule builds the foot/peak schedule from a PulseShape.
  • saturation — how clamp acts: :smooth (default, the factor above) or :min, the hard limit δn = min(clamp, δn_linear) of Strozzi et al., PRL 118, 025002 (2017), i.e. a factor min(1, clamp/δn), as in HYDRA's inline model, so its clamp values carry over one to one. At δn = clamp the smooth factor is √2 lower.

Any plasma parameter and multiplier may be a number, a function of time (ns), or a (times, values) table. The exchange of each pair uses the geometric-mean beam area at the LEH, so total power is conserved. The coupled exchange is integrated with nsteps RK4 steps. Beam detuning comes from Beam.dlambda (Å at the beam wavelength — NIF quotes Δλ at 1ω, so divide those values by 3 for 3ω beams).

The pair coefficients and the exchanges at the two LEHs are computed on threads; the results are identical for every thread count.

The outside zone lies outside the LEH, in the supersonic outflow, where the Doppler shift of the flow moves power from the inner cones to the outer ones even without detuning. The inside zone lies inside the LEH, in slow flow, where the transfer is set by the wavelength detuning; red-detuned inner cones take power from the outer cones.   1. and Mach 0 inside. Both are engineering defaults; neither is measured. In a low-fill

hohlraum the net transfer is the balance of the two. In Pickworth et al. 2020's HYDRA runs, power is transferred from the inner to the outer cones outside the LEH and from the outer to the inner cones inside. The joint three-shot fit of hybride_cbet (same data, model, Te ≤ 5.5 keV bound and fitted hard-minimum clamps) with the Δλ null constraint corrected. NC24's 1–1.55 Å for HYBRID-E is the detuning that minimized hot-spot P2 in the 2020–21 shots, which also compensates the bubble absorption of the inner beams, so it lies above the transfer null. The published statement on the null itself is I-Raum's (0.5 Å "mostly to negate the flow-induced CBET", NC24). Here the null at N221204's peak (5.6 and 7.0 ns) is held in 0.25–0.5 Å. The lower edge was chosen in Briareus so that a flow-driven transfer remains at Δλ = 0.

The outside zone has Mach 1.49 (± 0.8) and multiplier 0.072 (×/÷ 5), and the inside zone Mach 0 and multiplier 1.60 (± 31%). The fitted clamps are 9.1×10⁻⁴ (foot, not binding, unconstrained) and 1.4×10⁻⁴ (peak, binding, ± 29%). The peak clamp lies below the 5×10⁻⁴–7.5×10⁻³ of HYDRA decks and is correlated with the inside multiplier and Te (correlations −0.92 to −0.97). The fitted Te(t) is 3.2 keV at 1 ns, 4.5 at 4, 2.5–3.8 in the peak and 5.5 (the bound) at 8 ns (± 7–15%).

The fit (107 rows, 0.8–8.4 ns) has a cone-fraction rms of 0.024 (N221204), 0.016 (N220919) and 0.028 (N210808), and 0.024 over all shots, against 0.027 / 0.016 / 0.047 and 0.033 over all shots for hybride_cbet. Its loss is 0.059, against 0.120 with the null in 1–1.55 Å. The null is 0.24 ± 0.20 Å at 5.6 ns (0.29 at 7 ns), and dCF/dΔλ is 0.137 ± 0.011 per Å at peak (2.5–2.75 Å), linear from 1 Å. HYDRA's cross-shot response stays 2–3× flatter, which is not supported by the measured hot-spot P2 sensitivity (research notes). delay and leh_z are as in hybride_cbet.

Derived (research notes, "Joint multi-shot CBET calibration: the null"): the parameters are effective values fitted to HYDRA and do not describe a measured plasma state.

Briareus.pulse_rise — Method
pulse_rise(pulse; lookback = 1.0, delta = 0.15) -> t

Start of the rise to peak power (ns) of pulse, a PulseShape or (times, powers). The start is the knee where the tangent at the half-maximum crossing t½ (slope over t½ ± delta) meets the power lookback ns before t½, the step the rise leaves. It is invariant to a scale factor on the power, so delivered and HYDRA-degraded inputs give nearly the same time: 4.11 ns on N221204's delivered pulse (n221204_pulse), 4.0 on PRE 109 Fig. 9's degraded input powers, where the HYDRA deck switches its CBET clamp ("until the beginning of the rise to peak power", 3.9–4.1 ns). This is an engineering definition for pulses with one main rise.
